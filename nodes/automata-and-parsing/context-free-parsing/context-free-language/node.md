A context-free language is a set of strings that some [context-free grammar](/context-free-grammar/) generates. The same class is the set of languages accepted by nondeterministic [pushdown automata](/pushdown-automaton/), so a context-free language is what a finite control with one unbounded stack can recognize. The class strictly contains the [regular languages](/regular-language/) and is the standard model of the nested syntax of programming languages, from brackets to blocks to expressions.

## Definition

Fix a finite [alphabet](/alphabet/) $\Sigma$. A language $L \subseteq \Sigma^*$ is **context-free** if $L = L(G)$ for some context-free grammar $G = (V, \Sigma, P, S)$, where $L(G) = \{w \in \Sigma^* : S \Rightarrow^* w\}$.

We use the grammar notation of the [context-free grammar](/context-free-grammar/) page: productions $A \to \alpha \mid \beta$, $\varepsilon$ for the empty string, $S$ for the start symbol, $n$ for the length of an input string and $|G|$ for the total length of the productions.

**Equivalent definitions.** For each $L \subseteq \Sigma^*$ the following are equivalent:

1. $L = L(G)$ for a context-free grammar $G$;
2. $L = N(M)$, the set of strings that empty the stack of some nondeterministic pushdown automaton $M$;
3. $L = L(M)$, the set of strings that bring some nondeterministic pushdown automaton $M$ to a final state;
4. $L = h(D_k \cap R)$ for some $k$, some regular language $R$ and some homomorphism $h$, where $D_k$ is the [Dyck language](/dyck-language/) on $k$ pairs of brackets.

The equivalence of 1, 2 and 3 is the [equivalence of CFGs and PDAs](/cfg-pda-equivalence/). The equivalence of 1 and 4 is the [Chomsky-Schützenberger theorem](/chomsky-schutzenberger-theorem/).

A context-free language is **deterministic** when a deterministic pushdown automaton accepts it by final state; see [deterministic context-free language](/deterministic-context-free-language/). It is **inherently ambiguous** when every grammar for it is [ambiguous](/ambiguity/); see [inherent ambiguity](/inherent-ambiguity/).

## Examples and non-examples

**Context-free:**

- $\{a^k b^k : k \ge 0\}$, by $S \to aSb \mid \varepsilon$. It is not regular, so the inclusion of the regular languages in the context-free ones is strict.
- Every regular language. A DFA with states $q$ and moves $\delta(q, a) = p$ becomes the grammar with a nonterminal $Q$ for each state $q$, a production $Q \to aP$ for each move, and $Q \to \varepsilon$ for each accepting state. A derivation from the start state's nonterminal follows a run of the DFA.
- The [Dyck language](/dyck-language/) of balanced brackets, by $S \to SS \mid (\,S\,) \mid \varepsilon$.
- The palindromes $\{w \in \{a, b\}^* : w = w^R\}$, by $S \to aSa \mid bSb \mid a \mid b \mid \varepsilon$.
- $\{a^i b^j c^k : i = j \text{ or } j = k\}$, by $S \to XC \mid AY$, $X \to aXb \mid \varepsilon$, $C \to cC \mid \varepsilon$, $A \to aA \mid \varepsilon$, $Y \to bYc \mid \varepsilon$. Its words of length at most 9 number 56. Each grammar for it is ambiguous; with this one, $abc$ has two parse trees, one through $X$ and one through $Y$. It is the standard [inherently ambiguous](/inherent-ambiguity/) language.
- The arithmetic expressions over $+$, $*$, parentheses and one operand $a$, by the grammar on the [context-free grammar](/context-free-grammar/) page.

**Not context-free:**

- $\{a^k b^k c^k : k \ge 0\}$. Pump a word $a^p b^p c^p$, with $p$ the constant of the [pumping lemma for context-free languages](/cfl-pumping-lemma/): the two pumped pieces lie within a window of length at most $p$, so they touch at most two of the three letters, and pumping up breaks the equality with the third.
- $\{ww : w \in \{a, b\}^*\}$, the copy language. Intersect it with the regular language $a^* b^* a^* b^*$ to get $\{a^i b^j a^i b^j\}$, and pump $a^p b^p a^p b^p$. Because context-free languages are closed under intersection with regular languages, a context-free copy language would make this intersection context-free.
- $\{a^m : m \text{ is prime}\}$ and $\{a^{m^2} : m \ge 0\}$. By [Parikh's theorem](/parikh-theorem/), every context-free language over one letter is regular, and these two are not.

**Closure, and the lack of it.** The class is closed under union, concatenation, Kleene star, reversal, homomorphism, inverse homomorphism and intersection with a regular language ([closure properties](/cfl-closure-properties/)). It is not closed under intersection: $\{a^i b^i c^j\}$ and $\{a^j b^i c^i\}$ are context-free, and their intersection is $\{a^i b^i c^i\}$, which is not. It is then not closed under complement either, because $L_1 \cap L_2 = \overline{\overline{L_1} \cup \overline{L_2}}$ and the class is closed under union.

**Decision questions.** For a grammar $G$ and a string $w$, membership $w \in L(G)$ is decidable in $O(n^3 |G|)$ time by the [CYK algorithm](/cyk-algorithm/) after a conversion to [Chomsky normal form](/chomsky-normal-form/) ([membership problem](/cfg-membership-problem/)). Emptiness ([CFG emptiness](/cfg-emptiness-problem/)) and finiteness ([CFG finiteness](/cfg-finiteness-problem/)) are decidable in time polynomial in $|G|$. Equivalence of two grammars, $L(G) = \Sigma^*$, ambiguity of a grammar and emptiness of $L(G_1) \cap L(G_2)$ are undecidable ([equivalence](/cfg-equivalence-undecidable/), [ambiguity](/cfg-ambiguity-undecidable/), [intersection](/cfl-intersection-undecidable/)).

## Related definitions

- [Regular languages](/regular-language/) are the context-free languages of right-linear grammars, and the languages of finite automata.
- [Deterministic context-free languages](/deterministic-context-free-language/) are a proper subclass. They are exactly the languages of [LR(k) grammars](/lr-grammar/) by [Knuth's theorem](/knuth-lr-theorem/), and they are closed under complement.
- A language is [inherently ambiguous](/inherent-ambiguity/) when no unambiguous grammar generates it; every deterministic context-free language has an unambiguous grammar.
- The [pumping lemma for context-free languages](/cfl-pumping-lemma/) gives a necessary condition, and [Parikh's theorem](/parikh-theorem/) says that the letter counts of a context-free language form a semilinear set.
- The [Chomsky-Schützenberger theorem](/chomsky-schutzenberger-theorem/) builds every context-free language from a [Dyck language](/dyck-language/), a regular language and a homomorphism.
- [CFL reachability](/cfl-reachability/) uses a context-free language to filter the paths of a graph.
