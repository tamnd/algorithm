---
title: After Goldstein's cuts every closed path of free edges has a zero sum of wrapped differences, so the flood fill is independent of the path, congruent to the data and exact on every free edge
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

Notation. The image has $m \times n$ pixels and $N = mn$. A primal edge joins two neighbouring pixels. For an edge walked from $a$ to $b$, $g(a, b) = W(\psi(b) - \psi(a))$, so $g(b, a) = -g(a, b)$ except when $\psi(b) - \psi(a) = \pm\pi$ exactly; we assume, as the code does implicitly, that no wrapped difference is exactly $\pm \pi$ (this holds with probability one for noisy data). A square $f$ is a $2 \times 2$ block of pixels, and $q(f)$ is its charge from line L1: the sum of $g$ around $f$ in the fixed orientation, divided by $2\pi$. The cut graph has the squares as nodes and a node for the outside; two squares (or a square and the outside) are joined when the primal edge between them is blocked. Line L10 runs on the free (not blocked) edges.

**Lemma 1 (charges).** $q(f)$ is an integer, and $|q(f)| \le 1$.

*Proof.* Around $f$, the true differences $\psi(b) - \psi(a)$ sum to zero, and each $g$ differs from them by a multiple of $2\pi$, so the sum of $g$ is a multiple of $2\pi$. Each of the four terms lies in $(-\pi, \pi)$, so the sum lies in $(-4\pi, 4\pi)$, and the multiple is $-1$, $0$ or $1$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (loop sums are enclosed charge).** For a closed walk $\gamma$ along primal edges, $\sum_{\gamma} g = 2\pi \sum_f w_\gamma(f)\, q(f)$, where $w_\gamma(f)$ is the winding number of $\gamma$ around the center of $f$.

*Proof.* Consider the integer combination $\Gamma = \sum_f w_\gamma(f)\, \partial f$ of square boundaries. An edge $e$ lies between two squares $f$ and $f'$ (or between a square and the outside, whose winding number is 0), and its coefficient in $\Gamma$ is $w_\gamma(f) - w_\gamma(f')$ with the sign of the orientation. Crossing $e$ changes the winding number by the net number of times $\gamma$ traverses $e$, so this coefficient equals the net number of traversals of $e$ by $\gamma$. So $\gamma$ and $\Gamma$ traverse every edge the same net number of times, and since $g$ is antisymmetric, $\sum_\gamma g = \sum_f w_\gamma(f) \sum_{\partial f} g = 2\pi \sum_f w_\gamma(f) q(f)$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the cut invariant).** When line L9 is done, every connected component $K$ of the cut graph that contains a residue either has total charge $q(K) = \sum_{f \in K} q(f) = 0$ or contains the outside node.

*Proof.* Lines L2 to L9 put every residue in exactly one tree: a residue joins a tree only if it is not yet in one, and the loop of line L2 starts a tree at every residue that is not yet in one. Line L6 joins the new residue $u$ to a member $t$ of the tree by a staircase path of squares, and blocks the edge crossed by each step, so $t$ and $u$ are in one component of the cut graph. So each tree lies inside one component. A tree ends in one of two states. Either its charge counter is 0, and then the sum of its charges is 0, since the counter adds the charge of each residue when it joins. Or line L7 or line L9 has cut a member of the tree to the border, along a straight path whose last step blocks a boundary edge, which joins the tree to the outside node. A component $K$ is a union of whole trees and of squares without charge. If one of its trees is cut to the border, $K$ contains the outside node. Otherwise all its trees have charge sum 0, and so $q(K) = 0$. $\square$

**Theorem 1 (path independence).** For every closed walk $\gamma$ that uses only free edges, $\sum_\gamma g = 0$.

*Proof.* Two squares joined by a blocked edge $e$ have the same winding number, because $\gamma$ does not use $e$ and crossing $e$ does not change $w_\gamma$. In the same way a square joined to the outside by a blocked boundary edge has $w_\gamma = 0$. So $w_\gamma$ is constant on each component $K$ of the cut graph, with the value 0 when $K$ contains the outside node. By Lemma 2, $\sum_\gamma g = 2\pi \sum_K w_\gamma(K)\, q(K)$, summed over the components that contain a residue (the other squares have charge 0). By Lemma 3 every term has $q(K) = 0$ or $w_\gamma(K) = 0$. $\square$

**Theorem 2 (what the flood fill returns).** Let $R$ be a region of pixels joined by free edges, and let $s$ be its seed. Line L10 gives (a) $\varphi(b) - \varphi(a) = g(a, b)$ for every free edge $(a, b)$ inside $R$, (b) $\varphi(p) - \psi(p) \in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$ for every $p$, and (c) for another seed $s'$ of $R$, the new result differs from $\varphi$ on $R$ by a constant multiple of $2\pi$.

*Proof.* (a) On the edges of the breadth first search tree, (a) holds by line L10. For any other free edge $(a, b)$ of $R$, the tree path from $s$ to $a$, the edge $(a, b)$ and the tree path from $b$ back to $s$ form a closed walk of free edges; by Theorem 1 its sum of $g$ is 0, and the two tree paths contribute $\varphi(a) - \varphi(s)$ and $\varphi(s) - \varphi(b)$, so $g(a, b) = \varphi(b) - \varphi(a)$. (b) $\varphi(s) = \psi(s)$, and each step of line L10 adds $W(\psi(b) - \psi(a)) \equiv \psi(b) - \psi(a) \pmod{2\pi}$. (c) Both results satisfy (a) on the connected region $R$, so their difference is constant on $R$; by (b) both are congruent to $\psi$, so the constant is a multiple of $2\pi$. $\square$

**Corollary (exact without residues).** If no square has a residue, no edge is blocked, the image is one region, and $\varphi$ is the unique image up to a multiple of $2\pi$ that is congruent to $\psi$ and satisfies $\varphi(b) - \varphi(a) = g(a, b)$ on every edge. If the true phase $\varphi^*$ changes by less than $\pi$ across every edge (Itoh's condition), then $\varphi = \varphi^* + 2\pi k$ for one integer $k$.

*Proof.* Lines L2 to L9 do nothing without residues. Under Itoh's condition, $g(a, b) = \varphi^*(b) - \varphi^*(a)$, so $\varphi - \varphi^*$ is constant by Theorem 2(a), and a multiple of $2\pi$ by Theorem 2(b), since $\varphi^* \equiv \psi$. $\square$

**What the theorems do not say.** The L0 cost of the result, the number of edges with $\varphi(b) - \varphi(a) \ne g(a, b)$, is at most the number of blocked edges by Theorem 2(a), and at least 1 when a residue exists (otherwise Lemma 2 with the boundary of that square gives a contradiction). It is not minimal in general. Minimizing it over congruent results is NP-hard (Chen and Zebker 2000). On the $3 \times 4$ image of the test with charges $+1$ at square $(0, 2)$, $-1$ at $(1, 1)$ and $+1$ at $(1, 0)$, Goldstein's order of search pairs $(0, 2)$ with $(1, 1)$ (two blocked edges) and sends $(1, 0)$ to the border (one more), cost 3, while the cut from $(1, 0)$ to $(1, 1)$ and from $(0, 2)$ to the top border gives cost 2; an exhaustive search over all congruent results confirms the minimum 2. Theorem 2 also says nothing about the correctness of the $2\pi$ multiples on blocked edges; whether the cuts lie where the true phase really jumps is a heuristic matter.

**Theorem 3 (termination and cost).** Let $R$ be the number of residues and $b$ the largest box radius. In the word-RAM model, with a Boolean array for the blocked edges, the algorithm takes $O(N + R b^3 + R \sqrt{N})$ time and $O(N)$ space.

*Proof.* Line L1 visits each square once, $O(N)$. Each residue joins exactly one tree, so the trees have $R$ members in total. A member is scanned with boxes of radius $k \le b$, each of $O(k^2)$ squares, at most once per radius, so the box scans cost $O(R \sum_{k \le b} k^2) = O(R b^3)$. A cut of line L6 joins squares at Chebyshev distance at most $k$, so it blocks at most $2k = O(b)$ edges, and there are at most $R$ such cuts. A cut to the border goes straight to the nearest side, at most $\min(m, n) \le \sqrt{N}$ edges, at most once per tree. Lines L4 to L8 end because $k$ grows to $b$. The flood fill of line L10 enqueues every pixel once and looks at four edges per pixel, $O(N)$. The charges, the tree marks, the blocked edges, the labels and $\varphi$ take $O(N)$ space. $\square$
