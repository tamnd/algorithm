---
title: The cut loop partitions the polygon into at most r + 1 pieces of concavity at most the tolerance
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

Let $P$ be a simple polygon with $n$ vertices and $R$ reflex vertices, and let $\tau \ge 0$. We work in the real RAM, and the code is exact because it uses rational arithmetic. A polygon here is a simple polygon listed counterclockwise with no vertex of a straight angle. The code removes straight angles after each cut, and this changes neither the region nor the reflex vertices. For a polygon $X$ with the hull $H$, the concavity of a vertex $v$ is $0$ if $v$ is a vertex of $H$. Otherwise it is the distance of $v$ to the bridge of $v$, the segment between the nearest vertices of $H$ before and after $v$ along $X$. The concavity of $X$ is the largest concavity of its vertices.

**Lemma 1 (concavity 0 means convex).** A reflex vertex of $X$ has a positive concavity. If $X$ has no reflex vertex, the concavity of $X$ is $0$.

*Proof.* Let $v$ be a vertex of $X$ on the boundary of $H$. There is a closed half-plane that holds $H$ with $v$ on its boundary line. $X$ is in $H$, so near $v$ the interior of $X$ is in the half-plane, and the interior angle at $v$ is at most $\pi$. So a reflex vertex is not on the boundary of $H$. A bridge is an edge of $H$, so it is on the boundary of $H$, and the distance of a reflex vertex to its bridge is positive. If $X$ has no reflex vertex, then all its angles are less than $\pi$, so $X$ is convex, $X = H$, every vertex of $X$ is a vertex of $H$, and every concavity is $0$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (the cut is a chord).** Let $r$ be a reflex vertex of $X$ with the neighbors $p$ before and $m$ after it, let $d$ be $r - p$ or $r - m$, and let $q$ be the first point of the boundary of $X$, other than $r$, on the ray $r + s\,d$ with $s > 0$. Then $q$ exists, the open segment from $r$ to $q$ is in the interior of $X$, and the code finds $q$.

*Proof.* The interior of $X$ is to the left of each edge. So the interior angle $\theta \in (\pi, 2\pi)$ at $r$ is swept counterclockwise from the direction $m - r$ to the direction $p - r$. The direction $r - p$ is at the angle $\theta - \pi$ from $m - r$ in this sweep, and $r - m$ is at the angle $\pi$. Both values are strictly between $0$ and $\theta$, so the ray starts into the interior. $X$ is bounded, so the ray meets its boundary, and $q$ exists. The points of the ray just after $r$ are interior, the open segment from $r$ to $q$ is connected, and it does not meet the boundary, so it stays in the interior. The ray meets the edge $pr$ only at $r$, because it is on the line of that edge and goes away from $p$. It meets the edge $rm$ only at $r$, because $\theta \neq \pi$. So the code can skip these two edges. For each other edge it computes the exact intersection, or the ends of the edge if the edge is on the line of the ray, and it keeps the smallest $s > 0$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (a cut removes a reflex vertex).** Cut $X$ along the segment $rq$ of Lemma 2, with $q$ added as a vertex if it is inside an edge. The result is two polygons $A$ and $B$ with disjoint interiors and $A \cup B = X$, so $\operatorname{area}(A) + \operatorname{area}(B) = \operatorname{area}(X)$, and $A$ and $B$ have together at most $R_X - 1$ reflex vertices, where $R_X$ is the number of reflex vertices of $X$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2, $rq$ is a chord of $X$: its ends are on the boundary and the rest is in the interior. A chord splits a simple polygon into two simple polygons. Their boundaries are the two chains of the boundary of $X$ from $r$ to $q$, each closed by $rq$, and both are counterclockwise. The angles at the vertices other than $r$ and $q$ do not change. At $r$ the angle $\theta$ splits into $\theta - \pi$ and $\pi$, or into $\pi$ and $\theta - \pi$, and both are at most $\pi$, so $r$ is not reflex in $A$ or $B$. If $q$ is inside an edge, the angle $\pi$ at $q$ splits into two positive parts, because the segment does not run along the edge, so both parts are less than $\pi$. If $q$ is a vertex of angle $\varphi$, the angle splits into two positive parts with the sum $\varphi < 2\pi$, so at most one part is more than $\pi$, and none is more than $\pi$ if $\varphi < \pi$. So $r$ stops being reflex, and no vertex becomes reflex. $\square$

**Theorem.** The loop terminates after at most $R$ cuts. It returns at most $R + 1$ polygons with disjoint interiors whose union is $P$, and each has a concavity of at most $\tau$. If $\tau = 0$, each returned polygon is convex.

*Proof.* The invariant is: the polygons in the queue and the output together have disjoint interiors and the union $P$. It holds at the start, when the queue holds only $P$. Moving a polygon to the output keeps it. When the loop takes a polygon $X$ with a concavity above $\tau \ge 0$, Lemma 1 gives a reflex vertex of $X$, so line L6 has a choice, and Lemma 3 replaces $X$ by two polygons with the same union and disjoint interiors, so the invariant holds again. Let $\Phi$ be the total number of reflex vertices in the queue. At the start $\Phi = R$, a cut lowers $\Phi$ by at least 1 by Lemma 3, and moving a polygon to the output does not raise it. So there are at most $R$ cuts. Each cut adds one polygon, so the output has at most $R + 1$ polygons, and the loop runs at most $2R + 1$ times. When the queue is empty, the invariant says that the output is a partition of $P$, and line L5 adds a polygon to the output only if its concavity is at most $\tau$. If $\tau = 0$, Lemma 1 says that a polygon with a reflex vertex has a positive concavity, so each output polygon has no reflex vertex and is convex. $\blacksquare$

**Remarks.** The theorem does not depend on the choice of the reflex vertex at line L6 or on the choice of the edge at line L7. Any reflex vertex and either extension give a correct result. These choices change only the number and the shape of the pieces. With $\tau = 0$ the bound of $R + 1$ pieces is the usual bound for removing the reflex vertices of a polygon one at a time. In 3D there is no such linear bound: Chazelle (1984) gives polyhedra with $r$ reflex edges for which every convex decomposition has $\Omega(r^2)$ pieces.
