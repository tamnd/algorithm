title: Marching tetrahedra outputs the level set of the piecewise linear interpolant as a closed oriented 2-manifold
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

We use the names of the pseudocode. A sample is inside when its value is less than $c$, and we assume that no sample equals $c$. A tetrahedron edge is active when its two samples are on different sides of $c$; its crossing parameter is then in the open interval $(0, 1)$, so its vertex is in the open interior of the edge. Let $\ell$ be the function that is linear on each tetrahedron of the grid and equals $f$ at the samples.

**Claim.** (1) For each tetrahedron $\tau$, the triangles that $\tau$ adds in line L8 form a triangulation of the set $\{x \in \tau : \ell(x) = c\}$, with normals toward the side where $\ell > c$. (2) Every mesh edge that is not on the border of the box is in exactly two triangles, once in each direction. (3) The link of each vertex that is not on the border of the box is a single cycle. Hence, if every border sample is outside, the mesh is a closed, oriented combinatorial 2-manifold whose underlying set is $\{\ell = c\}$.

**Step 0: the tetrahedra form a face-to-face subdivision.** For a cell with local coordinates $u \in [0, 1]^3$, the path that adds the axes in the order $a, b, d$ gives the tetrahedron $\{u_a \ge u_b \ge u_d\}$. The 6 orders cover the cube, and two of them overlap only where some coordinates are equal, so the 6 tetrahedra subdivide the cell. Two tetrahedra of one cell meet in the common face of their two simplices, because each is the convex hull of a chain of the corners ordered by inclusion of their sets of axes, and two chains meet in a common sub-chain. On a cube face, for example $u_d = 0$, the trace of the subdivision is the cut of the square $\{u_a \ge u_b\} \cup \{u_b \ge u_a\}$, along the diagonal from the corner with both coordinates 0 to the corner with both equal to 1. This rule depends only on the face and not on the cell, so two cells that share a face cut it along the same diagonal. So the tetrahedra of the grid form a simplicial complex: two of them meet in a common face, edge, vertex, or not at all. Each tetrahedron edge is a cell edge, a face diagonal or the body diagonal of a cell, and line L6 keys its vertex by its two grid points, so one edge gets one vertex for all tetrahedra that contain it, and $\ell$ is well defined and continuous.

**Step 1: one tetrahedron.** On $\tau$, $\ell$ is linear and not constant (it takes values on both sides of $c$ when $m \ne 0, 15$). So $\{\ell = c\} \cap \tau$ is the intersection of a plane with $\tau$, which is the convex hull of its points on the edges of $\tau$, and these are exactly the crossing points of the active edges, at the parameter of CROSS. If one vertex $a$ is alone on its side, the active edges are the 3 edges at $a$, and the set is the triangle of line L11. If two vertices $a, b$ are inside and $c, d$ outside, the active edges are $ac, ad, bc, bd$. The plane section is a convex quadrilateral whose sides lie on the faces of $\tau$: the face $acd$ contains $ac$ and $ad$, the face $bcd$ contains $bc$ and $bd$, the face $abd$ contains $ad$ and $bd$, and the face $abc$ contains $ac$ and $bc$. So the cyclic order of the quadrilateral is $ac, ad, bd, bc$, and the two triangles of line L12 share the diagonal from $ac$ to $bd$ and cover it. If $m$ is 0 or 15, no edge is active and the set is empty. This proves the first part of (1).

**Step 2: orientation.** Line L13 orients the triangles on the reference tetrahedron so that the normal points toward an outside vertex, that is, toward the side of the plane where $\ell > c$. On the reference, with the crossing points at the edge midpoints, the cut is still a plane that separates the inside vertices from the outside vertices, so the test is well defined. An affine map $A$ from the reference to $\tau$ that sends vertex $i$ to $\tau_i$ sends crossing points to crossing points (the order of the points along each edge, and the side of each vertex, are kept), and the triangles to triangles with the same combinatorics. Every tetrahedron of line L3 is listed with a positive determinant, as is the reference, so $A$ keeps orientation: a triangle whose normal points toward a vertex in the reference has a normal that points toward the image of that vertex. This proves the rest of (1). In particular, the boundary of each triangle, read from the side where $\ell > c$, is counterclockwise.

**Step 3: one face of a tetrahedron.** Let $\sigma$ be a triangular face of $\tau$ with a sign change. Two of its edges are active, and $\{\ell = c\} \cap \sigma$ is the segment between their two crossing points, which depends only on the three samples of $\sigma$. By step 1, this segment is a side of exactly one triangle of $\tau$ (the sides of the triangle or of the quadrilateral of $\tau$ are the sections of its faces, and the diagonal of the quadrilateral joins $ac$ and $bd$, which share no face of $\tau$). A segment that joins two crossing points on edges of $\tau$ that do not share a face of $\tau$ is in the interior of $\tau$ except at its ends.

**Step 4: statement (2).** Take a mesh edge $s$ between the vertices on tetrahedron edges $e$ and $e'$. If $e$ and $e'$ share no face of the tetrahedron $\tau$ that produced $s$, then $s$ is the diagonal of the quadrilateral of $\tau$, which is in exactly the two triangles of $\tau$, with opposite directions, as in any triangulation of a polygon. Its interior is in the interior of $\tau$, so by step 0 no other tetrahedron has a side on it. Otherwise $s$ lies on a face $\sigma$ of $\tau$, and it is the section of $\sigma$. If $\sigma$ is not on the border of the box, then by step 0 it is shared by exactly one other tetrahedron $\tau'$, and by step 3 the same segment is a side of exactly one triangle of $\tau$ and one of $\tau'$. Because $\tau$ and $\tau'$ lie on opposite sides of $\sigma$ and both triangles point toward the side where $\ell > c$, the two triangles meet along $s$ like the two halves of a surface that is bent at $\sigma$, and their boundaries traverse $s$ in opposite directions. To see this, let $p$ be a point of $s$ and $n_\sigma$ a vector in the plane of $\sigma$ that crosses $s$ toward the side of $\sigma$ where $\ell > c$; the oriented boundary of the triangle of $\tau$ goes along $s$ in the direction $n_\tau \times n_\sigma$ (up to a positive factor), where $n_\tau$ is the outward normal of $\tau$ at $\sigma$, and for $\tau'$ the outward normal is $-n_\tau$. No third tetrahedron contains $s$: a segment on the face $\sigma$ that crosses its interior is in exactly the tetrahedra that contain $\sigma$, which are $\tau$ and $\tau'$. So every non-border mesh edge is in exactly two triangles, once in each direction.

**Step 5: statement (3).** Let $P$ be the vertex on an active edge $e$ that is not on the border. The tetrahedra that contain $e$ form a cycle $\tau_1, \ldots, \tau_k$ around $e$ in which consecutive tetrahedra $\tau_i$ and $\tau_{i+1}$ share a triangular face $\sigma_i$ that contains $e$, because the tetrahedra of step 0 subdivide a neighborhood of the interior of $e$. Each $\tau_i$ contains $e$, which is active, so by step 1 the triangles of $\tau_i$ at $P$ form a fan whose link is a path from the other end $R_{i-1}$ of the section of $\sigma_{i-1}$ to the other end $R_i$ of the section of $\sigma_i$. This path is one edge when $P$ is in one triangle of $\tau_i$, and two edges through the other end of the diagonal when $P$ is an end of the diagonal of a quadrilateral. By step 3 the end $R_i$ is the same vertex for $\tau_i$ and $\tau_{i+1}$. So the $k$ paths join into one cycle, and every triangle at $P$ is in one of the fans. The link of $P$ is therefore a single cycle.

**Step 6: conclusion.** If every border sample is outside, no edge on the border of the box is active, because the border faces of the box are subdivided by tetrahedron edges between border samples only. So no mesh edge is on the border, and steps 4 and 5 apply everywhere. A finite set of triangles in which every edge is in exactly two triangles with opposite directions and every vertex link is one cycle is a closed oriented combinatorial 2-manifold. By statement (1) and step 0 its underlying set is $\bigcup_\tau (\{\ell = c\} \cap \tau) = \{\ell = c\}$. Each component is a closed orientable surface of some genus $g_i$, and $V - E + F = \sum_i (2 - 2 g_i)$.

**Termination.** Line L10 runs over 16 patterns with bounded work each, and line L2 over the $n$ cells with 6 tetrahedra each and at most 2 triangles per tetrahedron. $\blacksquare$
