---
title: The ball-pivoting algorithm outputs empty-ball triangles that form an oriented surface with boundary, and it stops
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

We use the names of the pseudocode. For a triangle $(a, b, c)$ let $N = (p_b - p_a) \times (p_c - p_a)$, and let $B(a, b, c)$ be the closed ball of radius $\rho$ through $p_a, p_b, p_c$ with center on the side of $N$. The mesh $T$ is the set of triangles added so far, and $D$ is the set of their directed edges: $(a, b), (b, c), (c, a)$ for a triangle $(a, b, c)$. The front $F$ is the set of edges $(i, j) \in D$ whose reverse $(j, i)$ is not in $D$.

**Claim.** (1) For every output triangle $(a, b, c)$, the open ball of $B(a, b, c)$ contains no input point, and $N \cdot \nu_v > 0$ for $v = a, b, c$. Hence every output triangle is a 2-face of the alpha complex of the points for $\alpha = \rho$. (2) No directed edge is in two triangles of $T$. Hence every edge is in at most two triangles, and if it is in two, they traverse it in opposite directions. (3) The algorithm stops after $O(n d)$ pivots, where $d$ bounds the number of points within $2 \rho$ of a point.

**Step 1: statement (1).** A triangle enters $T$ in line L3 or line L10, and in both lines it is added only if it is valid, which is exactly the condition of (1). For the alpha complex: a triangle of the points belongs to the alpha complex for $\alpha = \rho$ when some ball of radius $\rho$ has its three vertices on its boundary and no point in its interior (the triangle is $\rho$-exposed). $B(a, b, c)$ is such a ball.

**Step 2: statement (2), the invariant.** We show that $D$ never gets a directed edge twice. A seed triangle $(a, b, c)$ in line L3 has $a$ unused, so no edge of $D$ has $a$ as an end, and the edges $(a, b)$ and $(c, a)$ are new; line L2 skips pairs with $(b, c) \in D$, so $(b, c)$ is new as well. A pivot triangle $(j, i, k)$ in line L10 has the edges $(j, i)$, $(i, k)$, $(k, j)$. The edge $(i, j)$ is in $F$, so $(j, i) \notin D$ by the definition of $F$. Line L9 checks that $(i, k) \notin D$ and $(k, j) \notin D$. So the three edges are new. Since every edge of every triangle of $T$ is in $D$ and none is added twice, no directed edge is in two triangles. If an undirected edge $\{u, v\}$ were in three triangles, two of them would use the same direction, which is impossible; if it is in two, they use the two different directions.

**Step 3: the front and the glue.** After a triangle is added, an edge $(u, v)$ of it is in $F$ exactly when $(v, u)$ is not in $D$. So the update of line L10, which removes $(v, u)$ from $F$ when it is there and adds $(u, v)$ otherwise, keeps $F$ equal to the set of edges of $D$ without their reverse. An edge leaves $F$ only by such a glue, which gives it its second triangle, and then it never comes back, because $D$ only grows. The vertex condition of line L9 (the point $k$ is unused, or it is an end of an edge of $F$) keeps the new triangle away from the vertices whose star is already closed; it is not needed for (1) or (2).

**Step 4: statement (3).** Each iteration of the inner loop removes an edge from the stack of edges to process. It either adds one triangle and pushes at most two edges, or it pushes nothing. By step 2 every triangle adds three new directed edges to $D$, and every directed edge is between two points within distance $2 \rho$ (both are on a ball of radius $\rho$), so $|D| \le n d$ and $T$ has at most $n d / 3$ triangles. So the number of pushes, and the number of pivots, is at most $3 + 2 n d / 3$ per seed plus the pivots of the seed edges, and there are at most $n$ seeds, each of which makes its first point used. Each pivot and each seed test scans a finite set of points. The seed scan moves through the points in order: a point that fails as a seed fails again later, because it stays unused, the set of allowed partners (unused or on the front) and the set of absent edges only shrink, and the emptiness of a ball does not depend on $T$. So the outer loop stops after at most $n$ seeds and one pass over the points. $\blacksquare$
