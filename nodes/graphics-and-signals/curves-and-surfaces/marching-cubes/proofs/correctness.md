title: Marching cubes with a face-consistent table outputs a closed oriented 2-manifold
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

We use the names of the pseudocode. A sample is inside when its value is less than $c$, and we assume that no sample equals $c$, so every crossing parameter is in the open interval $(0, 1)$ and every vertex is in the open interior of its grid edge. A face segment is a pair of cube edges produced in line L2 for one face of one cell.

**Claim.** (1) The vertex list has exactly one vertex on each grid edge whose two samples are on different sides of $c$, and no other vertex. (2) Every mesh edge that does not lie on the border of the box is in exactly two triangles, once in each direction. (3) The link of each vertex that is not on the border of the box is a single cycle. Hence, if every border sample is outside, the mesh is a closed, oriented combinatorial 2-manifold with an even Euler characteristic.

**Step 1: one face.** A face has 4 corners, and going around it the sign changes an even number of times, so it has 0, 2 or 4 active edges (edges with a sign change). The square table pairs them: each active edge of the face is in exactly one face segment of that face. Each cube edge lies on exactly two faces of the cube, so in the set $S$ of line L2 each active cube edge is the start of exactly one segment and the end of exactly one: the orientation with the inside on the left, seen from outside, makes one of its two faces enter the edge and the other leave it (this is the same argument as step 3 of the proof for [marching squares](/marching-squares/), applied on the surface of the cube, and it is also checked for all 256 cases by the implementation). So the directed graph on active edges given by $S$ has in-degree and out-degree 1 at every vertex, and line L3 splits it into disjoint cycles.

**Step 2: one cell.** Line L4 triangulates each cycle as a polygon. In a triangulation of a polygon with vertices $v_1, \ldots, v_m$, each side $v_i v_{i+1}$ is in exactly one triangle, with the direction of the polygon, and each chord is in exactly two triangles, with opposite directions. The triangles at a vertex $v_i$ form a fan from $v_{i-1}$ to $v_{i+1}$. Line L4 reverses the cycle first, so the triangle sides of the cell are exactly the face segments of $S$ with their directions reversed. Different cycles of one cell share no vertex. Line L4 also uses no chord between two cube edges of one face; so every chord joins vertices that are not on a common face, and no other cell can have a triangle side between the same two grid edges.

**Step 3: shared faces.** Let a face $Q$ be shared by cells $A$ and $B$. The face segments of $Q$ come from the square table applied to the 4 samples of $Q$ alone, with the fixed separated entry on an ambiguous face. Cell $A$ sees $Q$ from one side and cell $B$ from the other, so the counterclockwise order of $Q$ for $B$ is the reverse of that for $A$. Reversing the corner order of a square exchanges left and right of each segment, so the rule "inside on the left" gives the same segments for $B$ as for $A$, with opposite directions. With step 2, a segment $P \to R$ of $Q$ in $A$ is the side $R \to P$ of one triangle of $A$, and the same segment is $R \to P$ in $B$ and the side $P \to R$ of one triangle of $B$.

**Step 4: statement (1).** Line L11 adds a vertex for a key only once, so each grid edge has at most one vertex. A grid edge gets a vertex when some triangle uses it, and by steps 1 and 2 the triangles of a cell use exactly the active edges of the cell. Every grid edge belongs to at least one cell, and an edge is active in one cell if and only if it is active in all cells that contain it, because activity depends only on its two samples.

**Step 5: statement (2).** Take a mesh edge between the vertices on grid edges $e$ and $e'$, used by a triangle of cell $A$. If $e$ and $e'$ lie on a common face $Q$ of $A$, then by step 2 the side is a face segment of $Q$, not a chord, and by step 3 it is in exactly one triangle of $A$ and one triangle of the cell $B$ on the other side of $Q$, if $Q$ is not on the border, with opposite directions. No third cell contains both $e$ and $e'$ on a face: two distinct grid edges on a common face of a cell span that face, and a face belongs to at most two cells. If $e$ and $e'$ do not share a face, the side is a chord of a cycle of $A$, in exactly two triangles of $A$ with opposite directions, and in no triangle of another cell. Indeed, a triangle side of any cell joins two edges of that cell. Since $e$ and $e'$ share no face of $A$, their four end points do not agree in any coordinate, so their bounding box is the whole cube $A$, and $A$ is the only unit cell that contains both. So every non-border mesh edge is in exactly two triangles, once in each direction.

**Step 6: statement (3).** Let $P$ be the vertex on an active grid edge $e$ inside the box. The cells that contain $e$ are four, $A_1, A_2, A_3, A_4$ in cyclic order around $e$, and consecutive cells $A_i$, $A_{i+1}$ share a face $Q_i$ that contains $e$. In each cell $A_i$, by step 1, $P$ is on exactly one cycle, and its two neighbors on that cycle are the other ends of the face segments at $P$ on the two faces of $A_i$ that contain $e$, which are $Q_{i-1}$ and $Q_i$. By step 2, the triangles of $A_i$ at $P$ form a fan whose link is a path from the neighbor $R_{i-1}$ on $Q_{i-1}$ to the neighbor $R_i$ on $Q_i$. By step 3, the neighbor on $Q_i$ is the same vertex for $A_i$ and $A_{i+1}$. So the four paths join end to end into one cycle $R_0, \ldots, R_1, \ldots, R_2, \ldots, R_3, \ldots, R_0$, and every triangle at $P$ is in one of the four fans. The link of $P$ is therefore a single cycle.

**Step 7: conclusion.** If every border sample is outside, no border edge of the box is active, so there are no mesh edges on the border, and steps 5 and 6 apply to every edge and vertex. A finite set of triangles in which every edge is in exactly two triangles with opposite directions and every vertex link is one cycle is a closed oriented combinatorial 2-manifold. Each of its components is a closed orientable surface of some genus $g_i$, with Euler characteristic $2 - 2 g_i$, so $V - E + F = \sum_i (2 - 2 g_i)$ is even.

**Termination.** Line L1 runs over 256 cases with bounded work each, and line L6 over the $n$ cells with at most 5 triangles each. $\blacksquare$
