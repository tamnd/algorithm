---
title: Dual contouring places each vertex at a constrained QEF minimizer and outputs a closed oriented quad surface
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

We use the names of the pseudocode. A grid point is inside when $f < c$. An edge is active when its ends are on different sides of $c$. For a cell $C$ with Hermite data $(p_i, n_i)$, $i = 1, \ldots, m$, let $A$ have the rows $n_i^{\mathsf T}$, let $b_i = n_i \cdot p_i$, let $M = A^{\mathsf T} A = \sum_j \lambda_j e_j e_j^{\mathsf T}$ with orthonormal $e_j$, and let $K = \{j : \lambda_j > \tau^2 \lambda_{\max}\}$.

**Claim.** (1) $v_C$, before clamping, is the unique minimizer of $E(x) = \lVert A x - b \rVert^2$ over the affine set $S = m + \operatorname{span}\{e_j : j \in K\}$. If $K = \{1, 2, 3\}$, it is the minimizer of $E$ over all of space; if moreover the planes $n_i \cdot x = b_i$ have a common point, it is that point. (2) Each quad has 4 distinct vertices. If every border sample is outside, then every undirected mesh edge is in an even number of quads, 2 or 4, and it is traversed equally often in the two directions. The quads, with the vertices $v_C$ replaced by the cell centers, are exactly the faces of the boundary of the union $U$ of the closed cubes of side $h$ centered at the inside grid points, each oriented with its normal out of $U$.

**Step 1: the QEF.** Write $x = m + y$. Then $E(m + y) = E(m) + 2 y^{\mathsf T} (M m - A^{\mathsf T} b) + y^{\mathsf T} M y$. Let $r = A^{\mathsf T} b - M m$. On $S$, $y = \sum_{j \in K} t_j e_j$, and the expression becomes $E(m) + \sum_{j \in K} (\lambda_j t_j^2 - 2 t_j\, e_j \cdot r)$. Each term is a strictly convex quadratic in $t_j$, because $\lambda_j > 0$ for $j \in K$ (if $\lambda_{\max} = 0$ then $K$ is empty and $v_C = m$). Its unique minimum is at $t_j = e_j \cdot r / \lambda_j$. So the minimizer over $S$ is $m + \sum_{j \in K} \lambda_j^{-1} e_j e_j^{\mathsf T} r = m + A^{+}_{\tau} r$, which is line L5. If $K$ contains all three indices, $S$ is all of space and this is the global minimizer. If the planes have a common point $q$, then $E(q) = 0$, so $q$ is a global minimizer, and if $M$ is invertible the minimizer is unique, so $v_C = q$.

**Step 2: the quads are dual cube faces.** Give each grid point $g$ the dual cube $D_g$ of side $h$ centered at $g$; its 8 corners are the centers of the 8 cells that contain $g$. For an edge $e = (g, g')$ along an axis $a$, $D_g$ and $D_{g'}$ share one square face, perpendicular to $a$, whose 4 corners are the centers of the 4 cells around $e$. Line L8 lists these cells in counterclockwise order seen from the + end of $e$, that is, with the right hand normal along $+a$. Line L9 reverses the order when the + end is inside, so in both cases the normal points from the inside end to the outside end of $e$. The 4 cells are distinct, so a quad has 4 distinct vertices, because each cell has one vertex.

**Step 3: the boundary of $U$.** The dual cubes tile space, and two of them share a square face exactly when their centers are the ends of a grid edge. A square face is on the boundary of $U$ exactly when one of its two cubes is in $U$ and the other is not, that is, exactly when the edge is active. If every border sample is outside, an active edge has at least one end strictly inside the box, and it is not on a border face of the box (both ends of such an edge are border samples, which are outside), so the 4 cells around it exist and line L7 does not skip it. So the quads are exactly the boundary faces of $U$, each with the normal out of $U$ by step 2.

**Step 4: the edges.** The boundary of a union of closed cubes of a grid is a closed surface in the combinatorial sense that each edge of a boundary square is in an even number of boundary squares, and it is traversed equally often in the two directions by the oriented squares, since the oriented boundary of a 3-chain has no boundary: the oriented boundary of each cube is a closed oriented surface, and the boundary of $U$ is their sum, in which the faces between two cubes of $U$ cancel. Concretely, an edge of the dual grid is surrounded by 4 dual cubes, the duals of the 4 corners of a grid face $Q$, and the boundary squares at that edge are the sides between consecutive cubes with different status. Going around $Q$ the status changes an even number of times, 0, 2 or 4, and it changes 4 times exactly when the inside and outside corners of $Q$ alternate. Moving the corners of the squares from the cell centers to the $v_C$ does not change which squares share which edges, so the same holds for the quads.

**Termination.** Line L1 visits each of the $3 N (N + 1)^2$ grid edges once, line L2 makes a fixed number of bisection steps, line L4 visits each cell once, and the Jacobi iteration of line L5 is stopped after a fixed number of sweeps. $\blacksquare$
