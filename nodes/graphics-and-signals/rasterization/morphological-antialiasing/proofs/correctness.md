title: The MLAA blend weights are the trapezoid areas under the reconstructed line, every blend is a convex combination, and the pass along rows reproduces the exact coverage of a straight edge through the middles of the secondary edges
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

Coordinates are in pixel units with $y$ pointing down; pixel $(i, j)$ is the cell $[i, i+1] \times [j, j+1]$. MLAA here is the procedure of lines L1 to L9: separation lines are maximal runs of differing vertical neighbors along the boundary between two rows; at each end vertex of a run, a *secondary edge* is a separation segment between the two columns that meet at the vertex, in exactly one of the two rows next to the run; the run is split into L shapes as in lines L4 and L5; each L shape of primary length $d$ blends the cells on the side of its secondary edge. The pass along columns is the same procedure on the transposed image.

**Lemma 1 (the weights).** For an L shape with primary length $d$, a positive multiple of $1/2$, let $\ell$ be the segment from the middle of the secondary edge, at height $1/2$ above the primary edge, to the far vertex of the primary edge at distance $d$. The weight that lines L7 and L8 give to the $k$-th cell along the primary edge, $k = 0, 1, \dots, \lceil d \rceil - 1$, is the area of the part of that cell between $\ell$ and the primary edge. The weights lie in $[0, 1/2)$ and sum to $d/4$.

*Proof.* Measure $x$ along the primary edge from the secondary edge, and the height away from the primary edge, into the cells on the side of the secondary edge. Then $\ell$ is the graph of $h(x) = \frac12 (1 - x/d)$ on $[0, d]$, with $0 \le h \le 1/2$, so the region between $\ell$ and the primary edge lies inside the row of cells next to the primary edge. Its part inside cell $k$ is $\{(x, y) : k \le x \le \min(k+1, d),\ 0 \le y \le h(x)\}$, whose area is $\int h$ over that interval. Since $h$ is affine, the integral is the length of the interval times the mean of $h$ at its ends, which is the formula of `l_shape_areas`. The interval has length at most 1 and $h < 1/2$ except at $x = 0$, so each area is below $1/2$. The total is the area of the triangle with legs $d$ and $1/2$, which is $d/4$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (convexity).** Each step of line L8 replaces a color $c$ by $(1 - a) c + a o$ with $a \in [0, 1/2)$, where $o$ is a pixel of the input. So each channel of the output lies between the smallest and the largest value of that channel in the input. For a black-and-white image the output does not depend on the order of the blends: a pixel of input value $c \in \{0, 1\}$ that receives the weights $a_1, \dots, a_r$ ends with the value $o + (c - o) \prod_q (1 - a_q)$, where $o = 1 - c$.

*Proof.* The first claim is induction over the blends: a convex combination of two values in an interval stays in it. For the second, every blend of a cell uses the cell on the other side of a separation segment, which in a black-and-white image has the value $o = 1 - c$. The map $x \mapsto (1 - a) x + a o$ fixes $o$ and multiplies $x - o$ by $1 - a$, and these maps commute. $\square$

**Theorem (straight edges).** Let $L \ge 1$ and $m$ be integers, put $c = 1/2 + m/L$, and let the image be the point sampling at pixel centers of the half-plane $y > c + x/L$, with the value 1 inside and 0 outside. Then the pass along rows sets each pixel $(i, j)$ whose column satisfies $L \le i < W - L$ to the exact area of the half-plane inside the cell $[i, i+1] \times [j, j+1]$.

*Proof.* Pixel $(i, j)$ is 1 when $j + 1/2 > c + (i + 1/2)/L$, that is, when $jL - m - i > 1/2$. The left side is an integer, so there are no ties, and the condition is $i < s_j$ with $s_j = jL - m$. So row $j$ is 1 on the columns $i < s_j$, row $j+1$ on the columns $i < s_j + L$, and the separation line between rows $j$ and $j+1$ is the run of columns $s_j \le i < s_j + L$ (cut to the image). At its left vertex $x = s_j$, the columns $s_j - 1$ and $s_j$ differ in row $j$ and agree in row $j + 1$, so the secondary edge is on the side of row $j$. At its right vertex $x = s_j + L$ they agree in row $j$ and differ in row $j+1$. So the run is a Z shape of length $L$, split at its middle.

The boundary of the half-plane is the line $y = c + x/L = 1/2 + (x + m)/L$. At $x = s_j$ it has the height $j + 1/2$, the middle of the left secondary edge, and at $x = s_j + L/2$ it has the height $j + 1$, the middle of the run. So the segment $\ell$ of the left L shape lies on the boundary line, and so does the segment of the right L shape, from height $j + 3/2$ at $x = s_j + L$ to height $j + 1$ at the middle. By Lemma 1, the left L shape gives each cell of row $j$ in the columns $[s_j, s_j + L/2)$ the weight $a$ equal to the area of the cell between the line and the row boundary $y = j + 1$, which is the area of the cell inside the half-plane. These cells hold 0 and the opposite cells hold 1, so line L8 sets them to $a$, the exact area. In the same way, the right L shape gives each cell of row $j + 1$ in the columns $[s_j + L/2, s_j + L)$ the area $a$ of its part outside the half-plane, and these cells hold 1 against 0, so they become $1 - a$, again the exact area.

It remains to see that every cell crossed by the line gets exactly one of these blends and no other cell gets one. Within row $j$, the line runs from height $j$ at $x = s_j - L/2$ to height $j + 1$ at $x = s_j + L/2$. The cells of row $j$ in the columns $[s_j - L/2, s_j)$ are blended by the right half of the run between rows $j - 1$ and $j$, since $s_{j-1} + L = s_j$, and those in $[s_j, s_j + L/2)$ by the left half of the run between rows $j$ and $j+1$. If $L$ is odd, the column that contains $s_j + L/2$ is blended once in row $j$ and once in row $j+1$, which are different cells. Every other cell of row $j$ lies wholly on one side of the line, gets no blend, and keeps its correct value 0 or 1. For a column with $L \le i < W - L$, the runs that blend it start at or after column $i - L + 1 \ge 1$ and end at or before column $i + L - 1 \le W - 2$, so they are not cut by the image border and the argument above applies. $\blacksquare$

For $L \ge 2$, the pass along columns then finds, at every step of the staircase, a separation line of length 1 with secondary edges on opposite sides. It treats it as a Z shape and blends the two cells beside the step by $1/8$ each. This is the extra blur that Reshetov notes for separation lines that belong to more than one pattern; by Lemma 2 it moves each of those values toward the opposite color by at most $1/8$ of the difference.
