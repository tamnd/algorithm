---
title: The immersion algorithm computes the recursion of Definition 3.2 exactly
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

Fix an integer image $f$ on a pixel grid with 4 or 8 neighbors, with least value $h_{\min}$ and largest value $h_{\max}$. Write $T_h = \{p : f(p) \le h\}$, $d_A$ for the geodesic distance inside a set $A$, and $\min_h$ for the union of the regional minima at level $h$. For disjoint sets $B_1, \ldots, B_k$ inside $A$, $IZ_A(B_1, \ldots, B_k)$ is the set of $p \in A$ with $d_A(p, B_i) < d_A(p, B_j)$ for one $i$ and every $j \ne i$, and such a $p$ belongs to the zone of $B_i$. The recursion is $X_{h_{\min}} = T_{h_{\min}}$ split into its connected parts, and $X_{h+1} = \min_{h+1} \cup IZ_{T_{h+1}}(X_h)$, where each part of $\min_{h+1}$ is a new basin.

**Invariant.** At the start of the pass for level $h$ (line L3), the pixels with a basin label are exactly the basins of $X_{h-1}$, each with its own label, and the W-set is exactly $T_{h-1} \setminus X_{h-1}$. Before the first pass, there are no basins and the W-set is empty.

**Lemma 1 (shortest paths avoid basins).** Let $Q = T_h \setminus X_{h-1}$, let $p \in Q$ be at finite distance $\delta = \min_i d_{T_h}(p, B_i) \ge 1$ from the basins, and let $\pi$ be a path of length $\delta$ in $T_h$ from $p$ to a basin $B_i$. Then every pixel of $\pi$ except the last is in $Q$, and the pixel at distance $t$ from $p$ along $\pi$ has distance $\delta - t$ to the basins.

*Proof.* If a pixel of $\pi$ before the last were in some basin, it would be at distance less than $\delta$ from $p$, against the choice of $\delta$. A pixel at position $t$ has distance at most $\delta - t$ to $B_i$ along the rest of $\pi$, and at least $\delta - t$ to every basin, because a shorter path would give $p$ a path of length less than $\delta$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (the label sets).** After line L6, a pixel $q \in Q$ has $d(q) = \delta(q)$, the distance in $T_h$ to the nearest basin, exactly when $\delta(q)$ is finite, and $L(q)$ is the set of labels $i$ with $d_{T_h}(q, B_i) = \delta(q)$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 1, the distance to the basins in $T_h$ equals the distance in the graph on $Q$ from the set of pixels of $Q$ that have a basin neighbor, plus one. Line L5 gives these pixels $d = 1$, and the breadth-first search of line L6 inside $Q$ gives every other reached pixel its graph distance plus one, so $d(q) = \delta(q)$. For the labels, use induction on $\delta$. If $\delta(q) = 1$, the basins at distance 1 are those of the neighbors of $q$, which is the set of line L5. If $\delta(q) = t > 1$, a basin $B_i$ is at distance $t$ from $q$ exactly when some neighbor $p$ of $q$ in $T_h$ is at distance $t - 1$ from $B_i$. By Lemma 1 such a $p$ is in $Q$ with $\delta(p) = t - 1$, and $B_i$ is one of its nearest basins, so $i \in L(p)$ by induction. Conversely, every $i \in L(p)$ for a neighbor $p$ with $d(p) = t - 1$ gives $d_{T_h}(q, B_i) \le t$, so equality holds. Line L6 takes the union over exactly these neighbors. $\square$

**Theorem.** Each pass for level $h$ that is not skipped turns $X_{h-1}$ into $X_h$, and the invariant holds at the next pass. A skipped level leaves $X_h = X_{h-1}$. At the end, the basin labels are the basins of $X_{h_{\max}}$ and the W pixels are $D \setminus X_{h_{\max}}$.

*Proof.* Assume the invariant. By line L3, the pixels with $f = h$ are $P = T_h \setminus T_{h-1}$, so $Q = P \cup (T_{h-1} \setminus X_{h-1}) = T_h \setminus X_{h-1}$. A pixel of a basin $B_i$ has distance 0 to $B_i$ and positive distance to every other basin, so it is in the zone of $B_i$, and the pass leaves it alone. For $q \in Q$, by Lemma 2 the pixel is in the zone of $B_i$ exactly when $L(q) = \{i\}$, which is the test of line L7. A pixel with two or more nearest basins goes to the W-set. A pixel of $Q$ that no basin reaches lies in a connected part $C$ of $T_h$ that contains no basin pixel. Every pixel of $T_{h-1} \setminus X_{h-1}$ was reached by some basin at the pass that put it in the W-set, and basins only grow, so it is still reached, and $C$ consists of pixels with $f = h$. Every neighbor of $C$ outside $C$ has a value above $h$, so $C$ is a plateau with no lower neighbor, a regional minimum at level $h$. Conversely, a regional minimum at level $h$ has no neighbor of lower value, so it is a connected part of $T_h$ with no basin pixel, and no basin reaches it. So line L8 labels exactly the parts of $\min_h$, each with a new label, and after the pass the labels are the basins of $X_h$ and the W-set is $T_h \setminus X_h$. For the first pass, there are no basins, $Q = T_{h_{\min}}$, nothing is reached, and line L8 labels the connected parts of $T_{h_{\min}}$, which is $X_{h_{\min}}$.

For a skipped level $h$, $P$ is empty and the last pass that ran, say for level $g < h$, changed no label. A pass that has new pixels labels them, so it counts as a change; hence level $g$ had no pixels, and every level from $g$ to $h$ has no pixels, so $T_g = T_{g+1} = \cdots = T_h$ and $\min_g, \ldots, \min_h$ are empty. The pass for $g$ computed $X_g = IZ_{T_g}(X_{g-1})$ and changed no label, so $X_g = X_{g-1}$, that is $IZ_{T_g}(X_{g-1}) = X_{g-1}$. Then $X_{g+1} = IZ_{T_g}(X_g) = IZ_{T_g}(X_{g-1}) = X_{g-1} = X_g$, and in the same way $X_h = X_g$. So skipping levels $g+1$ to $h$ leaves the correct basins and W-set. By induction, the invariant holds through the last level, and the claim on the output follows. $\square$

**Remark (the marker flood).** In `watershed_from_markers`, the key of a queued pixel $q$ is $\max(f(p), f(q))$ for the labeled pixel $p$ that pushed it, which is the weight of the edge $pq$ in a grid graph with edge weights $\max(f(p), f(q))$. Labeling the least key first, with the label of $p$, is Prim's algorithm run from all markers at once, which builds a minimum spanning forest with one tree per marker. The path in a minimum spanning forest from a pixel to the root of its tree is a minimax path: no path in the graph from that pixel to any marker has a lower highest edge. The tests check this property for every pixel by a separate computation of the minimax value to each marker.
