---
title: Correctness of the watertight ray-triangle test
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

We prove three things: the test is exact in exact arithmetic, its decision in floating point is the exact decision for the transformed vertices, and as a result it does not leak through shared edges.

**1. The transform.** Let $k_x, k_y, k_z$ be the axes of lines L1 and L2, and let $\sigma$ map a vector $P$ to $(P[k_x] - S_x P[k_z],\ P[k_y] - S_y P[k_z],\ P[k_z])$. The map $\sigma$ is linear and invertible (a permutation followed by a shear with determinant 1), so it maps lines to lines and triangles to triangles. It maps $d$ to $(d[k_x] - S_x d[k_z],\ d[k_y] - S_y d[k_z],\ d[k_z]) = (0, 0, d[k_z])$. So after the translation by $-o$ of line L5, the ray $o + t\,d$ becomes the line $\{(0, 0, t\,d[k_z])\}$, which is the $z$ axis. The ray meets the triangle $A B C$ if and only if the $z$ axis meets the triangle $\sigma(A - o)\,\sigma(B - o)\,\sigma(C - o)$, that is, if and only if the 2D point $(0, 0)$ lies in the closed 2D triangle $A' B' C'$, unless the triangle is parallel to the $z$ axis in the sheared space.

**2. The inside test in exact arithmetic.** For a 2D point $X$ write $E_{PQ}(X) = (P - X) \times (Q - X)$ for the 2D cross product. At $X = 0$ this is $P_x Q_y - P_y Q_x$, and $U = E_{C'B'}(0)$, $V = E_{A'C'}(0)$, $W = E_{B'A'}(0)$. The three values sum to $\det = E_{C'B'} + E_{A'C'} + E_{B'A'}$, which is twice the signed area of the triangle $C' B' A'$ (a constant, independent of $X$). Each value is twice the signed area of the triangle that $X$ forms with one edge. A point $X$ lies in the closed triangle if and only if the three areas do not have opposite signs, that is, they are all $\ge 0$ or all $\le 0$ (with $\det \ne 0$). This is the test of line L9 and line L10. When $\det \ne 0$, the barycentric coordinates of $X = 0$ with respect to $A', B', C'$ are $U / \det$, $V / \det$, $W / \det$, because the area of the sub-triangle opposite a vertex divided by the total area is the weight of that vertex. Barycentric coordinates are kept by the affine map $\sigma$ and the translation, so the same weights give the hit point $uA + vB + wC$ in the original space. Its $z$ value in the sheared space is $u A[k_z] + v B[k_z] + w C[k_z]$ (with $A[k_z]$ after line L5), and since the ray point with $z$ value $\zeta$ has parameter $t = \zeta / d[k_z] = \zeta\,S_z$, we get $t = T / \det$ with $T$ from line L11. Line L12 tests $0 \le T / \det \le t_{\max}$ after multiplying by $|\det| > 0$. If $\det = 0$ the projected triangle has zero area, the ray is in the plane of the triangle or the triangle is degenerate, and the problem returns MISS. So in exact arithmetic the algorithm is correct.

**3. Exact signs in floating point.** Now let the operations of lines L5 and L6 round. Call the results $A'$, $B'$, $C'$; they are floating point 2D points. Lines L7 and L8 compute $U = \mathrm{fl}(\mathrm{fl}(C'_x B'_y) - \mathrm{fl}(C'_y B'_x))$. Rounding to nearest is monotone: $a \le b$ implies $\mathrm{fl}(a) \le \mathrm{fl}(b)$. If $C'_x B'_y > C'_y B'_x$ then $\mathrm{fl}(C'_x B'_y) \ge \mathrm{fl}(C'_y B'_x)$, so the computed difference is $\ge 0$, and it is $> 0$ unless it is $0$. The same holds with the inequalities reversed. So the computed $U$ has the sign of the exact value $C'_x B'_y - C'_y B'_x$, or it is $0$. (This assumes no overflow; underflow of a product can only push it to zero, and keeps the monotone argument.) If any of the three computed values is zero, line L8 recomputes all three exactly, so after line L8 each of $U$, $V$, $W$ has the exact sign of the 2D cross product of the rounded points. Hence the decision of lines L9 and L10 is the exact decision of step 2 for the 2D triangle $A' B' C'$ with rounded vertices.

**4. Neighbor triangles agree.** The rounded point $A'$ is computed from $A$, $o$ and the ray constants of lines L1 to L4 only. So a vertex shared by several triangles has the same rounded point in each of them. Consider an edge $B C$ shared by the triangles $A B C$ and $D C B$, as in a consistently oriented mesh. The first computes $U = \mathrm{fl}(\mathrm{fl}(C'_x B'_y) - \mathrm{fl}(C'_y B'_x))$ for the edge, and the second computes $\mathrm{fl}(\mathrm{fl}(B'_x C'_y) - \mathrm{fl}(B'_y C'_x))$. Multiplication commutes, and $\mathrm{fl}(y - x) = -\mathrm{fl}(x - y)$ because the rounding to nearest is symmetric, so the second value is exactly $-U$. With the exact recomputation of line L8 the same holds, because the exact values are negatives of each other.

**5. No leak.** By steps 3 and 4, every triangle of the mesh is tested exactly against the mesh of the rounded 2D points, in which shared vertices are shared. Suppose the rounded 2D triangles $A' B' C'$ and $D' C' B'$ lie on opposite sides of the line through $B'$ and $C'$ (the edge is not a silhouette for this ray), and the origin lies on the closed segment $B' C'$. Then $U = 0$ for both triangles. The other two values of the first triangle are the signed areas of the origin with the edges $C' A'$ and $A' B'$; on the segment $B' C'$ they are both of the sign of $\det$ or zero, since the closed triangle contains its edges. So the first triangle passes line L9, and so does the second. If the origin lies close to an inner point of the segment but not on it, then it is in one of the two closed triangles, because two triangles on opposite sides of a common edge cover a neighborhood of each inner point of the edge. In both cases at least one triangle passes. At an inner vertex, the projected triangles around the vertex, if they do not fold over, cover a neighborhood of it, so the same argument applies to the triangle that contains the origin. Then line L10 has $\det \ne 0$ for a non-degenerate projected triangle, and the ray hits it, with the distance test of line L12 as for any other hit.

So in floating point the decision is the exact decision for the rounded transformed vertices, and a ray through the inside of a non-silhouette shared edge or an inner vertex of a mesh hits at least one of its triangles. $\blacksquare$
