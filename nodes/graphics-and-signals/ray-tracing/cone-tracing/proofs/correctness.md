---
title: The cone test is exact, the coverages are exact areas, and the mixing is a convex combination
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Setting.** The cone has apex $O$, unit axis $D$ and spread angle $A \in (0, \pi/2)$. Its solid region is $K = \{O + x(D + u) : x \ge 0,\ u \perp D,\ \lVert u\rVert \le \tan A\}$. A ray of the cone is a half-line $\{O + x(D + u) : x > 0\}$ with $u \perp D$, $\lVert u\rVert \le \tan A$, and we measure sets of rays by the area of the set of their $u$, which is the uniform measure on any cross-section of the cone perpendicular to $D$, up to a scale factor. The coverage of an object is the measure of the rays that the method counts as blocked, divided by the measure $\pi \tan^2 A$ of all rays.

**Lemma 1 (the fast test).** Let $T = (C - O) \cdot D$ and $SEP = \lVert C - O - T D\rVert$. The distance from $C$ to $K$ is less than $R$ if and only if $SEP < T \tan A + R/\cos A$, in the case $T \cos A + SEP \sin A \ge 0$, and if and only if $T^2 + SEP^2 < R^2$ otherwise.

*Proof.* $K$ is invariant under rotations about the axis, so the distance from $C$ to $K$ equals the distance in the plane spanned by $D$ and $C - O$ (any plane through the axis if $SEP = 0$). In coordinates $(x, y)$ along $D$ and along $C - O - TD$, the section of $K$ is the wedge $W = \{x \ge 0, |y| \le x \tan A\}$ and $C$ is $p = (T, SEP)$ with $SEP \ge 0$. If $p \in W$, the distance is 0, and both conditions hold: $SEP \le T \tan A < T \tan A + R/\cos A$, and $T \ge 0$ gives the first case. If $p \notin W$, then since $SEP \ge 0$, the nearest point of $W$ is on the upper edge $\{\lambda e : \lambda \ge 0\}$, $e = (\cos A, \sin A)$; the lower edge is farther, because reflecting it in the $x$ axis brings it to the upper one and $p$ is on the upper side. The nearest point of the half-line is $\lambda e$ with $\lambda = \max(0, p \cdot e) = \max(0, T \cos A + SEP \sin A)$. If $\lambda > 0$, the distance is the distance to the edge line, $|SEP \cos A - T \sin A|$; as $p$ is outside $W$ on the upper side, it is $SEP \cos A - T \sin A > 0$, and it is less than $R$ if and only if $SEP < T \tan A + R/\cos A$. If $\lambda = 0$, the nearest point is the apex, at distance $(T^2 + SEP^2)^{1/2}$. $\square$

A sphere meets the cone if and only if this distance is less than $R$, so the test of line L3 rejects exactly the spheres that no point of $K$ reaches.

**Lemma 2 (lens and segment).** For disks of radii $r_1, r_2$ with centers $s$ apart, $|r_1 - r_2| < s < r_1 + r_2$, the area of their intersection is the lens formula of the page. The fraction of the unit disk where $x < h$, for $|h| \le 1$, is $f(h) = (\arcsin h + h(1 - h^2)^{1/2} + \pi/2)/\pi$.

*Proof.* The lens is the union of two circular segments cut by the common chord. The segment of disk $i$ has central half-angle $\alpha_i$ with $\cos \alpha_i = (s^2 + r_i^2 - r_j^2)/(2 s r_i)$, by the law of cosines in the triangle of the two centers and an end of the chord, and its area is $r_i^2 \alpha_i - r_i^2 \sin\alpha_i \cos\alpha_i$. The two triangles $r_i^2 \sin\alpha_i\cos\alpha_i$ together form the kite with diagonals $s$ and the chord, whose area is twice the area of the triangle with sides $s, r_1, r_2$; Heron's formula gives $\tfrac12\sqrt{(-s + r_1 + r_2)(s + r_1 - r_2)(s - r_1 + r_2)(s + r_1 + r_2)}$ for the kite. For the segment, $\int_{-1}^{h} 2(1 - x^2)^{1/2}\,dx = \bigl[x(1 - x^2)^{1/2} + \arcsin x\bigr]_{-1}^{h}$, divided by $\pi$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (planes).** If the apex is strictly on the side of the plane where its unit normal $n$ points, the coverage computed for the plane is the exact fraction of the rays of the cone that hit the plane.

*Proof.* The ray with offset $u$ hits the plane if and only if its direction goes toward the plane, $(D + u) \cdot n < 0$. Write $n = (D \cdot n) D + n_\perp$ with $n_\perp \perp D$, $\lVert n_\perp\rVert = \sin\theta$. For $\sin\theta > 0$ the condition is $u \cdot \hat n < -(D \cdot n)/\sin\theta$ with $\hat n = n_\perp/\sin\theta$, a half-plane in the disk $\lVert u\rVert \le \tan A$. Scaling by $1/\tan A$ maps it to $\{x < h\}$ in the unit disk with $h$ as on the page, so Lemma 2 gives the fraction, with $f = 0$ for $h \le -1$ and $f = 1$ for $h \ge 1$. For $\sin\theta = 0$, all rays hit if $D \cdot n < 0$ and none do otherwise. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (spheres, a lower bound).** If $T > 0$, the coverage computed for a sphere is at most the fraction of the rays of the cone that hit the sphere.

*Proof.* Take the cross-section $S$ of $K$ by the plane through $C$ perpendicular to $D$, at distance $T$ from the apex; it is the disk of radius $r_c = T \tan A$ around $CP$, and each of its points lies on exactly one ray of the cone, with the measure of rays proportional to area in $S$. The sphere meets that plane in the disk of radius $R$ around $C$. A ray through a point $q$ of this disk passes through $q$, which is in the closed ball, at the parameter $x = T > 0$, and since $\lVert q - C\rVert < R$ for interior points, the ray enters the open ball; so it hits the sphere. The rays through the lens of the two disks are therefore a subset of the rays that hit, and the lens fraction of Lemma 2 is their measure. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (mixing).** Let the list hold $f_1, \dots, f_m$ in depth order, each in $[0, 1]$. After line L9, $left = \prod_{j \le m}(1 - f_j)$, and the returned color is $\sum_i w_i c_i + w_0 c_{\text{bg}}$ with $w_i = f_i \prod_{j < i}(1 - f_j)$, $w_0 = left$, all $w \ge 0$ and $\sum_{i \ge 0} w_i = 1$.

*Proof.* By induction over the loop, $left$ is the product of the $1 - f_j$ seen so far and line L8 adds $c_i$ with weight $f_i \cdot left$. The weights telescope: $\sum_{i \le m} f_i \prod_{j < i}(1 - f_j) = 1 - \prod_{j \le m}(1 - f_j)$, by induction on $m$. Nonnegativity follows from $f_j \in [0, 1]$. $\square$

If $E_i$ is the set of rays that object $i$ blocks, with measure fraction $f_i$, the exact share of object $i$ is the fraction of $E_i \setminus \bigcup_{j < i} E_j$. This equals $w_i$ when each $E_i$ is independent of $\bigcup_{j < i} E_j$ under the measure, and in particular when the $E_j$ for $j < i$ are all contained in $E_i$ and $E_i$ is the whole cone. For two disjoint sets it does not: the exact shares are $f_1, f_2$, and $w_2 = (1 - f_1) f_2 < f_2$ when $f_1 > 0$.

**Theorem.** TRACE-CONE stops after one pass over the $n$ objects and a pass over at most 8 list entries. Among the spheres with center in front of the apex ($T > 0$), it rejects exactly those that do not meet the cone, and it rejects exactly the planes that no ray of the cone meets. The coverages are the exact areas described on the page, the plane coverage is the exact fraction of hitting rays and the sphere coverage a lower bound for it, and the returned color is a convex combination of the colors of the at most 8 nearest objects and the background.

*Proof.* Termination: the loops of lines L2 and L7 are over finite lists, and the list never exceeds 8 entries, as line L5 truncates it after each insertion. The rest is Lemmas 1 to 5. For a plane, line L3 rejects exactly when the coverage of Lemma 3 is 0, that is when no ray hits it. $\blacksquare$
