A repetition penalty changes the logits of a [language model](/language-model/) before each decoding step so that tokens that already occur in the text become less likely. It fights the loops in which a decoder writes the same phrase again and again, which are common with [greedy decoding](/greedy-decoding/) and [beam search](/beam-search/) on trained models. Two forms are in use. The scaling form of Keskar et al. 2019 (CTRL) divides the logit of each seen token by a factor $\theta > 1$; libraries fix its sign problem by multiplying negative logits by $\theta$ in place of dividing them. The additive form, which commercial APIs expose as the frequency penalty and the presence penalty, subtracts an amount for each occurrence of a token and an amount for its first occurrence. Both are heuristics: the strength is tuned by hand, and a penalty that removes loops can also forbid repetitions that the text needs, such as names and function words.

## In plain words

Models sometimes get stuck and say the same thing over and over. A repetition penalty makes a word less likely to be chosen again once it is already in the text. One kind cuts the score of a used word by a fixed factor. The other kind takes points off the score: some points the first time a word is used, and more points each time it is used again. A small penalty breaks loops. A large one also blocks words that a good text must repeat, such as names or "the".

## When it applies

Use a repetition penalty with any decoder that works on next-token logits: greedy decoding, beam search, [temperature sampling](/temperature-sampling/), [top-k sampling](/top-k-sampling/) and [nucleus sampling](/nucleus-sampling/). It applies before the temperature and the truncation. It is most useful where the decoder is deterministic and loops are likely, which is why CTRL used it with greedy decoding. Decide which tokens count as history: the generated tokens only, or the prompt as well. Libraries often include the prompt by default, which also penalizes words the user wrote.

## The template

The history is the list of tokens so far, and $c_j$ is the number of times token $j$ occurs in it.

```pseudo id=main
PENALIZE(z, history, form)            ▷ z = next-token logits
L1  c ← the count of each token in history
L2  for each token j with c[j] > 0 do
L3      scaling:  z[j] ← z[j] / θ if z[j] > 0, else z[j] · θ
L4      additive: z[j] ← z[j] − c[j] · α_f − α_p
L5  return z
```

Line L3 is the sign-aware scaling form, and line L4 the additive form with a frequency weight $\alpha_f$ and a presence weight $\alpha_p$. A decoder uses one of the two lines, or both. The proof is in [proofs/correctness.md](proofs/correctness.md): the sign-aware form never raises the logit of a seen token for $\theta \ge 1$, the plain division of CTRL can raise the probability of a seen token whose logit is negative, the additive form changes the odds of a token by exactly $e^{-\alpha_f}$ per occurrence and $e^{-\alpha_p}$ once, and only the additive form ignores a shift of all logits.

## Classic examples

**Breaking a greedy loop.** A toy model gives the same logits $(2, 1.5, 1)$ for tokens a, b and c after every prefix, so greedy decoding writes a a a a a a. With the scaling form and $\theta = 1.5$ it writes a b a a a a: after the first a, its logit drops to 1.333 and b wins once, but after that both a and b are seen, the penalty is the same for both, and a wins for good. With the additive form, $\alpha_f = 0.4$ and $\alpha_p = 0$, it writes a a b a b c: the penalty grows with each use, so the tokens keep rotating.

**The sign problem.** For the logits $(2, -1, 0)$ with token 1 seen and $\theta = 2$, the plain division gives $-1 / 2 = -0.5$, which is larger than $-1$: the probability of the seen token goes up from 0.0420 to 0.0674. The sign-aware form gives $-1 \cdot 2 = -2$ and lowers it to 0.0159. Keskar et al. state only the division; the multiplication of negative logits is how libraries such as Hugging Face Transformers implement the CTRL penalty.

**Two seen tokens.** Even the sign-aware form does not always lower the probability of every seen token. For the logits $(4, 0.5, 0)$ with tokens 0 and 1 seen and $\theta = 2$, the new logits are $(2, 0.25, 0)$, and the probability of token 1 rises from 0.0288 to 0.1327, because token 0 loses much more.

**The offset of the logits.** The logits $(2, 1, 0)$ and $(-1, -2, -3)$ give the same distribution, since a softmax ignores a common shift. With token 0 seen and $\theta = 1.5$, the scaling form gives it probability 0.5050 in the first case and 0.5465 in the second. The additive form gives the same answer in both cases.

**API penalties.** OpenAI documents its frequency and presence penalties as $\mu_j - c_j \alpha_{\text{frequency}} - [c_j > 0] \alpha_{\text{presence}}$ on the logit $\mu_j$. Since the change is an amount on the log scale, a frequency penalty of 0.4 on a token used three times divides its odds against an unused token by $e^{1.2} = 3.32$.

## Variations

- **Presence only.** $\alpha_f = 0$: a fixed cost for any token already used, however often.
- **Frequency only.** $\alpha_p = 0$: a cost that grows with each use.
- **A window.** Count only the last $w$ tokens, so that words can come back after a while.
- **No repeated n-grams.** Forbid any token that would complete an n-gram already in the text, by setting its logit to $-\infty$. This is a hard rule, common in beam search for summarization.
- **Negative penalties.** $\theta < 1$ or negative weights reward repetition, for example to make a model copy from its input.
- **Similarity in place of identity.** [Contrastive search](/contrastive-search/) penalizes candidates whose hidden states are similar to the context, which also catches repetition with different words.

## Pitfalls

- **Dividing a negative logit.** It rewards the token. Use the sign-aware form or the additive form.
- **Logit offsets.** The scaling forms act differently on logits that differ only by a constant, and a logit of exactly 0 is not changed at all. The additive form does not have this problem.
- **Necessary repetition.** Names, numbers, code identifiers and function words must repeat. A strong penalty makes the model swap in synonyms or misspell names. Prefer a mild penalty, a window, or excluding some tokens.
- **The prompt counts.** If the history includes the prompt, the model avoids the words of the question in its answer.
- **Tokens, not words.** The penalty works on subword tokens. A common subword shared by many words gets penalized for all of them.
- **It treats a symptom.** Loops come from the model and from searching for high-probability text (see [inadequacy of the mode](/inadequacy-of-the-mode/) and [exposure bias](/exposure-bias/)); the penalty changes the output, not the model.

## Exercises

1. Show that the plain division raises the probability of a seen token with a negative logit when it is the only seen token.
2. Prove that the sign-aware form with $\theta \ge 1$ never lowers the probability of an unseen token.
3. Find logits and a history for which the sign-aware form raises the probability of a seen token.
4. Show that the additive form gives the same distribution for $z$ and $z + a$, and that the scaling form need not.
5. For the toy model with logits $(2, 1.5, 1)$, find the smallest $\theta$ at which the scaling form makes the second token b.
