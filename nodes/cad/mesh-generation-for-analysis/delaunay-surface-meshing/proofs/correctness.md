---
title: The oracle is exact, the dual Voronoi edge is the segment or ray between circumcenters, the incremental update is correct, and the loop stops with persistent facets intact
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

**Claim.** Assume general position: no five points of $E$ on a sphere or four on a plane, no Voronoi vertex on $S$. Let $\delta = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}$, the largest distance that rounding to the integer grid moves a point.

1. For a line $x(s) = (A + sB)/W$ with integer $A, B, W > 0$, the polynomial computed for the sphere is $W^2 F(x(s))$ and for the torus $W^4 F(x(s))$, and the Sturm count gives exactly the number of distinct points of $S$ on the part of the line with $s$ in the given interval.
2. Let $f = abc$ be a triangle of the Delaunay triangulation $D$. If both its tetrahedra $t_1 = f \cup \{d_1\}$ and $t_2 = f \cup \{d_2\}$ are real, with circumcenters $c_1, c_2$, its dual Voronoi edge (the points whose nearest points in $E$ include $a, b, c$) is the segment $c_1 c_2$. If $t_2$ is a ghost, it is the ray from $c_1$ along the normal of $f$ that points away from $d_1$.
3. After an insertion, recomputing the triangles of the removed and the new tetrahedra gives the same restricted triangles and surface balls as computing all of them again.
4. If $\sigma \ge \sigma_0 > \delta$ on a neighborhood of $S$, the loop stops.
5. If a facet of the initial sample has a surface Delaunay ball $B(c_i, r_i)$ with $r_i + \delta \le \frac13 \sigma(c_i)$, it is a restricted triangle at the end.

**What is cited.** Sturm's theorem: for a square-free polynomial $p$ with Sturm sequence $p_0 = p, p_1 = p', p_{k+1} = -\mathrm{rem}(p_{k-1}, p_k)$, the number of distinct real roots in $(a, b]$ is $V(a) - V(b)$, where $V$ counts sign changes, zeros dropped. The bounds on the output (homeomorphism for $\varepsilon \le 0.091$, Hausdorff distance, size) are Theorems 4.3 to 4.5, 5.1 and 5.4 and Lemmas 8.2 to 8.4 of Boissonnat and Oudot (2005) and are not repeated.

**Step 1: the polynomial and the count (part 1).** For the sphere $F(x) = |x|^2 - R^2$, so $W^2 F(x(s)) = \sum_k (A_k + s B_k)^2 - R^2 W^2$, which is what is computed. For the torus $F(x) = (|x|^2 + R^2 - r^2)^2 - 4R^2 (x_1^2 + x_2^2)$; multiplying by $W^4$ gives $q^2 - 4R^2 W^2 (X_1^2 + X_2^2)$ with $X = A + sB$ and $q = |X|^2 + (R^2 - r^2) W^2$, which is what is computed. Since $W > 0$, the roots in $s$ are the parameters where the line meets $S$. All coefficients are integers, so the Sturm sequence is computed exactly in rationals. If the last element of the sequence is not constant, it is $\gcd(p, p')$ up to a factor, and $p / \gcd(p, p')$ has the same distinct roots, all simple; the implementation counts with that. A root exactly at the lower end is tested directly by evaluating $p$ there, and Sturm's theorem counts the rest in $(a, b]$. For a ray, the interval is cut at Cauchy's bound $1 + \max_i |p_i / p_d|$, beyond which there are no roots. The count is exact. Each root is then bracketed by exact bisection to an interval of length at most $\frac{1}{8 \lceil |B| / W \rceil}$ in $s$, so the computed point is within $\frac18$ of the exact point of $S$ along the edge, and it lies in the interval.

**Step 2: the dual Voronoi edge (part 2).** The points equidistant from $a, b, c$ form a line $\ell$ normal to $f$ through its circumcenter. Parametrize it as $x(s)$, affine in $s$. For a point $y$ of $E$, set
$$g_y(s) = |y - x(s)|^2 - |a - x(s)|^2 = 2 (a - y) \cdot x(s) + |y|^2 - |a|^2,$$
which is affine in $s$. The point $x(s)$ has $a, b, c$ among its nearest points exactly when $g_y(s) \ge 0$ for all $y$, so the Voronoi edge is the intersection of half-lines, an interval $I$. A tetrahedron $t_j$ of $D$ has an empty circumsphere, centered at $c_j = x(s_j)$, so $g_y(s_j) \ge 0$ for all $y$ and $s_j \in I$. By convexity $[s_1, s_2] \subseteq I$ (with $s_1 < s_2$ after renaming). Now $g_{d_2}(s_2) = 0$ because $d_2$ is on the circumsphere of $t_2$, and $g_{d_2}(s_1) > 0$ because by general position $d_2$ is not on the circumsphere of $t_1$, and it is not inside it. An affine function that is positive at $s_1$ and zero at $s_2$ is negative beyond $s_2$, so $I$ ends at $s_2$; in the same way $g_{d_1}$ ends it at $s_1$. So $I = [s_1, s_2]$, the segment $c_1 c_2$.

If $t_2$ is a ghost, $f$ is a face of the convex hull, and every point of $E$ is on the side of its plane that contains $d_1$, or on the plane. Let $n$ be the normal of $f$ pointing away from $d_1$, and $x(s) = c_1 + s n$. The slope of $g_y$ is $2(a - y) \cdot n \ge 0$ for every $y$, since $y - a$ points to the inner side or along the plane. So each $g_y$ is nondecreasing, and $g_y(0) \ge 0$ makes it nonnegative on $[0, \infty)$. The slope for $d_1$ is positive and $g_{d_1}(0) = 0$, so $I$ ends at $s = 0$ on the other side. Hence $I$ is the ray from $c_1$ along $n$. In both cases every computed center lies on $I$, so its ball through $a, b, c$ contains no point of $E$ in its interior.

The ghost test of the insertion uses the same fact: a new point conflicts with a ghost tetrahedron exactly when it is strictly outside the hull face, which is the limit of the insphere test as the fourth vertex goes to infinity on the outer side. With this rule, Bowyer-Watson insertion with ghosts produces the Delaunay triangulation of $E \cup \{p\}$ together with ghosts on the new hull faces, as for points inside the hull.

**Step 3: the incremental update (part 3).** Inserting $p$ removes the tetrahedra (and ghosts) in conflict with $p$ and creates new ones joined to $p$; every other tetrahedron is unchanged. By Step 2 the Voronoi edge of a triangle, and so its surface balls, depends only on the triangle and its two tetrahedra. A triangle that is not a face of a removed or new tetrahedron has the same two tetrahedra before and after, so its result does not change. Every other triangle that still exists is a face of a new tetrahedron and is recomputed; one that no longer exists is removed. The surface balls are computed by the same function either way, so the stored set equals a fresh computation.

**Step 4: termination (part 4).** When a center $x$ of a bad ball $B(x, r)$ is inserted, $r > \sigma(x') \ge \sigma_0$ where $x'$ is the rounded point that is evaluated, and by Step 2 no point of $E$ lies within distance $r$ of $x$. The inserted point $x'$ is within $\delta$ of $x$, so it is at distance at least $r - \delta > \sigma_0 - \delta > 0$ from every earlier point. So the inserted points are pairwise at least $\sigma_0 - \delta$ apart, and they lie within $\delta + \frac18$ of $S$ by Step 1. The open balls of radius $\frac12 (\sigma_0 - \delta)$ around them are disjoint and lie in a bounded region, so there are finitely many, and the loop, which inserts one point per round, stops.

**Step 5: persistent facets (part 5).** Suppose a point $x'$, rounded from the center $x$ of a bad ball $B(x, r)$, is the first inserted point inside the open ball $B(c_i, r_i)$; the ball was bad because $r > \sigma(x')$. Since $|x' - c_i| < r_i$ and $\sigma$ is 1-Lipschitz, $\sigma(x') > \sigma(c_i) - r_i \ge \frac23 \sigma(c_i) + \delta$. For a corner $v$ of the facet,
$$|v - x| \le |v - c_i| + |c_i - x'| + |x' - x| < 2 r_i + \delta \le \tfrac23 \sigma(c_i) - \delta < \sigma(x') < r.$$
So $v$ lies in the interior of $B(x, r)$, which is empty by Step 2, a contradiction. Hence no point is ever inserted inside $B(c_i, r_i)$. That ball stays empty, its center stays on $S$ and is equidistant from the three corners, so it stays a surface Delaunay ball of the facet, and the facet stays in $\mathrm{Del}|_S(E)$.

**What is not proved.** That the output is homeomorphic to $S$ is Boissonnat and Oudot's theorem for loose $\varepsilon$-samples of points on $S$ with $\varepsilon \le 0.091$. The implementation's points are within $\delta + \frac18$ of $S$, not on it, and the tests use $\sigma$ far above $0.091\, d_M$, so for them the topology is checked directly: every edge in two triangles, every vertex link one cycle, the Euler characteristic, and orientability. The choice of the worst ball first is not needed by the proofs. $\blacksquare$
