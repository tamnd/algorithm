---
title: A quad mesh has an even number of boundary edges, and an accepted Q-Morph step gives a conforming mixed mesh with one new quad
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**What is proved and what is not.** The proof covers the parity rule for quad meshes and the correctness of one Q-Morph step as implemented here: side edges, top edge recovery by flips, and the merge of the triangles inside the quad. Nothing is proved about Q-Morph as a whole. Owen and his coauthors give no proof that the front always closes and no bound on the angles of the quads; the method relies on side edge swaps and splits, smoothing and closure rules that are tuned by experiment. One fact about recovery is also not proved here: that a crossing edge that can be flipped always exists. The code reports a failure if none exists, and the tests measure that this never happened.

**Theorem 1 (parity).** In a mesh of a planar region by $Q$ quads, in which every edge lies in one quad (a boundary edge) or two quads (an interior edge), the number $E_b$ of boundary edges is even.

*Proof.* Count pairs (quad, edge of that quad), the edge sides. Each quad has 4 edges, so there are $4Q$ pairs. Each interior edge is in two pairs and each boundary edge in one, so $4Q = 2E_i + E_b$. Then $E_b = 4Q - 2E_i$ is even. $\square$

So Q-Morph needs an even number of edges on the boundary of the triangle mesh, and an even number of edges on every front loop, before it can end with quads only. A triangle mesh of a region whose boundary has an odd number of edges cannot become an all-quad mesh by merging alone.

**Setting.** The input is a conforming triangle mesh $T$: counterclockwise triangles with exact coordinates, no directed edge used twice, and no vertex in the interior of an edge. The front edge $a \to b$ has a triangle on its left. The step picks side edges $ac$ and $bd$, which are edges of $T$, and accepts them only if $c \ne d$ and the quad $q = (a, b, d, c)$ is strictly convex and counterclockwise (L3, L4).

**Lemma 2 (a flip keeps the mesh).** Let $uv$ be an edge with the triangles $(u, v, k)$ and $(v, u, l)$, and let the quad $u, l, v, k$ be strictly convex. Then replacing the two triangles by $(k, u, l)$ and $(l, v, k)$ gives a conforming mesh of the same region, with the same boundary edges and the same total area.

*Proof.* The code accepts the flip only if $\operatorname{orient}(k, u, l) > 0$ and $\operatorname{orient}(l, v, k) > 0$. Since $k$ is left of $u \to v$ and $l$ right of it, these two tests and the two old triangles make the quad strictly convex, and its two diagonals split it into two counterclockwise triangles each. So the new triangles cover the same quad with disjoint interiors, and their areas add up to the same value. The four outer directed edges $u \to l$, $l \to v$, $v \to k$, $k \to u$ are the same as before, so the neighbours see the same edges. The new edge $kl$ is used once in each direction. No vertex lies inside $kl$, because the open diagonal of a strictly convex quad lies in the interior of the quad, which contains no vertex of a conforming mesh. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (recovery).** If RECOVER returns a mesh, it is a conforming mesh of the same region, $cd$ is an edge of it, and no edge crosses the open segment $cd$. The quad edges $ab$, $bd$ and $ca$ are never flipped. The number of flips is at least the number of edges of $T$ that cross $cd$.

*Proof.* Each step is a flip of Lemma 2, so the mesh stays conforming. The loop stops only when no edge crosses $cd$, and the code then checks that $cd$ is an edge. A vertex on the segment $cd$ makes the code fail before the loop, so it is not on the path. The code flips only edges that cross the open segment $cd$ at a point inside both. The edges $ca$ and $bd$ share an end with $cd$, so the orientation of that end against the line $cd$ is zero, and they do not cross. The edge $ab$ is the opposite side of $cd$ in the strictly convex quad, so it does not meet $cd$. A flip removes one edge and adds one, so the number of crossing edges falls by at most one per flip, which gives the lower bound. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (each triangle is inside or outside).** After recovery, every triangle lies either inside the closed quad $q$ or outside its interior.

*Proof.* The four sides of $q$ are edges of the mesh, by Lemma 3. The interior of a triangle meets no edge of a conforming mesh, so it does not meet the boundary of $q$. The interior of a triangle is connected, so it lies in the interior of $q$ or outside the closed quad. $\square$

**Theorem 5 (one step).** If the step returns a quad $q$ and a list $R$ of triangles, then $R \cup \{q\}$ is a conforming mesh of the same region as $T$: every cell is strictly convex and counterclockwise, no directed edge is used twice, the boundary edges and the total area are the same, the quad contains $ab$ and the top edge $dc$, and the deleted triangles are exactly the triangles of the recovered mesh that lie inside $q$.

*Proof.* The flood fill (L7) starts at the triangle left of $a \to b$. That triangle has points near the middle of $ab$ on the left of $ab$, which are inside $q$, so by Lemma 4 it lies inside $q$. The fill moves only across edges that are not sides of $q$, and a triangle inside $q$ shares such an edge only with a triangle whose interior meets the interior of $q$; by Lemma 4 that triangle is inside too. So every filled triangle is inside $q$. The filled triangles have disjoint interiors, and L8 checks that their areas add up to the area of $q$. A closed subset of $q$ with the full area of $q$ is $q$, so the filled triangles cover $q$. Then every triangle inside $q$ is filled, because its interior would overlap the cover otherwise. This is the set that the test compares with the brute force of centroids. Inside $q$, no vertex lies on a side other than at a corner: the sides $ab$, $bd$, $ca$ were mesh edges before recovery and stay mesh edges, and L5 rejects a vertex on $cd$. So the boundary edges of the filled set are exactly $a \to b$, $b \to d$, $d \to c$, $c \to a$, the directed edges of $q$. Replacing the filled triangles by $q$ keeps every directed edge outside $q$ and puts the same four directed edges back, so no directed edge is used twice, the neighbours across the sides see the same edges, and the area is the same. The vertices strictly inside $q$ leave the mesh with their triangles. $\square$

**Termination.** Side edge selection looks at the neighbours of two vertices. Recovery does at most a fixed cap of flips, here 1000, and each flip costs $O(n)$ in this simple code. The flood fill visits each triangle once. So the step always stops. Whether recovery always succeeds without the cap is the open point named above. On 37 random recoveries in meshes of 36 vertices, with up to 15 crossing edges, recovery never failed, and in 23 of them the number of flips equalled the lower bound of Lemma 3.

**What is not proved.** No bound on the angles or the shape of the quad is proved. The side edges are the mesh edges closest to the ideal direction, but the closest edge can still be far from it, and the quad can be thin; Q-Morph improves the side edges by swaps and splits and smooths the mesh afterwards, and the quality of the result is measured, not proved. The step can fail when the two side edges end at the same vertex or the quad is not convex; Q-Morph then uses other side edges or a split, which are not here.
