---
title: The shrinking ball finds the largest empty tangent disk, and the medial midline is exact on a strip and an annular sector
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

We use the notation of the page. Arithmetic is exact. For a sample $p$ with unit inward normal $n$ and $r > 0$, let $c(r) = p + r n$ and $B(r) = \{x : |x - c(r)| \le r\}$. For a sample $q \ne p$, let $\rho(q) = |q - p|^2 / (2 n \cdot (q - p))$ if $n \cdot (q - p) > 0$, and $\rho(q) = \infty$ otherwise. Let $\rho^* = \min_{q \ne p} \rho(q)$.

**Lemma 1 (inside test).** A point $q \ne p$ lies in the interior of $B(r)$ if and only if $r > \rho(q)$. It lies on the circle if and only if $r = \rho(q)$.

*Proof.* $|q - c(r)|^2 - r^2 = |q - p|^2 - 2 r \, n \cdot (q - p)$, because $|n| = 1$. If $n \cdot (q - p) \le 0$, this is at least $|q - p|^2 > 0$, so $q$ is outside $B(r)$ for every $r$, which matches $\rho(q) = \infty$. If $n \cdot (q - p) > 0$, the sign of the expression is the sign of $\rho(q) - r$. $\square$

So the disks $B(r)$ grow with $r$, and $B(r)$ holds no sample in its interior exactly when $r \le \rho^*$.

**Theorem 1 (the shrinking ball).** Let $r_0 > \rho^*$. Lines L2 to L5 stop after at most $n - 1$ shrinks, with $r = \rho^*$ and a touching sample $p_k$ with $\rho(p_k) = \rho^*$, which lies on the final circle. If $\rho^* = \infty$, they stop at once with $r = r_0$ and no touching sample.

*Proof.* Invariant: $r \ge \rho^*$. It holds at the start. Line L5 sets $r = \rho(p_j)$, which is at least $\rho^*$ by the definition of the minimum. Stop test: the sample $p_i$ lies on every circle, at distance $r$ from $c$. So if the nearest sample $p_j$ to $c$ is $p_i$, or if $|p_j - c| \ge r$, then no sample lies in the interior of $B(r)$, and Lemma 1 gives $r \le \rho^*$. With the invariant, $r = \rho^*$. Shrink step: if the test fails, $p_j \ne p_i$ lies in the interior, so $r > \rho(p_j)$ by Lemma 1, and line L5 decreases $r$ strictly. Each new value of $r$ is $\rho(q)$ for a sample $q \ne p_i$, and the values strictly decrease, so no sample is used twice, and there are at most $n - 1$ shrinks. At the end the last touching sample $p_k$ has $\rho(p_k) = r = \rho^*$, so it lies on the circle by Lemma 1. If $\rho^* = \infty$, no sample satisfies $r > \rho(q)$, so the first test succeeds and $r$ stays $r_0$. $\square$

**Theorem 2 (the strip).** Let $R = [0, L] \times [-t/2, t/2]$ with $L > t$. The medial axis of $R$ is the segment $S$ from $(t/2, 0)$ to $(L - t/2, 0)$, where the maximal disks have radius $t/2$ and the separation angle is $180°$, together with four segments on the corner bisectors, where the separation angle is $90°$. So pruning with any $\theta_{\min}$ in $(90°, 180°]$ keeps exactly $S$, and $S$ is shorter than the midline $[0, L] \times \{0\}$ of the plate by $t/2$ at each end.

*Proof.* For an interior point $x = (x_1, x_2)$, the distance to the boundary is $\delta(x) = \min(x_1, L - x_1, t/2 - x_2, t/2 + x_2)$, the distance to the nearest side. The nearest point on a side is the foot of the perpendicular, since $x$ projects inside every side of the rectangle. For a convex polygon, $x$ is on the medial axis exactly when at least two sides reach the minimum, or $x$ is a limit of such points. Two sides tie in these cases. The top and bottom tie when $x_2 = 0$, and they are nearest when also $x_1 \ge t/2$ and $L - x_1 \ge t/2$, which gives $S$. The feet are $(x_1, \pm t/2)$, opposite each other through $x$, so the separation angle is $180°$ and the radius is $t/2$. A long side and a short side tie on a corner bisector, for example $x_1 = t/2 - x_2$ for the left and the top side, and they are nearest for $0 \le x_2$ and $x_1 \le t/2$. The feet are $(0, x_2)$ and $(x_1, t/2)$, in the directions $(-1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ from $x$, so the separation angle is $90°$. The left and right sides cannot both be nearest, because that needs $x_1 = L/2$ and $L/2 \le t/2$, against $L > t$. The four bisector segments meet $S$ only at its ends, where the top and bottom also tie and the angle $180°$ is available. So the points with a separation angle above $90°$ form $S$, and $S$ ends at $x_1 = t/2$ and $x_1 = L - t/2$. $\square$

**Theorem 3 (the sampled strip).** Sample $R$ with points on its sides and with the inward normals of the sides. Let $p = (x_p, -t/2)$ be a sample on the bottom side, let $d$ be the smallest value of $|x_q - x_p|$ over the samples $q = (x_q, t/2)$ on the top side, let $r^* = t/2 + d^2 / (2 t)$, and assume $\min(x_p, L - x_p) > r^*$. Then the shrinking ball at $p$ ends with the radius $r^*$, the center $(x_p, d^2 / (2 t))$, and a touching sample on the top side, and the separation angle is $180° - 2 \arctan(d / t)$. The same holds for the top side with the sign of the center reversed. So line L7 keeps $p$ when $r_{\min} \le r^*$ and $d \le t \tan((180° - \theta_{\min}) / 2)$.

*Proof.* Here $n = (0, 1)$. A sample $q$ on the bottom side has $n \cdot (q - p) = 0$, so $\rho(q) = \infty$. A sample $q = (x_q, t/2)$ on the top side has $n \cdot (q - p) = t$ and $|q - p|^2 = (x_q - x_p)^2 + t^2$, so $\rho(q) = t/2 + (x_q - x_p)^2 / (2 t)$, which is smallest, equal to $r^*$, for the offset $d$. A sample $q$ on a short side, for example the left one, is at distance $x_p > r^*$ from the vertical line through the center $c(r^*) = (x_p, -t/2 + r^*)$, so it lies outside $B(r^*)$, and Lemma 1 gives $\rho(q) > r^*$. The right side is the same with $L - x_p$. So $\rho^* = r^*$, attained only on the top side, and Theorem 1 gives the radius $r^*$ and a touching sample $q$ on the top side with $\rho(q) = r^*$, so $|x_q - x_p| = d$. The center is $(x_p, -t/2 + r^*) = (x_p, d^2 / (2 t))$. For the angle, take the touching top sample $q = (x_p + d, t/2)$, with either sign of the offset. From $c$, the vector to $p$ is $(0, -r^*)$ and the vector to $q$ is $(d, t/2 - d^2/(2t))$, of length $r^*$. So the cosine of the angle is $-(t/2 - d^2/(2t)) / r^* = -(t^2 - d^2) / (t^2 + d^2) = -\cos(2 \arctan(d/t))$, and the angle is $180° - 2 \arctan(d/t)$. The last claim follows from line L7. $\square$

**Corollary.** If both long sides are sampled at the same values of $x$, then $d = 0$, and every kept sample of Theorem 3 gives a center on the midline $x_2 = 0$ with radius exactly $t/2$. If the samples are at spacing $s$ and the top side is shifted by $s/2$, then $d = s/2$, and the center and the radius are both off by $d^2 / (2t) = s^2 / (8 t)$, and this value is attained.

**Theorem 4 (the annular sector).** Let $A = \{(\rho \cos \theta, \rho \sin \theta) : r_1 \le \rho \le r_2, \ 0 \le \theta \le \varphi\}$, $t = r_2 - r_1$, $\rho_m = (r_1 + r_2)/2$, and assume $t < r_1$, $2 \beta < \varphi \le 180°$ with $\beta = \arcsin(t / (2 r_1))$. Let $\theta_0 = \arcsin(t / (2 \rho_m))$. (a) The points of the medial axis of $A$ with a separation angle above $90° + \beta$ form exactly the arc $\Gamma = \{\rho = \rho_m, \ \theta_0 \le \theta \le \varphi - \theta_0\}$, with radius $t/2$ and angle $180°$. (b) If both arcs are sampled at the same angles $\theta_k$, then for every sample $p$ on an arc with $\theta_0 \le \theta_k \le \varphi - \theta_0$, the shrinking ball gives the center $\rho_m (\cos \theta_k, \sin \theta_k)$ and the radius $t/2$ exactly.

*Proof.* Write $e(\theta) = (\cos \theta, \sin \theta)$ and take a point $x = \rho \, e(\theta)$ of $A$. Its distance to the inner arc is $\rho - r_1$ and to the outer arc is $r_2 - \rho$, with the nearest points $r_1 e(\theta)$ and $r_2 e(\theta)$, in the directions $-e(\theta)$ and $e(\theta)$ from $x$. So the distance to the nearer arc is at most $t/2$. Its distance to the line of the straight end $\theta = 0$ is $\rho \sin \theta$, with the foot in the direction $(0, -1)$, and the same holds for the end $\theta = \varphi$ with $\varphi - \theta$. The nearest point on an end segment is either this foot or a corner of $A$. A corner is never strictly nearer than an arc: for the corner $c = r_1 e(0)$, $|x - c| \ge |x| - |c| = \rho - r_1$, and for $c = r_2 e(0)$, $|x - c| \ge |c| - |x| = r_2 - \rho$, and the same for the corners at $\varphi$. So a medial point is a tie of the minimum distance between two of the four pieces, with the nearest points as above.

Arc and arc: $\rho - r_1 = r_2 - \rho$ gives $\rho = \rho_m$ and the distance $t/2$, and the ends must be no nearer. If $\theta < \theta_0$, the foot on the line $\theta = 0$ has the abscissa $\rho_m \cos \theta \ge \rho_m \cos \theta_0 = \sqrt{\rho_m^2 - t^2/4} = \sqrt{r_1 r_2}$, which lies in $[r_1, r_2]$, so the foot is on the end segment, at the distance $\rho_m \sin \theta < t/2$, and the end is strictly nearer. If $\theta_0 \le \theta \le \varphi - \theta_0$, then $\sin \theta \ge \sin \theta_0$ because $\varphi \le 180°$, so the distance to the line, and hence to the segment, is at least $t/2$. The end $\theta = \varphi$ is the same with $\varphi - \theta$. So the tie gives exactly $\theta_0 \le \theta \le \varphi - \theta_0$. The nearest points are opposite through $x$, so the angle is $180°$. This is $\Gamma$. Arc and end, for the end $\theta = 0$: the nearest point on the end is its foot, which needs $\theta < 90°$, and the distance $\rho \sin \theta$ is at most $t/2$, so $\sin \theta \le t / (2 r_1)$ and $\theta \le \beta$. The angle between $\pm e(\theta)$ and $(0, -1)$ has the cosine $\mp \sin \theta$, so it is $90° \pm \theta \le 90° + \beta$. The end $\theta = \varphi$ is the same with $\varphi - \theta$. End and end: the tie needs $\theta = \varphi / 2$ and $\rho \sin(\varphi/2) \le t/2$, so $\sin(\varphi/2) \le t/(2 r_1)$, against $\varphi > 2 \beta$. An arc or an end alone has a unique nearest point, since the origin is not in $A$. So the points with an angle above $90° + \beta$ form $\Gamma$, which proves (a).

For (b), let $p = r_1 e(\theta_k)$ with $n = e(\theta_k)$. The disk $B(t/2)$ has the center $c = \rho_m e(\theta_k)$. The outer sample $r_2 e(\theta_k)$ has $\rho = t^2 / (2 t) = t/2$. Another outer sample $q = r_2 e(\theta_j)$, with $\gamma = \cos(\theta_j - \theta_k) < 1$, has $|q - p|^2 = r_1^2 + r_2^2 - 2 r_1 r_2 \gamma$ and $n \cdot (q - p) = r_2 \gamma - r_1$. If $r_2 \gamma \le r_1$, then $\rho(q) = \infty$. Otherwise $\rho(q) \ge t/2$ is $r_1^2 + r_2^2 - 2 r_1 r_2 \gamma \ge (r_2 - r_1)(r_2 \gamma - r_1)$, which expands to $(r_2^2 + r_1 r_2)(1 - \gamma) \ge 0$, true, and strict since $\gamma < 1$. An inner sample $q = r_1 e(\theta_j)$ has $n \cdot (q - p) = r_1 (\gamma - 1) \le 0$, so $\rho(q) = \infty$. A sample $q$ on the end $\theta = 0$ lies on a line at distance $\rho_m \sin \theta_k \ge \rho_m \sin \theta_0 = t/2$ from $c$, so it is not in the interior of $B(t/2)$, and Lemma 1 gives $\rho(q) \ge t/2$; the end $\theta = \varphi$ is the same. So $\rho^* = t/2$, attained by $r_2 e(\theta_k)$, and Theorem 1 gives the radius $t/2$ and the center $p + (t/2) n = \rho_m e(\theta_k)$. A sample on the outer arc is the same with $n = -e(\theta_k)$ and the roles of the arcs swapped. $\blacksquare$
