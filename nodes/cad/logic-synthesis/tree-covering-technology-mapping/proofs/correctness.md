---
title: Tree covering gives a valid cover of least area among tree covers
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

Let $G$ be a subject graph: a directed acyclic graph of primary inputs (PIs), inverters and 2-input NAND gates, with a set of output gates. Let $V^*$ be the set of gates from which an output can be reached, and for $u \in V^*$ let $\mathrm{fo}(u)$ be the number of fanin slots of gates of $V^*$ that hold $u$. Let $R$ be the set of gates $u \in V^*$ with $\mathrm{fo}(u) > 1$ or $u$ an output, as at line L1. A match of library gate $g$ at $v$ is a map of the pattern of $g$ into $G$ that sends the pattern root to $v$, each inner node to a gate of the same kind, and the children of an inner node to the fanins of its image, in some order. Its covered set is the image of the inner nodes, and its inputs are the images of the pattern leaves. An *admissible* match at $v$ is one whose covered nodes other than $v$ are gates outside $R$, as at line L4.

**Lemma 1 (the trees).** Every gate $w \in V^* \setminus R$ has exactly one fanout gate in $V^*$, and that fanout reads $w$ in one slot. Following these fanouts from $w$ reaches a unique first node $r(w) \in R$. With $r(r) = r$ for $r \in R$, the sets $T(r) = \{w \in V^* : r(w) = r\}$ split the gates of $V^*$, and in each $T(r)$ the fanout edges form a tree rooted at $r$.

*Proof.* A gate $w \in V^*$ reaches an output, and it is not an output since $w \notin R$, so it has a fanout in $V^*$, and $\mathrm{fo}(w) = 1$ gives exactly one, in one slot. The fanout is also in $V^*$. The walk moves forward in a finite acyclic graph and can stop only at a node of $R$, since every node outside $R$ has a next node; it must stop because it reaches an output after finitely many steps and outputs are in $R$. The walk is unique, so $r(w)$ is well defined, the sets $T(r)$ are disjoint, and they cover $V^*$. The parent of $w \ne r$ in $T(r)$ is its unique fanout, so the edges form a tree. $\square$

For $v \in T(r)$, let $T_v$ be the set of nodes of $T(r)$ below $v$, that is $v$ and the gates of $T(r)$ whose walk passes $v$. A *cover of $T_v$* is a set $M$ of admissible matches with roots in $T_v$ such that $v$ is a root, every input of a match of $M$ that is a gate outside $R$ is a root of a match of $M$, and the roots are distinct. Its area is the sum of the areas of its gates.

**Lemma 2 (shape of an admissible match).** Let $m$ be an admissible match at $v \in V^*$. Then its covered nodes are in $T_v$, its inputs are distinct when they are gates outside $R$, and for distinct inputs $u, u'$ of $m$ that are gates outside $R$, the sets $T_u$ and $T_{u'}$ are disjoint and do not meet the covered set.

*Proof.* A covered node other than $v$ is a gate outside $R$, so it has one fanout, which is its parent in the image of the pattern. By induction from the root, each covered node is in $T_v$. An input $u$ that is a gate outside $R$ has one fanout slot, so only one pattern leaf can land on it. Its fanout is a covered node, so $u \in T_v$, and $T_u$ lies below that covered node. If $T_u$ and $T_{u'}$ met, or if $T_u$ held a covered node, one node would have two paths up to $v$ through the tree, which Lemma 1 rules out: a node of $T_u$ goes up through $u$, and a covered node or a node of $T_{u'}$ does not go through $u$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the dynamic program).** For every gate $v \in V^*$, line L6 sets $\mathrm{cost}(v)$ to the least area of a cover of $T_v$, and $\mathrm{choice}(v)$ is the root match of such a cover.

*Proof.* Induction on the topological order, which visits the inputs of a match before its root. Let $M$ be a cover of $T_v$ and $m$ its match at $v$. Let $U$ be the inputs of $m$ that are gates outside $R$. For $u \in U$, the matches of $M$ with roots in $T_u$ form a cover of $T_u$: $u$ is a root, and an input of such a match that is a gate outside $R$ lies in $T_u$, by Lemma 2 applied to that match. By Lemma 2 these sets of matches are disjoint for different $u$ and do not contain $m$. So the area of $M$ is at least $\mathrm{area}(m) + \sum_{u \in U} \mathrm{cost}(u)$, by induction, which is the value of line L5 for $m$, since leafcost is 0 for PIs and nodes of $R$. Conversely, for each admissible match $m$ at $v$, the union of $m$ with least covers of $T_u$ for $u \in U$ is a cover of $T_v$ with exactly that area. Line L4 tries every admissible match, so the least value of line L5 is the least area of a cover. $\square$

**Theorem.** Lines L8 to L11 return a cover of $G$ that computes the outputs of $G$, and its area is the least area of a tree cover, a cover whose matches are all admissible. The area equals $\sum_{r \in R} \mathrm{cost}(r)$.

*Proof.* *Validity.* Each gate in the cover has the match $\mathrm{choice}(v)$, and line L10 adds every gate input of that match to the work list, so every input of a chosen match is a PI or a gate of the cover. A match computes the function of its root from its inputs, because the library gate computes the function of its pattern, and the pattern has the same shape as the subject graph below the root. By induction in topological order, every gate of the cover computes the same function as in $G$, and so do the outputs.

*Which matches are read back.* Starting from the outputs, which are in $R$, line L10 adds $\mathrm{choice}(r)$ for a root $r \in R$, then the roots of the least cover of $T_r$ by Lemma 3, and at each input in $R$ it starts again at that root. Every $r \in R$ is reached: $r$ is in $V^*$, so a path leads from $r$ to an output, and the tree that contains the next node of the path on the way out has $r$ as an input of its cover, since every node of a tree is covered and every edge into a covered node from outside the covered set is an input. So the returned cover is the union over $r \in R$ of least covers of $T_r$, and its area is $\sum_{r} \mathrm{cost}(r)$.

*Optimality among tree covers.* Let $M$ be any tree cover. Every output gate is a root of $M$. Every gate $r \in R$ is a root of $M$ as well: some gate reads $r$, and the match that covers that gate is admissible, so $r$ is not one of its covered nodes but one of its inputs, which must be a root. For each $r \in R$, the matches of $M$ with roots in $T_r$ form a cover of $T_r$, by the same argument as in Lemma 3, and these sets are disjoint because the $T_r$ are disjoint. So the area of $M$ is at least $\sum_{r \in R} \mathrm{cost}(r)$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark.** A cover in which a match covers a node of $R$ other than its root duplicates the logic of that node, and it is not a tree cover. Such covers can have less area, as the second example of the page shows, so the theorem does not say that the result is a least area cover of $G$.

**Termination.** Each gate is handled once at lines L2 to L6, each node has finitely many matches, and line L10 adds a gate to the cover at most once.
