---
title: The contour packing of an O-tree is correct, takes linear time, and equals the packing of its left-child right-sibling B*-tree
proves: [correctness, complexity]
status: full
---

We use the pseudocode and the conventions of the page. An O-tree on $n$ blocks is an ordered tree with $n + 1$ nodes whose root $R$ is a virtual block with $x_R = 0$ and $w_R = 0$. It is given by a string $T$ of $n$ zeros and $n$ ones, a depth-first walk in which 0 goes down to the next child and 1 goes back up, and by the list $\pi$ of the blocks in the order in which the walk meets them. The packing rules are: a child $c$ of $p$ has $x_c = x_p + w_p$, the blocks are placed in the order $\pi$, and
$$y_b = \max\bigl(\{0\} \cup \{\, y_a + h_a : a \text{ before } b \text{ in } \pi,\ x_a < x_b + w_b,\ x_b < x_a + w_a \,\}\bigr).$$

**Lemma 1 (the string is a tree).** A string of $n$ zeros and $n$ ones is the walk of an ordered tree with $n + 1$ nodes if and only if no prefix has more ones than zeros. The tree is unique, and its nodes other than the root are met in preorder.

*Proof.* Read the string with a stack that holds the root. A 0 makes a new child of the top node, after the children made before, and pushes it. A 1 pops. The prefix condition says that a 1 never pops the root, and the equal counts say that the walk ends at the root. Each tree gives back its own walk, so the map is a bijection. A node is made when the walk first reaches it, after its parent and before its later siblings, which is preorder. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (the start segment).** When line L5 takes a block $b$ with parent $p$, the segment $s$ in the top entry of the stack is in the contour and $s.x_0 = x_b$.

*Proof.* If $b$ is the first child of $p$, the entry of $p$ was pushed when $p$ was placed, with the segment after the segment of $p$, which starts at $x_p + w_p = x_b$. No block was placed in between, because the bit after the 0 of $p$ is the 0 of $b$. For $p = R$ the segment is the head, which starts at $0 = x_b$. If $b$ has an earlier sibling $a$, line L9 stored the segment of $a$ in the entry of $p$, with $x_0 = x_a = x_p + w_p = x_b$. Between the placement of $a$ and that of $b$ only the subtree of $a$ is placed. Each of its blocks $c$ has $x_c \ge x_a + w_a > x_a$, by induction on the depth below $a$. A placement of $c$ changes the fields only of its own start segment, which starts at $x_c$, and unlinks only segments that start strictly between $x_c$ and $x_c + w_c$. So the segment of $a$ is not changed and stays in the contour. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the contour and the y values).** Before each block is placed, the list from the head is the contour of the placed blocks: increasing segments that cover $[0, \infty)$, where the height at each point $t$ is the largest top $y_a + h_a$ of a placed block with $x_a \le t < x_a + w_a$, or 0. Lines L6 and L7 set $y_b$ to the value of the formula.

*Proof.* At line L1 there are no blocks and one segment at height 0. By Lemma 2 the walk of line L7 starts at the segment that contains $x_b$ and visits the segments that meet $[x_b, x_b + w_b)$, so $y_b$ is the largest height on that interval. By the contour property this is the formula, because two half-open intervals meet if and only if $x_a < x_b + w_b$ and $x_b < x_a + w_a$. Line L8 sets the height on $[x_b, x_b + w_b)$ to $y_b + h_b$, which is not lower than the old heights there, and keeps all other heights. So the contour property holds after the placement. $\square$

**Theorem 1 (no overlap).** In the packing no two blocks overlap, each block lies on the floor or on a block below it, and the returned $W$ and $H$ are the width and the height of the bounding box.

*Proof.* Let $a$ be placed before $b$. If their $x$-ranges meet, then $y_b \ge y_a + h_a$ by Lemma 3; if not, a vertical line separates them. If $y_b > 0$, then $y_b$ is the top of a block whose $x$-range meets that of $b$, and $b$ rests on it. Line L8 and the next line keep the largest right side and the largest top. $\square$

**Theorem 2 (the B*-tree of an O-tree).** Let $B$ be the left-child right-sibling form of the O-tree without $R$: the left child of a block is its first child, the right child of a block is its next sibling, and the root of $B$ is the first child of $R$. Then $B$ is a B*-tree, the map from O-trees to B*-trees on the same blocks is a bijection, and the packing of the O-tree equals the packing of $B$.

*Proof.* The left-child right-sibling form is a bijection between ordered forests and binary trees, and the children of $R$ form an ordered forest of the $n$ blocks; FROM-B-STAR walks it back. The preorder of $B$ visits a block, then the subtree of its left child (the subtrees of its children in the O-tree) and then the subtree of its right child (its later siblings and their subtrees). This is the preorder of the O-tree, so the blocks are placed in the same order. The $x$ rules agree: the first child $c$ of $p$ is the left child of $p$ in $B$, and both rules give $x_p + w_p$. A later sibling $c$ of $a$ is the right child of $a$ in $B$, and the O-tree rule gives $x_p + w_p = x_a$, the B*-tree rule for a right child. The first child of $R$ and its siblings have $x = 0$ in both. With the same order and the same $x$ values, the formula for $y$ is the same, so the packings are equal. $\square$

**Not claimed.** The packing need not be compact to the left, and two O-trees can give the same packing. Take three unit squares. The O-tree $T = 001011$, $\pi = (A, B, C)$ makes $A$ the child of $R$ and $B$, $C$ the two children of $A$. It puts $A$ at $(0, 0)$, $B$ at $(1, 0)$ and $C$ at $(1, 1)$, and $C$ can move left to $(0, 1)$. The O-trees $T = 010011$, $\pi = (A, D, E)$ and $T = 001101$, $\pi = (A, E, D)$ are different, and both put $A$ at $(0, 0)$, $D$ at $(0, 1)$ and $E$ at $(1, 0)$. The tests check both examples. Guo, Cheng and Yoshimura prove, in the section on admissible placements of their DAC 1999 paper, that each admissible placement has an O-tree, and that a placement can be made admissible by turns of horizontal and vertical O-trees. This is cited. The tests check its consequence for $n \le 4$: the least area over all O-trees equals the least area of any packing.

**Complexity.** Each bit is read one time. A 1 pops one entry in $O(1)$ time. A 0 does $O(1)$ work plus one step for each segment of its walk. As for the B*-tree, every segment of a walk other than the first is unlinked or cut, and each placement makes at most one new segment, so all walks together take at most $2n + 1$ steps. The packing takes $O(n)$ time and $O(n)$ space, and so do PARENTS, TO-B-STAR and FROM-B-STAR. The number of O-trees is $C_n \cdot n!$, where $C_n$ is the number of tree strings with $n$ zeros, the $n$th Catalan number. $\blacksquare$
