---
title: The dexel merge computes regularized Boolean operations, and the cut subtracts the swept tool
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Setting.** A dexel is a finite list $I = [(l_1, u_1), \dots, (l_m, u_m)]$ of closed intervals with $l_1 < u_1 < l_2 < u_2 < \dots < l_m < u_m$, standing for the set $|I| = \bigcup_k [l_k, u_k]$. For a set $X \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ write $\operatorname{reg} X = \overline{\operatorname{int} X}$. For a Boolean function $\mathrm{op}$ of two arguments, the pointwise result is $X = \{z : \mathrm{op}(z \in |I|, z \in |J|)\}$.

**Claim.** For dexels $I$ and $J$ and any $\mathrm{op}$ with $\mathrm{op}(\text{false}, \text{false}) = \text{false}$, COMBINE returns a dexel $R$ with $|R| = \operatorname{reg} X$, in $O(|I| + |J|)$ steps. CUT replaces each ray $I$ by a dexel for $\operatorname{reg}(|I| \setminus S_q)$, where $S_q$ is the part of the ray inside the swept tool.

**Step 1 (cells).** Let $P = p_1 < \dots < p_n$ be the distinct endpoints of $I$ and $J$ (line L1). On each open cell $(p_s, p_{s+1})$, membership in $|I|$ is constant: no endpoint of $I$ lies inside the cell, so the cell lies inside one interval of $I$ or outside all of them. The same holds for $J$, so $X$ is constant on each cell. Outside $[p_1, p_n]$ the point is in neither set, and $\mathrm{op}(\text{false}, \text{false}) = \text{false}$, so $X$ has no points there. Hence $X$ equals the union of the open cells where $\mathrm{op}$ is true, plus possibly some points of $P$. A finite set of points has no interior, and adding or removing isolated points does not change the closure of the interior, so $\operatorname{reg} X$ is the union of the closed cells $[p_s, p_{s+1}]$ where $\mathrm{op}$ is true.

**Step 2 (the pointers).** Fix the cell $(p, p')$ of one pass of line L3. We show that line L6 sets $a$ to the membership of the cell in $|I|$. The loop of line L4 moves $i$ past the intervals with $u_i \le p$. Since $p$ only increases from pass to pass, the pointer never needs to go back, and after line L4, $i$ is the first interval with $u_i > p$, or $i > m$. If $i > m$, every interval ends at or before $p$, so the cell is outside $|I|$, and line L6 sets $a$ to false. Otherwise $u_i > p$, and $u_i$ is an endpoint, so $u_i \ge p'$. If $l_i \le p$, the cell lies in $[l_i, u_i]$, and $a$ is true. If $l_i > p$, then $l_i \ge p'$, the cell lies before interval $i$ and after interval $i - 1$, and $a$ is false. The same argument holds for $J$ and $b$ at line L5.

**Step 3 (the output).** Lines L7 to L9 append the closed cells with $\mathrm{op}(a, b)$ true, in increasing order, and line L8 joins a cell to the last output interval when they share the endpoint $p$. So the output covers exactly the union of Step 1. Two output intervals are never touching: a new interval is started only when the last one ends before $p$, that is, at some $p_{s'} < p$, and then there is a cell between them where $\mathrm{op}$ is false. Every output interval has positive length, since $p < p'$. So $R$ is a dexel and $|R| = \operatorname{reg} X$.

**Step 4 (cost).** Line L1 merges two sorted lists of $2|I|$ and $2|J|$ numbers in linear time. The loop of line L3 runs $n - 1 \le 2(|I| + |J|)$ times, and the pointers $i$ and $j$ only move forward, so lines L4 and L5 take $O(|I| + |J|)$ steps in total. Each pass appends or extends at most one interval. Every loop is finite, so COMBINE stops.

**Step 5 (the cut).** The swept tool is $S = \bigcup_{t \in [0,1]} T(c(t))$. Without a shank, $T(c)$ is the closed ball of radius $r$ about $c$, and $S$ is the set of points at distance at most $r$ from the segment $c_0 c_1$, a capsule. It is convex, because the distance to a convex set is a convex function. A convex set meets a line in an interval, so $S_q = [z_{lo}, z_{hi}]$ or empty. Its lower end $z_{lo}$ is the lowest point of the swept ball on the line, which is the closed form proved on the [z-map page](/z-map-model/), Steps 3 to 5 of its proof. Its upper end is the lowest point of the mirror image of $S$ in the plane $z = 0$, negated, and the mirror image is the capsule of the mirrored centers, so the same formula gives it. With a shank, Step 1 of the z-map proof shows that $S_q = [z_{lo}, \infty)$. Line L13 then applies COMBINE with $\mathrm{op}(a, b) = a \wedge \neg b$, which is false at (false, false), so by Steps 1 to 3 the ray becomes a dexel for $\operatorname{reg}(|I| \setminus S_q)$. A ray where $S_q$ has length zero, a tangent line, is left as it is, which is the same set after regularization. Rays outside the box of line L11 are more than $r$ from the path in $x$ or in $y$, so $S_q$ is empty for them. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (exactness).** Steps 1 to 4 use only comparisons of endpoints, so with integer or rational endpoints COMBINE is exact. The ends of $S_q$ involve square roots. The code computes them in floating point, and the tests compare them with an exact rational membership test, one part in $10^9$ on each side.
