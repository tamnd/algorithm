---
title: The discrete cell problem gives a symmetric tensor between the harmonic and arithmetic means, exact for laminates, and the design loop keeps the volume, never loses compliance in the angle step, and stops
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Setting.** All elements are bilinear squares (Q4) of side 1, with the stiffness integrals computed exactly. A cell is an $N \times N$ periodic grid of elements with conductivities $a_e > 0$. Let $W$ be the space of continuous functions that are bilinear on each element and periodic on the cell, and $|Y| = N^2$. For a unit vector $e_j$, the cell problem asks for $\chi_j \in W$ with

$$\sum_e a_e \int_{Y_e} (e_j + \nabla \chi_j) \cdot \nabla v = 0 \quad \text{for all } v \in W, \tag{1}$$

and the code returns $A_{ij} = \frac{1}{|Y|} \sum_e a_e \int_{Y_e} (e_i + \nabla \chi_i) \cdot e_j$. The design loop works on a grid of $n_x \times n_y$ unit elements with tensors $B_e$, the matrix $K(B) = \sum_e K_e(B_e)$ with $u^{\mathsf T} K_e(B) u = \int_{Y_e} \nabla u \cdot B \nabla u$, the nodes with $u = 0$ removed, and the compliance $c = f^{\mathsf T} u$ with $K u = f$.

**Claim.**

1. The cell problem has a solution, unique up to a constant, and the computed $A$ is symmetric with $\xi \cdot A \xi = \min_{\chi \in W} \frac{1}{|Y|} \sum_e a_e \int_{Y_e} |\xi + \nabla \chi|^2$ for every $\xi$.
2. (Wiener bounds.) For every unit vector $\xi$, $h \le \xi \cdot A \xi \le m$, where $m = \frac{1}{|Y|}\sum_e a_e$ is the arithmetic mean and $h = 1 / (\frac{1}{|Y|} \sum_e 1/a_e)$ is the harmonic mean of the element values.
3. (Laminates.) If $a_e$ depends only on the column of the element, then $A_{11} = h$, $A_{22} = m$ and $A_{12} = 0$ exactly.
4. (Refinement.) If every element of a cell is split into $2 \times 2$ elements with the same values, the new tensor is at most the old one in the order of symmetric matrices. In the $2 \times 2$ checkerboard with values $a$ and $b$, $A = \frac{a + b}{2} I$.
5. (The angle step.) For fixed hole sizes, line L6 does not increase the compliance.
6. (The volume.) Every design of the loop has $\sum_e \rho_e \le V^*$, where $\rho = 1 - (1 - \mu)(1 - \gamma)$.
7. (The bar.) For the bar problem (left edge at $u = 0$, total load $F$ on the right edge, length $L = n_x$), every design of the model has $c \ge F^2 L^2 / (V^*(1 - a_{\text{void}}) + n\, a_{\text{void}})$, and a laminate along the bar reaches this bound.
8. The loop stops after at most maxit iterations, and each bisection after 100 steps.

**Proof of 1.** The bilinear form $(w, v) \mapsto \sum_e a_e \int \nabla w \cdot \nabla v$ is symmetric and positive semidefinite on $W$. If it is 0 at $w$, then $\nabla w = 0$ on every element, so $w$ is constant on the connected cell. With the value at node 0 fixed to 0 the matrix is positive definite, so (1) has a unique solution with $\chi_j(0) = 0$, and adding a constant changes neither (1) nor $A$. Take $v = \chi_i$ in (1) for $j$: $\sum_e a_e \int (e_j + \nabla \chi_j) \cdot \nabla \chi_i = 0$. Adding this to the definition gives $A_{ij} = \frac{1}{|Y|} \sum_e a_e \int (e_i + \nabla \chi_i) \cdot (e_j + \nabla \chi_j)$, which is symmetric. For $\xi = \sum_j \xi_j e_j$, the function $\chi_\xi = \sum_j \xi_j \chi_j$ solves (1) for $\xi$, so $\xi \cdot A \xi = \frac{1}{|Y|}\sum_e a_e \int |\xi + \nabla \chi_\xi|^2$. The quadratic functional $J(\chi) = \sum_e a_e \int |\xi + \nabla \chi|^2$ on $W$ has (1) as its condition of stationarity and is convex, so $\chi_\xi$ is a minimizer.

**Proof of 2.** Upper bound: $\chi = 0$ in the minimum of claim 1 gives $\xi \cdot A \xi \le \frac{1}{|Y|} \sum_e a_e |\xi|^2 = m$. Lower bound: for every $\chi \in W$ the integral of $\nabla \chi$ over the cell is 0, because $\chi$ is periodic and continuous. So $\frac{1}{|Y|} \int \xi \cdot (\xi + \nabla \chi) = 1$, and by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

$$1 = \Big( \frac{1}{|Y|} \int a^{-1/2} \cdot a^{1/2}\, \xi \cdot (\xi + \nabla \chi) \Big)^2 \le \frac{1}{|Y|} \int \frac{1}{a} \cdot \frac{1}{|Y|} \int a |\xi + \nabla \chi|^2.$$

So $\frac{1}{|Y|} \int a |\xi + \nabla\chi|^2 \ge h$ for every $\chi$, and the minimum is at least $h$.

**Proof of 3.** Let $a_c$ be the value of column $c$ and let $\chi_1$ be the function of $x$ alone that is linear on each column with slope $h / a_c - 1$. These slopes sum to $N h \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_c 1/a_c - N = 0$, so $\chi_1$ is periodic, and it is in $W$. Then $a (e_1 + \nabla \chi_1) = h\, e_1$ everywhere, so $\frac{1}{|Y|}\int a |e_1 + \nabla \chi_1|^2 = \frac{1}{|Y|}\int h^2 / a = h$. This equals the lower bound of claim 2, so $\chi_1$ is a minimizer and $A_{11} = h$. For $e_2$, the test (1) with $\chi_2 = 0$ reads $\sum_c a_c \int_{\text{column } c} \partial v / \partial y = 0$, which holds because $v$ is periodic in $y$ and each column is a full period in $y$. So $\chi_2 = 0$ (up to a constant), $A_{22} = m$, and $A_{12} = \frac{1}{|Y|}\int a (e_1 + \nabla \chi_1) \cdot e_2 = 0$.

**Proof of 4.** The bilinear functions on the coarse elements are bilinear on each of the fine ones, so the coarse space is a subspace of the fine space and the conductivity is the same function. The minimum of claim 1 over a larger space is not larger, which is the claim for every $\xi$. For the $2 \times 2$ checkerboard, every one of the 4 nodes is a corner of all 4 elements, the right corner of the two elements in one column and the left corner of the two in the other. Each column holds one element of value $a$ and one of value $b$. The load of (1) at a node is $-\sum_e a_e \int \partial N / \partial x$, and $\int_{Y_e} \partial N / \partial x = \pm 1/2$ with the sign $+$ for a right corner. So the load is $-\frac{1}{2}(a + b) + \frac{1}{2}(a + b) = 0$, and the same holds in $y$. Then $\chi = 0$ and $A = m I = \frac{a + b}{2} I$.

**Proof of 5.** The element integrals of Q4 with a constant tensor are exact under the $2 \times 2$ Gauss rule with weights $1/4$, so $K(B) = G^{\mathsf T} D(B) G$, where $G$ maps nodal values to the gradients at all Gauss points and $D(B)$ is block diagonal with the blocks $B_e / 4$. For any fluxes $q$ at the Gauss points with $G^{\mathsf T} q = f$, the vector $q_0 = D G u$ with $K u = f$ satisfies $(q - q_0)^{\mathsf T} G u = 0$, so

$$q^{\mathsf T} D^{-1} q = q_0^{\mathsf T} D^{-1} q_0 + (q - q_0)^{\mathsf T} D^{-1} (q - q_0) \ge q_0^{\mathsf T} D^{-1} q_0 = u^{\mathsf T} K u = c.$$

So $c(B) = \min \{ q^{\mathsf T} D(B)^{-1} q : G^{\mathsf T} q = f \}$. Fix the old fluxes $q_0$. The new angles change only $D$, and the term of element $e$ is $\operatorname{tr}(B_e^{-1} S_e)$, with $S_e = \sum_g \frac14 q_g q_g^{\mathsf T}$ over its Gauss points (the function complementary in the code). With $B_e = R_\theta \operatorname{diag}(a_{11}, a_{22}) R_\theta^{\mathsf T}$ and $a_{11} \ge a_{22}$, this is $\frac{1}{a_{11}} s(\theta) + \frac{1}{a_{22}} (\operatorname{tr} S_e - s(\theta))$ with $s(\theta) = d_\theta^{\mathsf T} S_e d_\theta$ and $d_\theta = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$. Since $1/a_{11} \le 1/a_{22}$, it is least when $s(\theta)$ is largest, that is, when $d_\theta$ is a main eigenvector of $S_e$, at the angle $\frac12 \operatorname{atan2}(2 s_{12}, s_{11} - s_{22})$ of line L6. If $a_{11} < a_{22}$ the same holds with the angle turned by $\pi/2$. So the new $c$ is at most $\sum_e \operatorname{tr}(B_e^{\text{new} -1} S_e) \le \sum_e \operatorname{tr}(B_e^{\text{old} -1} S_e) = c^{\text{old}}$.

**Proof of 6.** The start has $\mu = \gamma = s = 1 - \sqrt{1 - v}$, so $\rho = 2s - s^2 = v$ and the volume is $V^*$. Assume the old design has volume at most $V^*$. The bisection keeps $hi$ at a value whose trial design has volume at most $V^*$, and returns the trial at $hi$. This holds at the start, $\lambda = 10^{40}$, as long as $|\partial c / \partial \mu_e| \le 10^{40}\, \partial \rho / \partial \mu_e$ wherever $\partial \rho / \partial \mu_e > 0$ (and the same for $\gamma$), which holds on every example of the page: there each factor with a positive $\partial \rho$ is at most 1, so each such width becomes at most its old value. A width with $\partial\rho = 0$ may grow, but $\partial \rho / \partial \mu = 1 - \gamma = 0$ means $\gamma = 1$ and $\rho = 1$ before, so $\rho$ does not grow there. In every case $\rho_e^{\text{new}} \le \rho_e^{\text{old}}$, so the trial at $10^{40}$ is feasible. This is in exact arithmetic; the code adds no tolerance, and the tests allow $10^{-9}$ for rounding.

**Proof of 7.** At the grid points of the table, the cell is the hole cell on an $N \times N$ grid with $N$ a multiple of the grid, so its arithmetic mean is exactly $\bar\rho = \rho + (1 - \rho) a_{\text{void}}$, and by claim 2 both $a_{11}$ and $a_{22}$ are at most $\bar\rho$. The map $(\mu, \gamma) \mapsto \bar \rho$ is bilinear, so bilinear interpolation reproduces it, and with nonnegative weights the interpolated $a_{11}, a_{22}$ are at most $\bar\rho$. A rotation does not change the eigenvalues, so $B_e \preceq \bar \rho_e I$, and $K(B) \preceq K(\bar\rho I)$. For a positive definite $K$, $c = f^{\mathsf T} K^{-1} f = \max_v (2 f^{\mathsf T} v - v^{\mathsf T} K v)$, so a smaller $K$ gives a larger $c$, and $c(B) \ge 2 f^{\mathsf T} v - v^{\mathsf T} K(\bar\rho I) v$ for every $v$ that is 0 on the left edge. Take $v = t x$. Then $f^{\mathsf T} v = t F L$, because all the load is on the right edge at $x = L$, and $v^{\mathsf T} K(\bar\rho I) v = t^2 \sum_e \bar \rho_e$. The best $t$ gives $c \ge F^2 L^2 / \sum_e \bar\rho_e$, and $\sum_e \bar\rho_e = (1 - a_{\text{void}}) \sum_e \rho_e + n a_{\text{void}} \le V^*(1 - a_{\text{void}}) + n a_{\text{void}}$. For the laminate $\mu = 0$, $\gamma = v$, $\theta = 0$ in every element (a grid point when $v$ is), the hole spans the cell in $x$, the cell is layered in $y$, and by claim 3 $a_{11} = \bar\rho$. Then $u = F x / (n_y \bar\rho)$ satisfies $K u = f$: a linear function with a constant tensor gives zero net flux at the interior and top and bottom nodes, and $F / n_y$ per unit length of the right edge, which is the consistent load. So $c = F^2 L / (n_y \bar\rho) = F^2 L^2 / (n \bar\rho)$, the bound.

**Proof of 8.** Line L3 runs at most maxit times, and the bisection has a fixed count of 100 halvings.

**What is not proved.** The loop as a whole is not proved to decrease the compliance or to converge: the size step of line L8 is a fixed point rule, not a descent step. On the heat sink example the compliance fell at every one of the measured iterations, which is a measurement. The rectangular hole is a choice of Bendsøe and Kikuchi, not an optimal microstructure: the optimal composites for compliance are laminates (rank-1 for the scalar problem with one load, rank-2 or rank-3 for elasticity), so the relaxed problem of this page is a restriction of the full relaxation, and its optimum is not proved to be the optimum of the relaxation. Elasticity is a vector problem with three cell load cases in 2D and a fourth order tensor; the claims above are for the scalar problem only.
