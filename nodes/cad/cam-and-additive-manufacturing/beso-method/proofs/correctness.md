---
title: BESO keeps feasible designs and stops, its sensitivity is the scaled derivative of the compliance, and on parallel bars it is optimal for equal areas and a greedy knapsack rule otherwise
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Setting.** A network of $n$ axial bars with Young's modulus $E = 1$. Bar $e$ has the length $L_e$, the area $A_e$, the volume $v_e = A_e L_e$ and the design variable $x_e \in \{x_{\min}, 1\}$ with $0 < x_{\min} < 1$. Its stiffness matrix is $x_e^p k_e$ with $k_e = (A_e/L_e)\begin{pmatrix}1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ and $p \ge 1$. The supports make $K(x) = \sum_e x_e^p K_e$ positive definite on the free nodes whenever every bar has $x_e > 0$ and every node is joined to a support by a path of bars. The compliance is $c(x) = f^{\mathsf T} u$ with $K(x) u = f$. Let $0 < V^* \le V_0 = \sum_e v_e$ and $0 < \text{ER} < 1$.

**Claim.**

1. Every design after the first has solid volume at most the current target $V_k$, and the targets reach $V^*$ after at most $\lceil \ln(V^*/V_0) / \ln(1 - \text{ER}) \rceil$ iterations and then stay there. The method stops after at most maxit iterations.
2. For every design, $\alpha_e v_e = -\frac{1}{2p} \partial c / \partial x_e$, so ranking by $\alpha$ is ranking by the derivative of the compliance per unit volume.
3. For bars in parallel between a support and a rigid plate with load $F$, the displacement is $\delta = F / \sum_e x_e^p A_e / L_e$, the compliance is $F \delta$, and $\alpha_e = x_e^{p-1} \delta^2 / (2 L_e^2)$.
4. If in addition all areas are equal and the lengths are distinct, the final design of BESO is the longest prefix of the bars sorted by increasing length whose volume is at most $V^*$, and it has the least compliance among all 0/1 designs with volume at most $V^*$.
5. With unequal areas, the problem is a 0/1 knapsack problem, BESO ranks the solid bars by value per weight, and its result can be worse than the optimum by any factor.

**Proof of 1.** Line L7 sets $V_{k+1} = \max(V^*, V_k(1 - \text{ER}))$, so $V_k \ge V^*$ for all $k$, the sequence does not increase, and $V_k = \max(V^*, V_0 (1 - \text{ER})^k)$. The second value is at most $V^*$ once $k \ge \ln(V^*/V_0)/\ln(1 - \text{ER})$. Line L8 adds elements in the ranked order only while the volume stays at most $V_{k+1}$. Line L9, when it applies, first takes some voids, at most $\lfloor \text{AR}_{\max} n \rfloor$ of them, all of which were in the set of line L8, and then solid elements while the volume stays at most $V_{k+1}$. In both cases the solid volume is at most $V_{k+1}$. The loop of line L2 runs at most maxit times.

**Proof of 2.** Differentiate $K u = f$ with respect to $x_e$: $K\, \partial u = -(\partial K) u$, so $\partial c / \partial x_e = f^{\mathsf T} \partial u = -u^{\mathsf T} K\, K^{-1} (\partial K) u = -u^{\mathsf T} (\partial K / \partial x_e) u = -p\, x_e^{p-1}\, u_e^{\mathsf T} k_e u_e$, using $K^{\mathsf T} = K$ and $f = K u$. Line L5 computes $\alpha_e = \frac{1}{2} x_e^{p-1} u_e^{\mathsf T} k_e u_e / v_e$, which is $-\frac{1}{2p} \partial c / \partial x_e$ divided by $v_e$. For a solid bar it is the strain energy per unit volume. For a void bar it is $x_{\min}^{p-1}$ times the energy density that the bar would have if it were solid with the same displacements.

**Proof of 3.** All bars join node 0 (fixed) and node 1 (the plate), so $K$ is the $1 \times 1$ matrix $\sum_e x_e^p A_e/L_e$, and $\delta = F / \sum_e x_e^p A_e / L_e$. Each bar has $u_e^{\mathsf T} k_e u_e = (A_e / L_e) \delta^2$, so $\alpha_e = \frac12 x_e^{p-1} (A_e/L_e) \delta^2 / (A_e L_e) = x_e^{p-1} \delta^2 / (2 L_e^2)$.

**Proof of 4.** Number the bars by increasing length, $L_1 < \dots < L_n$, all with area $A$. Write $P_j = \{1, \dots, j\}$.

*BESO keeps a prefix.* By induction on the iteration, the solid set is some $P_j$, and every void bar $r > j$ was removed at an iteration where the target was below $A(L_1 + \dots + L_r)$. At the start the solid set is $P_n$. Assume the solid set is $P_j$. By claim 3, a bar that is solid in this iteration and in the previous one has the averaged $\alpha_e = (\delta_k^2 + \delta_{k-1}^2)/(4 L_e^2)$, and every void bar has the factor $x_{\min}^{p-1} < 1$ on at least one of the two terms. So every solid bar has a larger $\alpha$ than every void bar (all void bars are longer than all solid bars), and among the solid bars $\alpha$ decreases with the length. Line L8 takes the solid bars in increasing length while they fit in $V_{k+1}$. If it stops inside $P_j$, the new solid set is a prefix $P_{j'}$ with $j' \le j$. If all of $P_j$ fits, the next element is a void bar $r > j$. Its volume would bring the total to at least $A(L_1 + \dots + L_{j+1})$, because $L_r \ge L_{j+1}$, and when bar $j + 1$ was removed the target was below this value. The targets do not increase, so $r$ does not fit, line L8 stops, and no void bar is added. So line L9 does nothing, and the solid set is $P_j$ again.

*The final prefix is the longest one that fits.* Once $V_k = V^*$, line L8 keeps the longest prefix of $P_j$ with volume at most $V^*$. Let $j^*$ be the largest $j$ with $A(L_1 + \dots + L_j) \le V^*$. While the target is above $V^*$, the solid set has volume at most the target and all prefixes up to $j^*$ fit in every target, so the solid set always contains $P_{j^*}$. At target $V^*$ the prefix $P_{j^* + 1}$ does not fit, so the solid set is exactly $P_{j^*}$.

*The prefix is optimal.* The compliance is $F^2 / (\sum_e x_e^p A/L_e)$, so the best 0/1 design maximizes $\sum_{e \in S} 1/L_e$ plus a term from the void bars that is the same for all sets of the same size only up to the factor $x_{\min}^p$. Ignore that term first. Let $S$ be any set with $A\sum_{e \in S} L_e \le V^*$ and $|S| = s$. The $s$ shortest bars have total length at most that of $S$, so $P_s$ is feasible and $s \le j^*$. The $s$ largest values of $1/L_e$ are those of $P_s$, so $\sum_{e \in S} 1/L_e \le \sum_{e \in P_s} 1/L_e \le \sum_{e \in P_{j^*}} 1/L_e$. Now include the voids: the stiffness of a design is $\sum_{e \in S} A/L_e + x_{\min}^p \sum_{e \notin S} A/L_e = (1 - x_{\min}^p) \sum_{e \in S} A/L_e + x_{\min}^p \sum_e A/L_e$. The last term does not depend on $S$, and $1 - x_{\min}^p > 0$, so the same set maximizes it. So $P_{j^*}$ has the least compliance.

**Proof of 5.** By the last formula, minimizing the compliance with volume at most $V^*$ is maximizing $\sum_{e \in S} A_e / L_e$ subject to $\sum_{e \in S} A_e L_e \le V^*$: a 0/1 knapsack problem with values $A_e/L_e$ and weights $A_e L_e$. By claim 3 the solid bars are ranked by $1/L_e^2$, which is value per weight, and line L8 takes a prefix of this order. Two bars with $(L, A) = (1, 1)$ and $(1.1, 10)$ and $V^* = 11$ give the counterexample. The ranking puts the first bar first (its $1/L^2$ is larger), the first prefix of volume at most $11.76$ is the first bar alone (adding the second gives 12), and the second bar never returns, as in the proof of claim 4. The optimum is the second bar alone, with stiffness $100/11$ against 1, so BESO is $100/11 \approx 9.09$ times softer, up to the $x_{\min}^p$ terms. Scaling the second bar's area and the target makes the factor as large as wanted.

**What is not proved.** For general structures the history averaging, the filter and the admission limit have no proof of convergence: the 0/1 iterates can cycle, and nothing bounds the gap to the optimum. The stop rule of line L4 measures the change of the compliance over two windows of iterations. It is a test, not a proof that a stationary design was reached.
