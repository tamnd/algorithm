A contour-parallel toolpath clears a [pocket](/pocket-machining/) with a milling tool by moving the tool center along nested [offset curves](/offset-curve/) of the pocket boundary, one loop at a time, from the wall inward. The first loop lies at the tool radius $r$ from the boundary, and each next loop lies one step-over $s$ further in. This is a manufacturing method of mechanical CAD: the words placement and routing here mean the placement of a cutter and the route it takes, not the placement and routing of an electronic circuit. The page proves that with $s \le r$ the loops cut all of the material that any tool of radius $r$ can reach without leaving the pocket. The code builds the loops of a convex pocket with exact rational offsets.

## In plain words

Think of a lawn with a fence around it. You mow one lap along the fence, then a lap a little further in, and so on to the middle. Each lap has the shape of the fence, only smaller.

The mower blade has a fixed width. If two laps are too far apart, a strip of grass stays between them. If each new lap is at most half a blade width inside the last one, then every blade of grass that the mower can reach is cut. Some grass in sharp corners can never be reached, because the round blade does not fit into the corner. No path can fix that.

The tool always turns the same way around the loops, so the cutting conditions stay steady. The hard part in a real pocket is to compute the smaller copies of the boundary when the shape is not convex.

## The problem

The pocket is a closed bounded region $P$ of the plane. The tool is a disk $D_r$ of radius $r$, and the tool center follows a path $\Gamma$. The tool removes the material in $\Gamma \oplus D_r$, the [Minkowski sum](/minkowski-sum/) of the path and the disk. The tool must stay in the pocket, so every disk around a point of $\Gamma$ lies in $P$.

The depth of a point $p$ is its distance to the outside of the pocket, $\delta(p) = \operatorname{dist}(p, \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus P)$. The offset at distance $d$ is the set $P_d = \{p \in P : \delta(p) \ge d\}$, and its boundary $\partial P_d$ is the offset curve. A disk of radius $r$ around $c$ lies in $P$ if and only if $\delta(c) \ge r$, so the tool centers that keep the tool in the pocket form exactly $P_r$. Hence no path can cut more than the [morphological opening](/morphological-opening/) $O_r(P) = P_r \oplus D_r$. The parts of $P$ outside $O_r(P)$ are the corners that a round tool cannot reach.

For a [convex polygon](/convex-polygon/) $P = \{x : n_i \cdot x \le c_i,\ i = 1, \dots, n\}$ with unit outward normals $n_i$, the depth of a point $p \in P$ is $\delta(p) = \min_i (c_i - n_i \cdot p)$, so the offset is the [intersection of half-planes](/half-plane-intersection/) $P_d = \{x : n_i \cdot x \le c_i - d\}$. If every edge has a rational length, every $n_i$ is rational and every offset has rational vertices.

## The algorithm

```pseudo id=main
CONTOUR-PARALLEL(P, r, s)
L1  d ← r
L2  loop
L3      Q ← OFFSET(P, d)                  ▷ P_d: all points at depth at least d
L4      if Q is empty then exit the loop
L5      append the loop (d, ∂Q)
L6      d ← d + s
L7  link the loops in order and return the path
```

For a convex pocket, OFFSET clips $P$ by the $n$ half-planes moved inward by $d$, with the [Sutherland-Hodgman](/polygon-clipping/) rule. Each clip computes the sign of $c_i - d - n_i \cdot v$ at each vertex $v$ with exact rationals, and a vertex with value zero is kept and not duplicated. A loop can be a polygon, a segment or a single point. A segment loop is cut out and back. A loop of zero area appears when $P_d$ has no interior, for example at the center of a disk or along the middle line of a long rectangle.

For a pocket that is not convex, OFFSET is the hard part. Moving each edge inward and joining the moved edges gives a raw offset with local loops at reflex corners and global self-intersections where two far parts of the boundary meet. These must be trimmed, and the trimmed offset can split into several closed curves. [Curve offsetting](/curve-offsetting/) covers the trimming, and [Voronoi pocket machining](/voronoi-pocket-machining/) reads every offset directly from the [medial axis](/medial-axis/). At a reflex vertex the true offset is a circular arc, not a corner.

Line L7 joins the loops. The tool moves from a point of one loop to a point of the next loop inward, along a short segment inside the offset region. When the offsets split into several curves, the loops form a tree, and the tool clears one branch to its end before it moves to the next branch. A move between branches that crosses material that is already cut is a rapid move or a retraction.

## Example

Take the right triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(12, 0)$ and $(0, 9)$, a tool of radius $r = 1$ and a step-over $s = 1$. The edges have lengths 12, 15 and 9, and the inward unit normals are $(0, 1)$, $(-\tfrac35, -\tfrac45)$ and $(1, 0)$. The inradius is 3, with the incenter at $(3, 3)$, and each offset is the triangle scaled toward the incenter by the factor $(3 - d)/3$.

| step | $d$ | loop $\partial P_d$ | length |
|---|---|---|---|
| L3 to L5 | 1 | $(1, 1)$, $(9, 1)$, $(1, 7)$ | 24 |
| L3 to L5 | 2 | $(2, 2)$, $(6, 2)$, $(2, 5)$ | 12 |
| L3 to L5 | 3 | the point $(3, 3)$ | 0 |
| L3, L4 | 4 | empty, stop | |

The links $(1, 1) \to (2, 2) \to (3, 3)$ add $2\sqrt2$, so the path has length $36 + 2\sqrt2 \approx 38.83$. The triangle has area 54. The opening has area $\operatorname{area}(P_1) + r \cdot \operatorname{perimeter}(P_1) + \pi r^2 = 24 + 24 + \pi \approx 51.14$, so $6 - \pi \approx 2.86$ of material stays in the three corners. No path with this tool can remove it.

With $s = 2$ instead, the loops are at $d = 1$ and $d = 3$. The point $(2.1, 2.1)$ is in the opening, but its distance to the first loop is $1.1$ and its distance to the point $(3, 3)$ is $0.9\sqrt2 \approx 1.27$. Both are more than $r$, so a small island of material stays near the right-angle corner.

## Correctness

**Theorem.** Let $P$ be a compact region, $r > 0$ and $0 < s \le r$, and let $P_r$ be nonempty. CONTOUR-PARALLEL stops after a finite number of loops. Each disk of radius $r$ around a point of the path lies in $P$, and the path cuts exactly the opening $O_r(P)$.

The proof rests on one fact about the depth: for $\delta(p) \ge d$, the distance from $p$ to the offset curve $\partial P_d$ is exactly $\delta(p) - d$. Walk from $p$ straight toward the nearest point outside $P$. The depth drops at rate exactly 1 along this walk, so after $\delta(p) - d$ it equals $d$, and that point is on $\partial P_d$. A point $p$ deep in the pocket lies between the loop at $d_k$ and the loop at $d_k + s$, so its distance to the loop at $d_k$ is less than $s \le r$. A point near the wall is within $r$ of a tool center $c \in P_r$, and the segment from $c$ to $p$ crosses $\partial P_r$, the first loop, at a point within $r$ of $p$. The full proof is in the proof file.

The condition $s \le r$ is sufficient for every pocket. It is not necessary: the uncut islands come from the corners of the offsets, and a pocket with only gentle corners is covered with a larger step.

## Complexity

Let the convex pocket have $n$ edges, and let $\delta_{\max}$ be its inradius. The loop of lines L2 to L6 runs $K + 1$ times, with $K = \lfloor (\delta_{\max} - r)/s \rfloor + 1$ loops when $\delta_{\max} \ge r$. Each OFFSET clips by $n$ half-planes, and each clip takes time linear in the current polygon, which has at most $n$ vertices. So the time is $O(K n^2)$ operations on rationals, and the space is $O(n)$ for the current offset, plus the output.

The bit size of the rationals grows with each clip in this simple code. An offset of a convex polygon can be computed in $O(n)$ time from the [straight skeleton](/straight-skeleton/), which for a convex polygon coincides with the medial axis and is built once in $O(n \log n)$ time. For a general polygon with $n$ edges, Held (1991) builds the Voronoi diagram once and reads each offset from it in time linear in the size of the diagram.

## Properties

- **Steady cutting direction.** The tool turns the same way around every loop, so the whole path is climb milling or the whole path is conventional milling.
- **Maximal cut for $s \le r$.** By the theorem, no other path with the same tool cuts more.
- **Overlap.** For $s < 2r$, neighbouring loops cut overlapping strips. With $s = r$, a point between two loops and away from the corners is within $r$ of both, so it is cut twice.
- **Sharp corners on the loops.** The inward offsets of a convex polygon keep sharp corners, so at each corner the tool turns in place and the engagement with the material jumps.
- **Exact for convex pockets.** With rational edge normals, the loops of the code are exact, and every coverage test is a sign test on rationals.

## Variants and improvements

- **Voronoi-based offsets.** [Voronoi pocket machining](/voronoi-pocket-machining/) computes all offsets of a pocket with arcs and islands from one Voronoi diagram, which handles splits and reflex corners.
- **Spiral and Fermat spiral linking.** Joining the loops into one spiral removes the link moves. The [connected Fermat spiral](/fermat-spiral-toolpath/) of additive manufacturing is the same idea for print paths.
- **Outward order.** Start at the deepest point and cut outward. This avoids a plunge at the wall, but the first loops cut in full slot.
- **Corner smoothing.** High-speed machining rounds the corners of the loops, which lowers the jump in engagement and the change of feed direction.
- **Raster offsets.** On a pixel grid, the offsets are the level sets of a [distance transform](/distance-transform/) of the pocket.
- **Islands.** A pocket with islands has a boundary with several components. The offsets of the outer wall and the offsets of the islands meet and merge, so the loops form a tree.

## When to use it

Use a contour-parallel path when the cutting direction must stay the same, for good finish on the walls, and when the pocket has thin features that a zigzag would cross many times. Use a [direction-parallel path](/direction-parallel-toolpath/) when the pocket is wide and open and the offsets of its boundary would be hard to compute, or when the machine is faster on straight moves.

## Pitfalls

- **Step-over above the radius.** Take a corner with interior angle $\alpha$ on two neighbouring loops of a convex pocket, with long sides. A point on the bisector at distance $x$ outside the inner corner has distance $x$ to the inner loop and distance $s - x\sin(\alpha/2)$ to the outer loop. It stays uncut when $r < x < (s - r)/\sin(\alpha/2)$, and such points exist when $s > r(1 + \sin(\alpha/2))$, that is $s > 1.707r$ for a right angle. A step-over between $r$ and $2r$ needs a check for these islands.
- **Mitered offsets of nonconvex shapes.** Moving each edge of a nonconvex polygon inward and joining the moved lines is not the Euclidean offset. At a reflex vertex the true offset is an arc, and the joined lines leave self-intersections.
- **Splits.** An offset of a nonconvex pocket can split into two or more curves. A program that expects one loop per depth then loses material.
- **Links that leave the offset.** A link between two loops must stay in $P_r$, or the tool cuts into the wall. In a convex pocket every straight link between loops is safe, because $P_r$ is convex. In a nonconvex pocket it is not.
- **Floating-point offsets.** A tiny edge of a floating-point offset can flip its direction and create a spurious loop. Exact rationals avoid this on convex pockets. On general pockets the sign tests of the trimming must be [exact](/exact-geometric-computation/).

## History

Persson (1978) machined pockets of any shape with offset curves of the boundary and built them from the bisectors between boundary elements. Held (1991) gave a full computational geometry of pocket machining, with offsets read from the Voronoi diagram of segments and arcs, and Held, Lukács and Andor (1994) described a complete contour-parallel pocketing system with checks for uncut regions.
