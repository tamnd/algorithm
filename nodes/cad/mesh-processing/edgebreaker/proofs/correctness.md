---
title: Edgebreaker decoding inverts encoding on every triangulated sphere, and the code takes exactly 2t - 1 bits
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Setting.** $M$ is a list of $t$ triangles, each a triple of three different vertex ids, such that every directed edge $a \to b$ is in exactly one triangle and $b \to a$ in exactly one other, the triangles are connected through shared edges, and $V - E + F = 2$. An edge is a pair of vertices, so each edge has exactly two triangles. ENCODE and DECODE are lines L1 to L15 of the page. For a triangle $t = (b, a, x)$ reached across the gate $a \to b$, the *left* triangle is the other triangle of the edge $\{a, x\}$ and the *right* triangle is the other triangle of the edge $\{x, b\}$. A vertex is *marked* once line L1 or L2 marks it; every marked vertex is a vertex of a visited triangle.

**Claim.**

1. $M$ is a triangulated sphere: the triangles at each vertex form one cycle around it, and $M$ is a closed orientable surface of genus 0.
2. ENCODE terminates, visits every triangle exactly once, and before each step the invariant below holds.
3. Let $\varphi$ number the vertices $0, 1, 2, \dots$ in the order in which the encoder marks them. Then DECODE(ENCODE($M$)) is the list of the triangles of $M$ in the order of their visit, with each vertex $v$ replaced by $\varphi(v)$.
4. The symbol string has exactly $t - 1$ symbols, of which $V - 3$ are C, and $n_E = n_S + 1$. The code C = 0, S = 100, R = 101, L = 110, E = 111 has exactly $2t - 1$ bits.

**Facts cited.** (F1) Every closed connected orientable surface has $\chi = 2 - 2g$ for its genus $g \ge 0$, and $g = 0$ means a sphere (the classification of surfaces). (F2) The closure of the complement of an open disk bounded by a polygonal simple closed curve on the sphere is a closed disk (the Schoenflies theorem). (F3) If $D$ is a closed disk made of triangles and $\gamma$ is a path of edges from a point of $\partial D$ to another point of $\partial D$ whose edges and inner vertices are not on $\partial D$, then $\gamma$ cuts $D$ into two closed disks; the boundary of each is $\gamma$ followed by one of the two arcs of $\partial D$ between the ends of $\gamma$, and every triangle of $D$ is in exactly one of them (the theta-curve theorem, a form of the Jordan curve theorem). A closed disk made of triangles is connected through edges, and a disk whose boundary is the boundary of one of its triangles is that triangle.

**Proof of 1.** At a vertex $v$, join two triangles of $v$ when they share an edge at $v$. Each edge $vx$ has exactly two triangles, so this graph has degree 2 everywhere and is a union of $k_v \ge 1$ cycles, the *fans* of $v$. Replace each vertex $v$ by $k_v$ copies, one for each fan. The result $\hat M$ is a closed surface: every vertex now has one fan, so it has a disk around it. $\hat M$ is connected through edges as $M$ is, and it is oriented by the same triangles. It has the same edges and triangles and $\sum_v (k_v - 1)$ more vertices, so $\chi(\hat M) = 2 + \sum_v (k_v - 1)$. By (F1) $\chi(\hat M) \le 2$, so every $k_v = 1$, $\hat M = M$ and $g = 0$. $\square$

**The invariant.** The code does not store loops; they are defined here for the proof. A *piece* is a set $D$ of unvisited triangles with a *loop* $P$, a cyclic sequence of vertices, such that:

- (I1) the union of the triangles of $D$ is a closed disk whose boundary is the closed path $P$, and $P$ passes each vertex at most once;
- (I2) an edge of a triangle of $D$ is an edge of $P$ exactly when its other triangle is not in $D$, and then that other triangle is visited and contains the edge in the direction of $P$;
- (I3) the gate of the piece is an edge $a \to b$ of $P$, and the triangle across it, which contains $b \to a$, is in $D$.

Before each step, the unvisited triangles are split into the pieces of the current gate and of the gates on the stack, one piece for each, with no triangle in two pieces. (For the code, "the stack" holds the triangle across each stored gate; the current gate is the one across which the next triangle is reached.)

**Lemma A.** In a piece $D$ with loop $P$: a vertex on $P$ is marked, and a vertex of a triangle of $D$ that is not on $P$ is not marked.

*Proof.* A vertex on $P$ is on an edge of $P$, which is in a visited triangle by (I2); the vertices of a visited triangle are marked (line L1 marks those of $f_0$, and a later triangle is visited across a gate whose two ends are on a loop, so marked, while its tip is marked at line L2 or was marked before). Now let $y$ be a vertex of a triangle of $D$ that is not on $P$. No edge at $y$ is an edge of $P$, so by (I2) every edge at $y$ that has one triangle in $D$ has both in $D$. Going around the single fan of $y$ (claim 1), all triangles of $y$ are in $D$, so none is visited and $y$ is not marked. $\square$

**Lemma B.** For the triangle $t = (b, a, x)$ across the gate $a \to b$ of a piece $D$ with loop $P$: the left triangle is visited exactly when $x$ is the vertex just before $a$ on $P$, and the right triangle is visited exactly when $x$ is the vertex just after $b$.

*Proof.* If the left triangle is visited, it is not in $D$, so by (I2) the edge $\{a, x\}$ is an edge of $P$ in the direction it has in the left triangle. That triangle has $x \to a$, because $t$ has $a \to x$. So $x \to a$ is an edge of $P$ and $x$ comes just before $a$. Conversely, if $x \to a$ is an edge of $P$, then by (I2) the other triangle of $\{a, x\}$, which is the left triangle, is visited. The right side is the same with $x \to b$ replaced by $b \to x$. $\square$

**Proof of 2.** *Start.* By claim 1 and (F2), the triangles other than $f_0 = (a, b, c)$ form a closed disk with boundary $a \to b \to c \to a$, the directions of $f_0$. With the gate $a \to b$ this is one piece: (I1) holds, the other triangle of each edge of $f_0$ is not $f_0$ and so is in $D$, and (I3) holds because the triangle across $a \to b$ contains $b \to a$.

*Step.* Let $D$ be the current piece with loop $P$, gate $a \to b$ and tip triangle $t = (b, a, x) \in D$ (I3), so $t$ is not visited and the check of the code never fires. The encoder marks $t$. Write $P = a \to b \to p_1 \to \dots \to p_m \to a$. There are five cases, and they are the five branches of lines L2 to L6.

- *x not marked (C).* By Lemma A, $x$ is not on $P$, so the edges $\{a, x\}$ and $\{x, b\}$ are not on $P$ and the path $a, x, b$ satisfies (F3). It cuts $D$ into $t$ (the side bounded by $a \to b \to x \to a$) and $D' = D \setminus \{t\}$ with loop $a \to x \to b \to p_1 \to \dots \to a$. The other triangle of $\{x, b\}$ is in $D$ by (I2), so in $D'$, and the new gate $x \to b$ satisfies (I3). (I2) holds on the two new loop edges, because $t$ is visited and has $a \to x$, $x \to b$.
- *x marked, left and right visited (E).* By Lemma B, $x$ is just before $a$ and just after $b$, so $P = a \to b \to x \to a$ and every edge of $t$ is on $P$. $D$ is connected through edges, and by (I2) no triangle of $D$ shares an edge with $t$, so $D = \{t\}$, and the piece is finished.
- *x marked, only the left visited (L).* By Lemma B, $x = p_m$ and $x$ is not just after $b$, so $\{x, b\}$ is not on $P$. The single edge $x, b$ satisfies (F3) and cuts $D$ into $t$ and $D'$ with loop $x \to b \to p_1 \to \dots \to p_m$; the vertex $a$ leaves the loop. The gate $x \to b$ satisfies (I3) as in case C.
- *x marked, only the right visited (R).* Symmetric: $x = p_1$, the edge $a, x$ cuts off $t$, $b$ leaves the loop, and the new gate is $a \to x$.
- *x marked, neither visited (S).* By Lemma A, $x$ is on $P$, so $x = p_r$ for some $1 \le r \le m$, and by Lemma B, $x \ne p_1$ and $x \ne p_m$. Neither $\{a, x\}$ nor $\{x, b\}$ is on $P$, by (I2). By (F3) the edge $a, x$ cuts $D$ into $D_L$ with loop $a \to x \to p_{r+1} \to \dots \to p_m \to a$ and a disk with loop $a \to b \to p_1 \to \dots \to x \to a$, and the edge $x, b$ cuts that disk into $t$ and $D_R$ with loop $x \to b \to p_1 \to \dots \to p_{r-1} \to x$. Each loop passes each vertex once, since $P$ does. The other triangles of $\{a, x\}$ and $\{x, b\}$ are in $D_L$ and $D_R$, so the gates $a \to x$ (pushed) and $x \to b$ (current) satisfy (I3).

In all cases the other pieces are not changed, so the invariant holds again. Each step visits one triangle that was not visited, so ENCODE stops after at most $t - 1$ steps. It stops only after an E with an empty stack, when there are no pieces left; then every triangle is visited, and the check "not connected" does not fire. $\square$

**Proof of 3.** *The lengths of line L7.* Consider the S at position $i$, with right piece $D_R$ and loop $R$ of $\ell_i$ edges. The stack is last in, first out, so the steps $i + 1, \dots, j$ that follow visit exactly the triangles of $D_R$ and of the pieces cut from it, and step $j$ is the E that finishes the last of them. Count the pieces cut from $D_R$ that are not finished: 1 at the start, plus 1 for each S and minus 1 for each E, so $j$ is the first position after $i$ where the E symbols are one more than the S symbols. Count also the edges on their loops: from the proof of 2, a C adds 1, an L or R removes 1, an S turns a loop of $\ell$ edges into loops with $\ell + 1$ edges in all, and an E removes a loop of 3 edges. The count goes from $\ell_i$ to 0, so $\ell_i + n_C - n_L - n_R + n_S - 3 n_E = 0$ with $n_E = n_S + 1$, that is $\ell_i = 3 + 2 n_S - n_C + n_L + n_R$, the rule of line L7. The code finds each $j$ in one pass: with $d$ the number of E minus the number of S so far, the S at $i$ sets $d$ to some value $d_i$ and waits for $d = d_i + 1$; an S nested inside waits for a smaller value, so the waiting S with the smallest target is always on top of the stack.

*Lock step.* We show by induction on the number of symbols read that the decoder's loops, current gate, stacked gates and faces are those of the encoder renamed by $\varphi$, and that the next new vertex number is the number of marked vertices. At the start both have the loop $\varphi(a) \to \varphi(b) \to \varphi(c) = 0 \to 1 \to 2$, the gate $0 \to 1$ and the face $(0, 1, 2)$. In a step, the decoder reads the symbol the encoder wrote for the triangle $t = (b, a, x)$ across the same gate, and it computes the same new loops as the proof of 2: for C a new vertex, which is $\varphi(x)$ since $x$ is the next vertex marked; for L the vertex before $a$; for R the vertex after $b$; for E the remaining vertex. For S, the right loop is $x \to b \to p_1 \to \dots \to p_{r-1} \to x$ with $\ell_i = r + 1$ edges, so $x = p_r$ is $\ell_i - 1$ steps after $b$ on $P$, which line L12 finds, and since $P$ passes each vertex once the vertex is unique. In each case line L14 appends $(\varphi(b), \varphi(a), \varphi(x)) = \varphi(t)$. Both pop gates in the same order, so the decoder stops after the last symbol, with every triangle of $M$ renamed by $\varphi$, in the order of the visit. $\square$

**Proof of 4.** One symbol is written for each triangle other than $f_0$, so there are $t - 1$. Each C marks one vertex, the other symbols mark none, and by claim 2 every vertex is in a visited triangle, so it is marked; $f_0$ marks 3, so $n_C = V - 3$. The number of pieces goes from 1 to 0, up by 1 at each S and down by 1 at each E, so $n_E = n_S + 1$. Each triangle has 3 edges and each edge 2 triangles, so $3t = 2E$, and $V - E + t = 2$ gives $t = 2V - 4$. The code has $n_C + 3(t - 1 - n_C) = 3t - 3 - 2(V - 3) = 3t + 3 - (t + 4) = 2t - 1$ bits. It is a prefix code, so the bit string splits back into the same symbols. $\square$

Claims 1 to 4 give what the page states: on every closed, oriented, edge-connected triangle mesh with $V - E + F = 2$, ENCODE stops, DECODE(ENCODE($M$)) equals $M$ up to the renumbering $\varphi$ of the vertices, and the code has exactly $2t - 1 < 2t$ bits. $\blacksquare$
