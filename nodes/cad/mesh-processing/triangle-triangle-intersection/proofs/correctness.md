---
title: The plane-side and interval test decides exactly whether two closed triangles meet
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Claim.** Let $P = (p_0, p_1, p_2)$ and $Q = (q_0, q_1, q_2)$ be triangles in $\mathbb{R}^3$, each with three vertices that are not collinear, and let the coordinates be exact numbers (integers or rationals). Then TRI-TRI$(P, Q)$ returns true if and only if the closed triangles $P$ and $Q$ (the convex hulls of their vertices) have a common point. It stops after a fixed number of arithmetic operations.

**Setup.** Write $n_P = (p_1 - p_0) \times (p_2 - p_0)$ and $n_Q = (q_1 - q_0) \times (q_2 - q_0)$. Both are non-zero because the triangles are not degenerate. Let $\pi_P$ and $\pi_Q$ be the planes of $P$ and $Q$. By the definition of ORIENT3D, $dq_i = n_P \cdot (q_i - p_0)$ and $dp_i = n_Q \cdot (p_i - q_0)$. So the sign of $dq_i$ tells on which side of $\pi_P$ the point $q_i$ lies, and $dq_i = 0$ means $q_i \in \pi_P$. All these values are exact, so each test of the pseudocode has the true answer.

**Step 1 (early rejection, lines L2 and L4).** If all $dq_i > 0$, the affine function $f(x) = n_P \cdot (x - p_0)$ is positive at each vertex of $Q$, so it is positive on their convex hull $Q$. But $f = 0$ on $P \subset \pi_P$. So $P \cap Q = \emptyset$, and false is correct. The cases "all $dq_i < 0$" and the two cases of line L4 are the same with signs or roles exchanged.

**Step 2 (the coplanar case, line L5).** If $dq = (0, 0, 0)$, then $q_0, q_1, q_2 \in \pi_P$, and since $Q$ is not degenerate, $\pi_Q = \pi_P$. Line L13 picks a coordinate $k$ with $|(n_P)_k|$ largest, so $(n_P)_k \ne 0$. Dropping coordinate $k$ is then a one-to-one affine map from $\pi_P$ onto the coordinate plane: a point of $\pi_P$ is fixed by its other two coordinates, because the equation of the plane can be solved for coordinate $k$. An affine bijection keeps segments, triangles and their intersections. So it is enough to decide whether the plane triangles $P'$ and $Q'$ meet.

*Lemma A.* Two closed, non-degenerate triangles $P'$ and $Q'$ in the plane meet if and only if an edge of $P'$ meets an edge of $Q'$, or $p'_0 \in Q'$, or $q'_0 \in P'$.

*Proof.* The "if" part holds because edges and vertices are subsets of the triangles. For "only if", let $x \in P' \cap Q'$ and assume that no edge of $P'$ meets an edge of $Q'$, so $\partial P' \cap \partial Q' = \emptyset$. The boundary $\partial P'$ is connected and does not meet $\partial Q'$, so it lies entirely in the interior of $Q'$ or entirely outside $Q'$. In the first case $p'_0 \in Q'$. In the second case $\partial P' \cap Q' = \emptyset$, and in the same way $\partial Q'$ lies in the interior of $P'$ (then $q'_0 \in P'$) or outside $P'$. If both boundaries lie outside the other triangle, then $Q'$ is connected and does not meet $\partial P'$, so $Q'$ lies in the interior of $P'$ or in its exterior. It contains $x \in P'$, and $x \notin \partial P'$, so $Q'$ lies in the interior of $P'$, and then $\partial Q' \subset P'$, a contradiction. $\square$

Lines L14 and L15 test exactly the conditions of Lemma A. The closed segment test is exact: two segments meet if they cross properly (strict opposite signs on both sides) or if an endpoint of one lies on the other, which the collinear branch checks with coordinate ranges. The point test accepts a point when the three orientation signs are all $\ge 0$ or all $\le 0$, which is the closed triangle. So the coplanar branch is correct.

**Step 3 (the general case).** Now $dq \ne (0, 0, 0)$, and the signs of $dq$ are not all strictly equal, and the same holds for $dp$. Then $\pi_P \ne \pi_Q$. The planes are not parallel: if they were parallel and different, $f$ would be a non-zero constant on $\pi_Q$, so all $dq_i$ would be equal and non-zero, which line L2 has excluded. So $L = \pi_P \cap \pi_Q$ is a line, with direction $D = n_P \times n_Q \ne 0$.

*Lemma B.* Let $T = (t_0, t_1, t_2)$ be one of the two triangles, let $s_i$ be the values of the affine function $g$ of the other plane at $t_i$ ($dp$ for $T = P$, $dq$ for $T = Q$), and assume that the $s_i$ are not all strictly of one sign and not all zero. Let $X$ be the set of vertices with $s_i = 0$ together with the points $c_{ij} = t_i + (t_j - t_i)\, s_i / (s_i - s_j)$ for the pairs with $s_i s_j < 0$. Then $X$ is not empty, and $T \cap \{g = 0\}$ is the convex hull of $X$.

*Proof.* If no $s_i$ is zero, two of them have opposite signs, so $X$ is not empty. Each point of $X$ is in $T$ (the factor $s_i / (s_i - s_j)$ lies in $(0, 1)$ when $s_i s_j < 0$) and has $g = 0$, because $g(c_{ij}) = s_i + (s_j - s_i) s_i / (s_i - s_j) = 0$. The set $T \cap \{g = 0\}$ is convex, so it contains the hull of $X$. Conversely, let $x = \sum_i \lambda_i t_i$ with $\lambda_i \ge 0$, $\sum_i \lambda_i = 1$ and $g(x) = \sum_i \lambda_i s_i = 0$. Let $Z$, $A$ and $B$ be the indices with $s_i = 0$, $s_i > 0$ and $s_i < 0$, and let $M = \sum_{i \in A} \lambda_i s_i = -\sum_{j \in B} \lambda_j s_j \ge 0$. If $M = 0$, then $\lambda_i = 0$ for all $i \in A \cup B$, so $x$ is a convex combination of the vertices of $Z$. If $M > 0$, set $\mu_{ij} = \lambda_i \lambda_j (s_i - s_j) / M \ge 0$ for $i \in A$ and $j \in B$. A direct computation gives
$$\sum_{i \in A, j \in B} \mu_{ij}\, c_{ij} = \sum_{i, j} \frac{\lambda_i \lambda_j (s_i t_j - s_j t_i)}{M} = \sum_{i \in A} \lambda_i t_i + \sum_{j \in B} \lambda_j t_j,$$
and in the same way $\sum_{i, j} \mu_{ij} = \sum_{i \in A \cup B} \lambda_i$. So $x = \sum_{i \in Z} \lambda_i t_i + \sum_{i, j} \mu_{ij} c_{ij}$ is a convex combination of points of $X$. Every pair $i \in A$, $j \in B$ is an edge of a triangle, so line L10 meets each such pair. $\square$

By Lemma B, $S_P = P \cap \pi_Q$ is the convex hull of the points that lines L9 to L11 collect for $P$, and $S_Q = Q \cap \pi_P$ is the same for $Q$. Both lie on $L$, because $P \subset \pi_P$ and $Q \subset \pi_Q$. Each point of $L$ is $x_0 + \lambda D$ for one real $\lambda$, and $D \cdot (x_0 + \lambda D) = D \cdot x_0 + \lambda |D|^2$ is strictly increasing in $\lambda$. So $x \mapsto D \cdot x$ maps $L$ one to one and in order onto $\mathbb{R}$, the segment $S_P$ onto $[a, b]$ and $S_Q$ onto $[c, e]$, the values that line L12 returns. Line L11 computes $D \cdot c_{ij}$ as $D \cdot t_i + (D \cdot t_j - D \cdot t_i)\, s_i / (s_i - s_j)$, which is the same number by linearity.

Finally $P \cap Q = (P \cap \pi_Q) \cap (Q \cap \pi_P) = S_P \cap S_Q$, because a point of $P \cap Q$ lies in both planes, and a point of $S_P \cap S_Q$ lies in both triangles. Two segments of $L$ meet if and only if their images meet, that is, if and only if $\max(a, c) \le \min(b, e)$. This is the test of line L8.

**Termination.** There are no loops except the loops over the 3 vertices and the 3 edges of each triangle, and over the 9 pairs of edges in the coplanar case. So the test stops after a fixed number of exact operations. $\blacksquare$
