---
title: The Tutte embedding of a triangulated disk with a strictly convex boundary is a bijection with no flipped triangle
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Claim.** Let $M$ be a triangulated disk: a finite simplicial complex of triangles that is homeomorphic to a closed disk, with vertex set $V = I \cup B$, where $B = (b_1, \ldots, b_k)$, $k \ge 3$, is the boundary cycle and $I$ is the set of inner vertices. The triangles are oriented coherently, and $b_1, \ldots, b_k$ is the order in which the oriented boundary edges run. Let $p_1, \ldots, p_k$ be the corners of a strictly convex polygon $P$ in counterclockwise order. For each $i \in I$ let $\lambda_{ij} > 0$ for the neighbours $j$ of $i$, with $\sum_j \lambda_{ij} = 1$. Then:

1. the system $x_{b_t} = p_t$, $x_i = \sum_j \lambda_{ij} x_j$ ($i \in I$) has exactly one solution;
2. every triangle $(a, b, c)$ of $M$ has ORIENT2D$(x_a, x_b, x_c) > 0$;
3. the map $\varphi : M \to \mathbb{R}^2$ that is linear on each triangle with $\varphi(v) = x_v$ at the vertices is a homeomorphism onto $P$.

With Fraction weights and corners, TUTTE-EMBED computes this solution exactly and stops.

**Step 1 (the system has one solution).** The system has $|I|$ unknown points and $|I|$ vector equations, so it is enough to show that the homogeneous system has only the zero solution. Let $h : V \to \mathbb{R}$ with $h_b = 0$ on $B$ and $h_i = \sum_j \lambda_{ij} h_j$ on $I$, and let $m = \max_V h$. Suppose $m > 0$, so $m$ is attained at an inner vertex $i$. Since $h_i$ is a convex combination with positive weights of values that are at most $m$, all neighbours of $i$ have the value $m$. The graph of a disk is connected, so there is a path from $i$ to a boundary vertex. Along the path, each inner vertex has the value $m$ and so passes it on to the next vertex. The first boundary vertex of the path then has $h = m > 0$, which is false. So $m \le 0$, and in the same way $\min_V h \ge 0$. So $h = 0$. Each coordinate of $x$ is such a system, so the solution exists and is unique.

**Step 2 (a lemma on cycles).** For a set $U$ of triangles, let $E_U$ be the set of edges that lie in exactly one triangle of $U$.

*Lemma A.* If $U$ is not empty, then $E_U$ is not empty, and every vertex has an even number of edges of $E_U$.

*Proof.* If $U$ is the set of all triangles, the $k \ge 3$ boundary edges of $M$ lie in exactly one triangle, so they are in $E_U$. If not, the dual graph of $M$ (triangles, adjacent when they share an edge) is connected, because $M$ is connected and every edge of a triangle is in at most two triangles, so some edge is shared by a triangle in $U$ and a triangle not in $U$. This edge is in $E_U$. For a vertex $w$, count the pairs (triangle $T \in U$ at $w$, edge of $T$ at $w$). Each triangle gives 2, so the count is even. An edge at $w$ in two triangles of $U$ is counted twice, and an edge in one is counted once. So the number of edges of $E_U$ at $w$ is even. $\square$

A nonempty set of edges in which every vertex has even degree contains a cycle, and in a simplicial complex a cycle has at least 3 vertices.

*Lemma B.* Let $Z$ be a nonempty set of vertices and let $U$ be the set of triangles with a vertex in $Z$. Every edge of $E_U$ either is a boundary edge of $M$ with an end in $Z$, or has both ends in $N(Z) \setminus Z$, the vertices outside $Z$ that have a neighbour in $Z$.

*Proof.* Let $e \in E_U$ lie in $T \in U$. If $e$ has an end $z \in Z$ and $e$ is an inner edge, the second triangle at $e$ contains $z$, so it is in $U$, and $e$ is in two triangles of $U$, which is false. So an edge of $E_U$ with an end in $Z$ is a boundary edge. If $e$ has no end in $Z$, the third vertex of $T$ is in $Z$, and both ends of $e$ are its neighbours. $\square$

**Step 3 (chords and 3-connectivity).** A *chord* is an inner edge whose two ends are boundary vertices.

*Lemma C.* If $M$ has no chord and at least 4 vertices, the graph $G$ of $M$ is 3-connected.

*Proof.* We show that no set $S = \{u, v\}$ of one or two vertices separates $G$ (for one vertex, let $u = v$). Suppose that $G - S$ has at least two components. If a component $Z$ has no boundary vertex, then by Lemma B every edge of $E_U$ has both ends in $N(Z) \setminus Z \subseteq \{u, v\}$. So $E_U \subseteq \{uv\}$, which by Lemma A is nonempty and contains a cycle, a contradiction. So every component contains a boundary vertex. The boundary cycle without $S$ is one arc if $u = v$, if $u$ or $v$ is inner, or if $u$ and $v$ are consecutive on the cycle, and then all boundary vertices except $u, v$ are in one component, and another component has no boundary vertex. So $u$ and $v$ are boundary vertices that are not consecutive, the cycle without them is two arcs $A_1$ and $A_2$, and they lie in different components. Let $Z$ be the component of $A_1$. Its boundary vertices are the vertices of $A_1$. By Lemma B, $E_U$ consists of the boundary edges of the path $u, A_1, v$ (they lie in one triangle, which has an end in $Z$) and possibly the edge $uv$. The path has degree 1 at $u$, so by Lemma A the edge $uv$ exists and is in $E_U$. As $u$ and $v$ are not consecutive, $uv$ is a chord, a contradiction. $\square$

**Step 4 (the theorem without chords).** Suppose $M$ has no chord. If $M$ has no inner vertex, every inner edge would be a chord, so $M$ has no inner edge, and it is one triangle $(b_1, b_2, b_3)$, which is mapped onto $P$ with positive orientation. Otherwise $M$ has at least 4 vertices, so $G$ is 3-connected by Lemma C. The disk gives a planar embedding of $G$ whose faces are the triangles of $M$ and the outside of the boundary cycle $B$. By [Tutte's spring theorem](/tutte-spring-theorem/), in the form with positive weights stated there, the solution of Step 1 draws $G$ with straight edges and no crossings, its faces are the faces of the embedding, every inner face is a convex polygon with nonzero area, and the outer face is the outside of $P$. So every triangle of $M$ is drawn as a nondegenerate triangle, and the triangles tile $P$ with disjoint interiors.

Orientation. Let $T$ be a triangle with a boundary edge, written in its orientation as $(b_t, b_{t+1}, c)$. The face $T$ lies inside $P$, which is to the left of the counterclockwise edge from $p_t$ to $p_{t+1}$, so ORIENT2D$(x_{b_t}, x_{b_{t+1}}, x_c) > 0$. Let $uv$ be an inner edge with triangles $(u, v, a)$ and $(v, u, c)$. They are two faces of a planar straight-line drawing on the two sides of the segment $uv$. If $a$ and $c$ were on the same side of the line $uv$, both triangles would contain the points on that side near the middle of $uv$, and their interiors would meet. So $a$ and $c$ are strictly on opposite sides, which means ORIENT2D$(x_u, x_v, x_a)$ and ORIENT2D$(x_v, x_u, x_c)$ have the same sign. The dual graph is connected, so every triangle has the sign of a boundary triangle, which is positive. The map $\varphi$ is linear and one to one on each triangle, the images tile $P$ with disjoint interiors, and two triangles that share an edge or a vertex are glued in the same way in $M$ and in the drawing. So $\varphi$ is a continuous bijection from the compact space $M$ onto $P$, hence a homeomorphism.

**Step 5 (chords, by induction on the number of triangles).** Suppose $M$ has a chord $ab$, with $a = b_s$ and $b = b_t$ not consecutive. The chord is an arc in $M$ from boundary to boundary whose inside lies in the interior of $M$. By the [Jordan curve theorem](/jordan-curve-theorem/) for polygonal curves, it cuts $M$ into two triangulated disks $M_1$ and $M_2$, each a union of triangles of $M$ with fewer triangles than $M$. The boundary of $M_1$ is the boundary arc $b_s, b_{s+1}, \ldots, b_t$ and the chord, and the boundary of $M_2$ is the other arc and the chord. An inner vertex of $M_1$ is not on the chord, so all its triangles and neighbours are in $M_1$, and its equation involves only vertices of $M_1$. The corners $p_s, \ldots, p_t$ are corners of $P$, so they are the corners of a strictly convex polygon $P_1$, in counterclockwise order, and the same holds for $P_2$ with the other corners. So the restriction of the solution to $M_1$ is the solution of the same problem on $M_1$ and $P_1$, and by induction every triangle of $M_1$ is positive and $\varphi$ maps $M_1$ onto $P_1$ one to one. The same holds for $M_2$ and $P_2$. The polygons $P_1$ and $P_2$ meet only in the segment $p_s p_t$, which is the image of the chord in both. So $\varphi$ is one to one on $M$ and maps it onto $P_1 \cup P_2 = P$. Steps 4 and 5 prove parts 2 and 3 for every triangulated disk.

**Step 6 (the computation).** TUTTE-EMBED builds the matrix of Step 1 and solves it by Gaussian elimination. With Fraction entries every operation is exact, and the matrix is nonsingular, so no pivot is zero after the row exchanges of partial pivoting, and the result is the exact solution. The elimination does a finite number of operations on an $|I| \times |I|$ matrix, so it stops. The signs of ORIENT2D on the exact positions are exact, which is what the tests check. $\blacksquare$

**What the hypotheses do.** If $P$ is convex but not strictly convex, some boundary vertices lie on a side of $P$, and a chord between two vertices of the same side cuts off a disk $M_1$ whose boundary vertices all lie on that side. By the argument of Step 1, the inner vertices of $M_1$ lie on the same segment, so the triangles of $M_1$ have zero area. Floater (2003) shows that the map is still one to one if no such chord exists. If $P$ is not convex, Step 4 fails, and the test file has a wheel with a non-convex boundary whose Tutte map has a triangle with negative orientation. If some $\lambda_{ij}$ is negative, as cotangent weights can be, Step 1 can fail, and so can the rest.
