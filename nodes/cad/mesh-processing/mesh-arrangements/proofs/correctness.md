---
title: The planar arrangement version labels every face with its exact winding numbers and returns the exact boundary and area of any Boolean of the inputs
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Setting.** The input is a finite list of closed polygons $P_1, \dots, P_r$ in the plane with rational vertices, each tagged with an input $k \in \{0, \dots, K - 1\}$. A polygon is any closed polyline: it may cross itself, overlap other polygons or touch them. Zero-length edges are dropped. For a point $p$ off all edges, $w_k(p)$ is the sum of the winding numbers of the polygons of input $k$ around $p$, and $w(p) = (w_0(p), \dots, w_{K-1}(p))$. A counterclockwise polygon, whose outward normal is on the right of each edge, winds $+1$ around its inside. A *rule* is a function $f$ from integer vectors to $\{\text{true}, \text{false}\}$ with $f(0) = \text{false}$, for example $w_0 > 0 \lor w_1 > 0$ for a union. The Boolean is the set $R_f$, the closure of $\{p : f(w(p))\}$.

**Claim.**

1. After L1 and L2, the subsegments form a planar straight-line graph $G$: two distinct subsegments meet at most at a common endpoint. Each subsegment $e$ carries the vector $m(e)$ of signed counts of input edges that contain it.
2. For a half-edge $h = (u \to v)$, the code's NEXT gives the next half-edge on the boundary of the face of $G$ to the left of $h$.
3. When the code finishes, the label of every half-edge $h$ equals $w(p)$ for the points $p$ of the face to the left of $h$. In particular, the code never finds two different labels for one half-edge.
4. The half-edges $h$ whose left label satisfies $f$ and whose right label does not are exactly the boundary of $R_f$, oriented with $R_f$ on the left, and half the sum of $\mathrm{cross}(u, v)$ over them is the area of $R_f$.

**Step 1 (part 1).** All points are computed with exact rational arithmetic, so equal points are equal tuples. Every point where two input edges meet is added as a cut point to both: a single crossing point when they are not parallel, and the endpoints of the overlap when they are collinear. Let $s$ be a subsegment of edge $e_i$ and $t$ one of edge $e_j$, and let $x$ be a common point in the interior of $s$. Then $x \in e_i \cap e_j$. If $e_i$ and $e_j$ are not parallel, $x$ is their crossing point, which is a cut point of $e_i$, but a subsegment has no cut point in its interior; a contradiction. If they are collinear, $x$ lies in their overlap, whose endpoints are cut points of both, so $s$ and $t$ lie in the overlap. Within the overlap, $e_i$ and $e_j$ have the same cut points: a point where a third edge meets $e_i$ inside the overlap also lies on $e_j$, and is added to $e_j$ by the same rule. So $s = t$ as segments, and L2 merges them into one key and adds their signed counts. Hence distinct subsegments meet only at endpoints.

**Step 2 (part 2).** At each vertex $v$ the outgoing directions are sorted counterclockwise with an exact comparison: first by the half-plane, then by the sign of a cross product. No two outgoing subsegments have the same direction, since by part 1 they would overlap. The face to the left of $h = (u \to v)$ near $v$ is the wedge between the direction from $v$ back to $u$ and the next outgoing direction clockwise from it. So the boundary of that face continues with that outgoing edge, which is what NEXT returns. This is the usual face rule of a [doubly connected edge list](/doubly-connected-edge-list/).

**Step 3 (labels are consistent with the winding numbers).** Two facts about $w$.

(a) $w$ is constant on each face of $G$, because $w_k$ changes only across edges of input $k$, and each face is a connected open set off all edges.

(b) If $p$ is just left of a point $x$ in the interior of a subsegment $e = (u \to v)$ and $q$ is just right of it, then $w(p) - w(q) = m(u \to v)$. For a single polygon edge through $x$ going from $u$ toward $v$, the half-open crossing count with a ray from $p$ or $q$ along the edge's normal direction differs by exactly one crossing of that edge, with sign $+1$ when the edge goes the same way as $u \to v$ and $-1$ when it goes the other way. Edges that do not contain $x$ do not separate $p$ from $q$. Summing over the input edges that contain $e$ gives $m$.

The code labels each half-edge with $w$ of its left face. A step to NEXT stays in the same face, so by (a) the label is copied. A step to the twin crosses the edge from left to right, so by (b) the new label is the old one minus $m(h)$. Both rules hold for the true values, so if the first label of a component is right, every label reached from it is right, and no conflict can appear.

The first label. Let $C$ be a connected component of $G$ and $v$ its least vertex in the order (x, then y). Each polygon is connected, so all its subsegments lie in one component, and $C$ is the union of whole polygons. Every point of $C$ has $x \ge v_x$, and the points with $x = v_x$ have $y \ge v_y$, so all outgoing directions at $v$ lie in the half-open half-plane from straight down (excluded) to straight up (included), and the direction $(-1, 0)$ lies in the wedge to the left of the outgoing half-edge with the largest angle. That is the half-edge the code picks. Let $p = v + \varepsilon(-1, 0)$ for a small $\varepsilon > 0$. The ray from $p$ to the left meets no point of $C$, so the polygons of $C$ wind 0 around $p$. The vertex $v$ is on no edge of a polygon outside $C$ (otherwise that polygon would share a vertex with $C$ by part 1 and be in $C$), so the winding numbers of the other polygons are the same at $p$ as at $v$, and the code computes them at $v$ with the exact half-open rule, which is correct at every point off the counted edges.

Every half-edge of $C$ is reached: from $h = (x \to y)$, the twin of $h$ followed by NEXT is the outgoing half-edge at $x$ just clockwise of $h$, so all half-edges at a vertex are reached, and $C$ is connected. This proves part 3.

**Step 4 (part 4).** By part 3, $f$ of the left label of $h$ says whether the face left of $h$ is in $R_f$. An edge is on the boundary of $R_f$ exactly when one side is in it and the other is not, and the code keeps the half-edge with $R_f$ on its left. The unbounded face has $w = 0$, so it is not in $R_f$ and $R_f$ is bounded. For each bounded face $F$, the half-edges with $F$ on the left form its boundary cycles, the outer one counterclockwise and the holes clockwise, and by Green's theorem half the sum of $\mathrm{cross}(u, v)$ over them is the area of $F$; a subsegment with the same face on both sides contributes $\mathrm{cross}(u, v) + \mathrm{cross}(v, u) = 0$. Summing over the faces of $R_f$, an edge between two faces of $R_f$ appears in both directions and cancels, which leaves the sum over the boundary half-edges. All operations are rational, so the area is exact.

**Termination.** L1 compares each pair of input edges once. L2 sorts finitely many cut points per edge. The search of L5 adds a half-edge to the queue only when it gets its first label, so it runs at most twice the number of subsegments times. $\blacksquare$

**The 3D method.** Zhou and coauthors apply the same argument to triangles in space: the cut of all triangles against each other gives a complex whose faces meet only along shared edges, cells take the place of faces, the patches around a non-manifold edge are sorted by an exact orientation test, and the winding number vector jumps across a face by its multiplicity. This page proves only the planar version that its code implements.
