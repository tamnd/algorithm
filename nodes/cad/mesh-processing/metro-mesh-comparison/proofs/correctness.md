---
title: Metro's sampled distance is a lower bound of the one-sided Hausdorff distance, and it is within the sample spacing of the true value
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Setting.** $A$ and $B$ are finite sets of triangles in $\mathbb{R}^3$, each of positive area, and they also denote the union of their triangles, which are closed sets. The distance from a point $p$ to $B$ is $d(p, B) = \min_{q \in B} |p - q|$, and the one-sided Hausdorff distance is $h(A, B) = \max_{p \in A} d(p, B)$. The symmetric distance is $H(A, B) = \max(h(A, B), h(B, A))$. A sample set $S \subset A$ is a finite set of points. The code reports $\hat h_S = \max_{p \in S} d(p, B)$, and the mean and root mean square of $d(p, B)$ over $S$. The subdivision sample $S_k$ of a triangle $(a, b, c)$ is the set of points $a + \frac{i}{k}(b - a) + \frac{j}{k}(c - a)$ with $i, j \ge 0$ and $i + j \le k$, and $S_k(A)$ is the union over the triangles of $A$.

**Claim.**

1. For a point $p$ and a triangle $T = (a, b, c)$, the function CLOSEST-POINT of the code returns the point of $T$ nearest to $p$, so $d(p, T)^2$ is computed exactly for rational input.
2. The uniform grid query returns $\min_{T \in B} d(p, T)^2$, the same value as the loop over all triangles.
3. For every sample set $S \subset A$, $\hat h_S \le h(A, B)$.
4. If every point of $A$ is within $\delta$ of a point of $S$, then $h(A, B) \le \hat h_S + \delta$. For $S_k(A)$, $\delta \le L / k$, where $L$ is the longest edge of $A$. Also $\hat h_{S_k} \le \hat h_{S_{2k}}$.
5. The symmetric value returned is $\max(\hat h_{S}(A, B), \hat h_{S'}(B, A))$, and it lies between $H(A, B) - \delta$ and $H(A, B)$ when both sample sets are $\delta$-dense.

**Step 1 (part 1).** The squared distance $f(u, v) = |a + u(b - a) + v(c - a) - p|^2$ is a convex quadratic on the convex set $\{u, v \ge 0, u + v \le 1\}$. Its minimizer is the unique point $q$ of $T$ with $(p - q) \cdot (x - q) \le 0$ for all $x \in T$. The plane around $T$ is split into seven regions: three corner regions, three edge regions and the inside. The corner region of $a$ is $\{(b - a) \cdot (p - a) \le 0,\ (c - a) \cdot (p - a) \le 0\}$; there $(p - a) \cdot (x - a) \le 0$ for all $x = a + u(b - a) + v(c - a)$ in $T$, so $q = a$. The same holds at $b$ and $c$ with the tests $d_3 \ge 0, d_4 \le d_3$ and $d_6 \ge 0, d_5 \le d_6$ of the code. If $p$ is in none of them, the code tests the edge regions: for edge $ab$, $v_c \le 0$ says that the projection of $p$ onto the plane is on the outer side of the line $ab$ (the quantity $v_c$ is the area coordinate of $c$ times twice the area times $|n|^2$), and $d_1 \ge 0 \ge d_3$ says that the foot on the line falls between $a$ and $b$; then $q = a + \frac{d_1}{d_1 - d_3}(b - a)$, the foot of the perpendicular on the segment, and $p - q$ is orthogonal to $ab$ and points away from $T$, which gives the variational inequality. The other two edges are the same. If $p$ is in no corner or edge region, all three area coordinates $v_a, v_b, v_c$ are positive and the projection of $p$ onto the plane lies in $T$, so $q$ is that projection, with barycentric weights $v_b / (v_a + v_b + v_c)$ and $v_c / (v_a + v_b + v_c)$. Every operation is a ring operation or a division by a nonzero rational, so the result is exact. The tests of the page also check it against another exact route on 3000 random cases.

**Step 2 (part 2).** Let the cell of a point $x$ be $\lfloor x / h \rfloor$ in each coordinate. Each triangle is stored in every cell that meets its bounding box, so a triangle is stored in the cell of each of its points. Let $p$ be in cell $c$. After the query has visited all cells $c'$ with $\|c' - c\|_\infty \le r$, any point $x$ in a cell with $\|c' - c\|_\infty \ge r + 1$ differs from $p$ by at least $r h$ in some coordinate, so $|x - p| \ge r h$. If $T$ is a triangle not yet seen, its point nearest to $p$ is in such a cell (else $T$ would be stored in a visited cell and seen), so $d(p, T) \ge r h$. The query stops when $(r h)^2 \ge$ the best value found, so no unseen triangle is closer. It also stops when the ring has passed all occupied cells, and then every triangle has been seen. In both cases it returns the minimum over all triangles.

**Step 3 (part 3).** Every sample is a point of $A$, so $d(p, B) \le h(A, B)$ for each sample and the maximum over $S$ is at most $h(A, B)$.

**Step 4 (part 4).** The function $p \mapsto d(p, B)$ is 1-Lipschitz: for $q \in B$ nearest to $y$, $d(x, B) \le |x - q| \le |x - y| + d(y, B)$. The maximum $h(A, B)$ is attained at some $x \in A$, because $A$ is compact and $d$ is continuous. Take $s \in S$ with $|x - s| \le \delta$; then $h(A, B) = d(x, B) \le d(s, B) + \delta \le \hat h_S + \delta$. For $S_k$, the subdivision of a triangle with corners $a, b, c$ into the $k^2$ small triangles with corners in $S_k$ covers the triangle, each small triangle is similar to it with ratio $1/k$, and a point of a triangle is within the diameter of that triangle, its longest edge, of each of its corners. So $\delta \le L / k$. Finally $S_k \subset S_{2k}$, since $i/k = 2i/2k$, so the maximum can only grow.

**Step 5 (part 5).** The code returns the larger of the two one-sided estimates. By parts 3 and 4 each is between $h - \delta$ and $h$ for its direction, and the maximum of two numbers each in such an interval is in $[\max - \delta, \max]$.

**What is measured, not proved.** The mean and the root mean square are estimates of the area-weighted mean $\frac{1}{|A|}\int_A d(p, B)\, dp$ and of the square root of $\frac{1}{|A|}\int_A d(p, B)^2\, dp$. The Monte Carlo samples choose a triangle with probability proportional to its area and a point in it with the reflection trick, which maps the uniform distribution on the unit square to the uniform distribution on the triangle; with $n$ samples the error of the mean shrinks like $1/\sqrt n$. The code draws the two coordinates from a grid of step $10^{-6}$, so the points are uniform up to that grid. The vertices of $S_k$ are not area-weighted (the edges of the mesh get more of them), so a mean over $S_k$ alone is biased, as the example of the page shows.

**Termination.** Sampling makes $O(k^2)$ points per triangle plus $n$ random points. Each grid query visits rings of growing radius and stops at the latest when the ring has passed all occupied cells, a finite number. $\blacksquare$
