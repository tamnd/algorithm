---
title: The winged-edge walks list each face and each vertex star exactly once, and the updates keep the wings correct
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

We use the pseudocode and the fields of the page. The input of BUILD is a list of faces, each a cyclic list $(w_0, \ldots, w_{k-1})$ of $k \ge 3$ distinct vertices. A face $f$ *uses* the directed pair $(w_i, w_{i+1})$, indices mod $k$. For an edge $e$ and a face $f \in \{f_{left}[e], f_{right}[e]\}$ with $f_{left}[e] \ne f_{right}[e]$, write $\mathrm{side}(e, f)$ for left or right, $\mathrm{next}(e, f)$ and $\mathrm{prev}(e, f)$ for the wings on that side, and $\mathrm{tail}(e, f)$ for $v_{start}[e]$ if the side is left and $v_{end}[e]$ if it is right. Write $\mathrm{head}(e, f)$ for the other end.

**Invariant W.** (a) For each edge $e$, $f_{left}[e] \ne f_{right}[e]$. (b) For each face $f$, the edges $e$ with $f \in \{f_{left}[e], f_{right}[e]\}$ can be listed as $e_0, \ldots, e_{k-1}$ so that $\mathrm{next}(e_i, f) = e_{i+1}$ and $\mathrm{prev}(e_i, f) = e_{i-1}$, indices mod $k$, and $\mathrm{head}(e_i, f) = \mathrm{tail}(e_{i+1}, f)$. (c) $face\_edge[f]$ is an edge of $f$, and $vertex\_edge[v]$ is an edge with $v$ as an end. (d) For each vertex $v$, the walk of lines L5 to L8 meets every edge with $v$ as an end.

The cyclic list $(\mathrm{tail}(e_0, f), \ldots, \mathrm{tail}(e_{k-1}, f))$ of (b) is the *face list* of $f$.

**Lemma 1 (BUILD).** If BUILD returns, then W holds, and the face list of each face $f$ is the input list of $f$, up to a cyclic shift.

*Proof.* BUILD makes one edge per unordered pair $\{u, v\}$ that some face uses. If the first face $f$ that uses the pair uses it as $(u, v)$, the edge gets $v_{start} = u$, $v_{end} = v$ and $f_{left} = f$. A later use is accepted only if it is $(v, u)$ by a face whose slot $f_{right}$ is still empty, and it sets $f_{right}$. Any other use fails. After the loop, BUILD fails if some $f_{right}$ is empty or equal to $f_{left}$. So if BUILD returns, each edge is used by exactly two different faces, the left face as $(v_{start}, v_{end})$ and the right face as $(v_{end}, v_{start})$, and (a) holds. In particular $\mathrm{tail}(e, f)$ is the first vertex of the pair that $f$ uses on $e$, and $\mathrm{head}(e, f)$ the second. A face with distinct vertices uses each pair at most once. Now take the input list $(w_0, \ldots, w_{k-1})$ of $f$ and let $e_i$ be the edge of $\{w_i, w_{i+1}\}$. These are $k$ different edges, they are all the edges of $f$, and the second loop of BUILD sets $\mathrm{prev}(e_i, f) = e_{i-1}$ and $\mathrm{next}(e_i, f) = e_{i+1}$ on the side of $f$. Each side of each edge belongs to one face, so no other step writes these fields. Also $\mathrm{tail}(e_i, f) = w_i$ and $\mathrm{head}(e_i, f) = w_{i+1} = \mathrm{tail}(e_{i+1}, f)$, which gives (b) and the claim about the face list. BUILD sets $face\_edge[f] = e_0$ and $vertex\_edge[v]$ to the first edge with $v$ as an end, and it fails if a vertex has none, so (c) holds. The last check of BUILD compares the length of the walk at each $v$ with the number of edges at $v$, which gives (d) once Lemma 3 shows that the walk lists distinct edges. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (FACE-EDGES).** Under W, FACE-EDGES($f$) stops and outputs the edges of $f$ once each, in the order of (b), starting at $face\_edge[f]$.

*Proof.* By (a), the test of line L3 decides $\mathrm{side}(e, f)$ correctly, so line L3 sets $e \leftarrow \mathrm{next}(e, f)$. By (b), $\mathrm{next}(\cdot, f)$ is the cyclic shift $e_i \mapsto e_{i+1}$ on the $k$ edges of $f$, and by (c) the walk starts on one of them. So the walk visits $e_i, e_{i+1}, \ldots$ and returns to $e_i$ after exactly $k$ steps, at which point line L4 stops it. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (VERTEX-EDGES).** Under W, for a vertex $v$ let $A_v$ be the set of edges with $v$ as an end, and for $e \in A_v$ let $\sigma_v(e)$ be the edge that line L7 picks. Then $\sigma_v$ is a permutation of $A_v$, and VERTEX-EDGES($v$) stops and outputs the cycle of $\sigma_v$ that contains $vertex\_edge[v]$, once each. With (d), this is all of $A_v$.

*Proof.* Let $e \in A_v$ and let $\varphi(e)$ be $f_{left}[e]$ if $v_{start}[e] = v$ and $f_{right}[e]$ if not. Then $\mathrm{tail}(e, \varphi(e)) = v$: the face $\varphi(e)$ uses $e$ as it leaves $v$. Line L7 sets $\sigma_v(e) = \mathrm{prev}(e, \varphi(e))$, and by (b), $\mathrm{head}(\sigma_v(e), \varphi(e)) = \mathrm{tail}(e, \varphi(e)) = v$. So $\sigma_v(e) \in A_v$, and $\varphi(e)$ uses it as it comes into $v$. The two ends of an edge are different, because the vertices of a face are, so exactly one of the two cases holds for each edge. Now let $p \in A_v$, and let $\psi(p)$ be the face that uses $p$ as it comes into $v$: $f_{left}[p]$ if $v_{end}[p] = v$ and $f_{right}[p]$ if not. If $\sigma_v(e) = p$, then $\varphi(e) = \psi(p)$, because $\varphi(e)$ uses $p$ into $v$ and only one face does so, and $e = \mathrm{next}(p, \psi(p))$ by (b). So $e$ is determined by $p$, and $\sigma_v$ is injective on the finite set $A_v$, hence a permutation. The walk of lines L5 to L8 applies $\sigma_v$ from $vertex\_edge[v] \in A_v$, which is in $A_v$ by (c), until it returns, so it outputs one cycle of $\sigma_v$ once each and stops. $\square$

Geometrically, $\varphi(e)$ is the face just counterclockwise of $e$ at $v$, seen from outside, and $\sigma_v(e)$ is the next edge counterclockwise. A cycle of $\sigma_v$ is a fan of faces at $v$, and (d) says that $v$ has one fan, which is the vertex condition of a 2-manifold.

**Lemma 4 (SPLIT-EDGE).** If W holds before SPLIT-EDGE($e$), it holds after it, every face list keeps its order with the new vertex $m$ put between $v_{start}[e]$ and $v_{end}[e]$, and $V - E + F$ does not change.

*Proof.* Let $a = v_{start}[e]$, $b = v_{end}[e]$, $f = f_{left}[e]$ and $g = f_{right}[e]$. On $f$ the edges are $\ldots, e, x, \ldots$ with $x = left_{next}[e]$, and on $g$ they are $\ldots, y, e, \ldots$ with $y = right_{prev}[e]$. The update makes $e_2$ from $m$ to $b$ with the same two faces, sets $v_{end}[e] = m$, and sets the wings so that the cycle of $f$ becomes $\ldots, e, e_2, x, \ldots$ and the cycle of $g$ becomes $\ldots, y, e_2, e, \ldots$. The heads and tails match: on $f$, $e$ now ends at $m$ where $e_2$ starts, and $e_2$ ends at $b$ where $x$ starts; on $g$, $y$ ends at $b$ where $e_2$ starts on that side, and $e_2$ ends at $m$ where $e$ starts on that side. No other face or wing changes. So (a) and (b) hold, $vertex\_edge[m] = e$ and the update of $vertex\_edge[b]$ keep (c), and the edges at $m$ are $e$ and $e_2$, which line L7 joins in one cycle, so (d) holds at $m$; at $b$ the edge $e_2$ takes the place of $e$ in the cycle. One vertex and one edge are new, so $V - E + F$ is the same. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (SPLIT-FACE).** If W holds before SPLIT-FACE($f, u, w$) and $u, w$ are vertices of $f$ that are not joined by an edge, then W holds after it, the face list of $f$ is the part of the old list from $w$ around to $u$, the face list of the new face $g$ is the part from $u$ to $w$, and $V - E + F$ does not change.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2, line L9 finds the cycle $e_0, \ldots, e_{k-1}$ of $f$. Let $e_i$ be the edge with tail $u$ and $e_j$ the edge with tail $w$, so $a_{out} = e_i$, $a_{in} = e_{i-1}$, $b_{out} = e_j$ and $b_{in} = e_{j-1}$. Since $u$ and $w$ are not joined, $j \ne i \pm 1$. The new edge $n$ runs from $u$ to $w$, with $f$ on its left and $g$ on its right, so $f$ uses it as $(u, w)$ and $g$ as $(w, u)$, and (a) holds for it. Line L12 gives the edges $e_i, \ldots, e_{j-1}$ to $g$, each on its side of $f$. Lines L11 and L13 make the cycle of $f$ equal to $e_{i-1}, n, e_j, \ldots$, that is $e_j, \ldots, e_{i-1}, n$, and the cycle of $g$ equal to $n, e_i, \ldots, e_{j-1}$. On $f$, $e_{i-1}$ ends at $u$ where $n$ starts, and $n$ ends at $w$ where $e_j$ starts. On $g$, $e_{j-1}$ ends at $w$ where $n$ starts on the side of $g$, and $n$ ends at $u$ where $e_i$ starts. The other wings of these edges point inside one of the two parts and are not changed, and the edges of other faces are not touched. So (b) holds, and the face lists are as claimed. Line L14 gives (c). For (d), at $u$ the old step $\sigma_u(e_i) = e_{i-1}$ becomes the two steps $\sigma_u(e_i) = n$ and $\sigma_u(n) = e_{i-1}$: the edge $n$ is put into the one cycle at $u$, and the same holds at $w$. Each new edge is not an edge between the same ends as an old one, because of the test, so the edges at $u$ stay distinct. One edge and one face are new, so $V - E + F$ is the same. $\square$

**Lemma 6 (JOIN-FACES).** If W holds before JOIN-FACES($n$), and the two faces $f = f_{left}[n]$ and $g = f_{right}[n]$ have only the two ends $u, w$ of $n$ in common and each of $u, w$ has at least 3 edges, then W holds after it, and the face list of $f$ is the face list of $f$ with the part of $g$ from $u$ to $w$ put in place of $n$.

*Proof.* The steps are the inverse of those of Lemma 5 with $e_{i-1} = left_{prev}[n]$, $e_j = left_{next}[n]$, $e_{j-1} = right_{prev}[n]$ and $e_i = right_{next}[n]$: the edges of $g$ other than $n$ get $f$ on their side of $g$, and the four wings that pointed to $n$ are joined. Since $f$ and $g$ share no vertex other than $u$ and $w$, they share no edge other than $n$, so no edge gets $f$ on both sides, and (a) holds. The cycle of $f$ becomes $e_j, \ldots, e_{i-1}, e_i, \ldots, e_{j-1}$, and the heads and tails match at $u$ and at $w$, which gives (b). The fields that pointed to $n$ are moved to $e_i$ and $e_j$, which gives (c). At $u$, the steps $\sigma_u(e_i) = n$ and $\sigma_u(n) = e_{i-1}$ become $\sigma_u(e_i) = e_{i-1}$, which removes $n$ from the cycle and keeps one cycle, and the same holds at $w$. The degree condition keeps at least 2 edges at each end, so no vertex is left with a face loop of length 2. So (d) holds. $\square$

**Theorem.** After BUILD and any sequence of SPLIT-EDGE, SPLIT-FACE and JOIN-FACES whose preconditions hold, FACE-EDGES($f$) outputs the face list of each face $f$ and VERTEX-EDGES($v$) outputs all edges at each vertex $v$, each once and in counterclockwise order, and both stop. The edge queries return the fields of the edge record, which are correct by W.

*Proof.* W holds after BUILD by Lemma 1, and after each update by Lemmas 4 to 6, by induction on the number of updates. Lemmas 2 and 3 give the output and the termination of the walks under W. $\square$

The tests check the same facts on the cube, the octahedron and a torus, and after each of 360 random steps, against a brute force that stores only the face lists and answers each query by a scan of all faces. They also check that BUILD rejects an open surface, a reversed face, an edge on three faces and two tetrahedra that share one vertex. $\blacksquare$
