---
title: TRACE-SEAMS finds every junction and seam of the medial surface of a convex polyhedron
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

We use the pseudocode of the page. $P = \{x : n_i \cdot x \le b_i,\ i \in F\}$ is a bounded convex polyhedron with nonempty interior, where $F$ indexes the facets of $P$, one plane per facet, and each $n_i$ is a unit vector. Distinct facets have distinct outer normals. For $x \in P$, $d_i(x) = b_i - n_i \cdot x \ge 0$, $\rho(x) = \min_{i \in F} d_i(x)$ and $T(x) = \{i : d_i(x) = \rho(x)\}$.

**Lemma 1 (nearest points are feet on nearest facets).** For $x$ in the interior of $P$, the distance from $x$ to the boundary is $\rho(x)$, and the nearest boundary points are the points $x + \rho(x) n_i$ for $i \in T(x)$, which are distinct. So $x$ has two or more nearest boundary points exactly when $|T(x)| \ge 2$.

*Proof.* Let $r = \rho(x) > 0$. For $z \in B(x, r)$ and every $i$, $n_i \cdot z \le n_i \cdot x + r \le b_i$, so the open ball lies in the interior of $P$ and every boundary point is at distance at least $r$. For $i \in T(x)$ the point $y = x + r n_i$ satisfies $d_i(y) = 0$ and $d_l(y) = d_l(x) - r\, n_l \cdot n_i \ge r - r = 0$ for every $l$, so $y \in P$ is on the plane of facet $i$, a boundary point at distance $r$. Conversely, let $z$ be a boundary point with $|z - x| = r$. Then $d_l(z) = 0$ for some $l$, and $r = |z - x| \ge n_l \cdot (z - x) = d_l(x) - d_l(z) = d_l(x) \ge r$. So $d_l(x) = r$, $l \in T(x)$, and equality in the Cauchy-Schwarz step gives $z - x = r n_l$. Distinct facets have distinct normals, so their feet are distinct. $\square$

**The lifted polytope.** Let $\hat{P} = \{(x, r) : r \ge 0 \text{ and } n_i \cdot x + r \le b_i \text{ for } i \in F\}$. It is bounded, since $x \in P$ and $0 \le r \le \rho(x)$, and it is the set of $(x, r)$ with $x \in P$ and $0 \le r \le \rho(x)$. Call $r \ge 0$ the *floor* constraint and $n_i \cdot x + r \le b_i$ the constraint of facet $i$; its row is $a_i = (n_i, 1)$. A face of $\hat{P}$ is the set where a given set of constraints is tight, and its dimension is $4$ minus the rank of the tight rows (for a face that is not empty).

**Lemma 2 (rank of tie rows).** For three distinct facets $i, j, k$, the rows $a_i, a_j, a_k$ are linearly independent, and $w = (n_i - n_j) \times (n_i - n_k) \ne 0$.

*Proof.* Three distinct unit vectors are not on one line, since a line meets the unit sphere in at most two points. So $n_i - n_j$ and $n_i - n_k$ are independent and $w \ne 0$. If $\alpha a_i + \beta a_j + \gamma a_k = 0$, the last coordinate gives $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 0$, so $\beta (n_j - n_i) + \gamma (n_k - n_i) = 0$ and $\beta = \gamma = 0 = \alpha$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (edges and vertices).** (a) A vertex of $\hat{P}$ with $r = 0$ is $(v, 0)$ for a vertex $v$ of $P$, and conversely. (b) An edge of $\hat{P}$ that is not in the floor has, at each relative interior point $(x, r)$, $r = \rho(x)$ and exactly the facet constraints of a set $S$ with $|S| \ge 3$ tight, and $S = T(x)$.

*Proof.* (a) The floor face is $P \times \{0\}$, whose faces are faces of $\hat{P}$, and its vertices are $(v, 0)$ for the vertices $v$ of $P$. (b) On the relative interior of an edge the tight set is constant and has rank 3. The floor is not tight there, so the tight set consists of facet constraints of some set $S$, and $|S| \ge 3$ for rank 3. Tightness gives $r = d_s(x)$ for $s \in S$, and $r \le d_l(x)$ for all $l$ gives $r = \rho(x)$ and $S = T(x)$. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (local tie sets).** Let $x \in P$ with $\rho = \rho(x)$ and $T = T(x)$, and let $v \ne 0$. For all small $t > 0$, $d_l(x + tv) = \rho - t\, n_l \cdot v$ for $l \in T$, and the facets of $T$ with the largest $n_l \cdot v$ are exactly the facets nearest to $x + tv$, provided $x + tv \in P$.

*Proof.* For $l \notin T$, $d_l(x) > \rho$, so by continuity $d_l(x + tv)$ stays larger than every $d_s(x + tv)$, $s \in T$, for small $t$. Among the facets of $T$ the smallest distance belongs to the largest $n_l \cdot v$. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (one step).** Let $z = (x, \rho(x))$ be a vertex of $\hat{P}$ and $T = T(x)$. The pairs $(w, S)$ that pass line L9 correspond one to one, up to the order in which they are found, to the edges of $\hat{P}$ at $z$ that are not in the floor. For each, line L11 returns the other end of the edge, which is a vertex of $\hat{P}$, and $S$ is the tie set on the open edge.

*Proof.* Let $e$ be an edge at $z$ not in the floor, and let $(x + tv, \rho(x) - t c)$, $0 \le t \le 1$, be a parametrization of it, with $v \ne 0$. (If $v = 0$ the edge would be vertical, but on an open vertical segment below the graph of $\rho$ no facet constraint is tight, and on one above it the point is not in $\hat{P}$.) By Lemma 3, the open edge has a constant tie set $S$ with $|S| \ge 3$, and by Lemma 4, $S$ is the set of $l \in T$ with the largest $n_l \cdot v$, and $c$ is that largest value. Take $i < j < k$ in $S$. Then $n_i \cdot v = n_j \cdot v = n_k \cdot v$, so $v$ is orthogonal to $n_i - n_j$ and $n_i - n_k$ and is a nonzero multiple of $w$ by Lemma 2. One of the two signs of line L7 makes $w$ a positive multiple of $v$. For it, line L8 computes $S$ and $r = n_i \cdot w$, and $i \in S$. If $\rho(x) = 0$, the edge leaves the floor, so its $r$ coordinate grows, and $r < 0$. So the pair passes line L9.

Conversely, let $(w, S)$ pass line L9, with $i, j, k \in S$. For small $t > 0$ the point $(x + tw, \rho(x) - tr)$ has $\rho(x) - tr > 0$, because either $\rho(x) > 0$ or $r < 0$. By Lemma 4 it lies in $\hat{P}$ on the graph of $\rho$, with exactly the facet constraints of $S$ tight, which have rank at least 3 by Lemma 2. So it is on an edge (a face of dimension at most 1 that contains a segment) not in the floor, and this edge contains $z$ and the direction $(w, -r)$. Two passing pairs with the same edge have parallel $w$ of the same sign, and line L11 records both under one key, so each edge is recorded once.

Along the edge, the facet $s \in S$ has $d_s = \rho(x) - tr$. A facet $l \notin S$ has $d_l(x + tw) = d_l(x) - t\, n_l \cdot w$, and $d_l(x) - \rho(x) \ge 0$, with equality only for $l \in T \setminus S$, where $n_l \cdot w < r$. So $l$ stays farther than $S$ for all $t > 0$ if $n_l \cdot w \le r$, and it becomes as near at $t = (d_l(x) - \rho(x)) / (n_l \cdot w - r) > 0$ otherwise. The floor is reached at $t = \rho(x) / r$ when $r > 0$. The edge ends at the first of these events, which is $t^*$ of line L10. The set is bounded, so some event happens. At the end point $y$ a new constraint is tight: the floor, whose row $(0, 0, 0, 1)$ has product $-r \ne 0$ with the direction $(w, -r)$, or a facet $l$, whose row has product $n_l \cdot w - r \ne 0$. All rows of $S$ have product 0 with the direction. So the new row is not in the span of the rows of $S$, the tight rows at $y$ have rank 4, and $y$ is a vertex. $\square$

**Lemma 6 (descent).** Every vertex $z$ of $\hat{P}$ with $r > 0$ is joined to a vertex in the floor by a path of edges of $\hat{P}$ that are not in the floor.

*Proof.* The linear function $f(x, r) = r$ has its minimum 0 over $\hat{P}$, so $z$ does not minimize it. At a vertex of a polytope that does not minimize a linear function, some edge at the vertex decreases the function, since the edge directions at a vertex generate the cone of feasible directions there. That edge starts at $r > 0$, so it is not in the floor. Its other end is a vertex with a smaller $r$. The polytope has finitely many vertices, so repeating this ends at a vertex with $r = 0$. $\square$

**Theorem.** TRACE-SEAMS terminates. The keys of $J$ are exactly the vertices of $P$ and the projections of the vertices of $\hat{P}$ with $r > 0$, each with its $\rho$ and $T$. The recorded seams are exactly the projections of the edges of $\hat{P}$ not in the floor, each with its tie set.

*Proof.* Every point pushed on $Q$ is a vertex of $\hat{P}$ projected: those of line L1 by Lemma 3(a), and those of line L11 by Lemma 5. Line L5 processes each point at most once, there are finitely many vertices, and each processed point pushes finitely many points, so the loop terminates. Every recorded seam is an edge by Lemma 5.

For completeness, consider the graph $G$ whose nodes are the vertices of $\hat{P}$ and whose arcs are the edges of $\hat{P}$ not in the floor. When a node is processed, Lemma 5 records all its arcs and pushes all its neighbours. So the processed nodes are closed under neighbours, and they include every floor vertex by line L1. By Lemma 6 every node is connected in $G$ to a floor vertex, so every node is processed. Every arc has a processed end, so every arc is recorded. $\square$

**Corollary (the medial surface).** An interior point $x$ of $P$ lies on the medial surface exactly when $|T(x)| \ge 2$, by Lemma 1, and then $(x, \rho(x))$ lies on a face of $\hat{P}$ of dimension at most 2 that is not in the floor. Such a face is a 2-face where the constraints of two facets $i$ and $j$ are tight (a sheet), or an edge (a seam), or a vertex (a junction). The sheet of $i$ and $j$ is the projection of the 2-face $\{r = d_i = d_j\} \cap \hat{P}$. This is a convex polygon whose vertices are vertices of $\hat{P}$ with $i, j \in T$, so it is the convex hull of the keys of $J$ whose tie sets contain $i$ and $j$. This is what `sheet_points` returns. If that 2-face has dimension below 2, the pair has no sheet.

**Corollary (size).** $\hat{P}$ is a 4-polytope with at most $|F| + 1$ facets. By the upper bound theorem, a $d$-polytope with $N$ facets has $O(N^{\lfloor d/2 \rfloor})$ faces, so $\hat{P}$ has $O(n^2)$ vertices, edges and 2-faces, and the medial surface of a convex polyhedron with $n$ facets has $O(n^2)$ junctions, seams and sheets.
