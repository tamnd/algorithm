---
title: Nef polygons are the unions of arrangement faces, are closed under Booleans and the topological operations, and the stored operations are exact
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

We use the notation and the pseudocode of the page. A *Nef polygon* is a subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$ obtained from finitely many open half-planes $\{a x + b y + c > 0\}$ by complement and intersection. Union and difference follow by De Morgan's laws. For a finite set $H$ of distinct lines, $\mathcal{A}(H)$ is its arrangement, whose faces are the classes of equal sign vectors, and a *union of faces* of $\mathcal{A}(H)$ is a set $\bigcup_{f \in M} f$ for some set $M$ of faces.

**Lemma 1 (faces are convex and the sign vector order is the closure order).** Each face of $\mathcal{A}(H)$ is a nonempty convex set, relatively open, of dimension 2 minus the rank of the lines on which it lies: a cell, an open edge or a vertex. For faces $f \ne g$, $f \subseteq \operatorname{cl}(g)$ exactly when $g_i = f_i$ for every $i$ with $f_i \ne 0$.

*Proof.* A face is the solution set of finitely many strict and non-strict linear constraints $s_i (a_i x + b_i y + c_i) > 0$ or $= 0$, so it is convex. It is relatively open in the affine set cut out by its equations. If $f \subseteq \operatorname{cl}(g)$ and $f_i \ne 0$, then a point of $f$ has a neighbourhood where line $i$ has the constant sign $f_i$, and this neighbourhood meets $g$, so $g_i = f_i$. Conversely, suppose $g$ agrees with $f$ where $f$ is nonzero. Take $p \in f$ and $q \in g$. For $t \in (0, 1]$, the point $p + t(q - p)$ has sign $g_i$ on every line: if $f_i = g_i \ne 0$, both ends have that sign; if $f_i = 0$, the value is $t$ times its value at $q$. So the half-open segment lies in $g$ and $p \in \operatorname{cl}(g)$. Since $f$ is convex and every point of it is in $\operatorname{cl}(g)$, $f \subseteq \operatorname{cl}(g)$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (local structure).** Let $p$ be a point of the face $f$. There is a disc $U$ around $p$ such that the faces that meet $U$ are exactly $f$ and the faces of its star, the faces $g$ with $f \subseteq \operatorname{cl}(g)$.

*Proof.* Let $U$ be a disc around $p$ that meets no line through which $p$ does not pass. On $U$ every line with $f_i \ne 0$ keeps the sign $f_i$, so every face that meets $U$ agrees with $f$ where $f$ is nonzero, and it is $f$ or in its star by Lemma 1. Conversely, every face $g$ of the star has $p$ in its closure (by the segment of the proof of Lemma 1), so it meets $U$. $\square$

**Theorem 1 (Nef polygons are the unions of faces).** A set is a Nef polygon exactly when it is a union of faces of $\mathcal{A}(H)$ for some finite $H$.

*Proof.* A Nef polygon is built from open half-planes with lines in some finite set $H$. An open half-plane over a line of $H$ is the union of the faces with sign $+1$ on that line. Unions of faces of a fixed arrangement are closed under complement (take the other faces) and intersection (take the common faces), because the faces partition the plane. So every Nef polygon over $H$ is a union of faces of $\mathcal{A}(H)$. Conversely, a face with sign vector $s$ is the intersection, over $i$, of the open half-plane $\{s_i (a_i x + b_i y + c_i) > 0\}$ if $s_i \neq 0$, and of the line $i$ if $s_i = 0$. A line is the complement of the union of its two open half-planes. So a face is a Nef polygon, and so is a finite union of faces. $\square$

**Theorem 2 (closure under the topological operations).** If $S$ is a union of faces of $\mathcal{A}(H)$, then so are $\operatorname{int} S$, $\operatorname{cl} S$, $\partial S$ and $\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int} S$. A face $f$ is in $\operatorname{int} S$ exactly when $f$ and its whole star are in $S$, and in $\operatorname{cl} S$ exactly when $f$ or some face of its star is in $S$. So Nef polygons are closed under interior, closure, boundary and regularization.

*Proof.* Let $p \in f$ and $U$ as in Lemma 2. If $f$ and its star are in $S$, then $U \subseteq S$, so $p \in \operatorname{int} S$. If some face of the star or $f$ is not in $S$, then every disc around $p$, shrunk inside $U$, meets that face (by Lemma 2 applied to smaller discs), so $p \notin \operatorname{int} S$. The closure is the complement of the interior of the complement, which gives the second rule. Both rules depend only on $f$, so the results are unions of faces. The boundary is $\operatorname{cl} S \setminus \operatorname{int} S$, and regularization composes the two. Theorem 1 turns these unions back into Nef polygons. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the arrangement in the code).** The constructor of `Arrangement` lists every face of $\mathcal{A}(H)$ once, and `up[f]` is exactly the star of $f$.

*Proof.* If $H$ is empty there is one face, the plane, with an empty star. Otherwise, every vertex lies on two lines that are not parallel and is found as their crossing. On line $i$, the vertices sorted by the parameter $p \cdot (-b_i, a_i)$ cut the line into open segments and two rays, or leave it whole if there are none. A segment between consecutive vertices crosses no other line, so it is one edge, and its midpoint, a point beyond each end, or any point of a line without vertices, gives its sign vector. Each edge has exactly one zero, since distinct canonical lines meet in at most one point, which would be a vertex. Every cell has an edge on its boundary, since $H$ is not empty and the cell is not the plane. The cells beside an edge on line $i$ are the sign vectors of the edge with $\pm 1$ at $i$. Both are realized by points near the middle of the edge, so every cell is listed. For the star: by Lemma 1, the faces above an edge $e$ are the two cells beside it. The faces above a vertex $v$ are the edges whose closure contains $v$, which are the edges with $v$ as an endpoint, and the cells whose closure contains $v$. Such a cell is a convex region with $v$ on its boundary, so it is a wedge between two consecutive lines through $v$ near $v$, and its boundary contains an edge that ends at $v$. So it is a cell of one of those edges. $\square$

**Theorem 3 (the operations are exact).** CONTAINS, COMBINE, INTERIOR, CLOSURE, BOUNDARY, the complement and `simplify` return the stated sets, and all of them terminate.

*Proof.* CONTAINS: $p$ lies in the face with sign vector $\sigma(p)$, which is marked exactly when that face is in the set. COMBINE: by canonical forms, $H_A \cup H_B$ has no duplicates, and for a face $f$ of the merged arrangement, every point of $f$ has the sign vector $f_A$ on the lines of $A$, so $f$ lies inside the face $f_A$ of $\mathcal{A}(H_A)$, and $f \subseteq A$ exactly when $f_A$ is marked. The same holds for $B$. So line L6 marks $f$ exactly when $f \subseteq \operatorname{op}(A, B)$, and since the faces partition the plane, the result is $\operatorname{op}(A, B)$. Complement: the faces partition the plane. Lines L8 to L10: Theorem 2 with Lemma 3. `simplify`: if the mark of every face depends only on its signs off line $i$, then the set is the union of the faces of $\mathcal{A}(H \setminus \{i\})$ that are projections of marked faces, since each face of the coarse arrangement is the union of the fine faces that project to it. So dropping line $i$ keeps the set. Each round removes a line, so there are at most $|H|$ rounds. Every loop runs over a finite list. $\square$

**Corollary (r-sets need regularization).** The r-sets, the closed regular sets $S = \operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int} S$, are not closed under intersection or complement. The closed half-planes $\{x \ge 0\}$ and $\{x \le 0\}$ are r-sets, but their intersection is the line $x = 0$, whose interior is empty, so $\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int}$ of it is empty and not the line. The complement of a nonempty bounded r-set is open and not closed, so not an r-set. The regularized operations $A \cap^* B = \operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int}(A \cap B)$ and $\complement^* A = \operatorname{cl}(\complement A)$ return r-sets, because $\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int}$ is idempotent. On one side, $\operatorname{int} X$ is an open subset of $\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int} X$, so $\operatorname{int} X \subseteq \operatorname{int}\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int} X$, and taking closures gives $\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int} X \subseteq \operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int}\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int} X$. On the other, $\operatorname{int}\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int} X \subseteq \operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int} X$, and taking closures gives $\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int}\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int} X \subseteq \operatorname{cl}\operatorname{int} X$. Nef polygons need no regularization, since by Theorems 1 and 2 each plain operation already returns a Nef polygon.
