---
title: The half-edge B-rep stores a closed oriented surface, and VALIDATE returns its true Euler-Poincaré counts
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

We use the pseudocode of the page. The input is a list of points and a list of faces, each face a list of loops, each loop a cyclic list of at least 3 distinct vertex indices. Write $\mathrm{tgt}(h) = origin[twin[h]]$ and $\sigma(h) = twin[prev[h]]$.

**Lemma 1 (BUILD).** If BUILD does not fail, then (a) $next$ and $prev$ are inverse permutations of the half-edges, and their cycles are exactly the loops of the input, in the input order; (b) $twin$ is an involution with no fixed point, and $\mathrm{tgt}(h) = origin[next[h]]$; (c) each undirected edge $\{u, v\}$ that occurs in a loop is used by exactly two half-edges, one in each direction.

*Proof.* Lines L2 and L3 make one half-edge for each position $i$ of each loop $(w_0, \ldots, w_{k-1})$, with origin $w_i$, and line L4 sets $next$ of position $i$ to position $i + 1 \bmod k$ and $prev$ to position $i - 1 \bmod k$ of the same loop. So on each loop, $next$ is a cyclic shift and $prev$ is its inverse, which gives (a). Line L3 fails if the key $(u, v)$ is already present, so if BUILD does not fail, each directed pair is the key of at most one half-edge. Line L5 sets $twin[h] = key[(v, u)]$ for the half-edge $h$ with key $(u, v)$, and fails if there is no such key. So $twin[h]$ has key $(v, u)$, its own twin has key $(u, v)$, which is $h$, and $twin[h] \ne h$ because $u \ne v$. The key of $h$ is $(origin[h], origin[next[h]])$, and the key of $twin[h]$ is the reverse, so $\mathrm{tgt}(h) = origin[twin[h]] = origin[next[h]]$. This is (b), and (c) follows because the directed pairs of $\{u, v\}$ each have exactly one half-edge. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (rotation).** In the structure of Lemma 1, $\sigma$ is a permutation of the half-edges, and it maps a half-edge out of $v$ to a half-edge out of $v$. If $h$ starts at $v$, then $face(h)$ and $face(\sigma(h))$ are the two faces of the edge of $prev[h]$, which has $v$ as an end.

*Proof.* $\sigma$ is the composition of the permutations $prev$ and $twin$, so it is a permutation. If $origin[h] = v$, then $prev[h]$ ends at $v$ by Lemma 1(b) applied to $prev[h]$, and its twin starts at $v$. The half-edge $prev[h]$ lies on the loop of $h$, so it has the face of $h$, and its twin $\sigma(h)$ has the other face of the same edge. $\square$

So the half-edges out of $v$ split into cycles of $\sigma$, and along a cycle consecutive faces share an edge at $v$. A cycle of $\sigma$ at $v$ is a fan of faces around $v$. Line L7 lists the cycle of $out[v]$, and it stops when it returns to $out[v]$, which it does because $\sigma$ is a permutation of a finite set.

**Lemma 3 (the surface is a closed oriented 2-manifold).** Assume that lines L8 and L9 pass, and let $X$ be the space made from the faces, each face a polygon with holes, glued along equal edges. Then $X$ is a closed 2-manifold, and the loop directions give it an orientation.

*Proof.* Each point inside a face has a disk neighborhood in that face. By Lemma 1(c), each edge is on exactly two face sides, so a point inside an edge has a neighborhood made of two half-disks glued along a segment, which is a disk. At a vertex $v$, the corners of the faces at $v$ are glued in the order of the cycles of $\sigma$ by Lemma 2. Line L9 checks that the cycle of $out[v]$ contains every half-edge out of $v$, so there is one cycle, and the corners form one disk around $v$. The surface is compact because it has finitely many faces. Each edge is used in opposite directions by its two faces, by Lemma 1(b), so the orientations of the faces that the loop directions give agree along each edge, and $X$ is oriented. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (the counts).** Assume in addition that each face is a disk with holes: its outer loop is a simple closed polygon and its rings are simple closed polygons inside it that do not meet each other. Then for each shell (component of $X$) the number $\chi = V - E + F - R$ of line L11 is its Euler characteristic, the shell has genus $g = (2 - \chi)/2$, and the $H$ that VALIDATE returns is the sum of the genera of the shells. Line L11 then passes, and $V - E + F - (L - F) - 2(S - H) = 0$.

*Proof.* Line L10 finds the components of the graph whose nodes are the faces and whose arcs join the two faces of each edge. Two faces in the same component are joined by a chain of faces that share edges, so they lie in one component of $X$. Conversely, a path in $X$ goes through faces and crosses from face to face only at edges or at vertices, and at a vertex the faces form one fan by Lemma 3, so consecutive faces of the path are joined by a chain of faces through edges. So line L10 finds the shells. In a face with $k$ rings, draw $k$ segments in the face, each from a vertex of a ring to a vertex of the outer loop, that do not cross. They cut the face into one disk, and they add $k$ edges and no vertex or face. After this for all faces, every face is a disk, and the Euler characteristic of the shell is $V - (E + R) + F$. A connected closed orientable surface has Euler characteristic $2 - 2g$ with $g \ge 0$ its genus, by the classification of surfaces. So $\chi \le 2$ and $\chi$ is even, line L11 passes, and it adds $g$ to $H$. Summing $V - E + F - R = 2 - 2g$ over the $S$ shells gives $V - E + F - R = 2S - 2H$, with $R = L - F$. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (volume).** Assume that $X$ is embedded in $E^3$, that is, the faces meet only along their shared edges and vertices, and that $X$ bounds the bounded set $Q$. Then $\frac{1}{6} \sum_f q_f \cdot \sum_{\ell \in f} N_\ell$ equals $\operatorname{vol}(Q)$ if every loop is oriented by the right-hand rule with the outward normal of $Q$, and $-\operatorname{vol}(Q)$ if every loop is reversed.

*Proof.* Take the vector field $F(p) = p / 3$, whose divergence is 1. By the divergence theorem, $\operatorname{vol}(Q) = \frac{1}{3} \oint_{\partial Q} p \cdot n \, dA$ with $n$ the outward unit normal. On a planar face $f$ with unit normal $n_f$, $p \cdot n_f$ has the same value $q_f \cdot n_f$ at every point $p$ of the face, so the integral over $f$ is $(q_f \cdot n_f) \operatorname{area}(f) = q_f \cdot A_f$, where $A_f = n_f \operatorname{area}(f)$ is the vector area. For a planar loop, $\frac{1}{2} N_\ell$ is its vector area, with the sign of the right-hand rule, and for a disk with holes whose rings turn the other way, $A_f = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{\ell \in f} N_\ell$. Summing over the faces gives $\operatorname{vol}(Q) = \frac{1}{6} \sum_f q_f \cdot \sum_\ell N_\ell$. If all loops are reversed, every $N_\ell$ changes sign. A cavity shell is oriented with its normals pointing into the cavity, away from $Q$, which is outward for $Q$, so it is counted with the outward sign. $\square$

Line L13 computes this sum with exact integers or fractions, so it decides the sign of the volume without rounding. Line L12 checks with exact arithmetic that $N_f \ne 0$, that every vertex $p$ of $f$ satisfies $N_f \cdot (p - q_f) = 0$, and that each ring $\ell$ has $N_f \cdot N_\ell < 0$, so each face is planar and its rings turn the other way from its outer loop.

**Theorem.** (a) BUILD stops after $O(n)$ steps and hash-map operations. If it does not fail, the queries return: FACE-BOUNDARY($f$) the loops of $f$ as given, EDGE-FACES($u$, $v$) the faces that use the edge from $u$ to $v$ and from $v$ to $u$, OUTGOING($v$) the cycle of $\sigma$ at $v$, ADJACENT-FACES($f$) the faces that share an edge with $f$, and SHELLS() the components of the face graph. (b) If VALIDATE returns, the surface is a closed, oriented 2-manifold made of planar faces, and the signed volume of its loops is positive. (c) If, in addition, the faces are disks with holes and the surface is embedded in $E^3$, then the surface is the boundary of a manifold solid $Q$, the returned counts satisfy $V - E + F - (L - F) - 2(S - H) = 0$ with $H$ the number of holes (the sum of the genera of the shells) of $Q$, and, if every shell is oriented outward, the returned volume is $\operatorname{vol}(Q)$.

*Proof.* (a) BUILD loops once over the positions of all loops and once over the keys, with $O(1)$ work and hash-map operations per half-edge. FACE-BOUNDARY walks $next$ from $loop\_edge[\ell]$ until it returns, which by Lemma 1(a) lists the loop $\ell$ in the input order. EDGE-FACES reads $key[(u, v)]$ and its twin, whose keys are $(u, v)$ and $(v, u)$ by Lemma 1. OUTGOING is the cycle of $\sigma$ by Lemma 2. ADJACENT-FACES collects $face(twin[h])$ over the half-edges of $f$, which are the faces across each edge of $f$ by Lemma 1(c), and SHELLS is a graph search on these arcs. (b) Lemma 3 gives the manifold and the orientation, and lines L12 and L13 check the planarity and the sign. (c) By the Jordan-Brouwer separation theorem, each component of an embedded closed surface cuts $E^3$ into a bounded part and an unbounded part. Let $Q$ be the set of points not in the unbounded part of an odd number of shells, that is, the points with odd nesting depth, with the surface added. It is bounded, closed and regular, and its boundary is $X$, so it is a manifold solid. Lemma 4 gives the counts, and Lemma 5 gives the volume when every shell is oriented outward. $\square$

The theorem does not claim that VALIDATE rejects every surface that crosses itself or every face whose rings lie outside its outer loop: those need geometric intersection tests, which the page lists as a pitfall. It also does not claim that a positive total volume means that every shell points outward. If the shells of a solid with a cavity are both oriented outward as seen from their own inside, the inner shell adds its volume instead of taking it away, and the total is still positive. Line L13 rejects a solid whose shells all point inward, and a shell with zero volume. A full check of the orientation of each inner shell needs a point-in-solid test against the shells that contain it.

**Termination.** Every loop of the pseudocode is a loop over a finite list, or a walk along a cycle of the permutation $next$ or $\sigma$ that stops when it returns to its start. So every operation stops, in the time that the page gives. $\blacksquare$
