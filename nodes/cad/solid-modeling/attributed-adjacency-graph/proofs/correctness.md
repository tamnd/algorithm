title: The algorithm computes the attributed adjacency graph exactly and reports the first matching template of each component
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

Let $B$ be a finite set of simple planar polygons with integer vertices that bound a solid $S$. Each polygon is listed counterclockwise when seen from outside $S$, every directed edge $(p, q)$ of a polygon occurs once, and its reverse $(q, p)$ occurs once, in another polygon. A *face* of $S$ is a maximal connected planar region of $\partial S$ with one outward normal, and the polygons of $B$ tile the faces. All arithmetic is on integers, so every sign is computed exactly.

**Lemma 1 (normals).** For a simple planar polygon $P$ listed counterclockwise when seen from the side of the unit normal $\nu$, the Newell vector $N_P$ equals $2 A \nu$, where $A > 0$ is the area of $P$.

*Proof.* The $x$ component $\sum_i (y_i - y_{i+1})(z_i + z_{i+1})$ is twice the signed area of the projection of $P$ to the $yz$ plane, by the trapezoid form of the shoelace formula, and the same holds for the other two components with the other two planes. The projection of a planar region of area $A$ to the plane orthogonal to the unit vector $e$ has signed area $A\, \nu \cdot e$, with the sign positive when the loop runs counterclockwise seen from $e$ and $\nu \cdot e > 0$. So $N_P = 2A(\nu \cdot e_1, \nu \cdot e_2, \nu \cdot e_3) = 2A\nu$. $\square$

By Lemma 1 and the orientation of $B$, $n_P = N_P$ is a positive multiple of the outward unit normal of $P$, and the signs at lines L2 and L3 are those of the unit normals.

**Lemma 2 (flat edges).** Let $(p, q)$ be an edge of $P$ whose reverse lies in $P'$, and let $u = q - p$. Then $n_P \times n_{P'}$ is parallel to $u$, and $s = 0$ if and only if $n_P$ and $n_{P'}$ are parallel. If $s = 0$ and $n_P \cdot n_{P'} > 0$, then $P$ and $P'$ lie in one plane, on the two sides of the edge, and belong to the same face. If $s = 0$ and $n_P \cdot n_{P'} < 0$, the solid has zero thickness or a crack of zero width along the edge.

*Proof.* The edge lies in both planes, so $u$ is orthogonal to both normals, and $n_P \times n_{P'}$, which is orthogonal to both normals as well, is a multiple of $u$ when the normals are not parallel; it is zero exactly when they are parallel. If the normals are parallel, the planes through the common edge with parallel normals coincide. With the same direction, seen from that one outward side, $P$ lies on the left of $(p, q)$ and $P'$ lies on the left of $(q, p)$, which is the other side of the edge line, so together they form a connected planar region with one normal. With opposite directions, the outward sides are opposite, so near the edge the material is either a half-plane fin of zero thickness between the two polygons or all of space except the slit between them. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the sign of an edge).** Let $(p, q)$ be an edge of $P$ shared with $P'$, with normals that are not parallel. The material angle of $S$ along the edge is less than $\pi$ if and only if $(n_P \times n_{P'}) \cdot (q - p) > 0$.

*Proof.* Scale so that $n = n_P$, $n' = n_{P'}$ and $u = (q - p)/|q - p|$ are unit vectors; this does not change the sign. Let $d = n \times u$. Seen from outside, $P$ runs counterclockwise, so $P$ lies on the left of the edge, which is the side of $d$; and $(d, n, u)$ is a right-handed orthonormal frame, since $d \times n = (n \times u) \times n = u$. As $n'$ is orthogonal to $u$, write $n' = \alpha d + \beta n$ with $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = 1$. Then $n \times n' = \alpha\, n \times d = -\alpha u$, so the sign of $(n \times n') \cdot u$ is the sign of $-\alpha$.

Work in the plane orthogonal to $u$ through a point of the edge, with coordinates $x$ along $d$ and $y$ along $n$. Face $P$ is the ray in direction $(1, 0)$, and the material is on its side $y < 0$, because $n$ points out. Polygon $P'$ holds the edge in the direction $-u$, so it lies in the direction $d' = n' \times (-u) = u \times n' = \alpha\, u \times d + \beta\, u \times n = \alpha n - \beta d$, which is the ray $(-\beta, \alpha)$. Its outward normal $(\alpha, \beta)$ is the ray turned clockwise by $\pi/2$, so the material lies on the counterclockwise side of the ray of $P'$. The material is thus the sector swept clockwise from the ray $(1, 0)$ to the ray $(-\beta, \alpha)$. Its angle is less than $\pi$ exactly when the second ray is in the open lower half plane, $\alpha < 0$; it equals $\pi$ when $\alpha = 0$, which Lemma 2 excludes, and exceeds $\pi$ when $\alpha > 0$. So the edge is convex if and only if $-\alpha > 0$. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (the graph).** After line L5, the merged groups are exactly the faces of $S$, and $G$ has an arc between two faces, labeled with the set of the labels of their common edges, exactly when they share an edge, with label 1 for a convex and 0 for a concave edge.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2, line L3 merges two polygons only when they are in one face, and so every merged group lies in one face. Conversely, let $P$ and $P'$ be polygons of one face $F$. The interior of $F$ is connected, and every point of it is in a polygon, on an edge of two polygons of $F$, or at a vertex whose neighborhood in $F$ is a disk covered by polygons of $F$ that meet in pairs along edges. So a path in the interior of $F$ from $P$ to $P'$ that avoids vertices crosses a chain of polygons, each sharing an edge with the next, and each such edge has $s = 0$ and $n_P \cdot n_{P'} > 0$, so line L3 merges the chain. An edge between polygons of different faces has non-parallel normals, again by Lemma 2, because two coplanar polygons with the same normal along an edge are in one face; by Lemma 3 its label is the convexity of the edge. Each edge of $S$ between two faces is a union of polygon edges, each counted with its label. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (matching).** MATCH$(t, C)$ returns true if and only if there is a bijection $\varphi$ from the nodes of $t$ to the nodes of $C$ such that, for all pairs $a \ne b$, the label of $\{a, b\}$ in $t$ equals the label of $\{\varphi(a), \varphi(b)\}$ in $C$, with "no arc" as a label of its own.

*Proof.* If the numbers of nodes differ, there is no bijection, and line L11 returns false. Otherwise, line L12 extends partial injective maps in a fixed order of the nodes of $t$, and line L13 keeps a partial map only if it preserves the labels of all mapped pairs. If the search returns true at line L14, the map covers all nodes, is injective between sets of the same size, so a bijection, and preserves all labels, because each pair was checked when its second node was mapped. Conversely, if $\varphi$ exists, each restriction of $\varphi$ to the first $j$ nodes of the order preserves the labels of its pairs and so passes line L13. The search tries every image for the $j$th node in turn, and reaches the restriction to the first $j + 1$ nodes unless it has already returned true. By induction on $j$, the search returns true. $\square$

**Theorem.** AAG-RECOGNIZE$(B, T)$ rejects $B$ if and only if $S$ has an edge where two faces meet with opposite normals. Otherwise it returns the attributed adjacency graph $G$ of $S$, and for each connected component $C$ of the graph $H$ obtained from $G$ by deleting the nodes all of whose arcs are labeled $\{1\}$, it reports the first template of $T$ that is isomorphic to $C$ as a labeled graph, or reports $C$ as unrecognized.

*Proof.* Line L4 runs exactly at an edge with $s = 0$ and $n_P \cdot n_{P'} < 0$, which by Lemma 2 is a knife edge. Otherwise, by Lemma 4, $G$ is the attributed adjacency graph. Line L6 deletes exactly the nodes all of whose arcs are convex, and line L7 visits each component of the rest once. For a component $C$, line L8 tries the templates in order and line L9 stops at the first $t$ for which MATCH returns true, which by Lemma 5 is the first template isomorphic to $C$; if there is none, line L10 reports $C$. Every quantity is an integer, so no step depends on rounding. $\blacksquare$

**What is not claimed.** The theorem says that the method computes the components and matches that Joshi and Chang define. It does not say that these are the features a process planner wants. That depends on the templates and on how the features interact, and it is judged on test parts, not proved.
