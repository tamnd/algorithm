---
title: ADD-FACE keeps the faces at each edge in counterclockwise order, and radial joins the uses that face the same wedge
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

We use the pseudocode and the records of the page. All points are integer vectors. For an edge $e$ write $d = v_1[e] - v_0[e] \ne 0$. For a face $f$ with normal $N_f \ne 0$ at $e$, let $(p, q)$ be the direction in which $f^+$ runs along $e$, and let the fin be $w_f = N_f \times (q - p)$. Write $[a, b] = \det[a, b, d] = (a \times b) \cdot d$.

**Lemma 1 (fins).** $w_f$ is a nonzero vector perpendicular to $d$, and it points from the edge into $f$.

*Proof.* $f$ is planar with normal $N_f$, and the edge lies in $f$, so $N_f \cdot (q - p) = 0$. The cross product of two nonzero perpendicular vectors is nonzero and perpendicular to both, so $w_f \ne 0$ and $w_f \cdot d = 0$. Seen from the tip of $N_f$, the loop of $f^+$ runs counterclockwise, so the face is on the left of each side, and the left of $q - p$ in the plane with normal $N_f$ is the direction $N_f \times (q - p)$. $\square$

So all fins at $e$ lie in the plane $P$ through the origin perpendicular to $d$. Orient $P$ so that $d$ points toward the viewer. For $a, b \in P$, $a \times b$ is parallel to $d$, and $[a, b] = |a| \, |b| \, |d| \sin \theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle that turns $a$ to $b$ counterclockwise about $d$.

**Lemma 2 (wedge test).** Let $a, b, w \in P$ be nonzero, with $w$ in the direction of neither $a$ nor $b$. The wedge test of the page (the function inside_wedge of the code) returns true exactly when $w$ lies in the open wedge that turns counterclockwise about $d$ from $a$ to $b$, the whole turn if $a$ and $b$ have the same direction.

*Proof.* Let $\alpha \in [0, 2\pi)$ be the angle from $a$ to $b$ and $\omega \in (0, 2\pi)$ the angle from $a$ to $w$; $\omega \ne 0$ because $w$ is not in the direction of $a$. The claim is that INSIDE is true exactly when $\omega < \alpha$, or when $\alpha = 0$. If $\alpha = 0$ the function returns true. If $0 < \alpha < \pi$, then $[a, b] > 0$, and $\omega < \alpha$ holds exactly when $\sin \omega > 0$ and $\sin(\alpha - \omega) > 0$: if $\omega < \alpha$ both angles are in $(0, \pi)$, and if $\omega > \alpha$ either $\omega \ge \pi$, so $\sin \omega \le 0$, or $\alpha - \omega \in (-\pi, 0)$, so the second sine is negative. These are the signs of $[a, w]$ and $[w, b]$. If $\alpha = \pi$, then $[a, b] = 0$ with $a \cdot b < 0$, and $\omega < \pi$ exactly when $[a, w] > 0$, since $\omega \ne \pi$. If $\pi < \alpha < 2\pi$, then $[a, b] < 0$, and $\omega < \alpha$ fails exactly when $w$ is in the open wedge from $b$ to $a$, which is less than a half turn, so the first case decides it with $a$ and $b$ swapped. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (which side faces which wedge).** Let $x$ be an edgeuse of a face $f$ at $e$ that runs from $v_0$ to $v_1$. Then the side of the faceuse of $x$ faces the wedge counterclockwise of $w_f$ about $d$, and the side of $mate[x]$ faces the wedge clockwise of it.

*Proof.* Let $s = +1$ if $f^+$ runs from $v_0$ to $v_1$ and $s = -1$ if not, so $q - p = s \, d$ and $w_f = s \, N_f \times d$. The direction a quarter turn counterclockwise of $w_f$ about $d$ is $d \times w_f / |d|$, and $d \times (N_f \times d) = N_f |d|^2 - d (d \cdot N_f) = N_f |d|^2$ by the expansion of the double cross product and $N_f \cdot d = 0$. So $d \times w_f = s |d|^2 N_f$: the side of $N_f$ faces the counterclockwise wedge if $s = +1$, and the side of $-N_f$ faces it if $s = -1$. If $s = +1$, then $x$ runs from $v_0$ to $v_1$ like $f^+$, so $x$ is on $f^+$, the side of $N_f$. If $s = -1$, then $f^+$ runs from $v_1$ to $v_0$ and $x$, which runs the other way, is on $f^-$, the side of $-N_f$. In both cases the side of $x$ faces the counterclockwise wedge. The side of $mate[x]$ is the other side of $f$, which faces the clockwise wedge. $\square$

**Invariant R.** For each edge $e$ with $k \ge 1$ faces, let $x_0 = eu[e]$ and $x_{i+1} = mate[radial[x_i]]$. Then (a) $x_0, \ldots, x_{k-1}$ are the edgeuses of $e$ that run from $v_0$ to $v_1$, one for each face at $e$, and $x_k = x_0$; (b) their fins are in strictly counterclockwise order about $d$: no fin at $e$ lies in the open wedge from the fin of $x_i$ to the fin of $x_{i+1}$; (c) $radial[x_i] = mate[x_{i+1}]$ and $radial[mate[x_{i+1}]] = x_i$. For a wire edge, its two edgeuses are the mate and the radial of each other. Also (d) for each face $f$, the edgeuses of $f^+$ form one cycle under $next$ in the order of the vertex list, those of $f^-$ form the reverse cycle, and $mate$ joins the use of each side of $f$ on $f^+$ with the use of the same side on $f^-$.

**Lemma 4 (RADIAL-FACES).** Under R, lines L1 to L4 output the faces at $e$ once each in counterclockwise order and stop after $k$ steps.

*Proof.* Line L3 maps $x_i$ to $mate[radial[x_i]] = x_{i+1}$ by the definition in R, and by (a) the $x_i$ are $k$ different edgeuses on $k$ different faces with $x_k = x_0$. The order is (b). $\square$

By (c) and Lemma 3, $radial$ joins $x_i$, which faces the wedge from $F_i$ to $F_{i+1}$, with $mate[x_{i+1}]$, which faces the wedge clockwise of $F_{i+1}$, that is the same wedge. So radial joins exactly the pairs of uses that face the same wedge, and SHELLS joins faceuses that bound the same region near an edge.

**Lemma 5 (ADD-FACE).** If R holds and ADD-FACE($f$) does not fail, then R holds after it, and no field of a face other than $f$ changes except four radial pointers at each edge of $f$ that already had faces.

*Proof.* Line L5 makes the edgeuses of $f^+$ and $f^-$ with the loops and mates of (d), and these fields are not changed later. Take a side of $f$ with edge $e$, and let $x$ be the new use that runs from $v_0$ to $v_1$ and $y = mate[x]$. If $e$ is new, line L9 sets $radial[x] = y$ and $radial[y] = x$, and $eu[e] = x$, so R holds for $e$ with $k = 1$: $x_1 = mate[radial[x]] = mate[y] = x$. If $e$ is a wire edge, ADD-FACE fails before any change. Otherwise $e$ has faces $F_0, \ldots, F_{k-1}$ in the order of R, and the check before the changes makes sure that the new fin $w$ (Lemma 1) has none of their directions. The open wedges from $F_i$ to $F_{i+1}$ for $i = 0, \ldots, k-1$ cover $P$ minus the $k$ rays of the fins, without overlap, by (b); for $k = 1$ the one wedge is the whole turn minus one ray. So exactly one wedge contains $w$, and by Lemma 2 the test of line L11 is true for it and false for the others. Line L11 visits $x_0, x_1, \ldots$ by Lemma 4, so it stops at the $u = x_i$ of that wedge within $k$ steps. Line L12 sets, with $z = radial[x_i] = mate[x_{i+1}]$, the four pointers $radial[x_i] = y$, $radial[y] = x_i$, $radial[x] = z$, $radial[z] = x$. Now the sequence of R is $x_0, \ldots, x_i, x, x_{i+1}, \ldots, x_{k-1}$: $mate[radial[x_i]] = mate[y] = x$ and $mate[radial[x]] = mate[z] = x_{i+1}$, and the other steps did not change. This gives (a) with $k + 1$ uses and (c). For (b), the wedge from $F_i$ to $F_{i+1}$ contained no fin other than $w$, so the two new wedges, from $F_i$ to $f$ and from $f$ to $F_{i+1}$, contain none, and the other wedges did not change. $eu[e]$ did not change and still runs from $v_0$ to $v_1$. The sides of $f$ are on different edges, because the vertices of $f$ are distinct, so each edge gets exactly one new face, and the steps for different sides change different edges. $\square$

**Theorem.** After any sequence of ADD-WIRE and ADD-FACE calls that do not fail, RADIAL-FACES($e$) outputs the faces at each edge $e$ once each in counterclockwise order about $v_1[e] - v_0[e]$, $radial$ joins exactly the edgeuses that face the same wedge, FACE-VERTICES and FACE-EDGES give the loop of each face, and each call stops. A failed call changes nothing.

*Proof.* R holds for the empty structure. ADD-WIRE makes a new edge with the pointers of R and fails before any change on an edge that exists. ADD-FACE keeps R by Lemma 5, and it checks the size, the distinct vertices, the area, the planarity, the wire edges and the fin directions before it changes anything. So R holds after each call by induction. Lemma 4 gives the output of RADIAL-FACES, the remark after it gives the radial claim, and (d) gives the face loops. Every loop in the code runs over a finite cycle or a finite list. $\square$

The tests check the same facts against a brute force that keeps only the face lists. For each edge it finds the faces by a scan and sorts their fins by an exact pseudo-angle with fractions, in a basis of the plane $P$, which is a different method from the wedge test. For random sets of unit cubes in a $3 \times 3 \times 3$ grid, with the faces inserted in a random order, it also finds the shells by walking the cells of the grid around each edge, and compares them with SHELLS. $\blacksquare$
