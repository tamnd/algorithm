title: Subdivision loses no intersection point and ends for regular patches
proves: [correctness]

Let $P$ and $Q$ be tensor-product Bézier patches of degrees $(p, q)$ and $(p', q')$ over $[0, 1]^2$. A *piece* of $P$ is a pair $(N, R)$ of a control net and a closed rectangle $R = [u_0, u_1] \times [v_0, v_1]$; it *represents* $P$ on $R$ if the patch of $N$ at $(a, b) \in [0, 1]^2$ equals $P(u_0 + a(u_1 - u_0), v_0 + b(v_1 - v_0))$. A *witness* for a pair of pieces $(N_P, R_P)$, $(N_Q, R_Q)$ is a quadruple $(u, v, s, t)$ with $(u, v) \in R_P$, $(s, t) \in R_Q$ and $P(u, v) = Q(s, t)$. The arithmetic is exact, so every comparison in SUBDIVIDE is decided correctly.

**Lemma 1 (quarters).** If $(N, R)$ represents $P$ on $R$, the four quarters computed by L3 represent $P$ on the four quarters of $R$.

*Proof.* For a Bézier curve, the two halves of the de Casteljau scheme at $t = 1/2$ are the control polygons of the curve on $[0, 1/2]$ and on $[1/2, 1]$, each reparametrized over $[0, 1]$; this is the subdivision property of the [de Casteljau algorithm](/de-casteljau-algorithm/). Write the patch as $\sum_i B_i(a) c_i(b)$, with $c_i(b) = \sum_j B_j(b) N_{ij}$. For fixed $b$ the patch is a Bézier curve in $a$ with control points $c_i(b)$, and these depend linearly on the columns $N_{\cdot j}$, so splitting every column at $a = 1/2$ splits the patch in $a$. Splitting every row of each half at $b = 1/2$ splits it in $b$ in the same way. Composing the affine maps of the parameters gives the claim. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (hull).** If $(N, R)$ represents $P$ on $R$, then $P(R)$ lies in the convex hull of the points of $N$, and so in box$(N)$.

*Proof.* The Bernstein polynomials of each degree are nonnegative on $[0, 1]$ and sum to one, so the products $B_i(a) B_j(b)$ are nonnegative and sum to one, and each value of the patch is a convex combination of the control points. The convex hull of a finite set lies in its axis-aligned box. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (no witness is dropped).** If a pair of pieces has a witness $(u, v, s, t)$, then line L1 keeps the pair, and if the pair is split at L3 or L5, one of the new pairs has the same witness.

*Proof.* The point $x = P(u, v) = Q(s, t)$ lies in box$(N_P)$ and in box$(N_Q)$ by Lemma 2, so the boxes share $x$ and overlap; L1 counts touching as overlap, so it keeps the pair. The four quarters of $R_P$ cover $R_P$, so one of them contains $(u, v)$, and by Lemma 1 it is a piece that represents $P$ there. The same holds for $Q$ at L5. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (flat pieces).** Assume $P_u \times P_v \ne 0$ on $[0, 1]^2$. There is a constant $C$ such that, for every piece of $P$ over a square of side $h \le 1$ made by Lemma 1, every control point lies within $C h^2$ of the plane through the corners $N_{00}$, $N_{p0}$ and $N_{0q}$, and these corners are not collinear when $h$ is small.

*Proof.* Let the square be $[\alpha, \alpha + h] \times [\beta, \beta + h]$, and let $\mathcal{B}$ be the blossom of $P$, the unique function that is symmetric and affine in each of its $p$ first and $q$ last arguments and agrees with $P$ on the diagonal. The control points of the piece are the blossom values $N_{ij} = \mathcal{B}(\alpha^{\langle p - i \rangle}, (\alpha + h)^{\langle i \rangle}; \beta^{\langle q - j \rangle}, (\beta + h)^{\langle j \rangle})$. Expand $\mathcal{B}$ around $(\alpha, \ldots; \beta, \ldots)$. Since it is a polynomial of degree one in each argument, $N_{ij} = P(\alpha, \beta) + \tfrac{i h}{p} P_u + \tfrac{j h}{q} P_v + E_{ij}$, where the partial derivatives are taken at $(\alpha, \beta)$ and $|E_{ij}| \le C_1 h^2$, with $C_1$ a bound on the mixed second partial derivatives of $\mathcal{B}$ over $[0, 1]$, which is finite because $\mathcal{B}$ is a polynomial. So every control point is within $C_1 h^2$ of the tangent plane $\Pi$ at $(\alpha, \beta)$. Let $m > 0$ be the minimum of $|P_u \times P_v|$ on $[0, 1]^2$ and let $\nu$ be the unit normal of $\Pi$. The three corners are $a = P(\alpha, \beta)$, $b = a + h P_u + O(h^2)$ and $c = a + h P_v + O(h^2)$, so $(b - a) \times (c - a) = h^2 (P_u \times P_v) + O(h^3)$. Its length is at least $h^2 m - O(h^3) > 0$ for small $h$, so the corners are not collinear, and its unit vector $\nu'$ satisfies $|\nu' - \nu| = O(h)$. For each control point, $N_{ij} - a = \tfrac{ih}{p} P_u + \tfrac{jh}{q} P_v + E_{ij}$ has length $O(h)$, and $\nu \cdot (N_{ij} - a) = \nu \cdot E_{ij} = O(h^2)$. Hence $|\nu' \cdot (N_{ij} - a)| \le |\nu \cdot (N_{ij} - a)| + |\nu' - \nu| \, |N_{ij} - a| = O(h^2)$. All $O$ constants depend only on bounds of the derivatives of $P$ and on $m$, so they are uniform over all squares. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (termination).** Under the depth cap, SUBDIVIDE ends on every input. If both patches satisfy the assumption of Lemma 4, it also ends without the cap, and every output leaf is flat.

*Proof.* With the cap, every path of the recursion has length at most the cap, and each call makes at most four calls, so the tree is finite. Without the cap, let $k_0$ be such that $C 4^{-k_0} \le \varepsilon$ and the corners are not collinear at side $2^{-k_0}$, for both patches (Lemma 4). Every piece is a square of side $2^{-j}$ after $j$ splits of its patch, and a piece with $j \ge k_0$ is flat. Line L2 splits $P$ only when $P$ is not flat, and L4 splits $Q$ only when $Q$ is not flat. So along a path, $P$ is split at most $k_0$ times and $Q$ at most $k_0$ times, the depth is at most $2 k_0$, and the tree is finite. A call that reaches L6 has two flat pieces, so its leaf is flat. $\square$

**Theorem.** SUBDIVIDE$(P, Q, \varepsilon)$ ends, and for every intersection point $P(u, v) = Q(s, t)$ there is an output leaf $(R_P, R_Q)$ with $(u, v) \in R_P$ and $(s, t) \in R_Q$. If both patches are regular, the leaves are flat without the depth cap, and every intersection point $x$ of a leaf lies within $\varepsilon$ of the corner planes of both pieces.

*Proof.* Termination is Lemma 5. The root pair, the two patches over $[0, 1]^2$, has every intersection point as a witness. By Lemma 3, a pair with a witness is never dropped at L1, and when it is split, the witness passes to a child. The recursion is finite, so following the witness from the root ends at a call that neither drops the pair nor splits it, and that call outputs a leaf, at L7 or at the depth cap; that leaf has the witness. For the last claim, the control points of a flat piece lie in the closed slab of half width $\varepsilon$ around its corner plane, and the slab is convex, so the convex hull of the control points, and by Lemma 2 the piece, lie in the slab. An intersection point of the leaf is on both pieces, so it is in both slabs. $\blacksquare$

**What is not claimed.** The theorem does not say that a leaf holds an intersection point: boxes are larger than the pieces, so a leaf can be empty. It does not bound the distance from the segments of line L6 to the curve; on the example of the page that distance is measured, a quarter of $\varepsilon$ in $|xy - 1/4|$. And it does not say that the leaves of a regular input are few: if the patches touch along a curve, the number of leaves grows like $\varepsilon^{-1}$. The proof needs exact comparisons at L1: with boxes that are rounded inward, Lemma 3 fails.
