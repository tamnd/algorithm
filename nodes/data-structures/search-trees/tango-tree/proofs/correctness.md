---
title: Tango tree access finds the key and keeps the preferred paths
proves: [correctness, invariant, termination]
status: full
---

Write $P$ for the reference tree, $I(y)$ for the key interval of the subtree of $y$ in $P$, and $\mathrm{pc}(y)$ for the preferred child of $y$ (a child of $y$ or none). A *path* is a chain $v_0, v_1, \ldots, v_t$ with $v_{j+1} = \mathrm{pc}(v_j)$ and $\mathrm{pc}(v_t)$ none or missing, where $v_0$ is the root of $P$ or a child that its parent does not prefer. The invariant (T1, T2) says the stored paths are exactly these chains, as sorted key sets, indexed by their tops.

**Lemma 1.** The paths partition the nodes of $P$. For a path $A$ with top $v_0$, all keys of $A$ lie in $I(v_0)$, and for each $j$, the nodes of $A$ below $v_j$ are exactly the nodes of $A$ with keys in $I(\mathrm{pc}(v_j))$.

*Proof.* Each node $y$ is on exactly one path: follow parents up while the parent prefers the current node; the node where this stops is the top, and $y$ is on its chain. The keys of a chain from $v_0$ are in $I(v_0)$. The nodes below $v_j$ on the chain are in the subtree of $\mathrm{pc}(v_j)$, and the nodes $v_0, \ldots, v_j$ are ancestors of $\mathrm{pc}(v_j)$, whose keys are not in $I(\mathrm{pc}(v_j))$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (exit node).** Let $A$ be a path with top $v_0$, with $x \in I(v_0)$ and $x$ not on $A$. Let $u$ be the deepest node of $A$ with $x \in I(u)$, and $c$ the child of $u$ on the side of $x$. Then $u$ is the deeper of the predecessor and the successor of $x$ among the keys of $A$, $c$ exists, $c$ is not preferred by $u$, and $c$ is the top of a path with $x \in I(c)$.

*Proof.* $u$ exists since $x \in I(v_0)$. Say $x < u$; the other case is symmetric. Then $x \in I(c)$ for the left child $c$ of $u$, which exists since $I(c) = [\min I(u), u - 1]$ holds $x$. If $\mathrm{pc}(u) = c$, then $c$ is on $A$ and is deeper than $u$ with $x \in I(c)$, which contradicts the choice of $u$; so $c$ is not preferred and is the top of a path. The nodes of $A$ in $I(u)$ are $u$ and the nodes below $u$, which are in $I(\mathrm{pc}(u))$ by Lemma 1; that is the right subtree of $u$ (or empty). So no key of $A$ is in $(x, u)$, and $u$ is the successor of $x$ on $A$. The predecessor, if any, is a key of $A$ smaller than $x$, hence outside $I(u)$, hence an ancestor of $u$: shallower. $\square$

**Termination and the result of the search.** At the start, $A$ holds the root, and $x \in I(\text{root})$. By Lemma 2, each round of lines L5 to L7 moves to a path whose top $c$ has $x \in I(c)$ and is deeper than before. The depth is bounded, so the loop stops, and it stops only at line L4, when $x$ is on $A$. The pairs $(u, c)$ recorded are exactly the nodes $u$ on the root-to-$x$ path of $P$ whose preferred child is not on the side of $x$, with $c$ the child on the side of $x$: on each path visited, the nodes of the root-to-$x$ path above $u$ have their preferred child toward $x$ (they are followed by the next node of $A$, which is on the way to $x$ because $u$ is below them with $x \in I(u)$).

**Updates.** Take the pairs from the top down. Before the update for $(u, c)$, the path that holds $u$ contains the root of $P$ (by induction: the first $u$ is on the root path, and each join adds the next path below the previous $u$). By Lemma 1, CUT removes exactly the nodes of that path below $u$; they form the chain from the old $\mathrm{pc}(u)$, whose parent will no longer prefer it, so they form a path with that top, as (T2) needs. JOIN adds the path with top $c$, whose keys are in $I(c)$, a range that holds no other key of the upper part (it is in the subtree of $u$, and the upper part has no node below $u$ now). After setting $\mathrm{pc}(u) = c$, the chain from the root follows $c$ and then the chain of $c$, which is the joined path, and $c$ is no longer a top. So (T1) and (T2) hold after each pair. Line L11 does the same at $x$ with the left child. After the access, every node $y$ on the root-to-$x$ path has $\mathrm{pc}(y)$ on the side of $x$, and $\mathrm{pc}(x)$ is left, which is the definition of preferred children after an access to $x$; all other preferred children are unchanged, as the definition requires, since the most recent access in their subtrees is unchanged. $\blacksquare$
