---
title: The HTN decomposition planner terminates, and the plans it can reach are exactly the solutions within the depth bound
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

We use the pseudocode and the definitions of the page. Fix a domain and a bound $d$. A task network $w$ has a finite set of nodes, each with a task and a depth, and a strict partial order $\prec$ on them. The implementation stores a set of pairs; its transitive closure is the order meant here, and we check below that L5 and L9 compute the right closure.

**Marked decomposition.** To decompose node $u$ by method $\mu$ *with a marker*, do the decomposition of the page and also add a node $x$, the *marker*, labelled $\mathit{pre}(\mu)$. The marker inherits every ordering of $u$, like the subtasks, and it precedes every subtask. A *full expansion* of $w$ is a network $W$ that arises from $w$ by a finite sequence of marked decompositions, each of a compound node of depth less than $d$, such that $W$ has no compound node. An *execution* of $W$ from a state $s$ is a linearization $(y_1, \ldots, y_r)$ of $W$ with states $z_0 = s, z_1, \ldots, z_r$ such that: if $y_i$ is a primitive node with operator $o$, then $\mathit{pre}(o) \subseteq z_{i-1}$ and $z_i = (z_{i-1} \setminus \mathit{del}(o)) \cup \mathit{add}(o)$; if $y_i$ is a marker labelled $p$, then $p \subseteq z_{i-1}$ and $z_i = z_{i-1}$. Its *plan* is the sequence of operators of its primitive nodes. Let $\mathrm{Sol}(s, w)$ be the set of plans of executions from $s$ of full expansions of $w$. Let $\mathrm{Reach}(s, w)$ be the set of plans at the leaves of the search tree of HTN-PLAN$(s, w)$, where every choice at L2 and L8 is explored; the pseudocode returns the first of them in backtracking order.

**Lemma 1 (the order of an expansion).** Let $W$ arise from $w$ by marked decompositions. Form the forest whose roots are the nodes of $w$ and in which the children of a decomposed node are its marker and its subtasks; the nodes of $W$ are its leaves. For two leaves $y \ne y'$, let $a, a'$ be the distinct nodes on their root paths just below their lowest common ancestor $c$, or their roots if they have none. Then $y \prec y'$ in $W$ if and only if $a \prec a'$ in the local order: the order of $w$ if $a, a'$ are roots, and otherwise the order of the method network that decomposed $c$, with its marker before all of its subtasks. In particular, $W$ does not depend on the order in which the decompositions were made.

*Proof.* By induction on the number of decompositions. With none, $a = y$ and $a' = y'$ are roots, and the claim is the definition. Suppose it holds for $W$, and decompose a leaf $z$ by $\mu$, with children $C$ (the marker and the subtasks). Pairs between two old leaves keep their $a, a'$, and they keep their order: a path through $z$ in the old order is replaced by a path through a node of $C$, which inherits both ends; $C$ is never empty, as it holds the marker. For $c \in C$ and an old leaf $y'$, the lowest common ancestor of $c$ and $y'$ is that of $z$ and $y'$, and the node below it on the path of $c$ is that of $z$, so the rule gives $c \prec y'$ exactly when $z \prec y'$, which is what inheritance gives, and likewise for $y' \prec c$. For $c, c' \in C$, the lowest common ancestor is $z$, and the rule gives the order of $\mu$ with the marker first, which is what the decomposition adds. The rule depends only on the forest and the local orders, so the order of the decompositions does not matter. $\square$

The same argument with $C$ the subtasks alone shows that an unmarked decomposition, as in L9, gives the order of the rule for the forest without markers, provided $C$ is not empty. If $C$ is empty, the implementation adds every pair from a predecessor of $z$ to a successor of $z$, so no path through $z$ is lost. Removal at L5 adds the same pairs; for a node with no predecessor it adds nothing, and the order of $w$ without $u$ is the restriction of the order of $w$.

**Lemma 2 (the first element).** Let $(y_1, \ldots, y_r)$ be an execution of a full expansion $W$ of $w$, with $r \ge 1$. Then either $y_1$ is a primitive node $u$ of $w$, or it is the marker of the decomposition of a compound node $u$ of $w$; in both cases $u$ has no predecessor in $w$.

*Proof.* Let $u$ be the root of $y_1$ in the forest of Lemma 1. If $v \prec u$ in $w$, take any leaf $y$ below $v$; one exists, since every decomposed node has its marker as a child. By Lemma 1 $y \prec y_1$, so $y_1$ is not first. Hence $u$ has no predecessor. If $u$ is primitive, it was never decomposed, and $y_1 = u$. Otherwise let $x$ be the marker of $u$. For any other leaf $y$ below $u$, the nodes below $u$ on the paths of $x$ and $y$ are $x$ and a subtask, so $x \prec y$ by Lemma 1. Hence $y_1 = x$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (termination).** HTN-PLAN visits finitely many calls.

*Proof.* Each call removes one node of its network, at L5 or by decomposition at L9, and adds only nodes of greater depth. No node is removed twice, so a branch has at most as many calls as nodes created on it. A node of depth $i$ is created only from a node of depth $i - 1 < d$, so depths stay at most $d$, and with $t$ nodes in $w_0$ and at most $k$ subtasks per method, a branch creates at most $t (1 + k + \cdots + k^d)$ nodes. Each call has finitely many children, so by König's lemma the search tree is finite. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (soundness).** $\mathrm{Reach}(s, w) \subseteq \mathrm{Sol}(s, w)$.

*Proof.* By induction on the height of the search tree below the call, which is finite by Lemma 3. If $w$ is empty, L1 gives the empty plan, which is the plan of the empty execution of $W = w$. Otherwise a plan of $\mathrm{Reach}(s, w)$ comes through one of two branches.

*L5 with a primitive node $u$ and operator $o$.* The plan is $o \cdot \pi'$ with $\pi' \in \mathrm{Reach}(s', w - u)$ and $s' = (s \setminus \mathit{del}(o)) \cup \mathit{add}(o)$. By induction $\pi'$ is the plan of an execution $\lambda'$ of a full expansion $W'$ of $w - u$. Apply the same decompositions to $w$; the result is $W = W' + u$, and by Lemma 1 its order restricted to $W'$ is the order of $W'$, since the local orders agree. The node $u$ has no predecessor in $w$, so by Lemma 1 none in $W$. So $u \cdot \lambda'$ is a linearization of $W$. It is an execution from $s$, because $\mathit{pre}(o) \subseteq s$ by L4.

*L9 with a compound node $u$ and method $\mu$.* The plan is in $\mathrm{Reach}(s, w')$, with $w'$ the result of decomposing $u$ by $\mu$; by L7 the depth of $u$ is less than $d$. By induction it is the plan of an execution $\lambda'$ of a full expansion $W'$ of $w'$. Decompose $u$ by $\mu$ with a marker $x$, then make the decompositions that gave $W'$; the result is $W = W' + x$. By Lemma 1, the order of $W$ restricted to $W'$ is the order of $W'$, and $x$ has no predecessor, since $u$ has none. So $x \cdot \lambda'$ is a linearization of $W$, and it is an execution from $s$ with the same plan, because $\mathit{pre}(\mu) \subseteq s$ by L8 and a marker does not change the state. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (completeness).** $\mathrm{Sol}(s, w) \subseteq \mathrm{Reach}(s, w)$.

*Proof.* By induction on the number of nodes of the full expansion. Let $\lambda = (y_1, \ldots, y_r)$ be an execution of a full expansion $W$ of $w$ from $s$. If $r = 0$, then $w$ is empty, since every node of $w$ has a leaf below it, and L1 returns the empty plan. Otherwise apply Lemma 2.

*$y_1$ is a primitive node $u$ of $w$ with operator $o$.* Then $u$ has no predecessor, so L2 can choose it; L3 sees that it is primitive, and L4 accepts it, since $\mathit{pre}(o) \subseteq z_0 = s$. The same decompositions applied to $w - u$ give $W - u$, by Lemma 1, and $(y_2, \ldots, y_r)$ is an execution of it from $z_1$, the state that L5 computes. By induction its plan is in $\mathrm{Reach}(z_1, w - u)$, so $o$ followed by it is in $\mathrm{Reach}(s, w)$.

*$y_1$ is the marker $x$ of the decomposition of $u$ by $\mu$.* Then $u$ has no predecessor, and L2 can choose it. Since $W$ is a full expansion, the depth of $u$ is less than $d$, so L7 passes; and $\mathit{pre}(\mu) \subseteq z_0 = s$, so L8 offers $\mu$. By Lemma 1 the decompositions can be made with that of $u$ first. Making the others on $w'$, the result of L9, gives $W - x$, again by Lemma 1, since $x$ has no predecessor and so lies on no path between other nodes. And $(y_2, \ldots, y_r)$ is an execution of $W - x$ from $z_1 = s$. By induction its plan, which is the plan of $\lambda$, is in $\mathrm{Reach}(s, w')$, hence in $\mathrm{Reach}(s, w)$. $\square$

**Theorem.** HTN-PLAN$(s_0, w_0, D, d)$ terminates. It returns a plan if and only if $\mathrm{Sol}(s_0, w_0)$ is not empty, and then the plan is in $\mathrm{Sol}(s_0, w_0)$, so it is executable from $s_0$ and is the primitive content of a full decomposition of $w_0$ within the bound.

*Proof.* Termination is Lemma 3. The pseudocode returns the first element of $\mathrm{Reach}(s_0, w_0)$ in backtracking order, or failure if it is empty, and $\mathrm{Reach}(s_0, w_0) = \mathrm{Sol}(s_0, w_0)$ by Lemmas 4 and 5. $\square$
