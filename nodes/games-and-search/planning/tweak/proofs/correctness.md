We use the pseudocode and the definitions of the page. There are infinitely many constants. A *model* of the bindings of a plan is a map $g$ from terms to constants that fixes every constant, gives codesignated terms the same constant and noncodesignated terms different ones. A set of codesignations and noncodesignations that unification accepts always has a model: give each class that contains a constant that constant, and give every other class its own fresh constant. A *completion* is a linearization $L$ with a model $g$; $g(x)$ for a proposition or a step means $g$ applied to every term. The state before a step in a completion is obtained by applying, in the order of $L$, first the ground delete list and then the ground add list of each earlier step, with START asserting the initial state $I$.

We assume, as on the page:

**(A)** For every schema and every value of its parameters that meets its own noncodesignations, the ground add list and the ground delete list are disjoint.

The plan's noncodesignations include those of each step's schema, so in every completion every step has disjoint ground add and delete lists.

**Lemma 1 (the criterion is sufficient).** If conditions 1 and 2 of the MTC hold for $p$ before $s$, then $g(p)$ holds before $s$ in every completion $(L, g)$.

*Proof.* Let $t$ and $q$ be as in condition 1. Every argument pair of $q$ and $p$ is codesignated, so $g(q) = g(p) =: v$, and $t$ comes before $s$ in $L$ because $t \prec s$. So $v$ holds right after $t$. Suppose $v$ does not hold right before $s$. Among the steps strictly between $t$ and $s$ in $L$ whose ground lists contain $v$, let $c$ be the last. It exists, since something removed $v$, and its last change to $v$ was a removal, so $v$ is in the ground delete list of $c$, and by (A) not in its add list. No step after $c$ and before $s$ asserts $v$. Now $c \ne s$ and $s \prec c$ is false because $L$ extends $\prec$, so $c$ is possibly before $s$. $c$ denies some $q'$ with $g(q') = v = g(p)$, so $g$ is a model of the bindings together with $q' \approx p$; hence unification of $q'$ with $p$ succeeds and $q'$ possibly codesignates with $p$. Condition 2 gives a step $w$ with $c \prec w \prec s$ and some $r \in \mathit{add}(w)$ with $r \approx p$ after adding $q' \approx p$. $g$ is a model of these bindings too, so $g(r) = v$. But $w$ is after $c$ and before $s$ in $L$ and asserts $v$, a contradiction. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (soundness).** If the MTC holds for every precondition of every step of $P$, then every completion of $P$ is an executable sequence of ground actions that ends in a state containing the goal, and $P$ has at least one completion.

*Proof.* The orderings are acyclic and the bindings have a model, so a completion exists. Fix a completion. By induction along $L$: if every step before a step $u$ found its preconditions true, applying the effects in order is the real execution, so the state before $u$ is the one of Lemma 1, and by Lemma 1 every precondition of $u$ holds in it. For $u$ = FINISH this says the goal holds at the end. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (progress and termination).** Measure a plan by the number of steps, the number of pairs in the closure of $\prec$, the number of terms minus the number of codesignation classes, and the number of noncodesignation pairs. Every recursive call of REFINE and of DECLOBBER receives a plan in which none of these has decreased and at least one has grown. Hence, for a fixed $n$, REFINE visits finitely many plans.

*Proof.* Constraints are only ever added, so no measure decreases. *DECLOBBER.* The clobberer $c$ of line L17 is not necessarily before $t$, so demotion $c \prec t$ is a new pair. It is possibly before $s$, so promotion $s \prec c$ is a new pair. For separation $x \not\approx y$, the terms are in different classes (the implementation refuses otherwise), and the pair is new, since an existing noncodesignation would make the unification of $q'$ with $p$ fail. For a white knight $w$: if $c \prec w$, $w \prec s$ and $r \approx p$ already held, $w$ would already be a white knight for $c$, which line L17 excluded; so something is added.

*REFINE.* A child from line L10 has one more step. A child from line L9 adds $t \prec s$ and $q \approx p$; if both already held, condition 1 of the MTC holds with $t$, so condition 2 fails for some $c$ and $q'$, since $(s, p)$ was chosen at line L8. This $c$ is not $t$: otherwise there is a model in which $g(q') = g(p) = g(q)$, and $t$ would assert and deny the same ground proposition, against (A). It is not necessarily before $t$: otherwise $t$ itself is a white knight for $c$, as $c \prec t \prec s$ and $q \approx p$. So line L17 finds a clobberer, and every plan that DECLOBBER outputs has grown.

With at most $n + 2$ steps there are at most $(n + 2) e$ terms, so every measure is bounded: $(n + 2)^2$ pairs, $(n + 2) e$ merges and $((n + 2) e)^2$ noncodesignation pairs. So every branch is finite, every node has finitely many children, and by König's lemma the tree is finite. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (completeness).** Let $\sigma = (a_1, \ldots, a_m)$ with $m \le n$ be a solution: each $a_k$ is a ground instance of a schema that meets the schema's noncodesignations, $\sigma$ is executable from $I$, and it ends in a state containing $G$. Then REFINE with the bound $n$ returns a plan.

*Proof.* Let position $0$ assert $I$, and let positions $1, \ldots, m$ hold $a_1, \ldots, a_m$. Call $P$ *faithful* if there are a one-to-one map $\phi$ from the steps other than FINISH to positions, with $\phi(\text{START}) = 0$, and a model $g$ of the bindings of $P$, such that $g(u) = a_{\phi(u)}$ for each step $u$ other than START and FINISH, and $u \prec u'$ implies $\phi(u) < \phi(u')$, with $\phi(\text{FINISH}) = m + 1$ for this purpose. The first plan is faithful. We show that a faithful plan whose precondition $(s, p)$ fails the MTC has a choice at lines L12 and L13 that gives a faithful plan. By Lemma 3 a path of such choices is finite, so it ends at a faithful plan for which line L7 succeeds, unless the depth-first search returns another plan first.

*Establishment.* Let $v = g(p)$. It holds before position $\phi(s)$ in $\sigma$: $v$ is a precondition of $a_{\phi(s)}$, or a goal if $s$ is FINISH. Let $k$ be the last position before $\phi(s)$ whose action asserts $v$ (position $0$ if no action does). If $k = \phi(t)$ for a step $t$, then $s \prec t$ is false, and $t$ has some $q \in \mathit{add}(t)$ with $g(q) = v$. Line L9 offers $P$ with $t \prec s$ and $q \approx p$: $g$ still is a model, because the arguments of $q$ and $p$ have the same values, and the new ordering agrees with $\phi$. If no step has position $k$, then $k \ge 1$, $P$ has fewer than $m \le n$ action steps, and line L10 offers a new step $t$ of the schema of $a_k$, with $q$ the element of its add list whose instance in $a_k$ is $v$. Extend $g$ to the new variables so that $g(t) = a_k$; this meets the schema's noncodesignations because $a_k$ does. Extend $\phi$ by $\phi(t) = k$. In both cases the plan $P'$ is faithful, and $k$ is the last position before $\phi(s)$ that asserts $v$.

*Declobbering.* Let $c$, $q'$ be the clobberer of line L17 in a faithful plan with the same $t$, $s$, $p$, $\phi$, $g$. Since $\phi$ is one to one, $\phi(c) \notin \{\phi(t), \phi(s)\}$. If $\phi(c) < \phi(t)$, demotion $c \prec t$ agrees with $\phi$. If $\phi(c) > \phi(s)$, promotion $s \prec c$ agrees with $\phi$. Otherwise $\phi(t) < \phi(c) < \phi(s)$. If $g(q') \ne g(p)$, some argument pair $(x, y)$ of $q'$ and $p$ has $g(x) \ne g(y)$, and separation $x \not\approx y$ keeps $g$ a model. If $g(q') = v$, then $a_{\phi(c)}$ deletes $v$ and, by (A), does not add it; no position after $\phi(c)$ and before $\phi(s)$ asserts $v$, by the choice of $k$; so $v$ is false before position $\phi(s)$, against $\sigma$ being executable and reaching $G$. So some option of line L19 is consistent and faithful, and by Lemma 3 the recursion of line L20 ends, after finitely many such choices, in a faithful plan that DECLOBBER outputs. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (the cut test).** If a round with bound $n$ ends with $\mathit{cut} = \mathit{false}$, then every round with a bound $n' > n$ visits the same plans and fails.

*Proof.* The bound appears only in line L10. Since line L11 never ran, at every precondition the round chose, either $P$ had fewer than $n$ steps, or no schema asserts a proposition with the predicate of $p$, in which case line L10 adds nothing under any bound. So the two rounds compute the same sets $E$ and make the same choices. $\square$

**Theorem.** If a solution with at most $N$ actions exists, TWEAK returns a plan whose every completion is a solution, and the plan has as few steps as a shortest solution. If TWEAK fails at line L5, no solution of any length exists. If it fails at line L6, no solution with at most $N$ actions exists.

*Proof.* Let $L^*$ be the length of a shortest solution. For $n < L^*$, a plan returned in round $n$ would have at most $n$ steps, and by Lemma 2 one of its completions would be a solution with fewer than $L^*$ actions; so round $n$ fails. If $L^* \le N$, round $n < L^*$ cannot end the loop at line L5, because by Lemma 5 round $L^*$ would then fail, against Lemma 4. So the round $L^*$ is reached, and by Lemma 4 it returns a plan, which has at most $L^*$ steps, and so exactly $L^*$. If the loop fails at line L5 in round $n$, every later round fails by Lemma 5, so by Lemma 4 there is no solution of any length. Failure at line L6 means that every round up to $N$ failed, so by Lemma 4 there is no solution with at most $N$ actions. Every round terminates by Lemma 3. $\square$
