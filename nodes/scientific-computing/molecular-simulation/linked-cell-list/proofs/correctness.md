---
title: The linked-cell list reports each pair within the cutoff exactly once
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

We use the names of the pseudocode. The box is $[0, L)^d$ with periodic boundaries, $d = 2$ or $3$, and the positions are wrapped into it. For two points $a, b$ the minimum image difference on axis $q$ is $\Delta_q = (a_q - b_q) - L \, \mathrm{round}((a_q - b_q)/L)$, so $|\Delta_q| \le L/2$, and the minimum image distance is $|\Delta| = (\sum_q \Delta_q^2)^{1/2}$. In a cubic box this is the distance from $a$ to the nearest periodic image of $b$, because the images are $b + L m$ with $m \in \mathbb{Z}^d$ and the squared distance splits into one term per axis, each made smallest by the rounding. We assume exact arithmetic for the floors of line L4. Let $s = \lfloor L / r_c \rfloor \ge 3$ and $\ell = L / s$, so $\ell \ge r_c$ because $s \le L / r_c$. Let $H$ be the set of offsets $o \in \{-1, 0, 1\}^d$ whose first nonzero entry is $+1$.

**Lemma 1 (chains).** After BUILD, each atom is in exactly one chain, the chain of the cell that holds its position, and each chain is finite. *Proof.* By induction on $i$. Before step $i$, the chains hold the atoms $1, \ldots, i-1$, each once, and each chain is a list that ends in $-1$. Line L5 sets `next[i]` to the old head of the chain of $c$, which is $-1$ or an atom less than $i$, and makes $i$ the head. So the chain of $c$ gains $i$ at its front, the other chains do not change, and the chain still ends in $-1$. Following `next` from any atom gives strictly smaller atoms, so each chain has at most $N$ atoms. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (neighboring cells).** If the atoms $i$ and $j$ have minimum image distance less than $r_c$, then on each axis $q$ their cell coordinates satisfy $k_q(j) - k_q(i) \equiv \delta_q \pmod s$ for some $\delta_q \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$. *Proof.* Fix an axis $q$ and let $u = x_{i,q}$ and $v = x_{j,q}$ in $[0, L)$. The nearest image of $v$ on this axis is $v' = v + L m$ for an integer $m$ with $|u - v'| = |\Delta_q| \le |\Delta| < r_c \le \ell$. So $u / \ell$ and $v' / \ell$ differ by less than 1, and their floors differ by at most 1. Since $L / \ell = s$ is an integer, $\lfloor v' / \ell \rfloor = \lfloor v / \ell \rfloor + s m = k_q(j) + s m$. So $k_q(j) + s m - k_q(i) \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (offsets).** Let $c$ and $c'$ be cells with $k(c') - k(c) \equiv \delta \pmod s$ for some $\delta \in \{-1, 0, 1\}^d$. Then $\delta$ is unique. If $\delta = 0$, then $c = c'$. If $\delta \ne 0$, then among the visits (cell, offset) of lines L6 to L8, exactly one pairs $c$ with $c'$: either the visit $(c, \delta)$ with $\delta \in H$, or the visit $(c', -\delta)$ with $-\delta \in H$. *Proof.* Since $s \ge 3$, the residues of $-1$, 0 and $1$ modulo $s$ are distinct, so $\delta$ is unique. A visit $(c, o)$ reaches $c'$ exactly when $o \equiv \delta$, that is $o = \delta$; a visit $(c', o)$ reaches $c$ exactly when $o = -\delta$. Visits from any third cell reach neither of the two as a pair. For $\delta \ne 0$, let its first nonzero entry be $\delta_q$. Then exactly one of $\delta_q$ and $-\delta_q$ is $+1$, so exactly one of $\delta$ and $-\delta$ is in $H$. The offset 0 is not in $H$, so a visit with offset 0 pairs a cell only with itself. $\square$

**Soundness.** Line L11 reports $(i, j)$ only after it computes the minimum image distance and finds it less than $r_c$. So every reported pair is within the cutoff.

**Completeness and no repeats.** Let $i \ne j$ have minimum image distance less than $r_c$, and let $c$ and $c'$ be their cells (Lemma 1). By Lemma 2 and Lemma 3, there is a unique $\delta$ with $k(c') - k(c) \equiv \delta$. If $\delta = 0$, then $c = c'$, and the only visit that reaches the pair is $(c, 0)$. In that visit, line L10 takes for each $i$ only the atoms after $i$ in the chain, so the unordered pair $\{i, j\}$ is tested exactly once, when the loop of line L9 reaches the one of the two that comes first in the chain. If $\delta \ne 0$, then $c \ne c'$, and by Lemma 3 exactly one visit pairs the two cells. In it, lines L9 and L10 test every atom of the one chain against every atom of the other, so the pair is tested exactly once. In both cases the test of line L11 succeeds, and the pair is reported once. A pair is never tested in a visit that does not pair its two cells, because lines L9 and L10 take $i$ from the chain of $c$ and $j$ from the chain of $c'$ only.

**Termination.** BUILD runs a loop of $N$ steps. ALL-PAIRS runs over the $s^d$ cells and the $1 + |H|$ offsets, and over two chains, which are finite by Lemma 1. So both stop. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (why $s \ge 3$).** With $s = 2$, the offsets $+1$ and $-1$ on one axis reach the same cell, so the two visits $(c, \delta)$ and $(c', -\delta)$ of Lemma 3 can both pair $c$ with $c'$ through different offsets, and a pair can be reported twice. With $s \ge 3$, the cutoff also satisfies $r_c \le \ell \le L / 3 < L / 2$, so the nearest image of each atom within the cutoff is unique.
