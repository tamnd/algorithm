---
title: The KIFMM uses each far box once, its fits are well defined for the log kernel, and exact fits give exact far fields
proves: [correctness]
status: partial
---

This file proves three parts of the correctness of the implementation on the page, for the kernel $K(x, y) = \log|x - y|$ in 2D. It does not prove a bound on the error as a function of the number $P$ of points on each circle; that error is measured on the page. We use exact arithmetic. A box at level $l \ge 2$ has side $w = 2^{-l}$, half-width $h = w / 2$ and center $c$. Its upward equivalent and downward check circles have radius $1.5 h$, and its upward check and downward equivalent circles have radius $2.5 h$, each with the $P$ points $c + R \omega^k$, $\omega = e^{2\pi \mathrm{i} / P}$, $k = 0, \ldots, P - 1$.

**Lemma 1 (each pair once).** The tree, the near neighbours and the interaction lists are those of the uniform [fast multipole method](/fast-multipole-method/). So, for a target in leaf $t$, every charge is used exactly once: in the near field if its leaf is a near neighbour of $t$, and otherwise through exactly one pair $(c_l, t_l)$ of ancestors with $c_l$ in the interaction list of $t_l$.

*Proof.* This is Lemma 1 of the proof of the fast multipole method, which uses only the tree and the lists. The downward check potential of $t_l$ receives the M2L term of $c_l$ at line L3, and the D2D step at line L4 passes the downward density of each box to all its children, so the term reaches the leaf $t$ once. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (the fits are well defined).** Let $x_i = R_x \omega^i$ and $y_j = R_y \omega^j$ with $R_x \ne R_y$, and let $R_> = \max(R_x, R_y)$, $R_< = \min(R_x, R_y)$ and $\rho = R_< / R_>$. The matrix $A_{ij} = \log|x_i - y_j|$ is circulant, with the eigenvalues

$$\lambda_0 = \log\left( R_>^P - R_<^P \right), \qquad \lambda_m = -\frac{P}{2} \sum_{k \ge 1,\; k \equiv \pm m \pmod P} \frac{\rho^k}{k} \quad (1 \le m \le P - 1).$$

So $A$ is invertible if and only if $R_>^P - R_<^P \ne 1$. In the implementation $R_> = 2.5 h \le 2.5 / 8 < 1$, so every fit matrix $A^{-1}$ exists.

*Proof.* Write $x_i - y_j = R_> \zeta (1 - \rho e^{\mathrm{i} \psi})$ with $|\zeta| = 1$ and $\psi = \pm 2\pi (i - j) / P$. For $\rho < 1$, $\log|1 - \rho e^{\mathrm{i}\psi}| = -\sum_{k \ge 1} \rho^k \cos(k \psi) / k$, so $A_{ij} = a_{(i - j) \bmod P}$ with $a_d = \log R_> - \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{\rho^k}{k} \cos(2\pi k d / P)$. A circulant matrix has the eigenvectors $v_m = (\omega^{m i})_i$ and the eigenvalues $\lambda_m = \sum_d a_d \omega^{-m d}$. Now $\sum_d \omega^{-md} = P$ if $m \equiv 0$ and $0$ otherwise, and $\sum_d \cos(2\pi k d / P) \omega^{-md} = \frac{P}{2} ([k \equiv m] + [k \equiv -m])$. This gives $\lambda_m$ for $m \ne 0$, a sum of positive terms with a minus sign, so $\lambda_m < 0$. For $m = 0$, both brackets hold exactly for $k = jP$, so $\lambda_0 = P \log R_> - P \sum_{j \ge 1} \frac{\rho^{jP}}{jP} = P \log R_> + \log(1 - \rho^P) = \log(R_>^P - R_<^P)$. A matrix is invertible exactly when no eigenvalue is 0. Finally $R_>^P - R_<^P < R_>^P < 1$. $\square$

The smallest eigenvalues in absolute value are near $m = P / 2$, of size about $\rho^{P/2}$, so the condition number of $A$ grows like $\rho^{-P/2}$. With $\rho = 0.6$ this is about $3 \times 10^4$ for $P = 40$. The normal equations $A^T A$ square it, which explains the loss of digits measured on the page.

**Lemma 3 (exact fits give exact far fields).** Let $f(x) = \sum_i q_i \log|x - z_i|$ for charges $z_i$ in a box $B$ with center $0$, and let $g(x) = \int \sigma(y) \log|x - y| \, ds(y)$ be the potential of a continuous density on the circle of radius $1.5h$. If $g = f$ on the whole circle of radius $2.5 h$, then $g = f$ for all $|x| \ge 2.5 h$. In the same way, if a density on the circle of radius $2.5h$ matches, on the whole circle of radius $1.5 h$, a potential whose charges all lie outside the radius $2.5 h$, then it matches it in the whole disk of radius $1.5 h$.

*Proof.* The charges lie within $\sqrt{2}\, h < 1.5 h$ of the center. For $|x| > 1.5 h$, both $f$ and $g$ are harmonic and have expansions $A \log|x| + \sum_{k \ge 1} \mathrm{Re}(b_k x^{-k})$ without a constant term, because $\log|x - y| = \log|x| - \mathrm{Re} \sum_{k \ge 1} (y / x)^k / k$ for $|y| < |x|$. The difference $f - g$ has such an expansion, and it is 0 on $|x| = 2.5h$. The constant Fourier mode on that circle is $(A_f - A_g) \log(2.5 h)$, and $\log(2.5h) \ne 0$, so $A_f = A_g$; each mode $k \ge 1$ gives $b_k^f = b_k^g$. So $f = g$ for $|x| > 1.5 h$. For the interior statement, both potentials are harmonic in the disk of radius $2.5 h$, and a harmonic function in a disk is fixed by its values on a smaller circle by the maximum principle. $\square$

**What remains.** With exact fits, Lemma 3 shows that every step of the method is exact: the upward density of a box reproduces the field of its charges outside its upward check circle, which contains the upward equivalent circles of its children (they reach $(\sqrt{2} + 1.5) h_{\text{child}} < 1.5 h$ from the center). The downward check circle of a box in its V list is at least $4h - 1.5h = 2.5h$ from its center, so the M2L step gives the true far field there. The charges of the far field of a box are at least $4h - \sqrt{2}\, h > 2.5h$ from its center, so its downward density reproduces the far field inside its downward check circle, which contains the downward check circles of its children (they reach $(\sqrt{2} + 1.5) h_{\text{child}} < 3 h_{\text{child}}$). Then Lemma 1 gives the exact potential. The implementation matches only at $P$ points, so the fits are exact for the Fourier modes that the $P$ points resolve and alias the others. A bound on this aliasing error, which falls geometrically in $P$, is not proved here.
