---
title: The Wisdom-Holman step is a composition of exact Hamiltonian flows, so it is symplectic, symmetric and of second order
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

Let $\eta_i = m_0 + \cdots + m_i$, let $\mathbf{X}_i = \eta_i^{-1} \sum_{k \le i} m_k \mathbf{x}_k$, and let $\mathbf{x}'_i = \mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{X}_{i-1}$ for $i \ge 1$ and $\mathbf{x}'_0 = \mathbf{X}_{N-1}$. Write $J$ for this linear map, $\mathbf{x}' = J \mathbf{x}$, and $m'_i = m_i \eta_{i-1} / \eta_i$.

**Lemma 1 (the splitting).** With $\mathbf{v}' = J \mathbf{v}$, the kinetic energy is $T = \frac{1}{2} \eta_{N-1} |\mathbf{v}'_0|^2 + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i \ge 1} m'_i |\mathbf{v}'_i|^2$. So with $\mathbf{p}'_0 = \eta_{N-1} \mathbf{v}'_0$ and $\mathbf{p}'_i = m'_i \mathbf{v}'_i$, the Hamiltonian is $H = H_K + H_I$ with $H_K$ and $H_I$ as on the page, and $H_I$ depends on $\mathbf{x}'$ only.

*Proof.* It is enough to show for each $i \ge 1$ that $\eta_{i-1} |\mathbf{V}_{i-1}|^2 + m_i |\mathbf{v}_i|^2 = \eta_i |\mathbf{V}_i|^2 + m'_i |\mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{V}_{i-1}|^2$, where $\mathbf{V}_i = \dot{\mathbf{X}}_i$. Write $\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{V}_{i-1}$. Then $\mathbf{V}_i = \mathbf{V}_{i-1} + (m_i / \eta_i) \mathbf{w}$, so $\eta_i |\mathbf{V}_i|^2 = \eta_i |\mathbf{V}_{i-1}|^2 + 2 m_i \mathbf{V}_{i-1} \cdot \mathbf{w} + (m_i^2 / \eta_i) |\mathbf{w}|^2$, and $m_i |\mathbf{v}_i|^2 = m_i |\mathbf{V}_{i-1}|^2 + 2 m_i \mathbf{V}_{i-1} \cdot \mathbf{w} + m_i |\mathbf{w}|^2$. Subtracting, the left side minus $\eta_i |\mathbf{V}_i|^2$ is $(m_i - m_i^2 / \eta_i) |\mathbf{w}|^2 = m'_i |\mathbf{w}|^2$. Starting from $m_0 |\mathbf{v}_0|^2 = \eta_0 |\mathbf{V}_0|^2$ and applying the identity for $i = 1, \ldots, N-1$ gives $\sum_k m_k |\mathbf{v}_k|^2 = \eta_{N-1} |\mathbf{V}_{N-1}|^2 + \sum_{i \ge 1} m'_i |\mathbf{v}'_i|^2$. Since $J$ is invertible and does not depend on time, the change $\mathbf{x}' = J\mathbf{x}$, $\mathbf{p}' = J^{-T} \mathbf{p}$ is canonical, and $\mathbf{p}'_i$ is as stated. The potential is a function of $\mathbf{x} = J^{-1} \mathbf{x}'$, and $H_I$ is the potential plus a function of $\mathbf{x}'$, so it depends on positions only. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (the kick).** The flow of $H_I$ for a time $\tau$ keeps $\mathbf{x}'$ and adds $\tau \mathbf{a}'_i$ to $\mathbf{v}'_i$, where $\mathbf{a}'$ is computed by lines L7 and L8.

*Proof.* Since $H_I$ does not depend on $\mathbf{p}'$, its flow keeps $\mathbf{x}'$, and $\dot{\mathbf{p}}'$ is constant, so $\mathbf{v}'_i$ changes by $\tau \mathbf{a}'_i$ with $\mathbf{a}'_i = -\partial H_I / \partial \mathbf{x}'_i / m'_i$ (and $\mathbf{a}'_0 = 0$, because the potential and the Kepler terms do not depend on $\mathbf{x}'_0$). For the potential part, the full Newtonian motion satisfies $\ddot{\mathbf{x}}' = J \ddot{\mathbf{x}}$, and since the kinetic energy is diagonal in $\mathbf{v}'$ with masses $m'_i$ (Lemma 1), $-\partial U / \partial \mathbf{x}'_i / m'_i = (J \mathbf{a})_i$, with $\mathbf{a}$ the Newtonian accelerations. This is line L7. For the term $G m'_i \eta_i / r'_i$, the derivative is $-G m'_i \eta_i \mathbf{x}'_i / r'^3_i$, which gives $+ G \eta_i \mathbf{x}'_i / r'^3_i$ after the division by $-m'_i$. This is line L8. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the Kepler drift).** For a bound orbit $\ddot{\mathbf{x}} = -\mu \mathbf{x} / r^3$, lines L9 and L10 give the exact state after time $\Delta t$. The Newton iteration of line L9 has a unique root to converge to.

*Proof.* Put the orbit in its plane with the pericenter on the first axis, so $\mathbf{x}(E) = (a(\cos E - e), b \sin E)$ with $b = a\sqrt{1 - e^2}$, $r = a(1 - e \cos E)$, and $\dot E = n a / r$ with $n = \sqrt{\mu / a^3}$. Then Kepler's equation $n t = E - e \sin E + \text{const}$ gives, with $E = E_0 + \Delta E$,

$$n \Delta t = \Delta E - e \cos E_0 \sin \Delta E + e \sin E_0 (1 - \cos \Delta E).$$

From $r_0 = a(1 - e \cos E_0)$ we get $e \cos E_0 = 1 - r_0 / a = e_c$, and from $r \dot r = \mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{v}$ and $\dot r = a e \sin E\, \dot E$ we get $\mathbf{x}_0 \cdot \mathbf{v}_0 = n a^2 e \sin E_0$, so $e \sin E_0 = \mathbf{x}_0 \cdot \mathbf{v}_0 / \sqrt{\mu a} = e_s$. The energy gives $1/a = 2/r_0 - v_0^2 / \mu$. So line L9 solves Kepler's equation. Its left side $F(\Delta E) = \Delta E - e_c \sin \Delta E + e_s(1 - \cos \Delta E)$ has $F' = 1 - e \cos E = r / a > 0$, so it is increasing and has one root; and $|F(\Delta E) - \Delta E| \le e \sqrt{(\sin \Delta E)^2 + (1 - \cos \Delta E)^2} \le 2e < 2$, so the root lies in $[n\Delta t - 2, n\Delta t + 2]$. Newton with bisection on this bracket converges.

The plane is two-dimensional and $\mathbf{x}_0 \times \mathbf{v}_0 = n a b \ne 0$, so $\mathbf{x} = f \mathbf{x}_0 + g \mathbf{v}_0$ with $f = (\mathbf{x} \times \mathbf{v}_0) / (\mathbf{x}_0 \times \mathbf{v}_0)$ and $g = (\mathbf{x}_0 \times \mathbf{x}) / (\mathbf{x}_0 \times \mathbf{v}_0)$, where $\times$ is the scalar cross product in the plane. With $\mathbf{v}_0 = (n a / r_0)(-a \sin E_0, b \cos E_0)$,

$$\mathbf{x} \times \mathbf{v}_0 = \frac{n a^2 b}{r_0} \left( (\cos E - e) \cos E_0 + \sin E \sin E_0 \right) = \frac{n a^2 b}{r_0} (\cos \Delta E - e_c),$$

so $f = (a / r_0)(\cos \Delta E - 1 + r_0 / a) = 1 - (a / r_0)(1 - \cos \Delta E)$. And $\mathbf{x}_0 \times \mathbf{x} = a b \left( (\cos E_0 - e) \sin E - \sin E_0 (\cos E - e) \right) = a b \left( \sin \Delta E - e \sin E + e \sin E_0 \right)$. By Kepler's equation $e \sin E_0 - e \sin E = n \Delta t - \Delta E$, so $g = (\sin \Delta E + n \Delta t - \Delta E) / n = \Delta t - (\Delta E - \sin \Delta E) / n$. Differentiating in $t$ with $d \Delta E / dt = n a / r$ gives $\dot f = -n a^2 \sin \Delta E / (r r_0) = -\sqrt{\mu a} \sin \Delta E / (r r_0)$ and $\dot g = 1 - (a / r)(1 - \cos \Delta E)$, and $\mathbf{v} = \dot f \mathbf{x}_0 + \dot g \mathbf{v}_0$. Finally $r = a(1 - e \cos(E_0 + \Delta E)) = a(1 - e_c \cos \Delta E + e_s \sin \Delta E)$. These are the formulas of line L10, and they do not depend on the choice of axes. $\square$

**Theorem.** Let $\varphi^K_\tau$ and $\varphi^I_\tau$ be the flows of $H_K$ and $H_I$. The step is $\Phi_h = \varphi^I_{h/2} \circ \varphi^K_h \circ \varphi^I_{h/2}$. It is symplectic, it satisfies $\Phi_{-h} = \Phi_h^{-1}$, and its local error is $O(h^3)$, so its global error over a fixed time is $O(h^2)$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2, lines L2 and L5 are $\varphi^I_{h/2}$. The flow of $H_K$ is the free motion of $\mathbf{x}'_0$ (line L4) and, for each $i \ge 1$, the Kepler flow with $\mu_i = G \eta_i$, because $\partial H_K / \partial \mathbf{p}'_i = \mathbf{p}'_i / m'_i$ and $-\partial H_K / \partial \mathbf{x}'_i = -G m'_i \eta_i \mathbf{x}'_i / r'^3_i$; by Lemma 3, line L3 is this flow. Lines L1 and L6 are the canonical change of Lemma 1 and its inverse. The flow of a Hamiltonian is symplectic, and compositions of symplectic maps are symplectic. Since $\varphi_{-\tau} = \varphi_\tau^{-1}$ for each flow, $\Phi_{-h} = \varphi^I_{-h/2} \circ \varphi^K_{-h} \circ \varphi^I_{-h/2} = (\varphi^I_{h/2} \circ \varphi^K_h \circ \varphi^I_{h/2})^{-1}$. For the order, write the flows as exponentials of the Lie derivatives $A$ and $B$ of $H_I$ and $H_K$; the symmetric Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula gives $e^{hA/2} e^{hB} e^{hA/2} = \exp\left(h(A + B) + h^3 C_3 + O(h^5)\right)$ with $C_3$ a combination of $[A, [A, B]]$ and $[B, [B, A]]$, so the local error is $O(h^3)$ and the step is consistent of order 2. The step map is Lipschitz with constant $1 + O(h)$ on a compact set away from collisions, and the standard argument for one-step methods turns this into a global error $O(h^2)$. $\square$

**Remark (the size of the error).** Every bracket in $C_3$ contains $A$, the Lie derivative of $H_I$, and $H_I$ is smaller than $H_K$ by a factor of order $\varepsilon = \max_{i \ge 1} m_i / m_0$. So the modified Hamiltonian of backward error analysis differs from $H$ by $O(\varepsilon h^2)$. This is an estimate under the assumption that the orbits stay separated, not a sharp constant.
