title: What can be proved about Raibert's hopping controller
proves: [correctness]

---

Raibert's three-part controller is a heuristic. There is no proof that the closed loop of the full hopper converges to the desired speed, height and attitude, and the tests show that on the model of the implementation the speed settles with an offset. This file proves the facts on which the three parts rest, for the model of the implementation: a body of mass $m$ and pitch inertia $I$ with the hip at the center of mass, a massless leg with spring stiffness $k$, damping $b$ and rest length $\ell_r$, and a foot that does not slip during stance.

**Lemma 1 (the leg force).** In stance, let $r$ be the vector from the foot to the hip, $\ell = |r|$, $u = r / \ell$ and $n$ the unit vector that is $u$ turned by $+90$ degrees. If the hip applies the torque $\tau$ to the body, the force of the leg on the body is $f = F_s u - (\tau / \ell) n$ with $F_s = k(\ell_r - \ell) - b \dot\ell$.

*Proof.* The leg has no mass, so the forces and the moments on it sum to zero. The forces on it are the ground force at the foot and $-f$ at the hip, so the ground force is $f$. The moments about the foot are the moment of $-f$ at the hip, $r \times (-f)$, and the hip torque on the leg, $-\tau$. So $r \times f = -\tau$. The axial part of $f$ is the spring and damper force $F_s$, and $r \times u = 0$, $r \times n = \ell$. Hence the normal part is $-\tau / \ell$. $\square$

This is the force in `stance_deriv`. The body then obeys $m \ddot p = f + m g$ and $I \ddot\phi = \tau$.

**Lemma 2 (energy).** If $b = 0$, $\tau = 0$ and $\ell_r$ is constant, then $E = \frac12 m |\dot p|^2 + \frac12 I \dot\phi^2 + m g z + \frac12 k (\ell_r - \ell)^2$ is constant in stance. In flight, $E$ without the spring term is constant. At touchdown and at lift-off the spring term is zero.

*Proof.* In stance, $\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac12 m |\dot p|^2 + m g z\right) = \dot p \cdot f = F_s \dot\ell$, because $u \cdot \dot p = \dot\ell$ and the normal part is zero. Also $\frac{d}{dt} \frac12 k (\ell_r - \ell)^2 = -k(\ell_r - \ell) \dot\ell = -F_s \dot\ell$, and $\dot\phi$ is constant. The sum is zero. Flight is ballistic. Touchdown happens at $\ell = \ell_0 = \ell_r$, and lift-off happens when $F_s = 0$, that is at $\ell = \ell_r$. $\square$

So with $b = 0$ the only change of energy is at line L6, where the rest length grows by $\delta$ during stance. The spring energy grows at that instant by $\frac12 k ((\ell_0 + \delta - \ell)^2 - (\ell_0 - \ell)^2) = k \delta (\ell_0 - \ell) + \frac12 k \delta^2 > 0$, where $\ell$ is the leg length at the switch. This is the thrust: it adds energy each hop, and the damper takes energy away. The hop height settles where the two are equal; that this balance is a stable fixed point is a measurement, not a proof.

**Lemma 3 (vertical stance time).** Let the leg be vertical, $b = 0$, $\delta = 0$, and let the hip touch down at the speed $v > 0$. Then the stance lasts $t_s = (2\pi - 2\alpha)/\omega$ with $\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$ and $\alpha = \operatorname{atan2}(v / \omega, m g / k)$, and the hip leaves at the speed $v$ upward.

*Proof.* With $y = z - (\ell_0 - m g / k)$, the stance equation $m \ddot z = k(\ell_0 - z) - m g$ is $\ddot y = -\omega^2 y$. At touchdown $y = m g / k > 0$ and $\dot y = -v$. Write $y = A \cos\psi$, $\dot y = -A \omega \sin\psi$ with $A > 0$ and $\psi = \omega t + \psi_0$; then $\psi_0 = \alpha \in (0, \pi/2)$. Lift-off is the first later time with $z = \ell_0$, that is $y = m g / k$ again, which is $\cos\psi = \cos\alpha$ with $\psi > \alpha$, so $\psi = 2\pi - \alpha$. At that point $\dot y = A \omega \sin\alpha = v$. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (the neutral point).** Let $b = 0$, $\tau = 0$ and $\ell_r$ constant. If at some instant $t^*$ of stance the hip is right above the foot and $\dot z = 0$, then the stance is symmetric about $t^*$: the lift-off state is the touchdown state with $x - x_f$ and $\dot z$ negated. In particular the forward speed at lift-off equals the forward speed at touchdown.

*Proof.* Put $\xi = x - x_f$. The stance equations for $(\xi, z)$ are $\ddot \xi = (F_s / m) \xi / \ell$ and $\ddot z = (F_s / m) z / \ell - g$ with $F_s = k(\ell_r - \ell)$ and $\ell = \sqrt{\xi^2 + z^2}$. Let $(\xi(t), z(t))$ be the solution and set $\hat \xi(t) = -\xi(2t^* - t)$ and $\hat z(t) = z(2t^* - t)$. The time reversal does not change the sign of a second derivative, so $\ddot{\hat \xi}(t) = -\ddot \xi(2t^* - t)$ and $\ddot{\hat z}(t) = \ddot z(2t^* - t)$. The length $\ell$ and the force $F_s$ depend only on $\xi^2$ and $z$, so they are the same for both curves. Hence $\ddot{\hat \xi} = (F_s / m) \hat \xi / \ell$ and $\ddot{\hat z} = (F_s / m) \hat z / \ell - g$, and $(\hat \xi, \hat z)$ is also a solution. At $t^*$ both solutions have $\xi = 0$, the same $z$, the same $\dot \xi$ (since $\dot{\hat \xi}(t^*) = \dot\xi(t^*)$) and $\dot z = 0$. By uniqueness they are equal. So the state at $t^* + s$ is the state at $t^* - s$ with $\xi$ and $\dot z$ negated and $\dot\xi$ unchanged. The lift-off condition $\ell = \ell_r$ is symmetric too, so lift-off is at $2 t^* - t_{td}$. $\square$

In a symmetric stance the foot is at the middle of the horizontal path of the hip. The path has length $D = \int \dot x \, dt$ over the stance, so the neutral foot position is $D / 2$ ahead of the hip at touchdown. Line L2 uses $\dot x T_s / 2$, which equals $D / 2$ only if the forward speed is constant during stance. With the foot ahead of the hip, the leg force slows the body in the first half and speeds it up in the second, so the forward speed dips during stance, and on the model of the implementation the result is $D < \dot x T_s$: the estimate is too far forward. The term $k_{\dot x}(\dot x - \dot x_d)$ moves the foot from this estimate; with a fixed $k_{\dot x}$ the speed settles where the two errors cancel, which is below $\dot x_d$ on this model. The size of the offset is measured in the tests, not derived.

**Decoupling.** Raibert's argument for treating the three parts as separate loops is that, with the hip at the center of mass, the thrust changes mainly the vertical energy, the foot placement changes mainly the forward speed, and the hip torque changes mainly the pitch. Lemma 1 shows that the hip torque also gives the normal force $-\tau / \ell$, so the attitude loop does change the speed, and the coupling is small only when $\tau / \ell$ is small next to the spring force. This is a heuristic.
