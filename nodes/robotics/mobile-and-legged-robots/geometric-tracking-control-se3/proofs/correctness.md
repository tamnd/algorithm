---
title: The attitude loop of the geometric controller is stable, and the position loop converges near the desired path
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

Line numbers refer to the pseudocode on the [geometric tracking control on SE(3)](/geometric-tracking-control-se3/) page. The frame convention is z up, as on the page: $m\dot v = fRe_3 - mge_3$. Lee, Leok and McClamroch write the same equations with z down; the attitude equations are the same in both conventions, and the position equations differ only by the signs of $g$ and $f$ (see Lemma 3). This proof covers the continuous-time law with exact $\Omega_d$ and $\dot\Omega_d$. The implementation holds the command for one control period and estimates $\Omega_d$ by backward differences, so its results are measurements; the tests check them.

Notation: $\hat a$ is the skew-symmetric matrix of $a \in \mathbb{R}^3$, with $\hat a b = a \times b$, and $(\cdot)^\vee$ is its inverse. $\Psi = \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(I - R_d^TR)$, $e_R = \tfrac12(R_d^TR - R^TR_d)^\vee$, $e_\Omega = \Omega - R^TR_d\Omega_d$.

**Lemma 1 (the errors in terms of the angle).** Let $R_d^TR = \exp(\hat\xi)$ with $\theta = \|\xi\| \in [0, \pi]$. Then $\Psi = 1 - \cos\theta$ and $e_R = (\sin\theta/\theta)\,\xi$ (with $e_R = \xi = 0$ at $\theta = 0$). So $\Psi \in [0, 2]$, $\Psi = 0$ only at $R = R_d$, $\Psi = 2$ exactly when the rotation error is $\pi$, and there $e_R = 0$.

*Proof.* By Rodrigues' formula, $\exp(\hat\xi) = I + \sin\theta\,\hat u + (1 - \cos\theta)\hat u^2$ with $u = \xi/\theta$. The trace of $\hat u$ is 0 and the trace of $\hat u^2$ is $-2$, so the trace of $\exp(\hat\xi)$ is $1 + 2\cos\theta$ and $\Psi = \tfrac12(3 - 1 - 2\cos\theta) = 1 - \cos\theta$. The skew part of $\exp(\hat\xi)$ is $\sin\theta\,\hat u$, so $e_R = \sin\theta\,u$. $\square$

The test `test_errors_match_the_logarithm` checks both formulas on 300 random cases to $10^{-12}$, and `test_upside_down_is_a_critical_point` checks $e_R = 0$ at $\theta = \pi$.

**Lemma 2 (error dynamics of the attitude).** Let $R_d(t)$ be smooth with $\dot R_d = R_d\hat\Omega_d$. Along the closed loop of lines L6 and L7,
$$\dot\Psi = e_R\cdot e_\Omega,\qquad \dot e_R = C(R_d^TR)\,e_\Omega \text{ with } \|C\| \le 1,\qquad J\dot e_\Omega = -k_Re_R - k_\Omega e_\Omega.$$

*Proof.* Let $Q = R_d^TR$. Then $\dot Q = -\hat\Omega_dQ + Q\hat\Omega = Q(\Omega - Q^T\Omega_d)^\wedge = Q\hat e_\Omega$, using $Q^T\hat aQ = (Q^Ta)^\wedge$. So $\dot\Psi = -\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(Q\hat e_\Omega)$. For any matrix $Q$ and vector $e$, $\operatorname{tr}(Q\hat e) = \operatorname{tr}(\tfrac12(Q - Q^T)\hat e) = -2\,\big(\tfrac12(Q - Q^T)\big)^\vee\cdot e$, because the symmetric part of $Q$ is orthogonal to $\hat e$ in the trace inner product and $\operatorname{tr}(\hat a\hat e) = -2a\cdot e$. Hence $\dot\Psi = e_R\cdot e_\Omega$. Next, $\dot e_R = \tfrac12(Q\hat e_\Omega + \hat e_\Omega Q^T)^\vee$, and the identity $(\hat xA + A^T\hat x)^\vee = (\operatorname{tr}(A)I - A)x$ gives $\dot e_R = \tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}(Q^T)I - Q^T)e_\Omega = C e_\Omega$. With $Q = \exp(\hat\xi)$, the eigenvalues of the symmetric part of $C$ are at most 1 in absolute value; Lee, Leok and McClamroch prove $\|C\|_2 \le 1$ in the appendix of their paper. Last, $\dot e_\Omega = \dot\Omega + \hat\Omega Q^T\Omega_d - Q^T\dot\Omega_d$, because $\tfrac{d}{dt}Q^T = -\hat e_\Omega Q^T$ and $\hat e_\Omega Q^T\Omega_d = \hat\Omega Q^T\Omega_d - (Q^T\Omega_d)\times(Q^T\Omega_d) = \hat\Omega Q^T\Omega_d$. Multiply by $J$ and put in $J\dot\Omega = M - \Omega\times J\Omega$ with $M$ from line L7: the terms $\Omega\times J\Omega$ and $J(\hat\Omega Q^T\Omega_d - Q^T\dot\Omega_d)$ cancel, and $J\dot e_\Omega = -k_Re_R - k_\Omega e_\Omega$ remains. $\square$

**Theorem 1 (the attitude stays away from the upside down set).** Let $k_R, k_\Omega > 0$ and let $\lambda_M$ be the largest eigenvalue of $J$. If $\Psi(0) < 2$ and $\tfrac12\lambda_M\|e_\Omega(0)\|^2 < k_R(2 - \Psi(0))$, then $V_0 = \tfrac12 e_\Omega\cdot Je_\Omega + k_R\Psi$ does not increase, and $\Psi(t) \le \psi_2 < 2$ for all $t \ge 0$ with $\psi_2 = V_0(0)/k_R$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2, $\dot V_0 = e_\Omega\cdot(-k_Re_R - k_\Omega e_\Omega) + k_Re_R\cdot e_\Omega = -k_\Omega\|e_\Omega\|^2 \le 0$. So $k_R\Psi(t) \le V_0(t) \le V_0(0) \le \tfrac12\lambda_M\|e_\Omega(0)\|^2 + k_R\Psi(0) < 2k_R$. $\square$

This is the step that uses the hypothesis $\Psi(0) < 2$: the sublevel set $\{\Psi \le \psi_2\}$ with $\psi_2 < 2$ does not contain the rotation errors of angle $\pi$, where Lemma 1 gives $e_R = 0$. Version 1 of the paper on arXiv states the bound on $\|e_\Omega(0)\|$ with the smallest eigenvalue of $J$; the argument above needs the largest one, which gives a smaller, safe bound. The test `test_attitude_lyapunov_function_does_not_increase` integrates the attitude loop with the moment evaluated at every Runge-Kutta stage and checks that $V_0$ does not increase by more than $10^{-9}$ per step, from 4 random starts.

**Theorem 2 (exponential stability of the attitude; Proposition 1 of the paper).** Under the hypotheses of Theorem 1, the equilibrium $e_R = e_\Omega = 0$ is exponentially stable: $\|e_R\|$ and $\|e_\Omega\|$ decay as $e^{-\beta t}$ for some $\beta > 0$.

*Proof.* On $\{\Psi \le \psi_2\}$, Lemma 1 gives $\tfrac12\|e_R\|^2 \le \Psi \le \|e_R\|^2/(2 - \psi_2)$, because $\|e_R\|^2 = \sin^2\theta = \Psi(2 - \Psi)$. Take $V_2 = V_0 + c_2\,e_R\cdot e_\Omega$ with a small $c_2 > 0$. By Lemma 2, $\dot V_2 = -k_\Omega\|e_\Omega\|^2 - c_2k_R\,e_R\cdot J^{-1}e_R + c_2\,Ce_\Omega\cdot e_\Omega - c_2k_\Omega\,e_R\cdot J^{-1}e_\Omega$. With $\|C\| \le 1$ and $z = (\|e_R\|, \|e_\Omega\|)$, this is at most $-z^TW_2z$ with $W_2 = \begin{pmatrix} c_2k_R/\lambda_M & -c_2k_\Omega/(2\lambda_m) \\ -c_2k_\Omega/(2\lambda_m) & k_\Omega - c_2\end{pmatrix}$, where $\lambda_m$ is the smallest eigenvalue of $J$. For $c_2$ small enough, $W_2$ is positive definite, and $V_2$ lies between two positive definite quadratic forms in $z$. Then $\dot V_2 \le -\beta' V_2$ for some $\beta' > 0$, and the decay follows. $\square$

**Theorem 3 (the position loop; Proposition 2 of the paper, quoted).** Assume the commanded force $A$ of line L2 is never zero, $b_{1d}$ is never parallel to $A$, and $\|mge_3 + m\ddot x_d\| < B$ for a known $B$. If $\Psi(0) < 1$ and the gains satisfy the inequalities of Proposition 2 of Lee, Leok and McClamroch, then the zero equilibrium of $(e_x, e_v, e_R, e_\Omega)$ is exponentially stable. If $1 \le \Psi(0) < 2$, the attitude first enters $\{\Psi < 1\}$ in finite time by Theorems 1 and 2, and from there the full error converges exponentially; the paper calls this almost global exponential attractiveness (Proposition 3).

*Proof sketch.* The thrust of line L3 is $f = A\cdot Re_3$, so $fRe_3 = A + X$ with $X = (A\cdot Re_3)Re_3 - A$. When $R = R_d$, $Re_3 = A/\|A\|$ and $X = 0$. In general $\|X\| = \|A\|\sin\phi$, where $\phi$ is the angle between $Re_3$ and $R_de_3$. A rotation by $\theta$ turns no unit vector by more than $\theta$, so $\phi \le \theta$. On $\{\Psi < 1\}$ the angle $\theta$ is less than $\pi/2$, so $\sin\phi \le \sin\theta = \|e_R\|$ by Lemma 1, and $\|X\| \le \|A\|\,\|e_R\| \le (k_x\|e_x\| + k_v\|e_v\| + B)\|e_R\|$. The position error then obeys $m\dot e_v = -k_xe_x - k_ve_v + X$, a stable linear system with a perturbation that decays with the attitude error. A Lyapunov function that is the sum of a quadratic form in $(e_x, e_v)$ and $V_2$, with cross terms, has a negative definite derivative under the gain conditions. The full argument is in the appendix of the paper. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the change of frame).** Let $P = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1, -1)$, the rotation by $\pi$ about $x$. If $(x, v, R)$ is a state in the z-up frame, then $(Px, Pv, PRP)$ is the same physical state in the z-down frame of the paper, with the body axes also turned by $P$, and the paper's thrust $f = -(-k_xe_x - k_ve_v - mge_3 + m\ddot x_d)\cdot Re_3$ equals the thrust $A\cdot Re_3$ of line L3.

*Proof.* The z-down force is $A' = P(-k_xe_x - k_ve_v + m\ddot x_d) - mge_3$, and $-mge_3 = P(mge_3)$, so $A' = PA$. The paper's body $z$ axis is $PRPe_3 = -PRe_3$. Then $-A'\cdot(-PRe_3) = PA\cdot PRe_3 = A\cdot Re_3$, because $P$ is orthogonal. $\square$

The test `test_thrust_matches_the_z_down_form_of_the_paper` checks Lemma 3 on 100 random states to $10^{-9}$.

**What the implementation adds.** Line L5 estimates $\Omega_d$ by $\log(R_{d,k-1}^TR_{d,k})^\vee/\Delta t$ and $\dot\Omega_d$ by a backward difference of that. This lags the true values by about half a period, so the hypotheses of Lemma 2 hold only approximately, and a small steady error remains on a moving reference. The test `test_tracks_an_elliptic_helix` measures it: below 5 mm after 5 s with $\Delta t = 5$ ms. The tests `test_hover_from_an_offset` and `test_recovery_from_almost_upside_down` check convergence to the set point from an offset and from a rotation error of 178 degrees.
