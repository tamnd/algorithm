The grasp wrench space of a grasp is the set of all forces and moments that the fingers can apply to the object when their forces are limited. Force closure asks whether the fingers can resist every wrench if they squeeze hard enough; the grasp wrench space asks which wrenches they can resist with a fixed budget of force. It is a convex polytope in the 3-dimensional wrench space of the plane or the 6-dimensional wrench space of space. The grasp is in [force closure](/force-closure/) exactly when the origin is inside it, and the size and shape of the polytope around the origin is the basis of the [Ferrari-Canny metric](/ferrari-canny-metric/) and many other [grasp quality measures](/grasp-quality-measures/).

## Definition

A rigid object is held by $c$ contacts. The friction cone of contact $i$ is replaced by a [polyhedral friction cone](/polyhedral-friction-cone/) with edges $e_{i1}, \ldots, e_{ik}$, each scaled so that its normal component is 1. Each edge gives a **primitive wrench** in the object frame,

$$w_{ij} = (p_i \times e_{ij},\ e_{ij}),$$

written moment first. A contact force $\sum_j a_{ij} e_{ij}$ with $a_{ij} \ge 0$ has normal component $\sum_j a_{ij}$ and applies the wrench $\sum_j a_{ij} w_{ij}$. Frictionless contacts have $k = 1$ and $e_{i1} = n_i$.

The force budget can be set in two ways (Ferrari and Canny 1992).

**$L_1$ grasp wrench space.** The sum of the normal forces of all fingers is at most 1:

$$W_{L_1} = \Big\{ \sum_{i,j} a_{ij} w_{ij} : a_{ij} \ge 0,\ \sum_{i,j} a_{ij} \le 1 \Big\} = \operatorname{conv}\Big(\{0\} \cup \bigcup_{i,j} \{w_{ij}\}\Big).$$

**$L_\infty$ grasp wrench space.** The normal force of each finger is at most 1:

$$W_{L_\infty} = \Big\{ \sum_{i,j} a_{ij} w_{ij} : a_{ij} \ge 0,\ \sum_j a_{ij} \le 1 \text{ for each } i \Big\} = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{c} \operatorname{conv}\big(\{0, w_{i1}, \ldots, w_{ik}\}\big),$$

where $\bigoplus$ is the [Minkowski sum](/minkowski-sum/). The Handbook defines the wrench space in this second way, for frictionless contacts.

The $L_1$ bound models a power supply shared by all fingers; the $L_\infty$ bound models fingers with separate motors of equal strength. With the exact friction cones in place of pyramids, the same two definitions apply with the edges replaced by the whole section of each cone at normal force 1; the sets are then convex but not polytopes.

**Force closure.** The grasp is in force closure exactly when the origin is in the interior of $W_{L_1}$, or equivalently of $W_{L_\infty}$. Indeed, the cone generated by either set is the cone of all contact wrenches, and a convex set that contains the origin generates the whole space as a cone exactly when the origin is in its interior. When the grasp is in force closure, the origin is in the interior of the convex hull of the primitive wrenches, so the point $0$ in the formula for $W_{L_1}$ can be dropped: $W_{L_1}$ is the [convex hull](/convex-hull/) of the $ck$ primitive wrenches.

Properties that follow from the definition:

- Both sets are convex polytopes, and $W_{L_1} \subseteq W_{L_\infty} \subseteq c\, W_{L_1}$. The first inclusion holds because a total of at most 1 bounds each finger by 1. The second holds because $c$ fingers with at most 1 each have a total of at most $c$.
- Both grow when a contact is added or when $\mu$ grows, because the set of allowed coefficients grows.
- Both depend on the reference point of the moments and on the length unit, because a moment is a force times a length. Moving the reference point shears the polytope; changing the unit stretches its moment axes. Their shape, and every measure built on it, changes with these choices; only the question whether the origin is interior does not.
- $W_{L_1}$ has at most $ck$ vertices. In $\mathbb{R}^6$ its number of facets can grow like the cube of the number of vertices, by the upper bound theorem for polytopes. $W_{L_\infty}$ is a Minkowski sum of $c$ polytopes, and it can have many more vertices.

## Examples and non-examples

**Two fingers on a box (planar).** Fingers at $(-1, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$, with inward normals $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$ and $\mu = 0.5$. In the plane each cone has two edges, $n \pm \mu t$. With the moment $p_x f_y - p_y f_x$, the primitive wrenches $(m_z, f_x, f_y)$ are

$$w_{11} = (-0.5, 1, 0.5),\ w_{12} = (0.5, 1, -0.5),\ w_{21} = (0.5, -1, 0.5),\ w_{22} = (-0.5, -1, -0.5).$$

They sum to zero, so the origin is their centroid and is inside. $W_{L_1}$ is the tetrahedron with these four vertices, and its faces lie at the distance $1/3$ from the origin. $W_{L_\infty}$ is the sum of the two triangles $\operatorname{conv}\{0, w_{11}, w_{12}\}$ and $\operatorname{conv}\{0, w_{21}, w_{22}\}$, a larger polytope whose faces are at least $0.5774$ from the origin. These two distances are the [Ferrari-Canny](/ferrari-canny-metric/) values of the grasp.

**The same grasp in other units.** Measure lengths in a unit in which the half-width of the box is $L$ instead of 1. The moment components of all primitive wrenches scale by $L$, and the faces of $W_{L_1}$ lie at the distance $L / \sqrt{4 + 5L^2}$ from the origin. For $L = 2$ this is $0.4082$; for $L = 0.5$ it is $0.2182$. The grasp did not change; the polytope did.

**A frictionless square.** The square of the [form closure](/form-closure/) page with four frictionless contacts has primitive wrenches $(-0.5, 1, 0)$, $(-0.2, -1, 0)$, $(0.3, 0, 1)$ and $(0.4, 0, -1)$. Their convex hull is a tetrahedron with the origin inside, at the distance $0.2665$ from its nearest face.

**Non-example: a grasp that is not closed.** The pinwheel contacts on the same square give four primitive wrenches with the moment $-0.5$ each. They lie in the plane $m_z = -0.5$, so $W_{L_1}$ is flat and does not contain the origin: no budget of force lets the grasp resist a moment of the other sign.

**Non-example: the task wrench space.** The set of wrenches that a task will put on the object, for example the weight and the expected tool forces, is a different set, often called the task wrench space. A grasp suits the task if a scaled copy of the grasp wrench space contains it.

## Related definitions

- [Ferrari-Canny metric](/ferrari-canny-metric/): the radius of the largest ball about the origin inside $W_{L_1}$ or $W_{L_\infty}$.
- [Grasp quality measures](/grasp-quality-measures/): measures from the volume of the grasp wrench space, from the [grasp matrix](/grasp-matrix/), and from the task.
- [Force closure](/force-closure/): the origin is in the interior of the grasp wrench space.
- [Contact wrench cone](/contact-wrench-cone/): the cone of wrenches that a flat contact of a foot can apply; the unbounded relative of the grasp wrench space used in legged locomotion.
- [Minkowski sum](/minkowski-sum/) and [quickhull](/quickhull/): the operations that build the two polytopes.
- [Support function](/support-function/): the support function of the grasp wrench space in a direction $u$ is the largest wrench component along $u$ that the grasp can apply, $\max_{i,j} u^T w_{ij}$ for $W_{L_1}$ of a force-closure grasp.
