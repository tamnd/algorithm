---
title: The octree occupancy map represents the clamped voxel map exactly, and its shape depends only on that map
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

We use the names of the pseudocode. The map has depth $D \ge 1$, so the voxel keys are the triples $k = (k_x, k_y, k_z)$ with $0 \le k_x, k_y, k_z < 2^D$. For a depth $d \le D$ and a key $k$, the *block* $B_d(k)$ is the set of keys that agree with $k$ in the $d$ highest bits of each coordinate; it has $2^{D - d}$ voxels on each side, $B_0(k)$ is the whole map and $B_D(k) = \{k\}$. The eight blocks of depth $d + 1$ inside a block of depth $d$ are its *octants*, and the octant that holds $k$ has the number $c(k, d)$ of line L4, made of bit $D - 1 - d$ of each coordinate. We assume exact arithmetic for the values; the clamp of line L7 and the comparisons of line L10 are exact in floating point too, so the only rounding is in the sums of line L7, and it is the same rounding as in a direct per-voxel computation.

**The abstract map.** The abstract value is a partial map $M$ from keys to log odds: $M(k)$ is undefined (the voxel is unknown) or a number in $[l_{\min}, l_{\max}]$. The empty map has every voxel unknown. The operation $\mathrm{UPDATE}(k, l)$ sets $M(k) \leftarrow \min(l_{\max}, \max(l_{\min}, M_0(k) + l))$, where $M_0(k) = M(k)$ if it is defined and $0$ (the log odds of the prior $0.5$) if not. $\mathrm{SEARCH}(k, d)$ asks for $\max\{M(v) : v \in B_d(k), M(v) \text{ defined}\}$, or "unknown" if the block has no known voxel.

**Invariant (T).** Each node $n$ has a depth $d(n)$ and a block $B(n)$; the root has depth 0 and the whole map as its block, and child number $c$ of a node of depth $d$ has depth $d + 1$ and the octant number $c$ of its parent's block.

1. *Shape.* A node is a leaf or an inner node with 8 child slots. Inner nodes have depth below $D$. Every inner node other than the root has at least one child.
2. *Abstraction.* A voxel $k$ is known in $M$ if and only if the descent from the root along the octants of $k$ reaches a leaf $n$ (with $k \in B(n)$); then $M(k)$ is the value of that leaf. So every voxel of the block of a leaf is known with the value of the leaf.
3. *Inner values.* The value of an inner node with children is the maximum of the values of its children. The value of the root with no children is "unknown".
4. *Bounds.* Every leaf value is in $[l_{\min}, l_{\max}]$.
5. *Fully pruned.* No inner node has 8 children that are all leaves with one equal value.

**Lemma 1 (block maxima).** Under parts 1 to 3, the value of every inner node $n$ with children is $\max\{M(v) : v \in B(n) \text{ known}\}$, and every node other than an empty root has at least one known voxel in its block. *Proof.* By induction from the leaves up. A leaf has all voxels of its block known with its value (part 2). An inner node $n$ has at least one child (part 1), the known voxels of $B(n)$ are the union of the known voxels of the blocks of its children (part 2: a voxel in an octant without a child is unknown), and the maximum of the children's block maxima is the block maximum of $n$ (part 3). $\square$

**Lemma 2 (search).** Under (T), SEARCH$(k, d_q)$ returns the answer of the abstract map. *Proof.* Lines L12 to L14 descend along the octants of $k$, one depth per step, and stop at depth $d_q$, at a leaf, or at a missing child. If a child is missing at depth $d \le d_q$, then by part 2 every voxel of that octant is unknown, so $B_{d_q}(k)$, which lies inside it, has no known voxel: line L15 answers "unknown", which is right. If the descent stops at a leaf $n$ of depth $d \le d_q$, then $B_{d_q}(k) \subseteq B(n)$, all its voxels are known with the value of $n$ (part 2), so the block maximum is that value. If it stops at an inner node of depth $d_q$, Lemma 1 gives the block maximum, and for the empty root part 3 gives "unknown". The loop runs at most $d_q \le D$ times. $\square$

With $d_q = D$ this returns $M(k)$, the value of the voxel.

**Lemma 3 (the descent of UPDATE).** After lines L1 to L6, the path $P$ holds the nodes of depth $0, \ldots, D - 1$ on the way to $k$, $n$ is a leaf of depth $D$ with $B(n) = \{k\}$, and its value is $M_0(k)$. The abstract map is unchanged, except that $k$ may now be known with the value $0$ if it was unknown. *Proof.* Line L3 replaces a leaf $n$ of depth $d < D$ by an inner node with 8 leaf children that carry its value. The blocks of the children cover $B(n)$, so by part 2 the abstract map does not change. Line L5 creates a missing child. At depth $d + 1 < D$ it is an inner node with no children, which makes no voxel known; at depth $D$ it is a leaf with the value 0, which makes exactly the voxel $k$ known with the value $0 = M_0(k)$. Since the descent follows the octants of $k$, at depth $D$ the node $n$ is the leaf of the single voxel $k$. If $k$ was known before, the descent reached its leaf, possibly through expansions, and that leaf (or the copy of its value made by line L3) holds $M(k) = M_0(k)$. $\square$

During the descent, part 1 can fail for a moment (a new inner node has no children yet), and parts 3 and 5 can fail on the path. Line L7 then sets the value of the leaf of $k$ to the new abstract value $\min(l_{\max}, \max(l_{\min}, M_0(k) + l_{\mathrm{meas}}))$, so part 2 holds for the updated map and part 4 holds for this leaf.

**Lemma 4 (the ascent of UPDATE).** After lines L8 to L11, (T) holds for the new map. *Proof.* Only the nodes on the path differ from a state where (T) held, and the path is processed from depth $D - 1$ up to the root. Claim: when line L9 pops a node $q$ of depth $d$, all nodes below depth $d$ (the subtrees of the children of $q$) satisfy parts 1 to 5. This holds for $d = D - 1$, since the children of $q$ are leaves of depth $D$ in the bounds. For the step, consider $q$. Each child of $q$ is either the child on the path, which the previous iteration fixed, or a node off the path, which no line touched since (T) last held, or a leaf created by line L3 with a value in the bounds. So the claim holds for the subtrees of the children. If line L10 finds 8 children that are leaves with one value $v$, it makes $q$ a leaf with value $v$: the blocks of the children cover $B(q)$, all their voxels are known with $v$, so part 2 holds for $q$, and part 4 holds since $v$ is a child's value. Otherwise line L11 sets the value of $q$ to the maximum of its children, which exist (the child on the path does), so parts 1 and 3 hold at $q$, and part 5 holds at $q$ because the test of line L10 failed. After the root is processed, (T) holds everywhere. $\square$

The child on the path is always present when line L10 runs: it was created or kept by the descent, and a pruning one level lower turns it into a leaf, it does not remove it. So line L11 never takes the maximum of an empty set.

**Theorem 1 (correctness).** Starting from the empty map and applying any sequence of UPDATE calls, the tree satisfies (T) for the abstract map of the same sequence of updates, and SEARCH answers as the abstract map. *Proof.* The empty tree (a root with no children and the value "unknown") satisfies (T) for the empty map. Lemmas 3 and 4 show that each UPDATE keeps (T) for the updated abstract map, and Lemma 2 gives the answers. $\square$

**Theorem 2 (canonical shape).** For each abstract map $M$ there is exactly one tree that satisfies (T): a node exists for a block if and only if the block has a known voxel and its parent block is not uniform, and a node is a leaf if and only if all voxels of its block are known with one value. So the number of nodes and the shape of the tree depend only on $M$, not on the order of the updates that produced it. *Proof.* Let a block be *uniform* if all its voxels are known with one value. By part 2, the block of a leaf is uniform. Conversely, let $n$ be an inner node with a uniform block. Its children cover the block, so all 8 exist (a missing child means unknown voxels); take one of the deepest inner nodes in the subtree of $n$ whose block is uniform. Its 8 children have uniform blocks and are leaves, since they are deeper. They carry the same value, against part 5. So a node is a leaf if and only if its block is uniform. A node of a block exists if and only if its parent node exists and is an inner node, and has a child there; by part 1 and Lemma 1 a child exists exactly when its octant has a known voxel. By induction from the root, the set of nodes is the set of blocks with a known voxel whose ancestors are not uniform. $\square$

**Termination.** The loop of lines L2 to L6 runs $D$ times, and the loop of lines L8 to L11 pops the $D$ nodes of the path, with constant work per node (8 children). SEARCH runs at most $D$ times.

**INSERT-SCAN.** Lines L17 to L20 build the set $O$ of end voxels and the set $F$ of the other voxels that the beams cross; the traversal of each beam is the 3D form of the traversal proved on [the occupancy grid map](/occupancy-grid-map/), with three kinds of crossings instead of two, and it returns, for a beam through no edge or corner of a voxel, exactly the voxels whose open cube meets the beam. Lines L21 and L22 call UPDATE once for each voxel of $F \setminus O$ with $l_{\mathrm{free}}$ and once for each voxel of $O$ with $l_{\mathrm{occ}}$, only for keys inside the map. The abstract UPDATE of one voxel does not change any other voxel, so updates of different voxels commute, and the result does not depend on the order of the two loops or of the sets. So after INSERT-SCAN, by Theorem 1, every voxel of the scan has received exactly one clamped update, and the end voxels have received $l_{\mathrm{occ}}$ even when another beam of the same scan crossed them. $\blacksquare$
