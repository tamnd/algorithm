We prove two things about the pseudocode of the page (lines L1 to L12). First, each static transaction whose record becomes SUCCESS takes effect at one point, the SC that sets its status, and a record that does not become SUCCESS has no effect. So the transactions are linearizable as $k$-word read-modify-write operations, and the user function $f$ only ever runs on a consistent snapshot. Second, the STM is lock-free. The second proof is a sketch, and we mark it so.

**Model.** The processes are asynchronous and may crash. The shared words are the data words, one ownership word own$(a)$ per data word $a$, and, for each record $r$, a status word status$(r)$ and a word old$(r)$. The primitives are atomic read and an ideal LL/SC: SC$(x, v)$ by $p$ succeeds if and only if no successful write to $x$ happened since the last LL$(x)$ of $p$, and reads of other words between them do not cancel the link. Only SC writes to shared words after the initial state. Times are positions in the run.

A record $r$ is created by its *initiator* at L12 with a sorted data set $D(r)$ and a function $f_r$, and is never changed after that. Any process that runs TRANSACTION$(r, \cdot)$ is a *helper* of $r$; the initiator is one of them. status$(r)$ starts as NONE and is set at most once, by the SC at L2 (to SUCCESS) or at L8 (to FAILURE), because both SCs follow an LL that returned NONE and status$(r)$ never returns to NONE. We say $r$ is *decided* once status$(r) \ne$ NONE. For a SUCCESS record, $T_r$ is the time of the SC that set SUCCESS, and $\text{new}_r = f_r(\text{old}_r)$, where old$_r$ is the content of old$(r)$ once it covers $D(r)$ (Lemma 3 shows it is well defined).

**Lemma 1 (ownership).** (a) A successful SC on own$(a)$ that writes $r$ happens only when $\mathrm{own}(a) = \mathrm{NONE}$. (b) own$(a)$ changes from $r$ to another value only by a SC at L11 by a helper of $r$, after that helper has seen $r$ decided. (c) If $\mathrm{status}(r) = \mathrm{SUCCESS}$, then $r$ owns every $a \in D(r)$ from $T_r$ until the first SC at L11 for $r$ on $a$.

*Proof.* (a) The SC at L7 runs only if the LL just before it returned NONE, and it succeeds only if own$(a)$ was not written since then. (b) The SCs on own$(a)$ are at L7 (from NONE, by (a)) and at L11. The SC at L11 for $r$ follows an LL that returned $r$, so if it succeeds it changes own$(a)$ from $r$ to NONE. A helper reaches L11 only from L3 or L4, after it read status$(r) \ne$ NONE. (c) The helper $h$ that set SUCCESS at L2 had, for each $a \in D(r)$, an LL at L5 that returned $r$, and then an LL of status$(r)$ that returned NONE, with no write to status$(r)$ up to the SC. At each of these LLs at L5, $r$ was undecided, so by (b) no L11 for $r$ had yet happened, and by (b) nothing else takes $a$ from $r$. So at $T_r$, $r$ owns all of $D(r)$, and by (b) it keeps $a$ until an L11 for $r$ on $a$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (writes to data words).** Every successful SC on a data word $a$ is made at L10 by a helper of a record $r$ with $\mathrm{status}(r) = \mathrm{SUCCESS}$, writes new$_r(a)$, and happens while own$(a) = r$.

*Proof.* By induction over the successful SCs on data words in time order. Take one, by helper $h$ of $r$, with its LL of $a$ at $t_1$ returning $u \ne v = \text{new}_r(a)$ (the test at L10), its read of own$(a) = r$ at $t_2 > t_1$, and its SC at $t_3 > t_2$. There is no successful write to $a$ in $(t_1, t_3)$. $h$ read $\mathrm{status}(r) = \mathrm{SUCCESS}$ at L3, before $t_1$. By the induction hypothesis, every earlier write to $a$ made while own$(a) = r$ wrote $v$.

We show that $r$ owns $a$ during all of $[t_2, t_3]$. Suppose not. Then by Lemma 1(b) the first SC at L11 for $r$ on $a$ happens at some $t_\text{rel} \in (t_2, t_3)$, by a helper $h'$ that finished its own loop at L10 for $a$ before $t_\text{rel}$. Let $t'_1$ be the LL of $a$ by $h'$; $t'_1 > T_r$, so by Lemma 1(c) $r$ owns $a$ in $[t'_1, t_\text{rel}]$. There are three cases for $h'$ at $a$:

1. $h'$ saw $a = v$ at $t'_1$ and skipped.
2. The SC of $h'$ succeeded and wrote $v$ at some time in $(t'_1, t_\text{rel})$.
3. The SC of $h'$ failed, so some write to $a$ happened in $(t'_1, t_\text{rel})$, while $r$ owned $a$; by the hypothesis it wrote $v$.

In each case $a = v$ at some time $s \in [t'_1, t_\text{rel})$. If $s > t_1$, then a write at $s$ or the value at $s$ conflicts with the facts that no write happened in $(t_1, t_3)$ and that $a = u \ne v$ at $t_1$. If $s \le t_1$, then from $s$ to $t_1$ the word $a$ is owned by $r$ (both times lie in $[t'_1, t_\text{rel}]$ or $t'_1 \le s$), so by the hypothesis every write in between wrote $v$, and $a = v$ at $t_1$, again a contradiction. So $r$ owns $a$ at $t_3$, and the SC writes $v = \text{new}_r(a)$ while own$(a) = r$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (agreed old values).** For a SUCCESS record $r$, each entry $(a, x)$ of old$(r)$ has $x$ equal to the value of $a$ at $T_r$, and each $a \in D(r)$ gets exactly one entry. So all helpers compute the same old$_r$ and the same new$_r$.

*Proof.* An entry for $a$ is added by a SC at L9 whose LL saw old$(r)$ without $a$; the SC fails if any other entry was added since, so each $a$ gets one entry and entries are never removed. The read of $a$ for the entry happens at $t$ between that LL and the SC, after the helper read SUCCESS at L3, so $t > T_r$. During $(T_r, t)$, $r$ owns $a$: no L11 for $r$ can come before some helper finished L10, which needs a complete old$(r)$, and old$(r)$ is not complete at $t$ (it has no entry for $a$ from the LL to the SC). By Lemma 2, the only writes to $a$ while $r$ owns it are L10 writes of helpers of $r$, and they also need a complete old$(r)$. So $a$ is not written in $[T_r, t]$. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (the update completes).** For a SUCCESS record $r$, at the first SC at L11 for $r$ on any word, every $a \in D(r)$ with $a \in$ new$_r$ holds new$_r(a)$, and it keeps that value until $r$ releases $a$.

*Proof.* The first L11 follows a full pass of some helper over L10. For each $a$, the three cases in the proof of Lemma 2 show that $a = \mathrm{new}_r(a)$ at some time during that pass, while $r$ owns $a$. After that, by Lemma 2, the only writes while $r$ owns $a$ write the same value. $\square$

**Theorem 1 (atomicity).** Order the SUCCESS records by $T_r$. Then (i) a record that is never SUCCESS changes no data word; (ii) each SUCCESS record $r$ reads, as old$_r$, the state that the records before it in this order produce from the initial state, and its writes new$_r$ take effect in that state; (iii) $T_r$ lies between the creation of $r$ at L12 and the return of its initiator. So the static transactions are linearizable, and each run of $f$ sees a consistent snapshot.

*Proof.* (i) is Lemma 2. For (ii), take $a \in D(r)$. By Lemma 1(c), $r$ owns $a$ at $T_r$. Every SUCCESS record $q$ with $a \in D(q)$ and $T_q < T_r$ owned $a$ at $T_q$, so by Lemma 1(a) it released $a$ before $r$ acquired it, and by Lemma 4 $a$ held new$_q(a)$ (if $q$ writes $a$) when $q$ released it. No data word is written while it is free (Lemma 2), and the only owner between $q$ and $r$ is a record that writes nothing unless it is SUCCESS with $T$ between $T_q$ and $T_r$. So the value of $a$ at $T_r$, which is old$_r(a)$ by Lemma 3, is the value that the earlier SUCCESS records give. Each record $q$ with $T_q > T_r$ and $a \in D(q)$ acquires $a$ only after $r$ releases it, when $a = \mathrm{new}_r(a)$ by Lemma 4. (iii) $T_r$ is after the creation of $r$, and the initiator returns SUCCESS only after it read SUCCESS at L3. Every helper, the initiator included, applies $f_r$ only to old$_r$, a consistent snapshot. $\blacksquare$

Since the transactions of this STM never show $f$ a value outside old$_r$, there is no zombie: a failed attempt has run no user code at all. In the history that the implementation records, the reads of an attempt appear only when it commits, so the opacity check of the tests is a check of Theorem 1.

**Theorem 2 (lock-freedom; proof sketch).** In every infinite run in which processes keep taking steps, records keep becoming SUCCESS.

*Sketch.* Suppose that after some time $t_0$ no record becomes SUCCESS, while processes keep taking steps. Each call of ACQUIRE ends in a bounded number of failed SCs, because each failed SC at L7 is caused by a successful SC on own$(a)$ by another process, and after $t_0$ there are only finitely many owners to acquire and release. Each helper call of TRANSACTION decides its record: L2 or L8 sets the status, or another helper already did. So after $t_0$ records are created, decided FAILURE and released, again and again. A record $r$ fails at the word $a$ that is owned by some record $o$. Since records acquire in increasing address order and keep, while undecided, every word they acquired, an undecided owner $o$ that blocks $r$ at $a$ can itself fail only at a word $a' > a$, owned by a third record. A chain of such failures has strictly increasing addresses, so it has at most $m$ links. Take the record $o^*$ that, among the undecided records, owns the highest address it needs. Every helper of $o^*$ (and each initiator that fails because of $o^*$ helps it) finds the remaining words of $o^*$ free or owned by records that are already decided, whose helpers release them in a bounded number of steps. So $o^*$ is not decided FAILURE by a conflict; it becomes SUCCESS, a contradiction. The helping at L4 is what lets a stopped initiator's record finish: without it, a crash after L1 leaves its words owned for ever, and the test `test_without_helping_a_stopped_owner_blocks_the_others` shows that run. $\square$

The full proof of Theorem 2 needs care with records that are decided but not yet released, and with helpers that acquire a word for a record that was decided after their check at L6 (Lemma 1 allows this; the next helper of that record releases the word). The original paper gives the full argument.
