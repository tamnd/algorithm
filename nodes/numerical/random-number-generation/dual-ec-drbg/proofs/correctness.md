title: Dual_EC_DRBG generate outputs truncated x coordinates of s_j Q for s_j = x(s_(j-1) P), and P = eQ lets one compute the next state from one block
proves: [correctness]
status: full

Let $E$ be the curve P-256 over $\mathbb{F}_p$, with group of prime order $n$ generated by $P$, and let $Q$ be a point of order $n$. For a finite point $R$ let $x(R)$ be its $x$ coordinate read as an integer in $[0, p)$, and set $x(\mathcal{O}) = 0$. We use the pseudocode of the page, with $\mathit{outlen} = 240$, and assume the reseed check at line L1 passes. Let $m = \lceil n_{\mathrm{bits}} / 240 \rceil$, where $n_{\mathrm{bits}}$ is the requested length.

**Lemma 1 (output).** Let $t_0$ be the value of $t$ after line L2. The loop L4 to L7 runs exactly $m$ times. Its $j$-th pass sets $s_j = x(t_{j-1} P)$ with $t_{j-1} = s_{j-1}$ for $j \ge 2$, and appends $x(s_j Q) \bmod 2^{240}$ to $W$. On exit the state is $s_m$, and line L8 replaces it by $x(s_m P)$.

*Proof.* By induction on the number of passes: after $j$ passes $|W| = 240 j$, $t = s_j$, and $W$ is the concatenation of the first $j$ blocks. The test at line L7 succeeds for the first time when $240 j \ge n_{\mathrm{bits}}$, that is, at $j = m$. The counter increases once per block. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (points with a given $x$).** If $R$ and $R'$ are finite points of $E$ with $x(R) = x(R')$, then $R' = R$ or $R' = -R$.

*Proof.* For a given $x$, the equation $y^2 = x^3 - 3x + b$ has at most two solutions $\pm y$ in the field $\mathbb{F}_p$, and $-(x, y) = (x, -y)$. $\square$

**Proposition 3 (the trapdoor).** Suppose $P = eQ$ for an integer $e$. Let $s$ be an integer with $sQ \ne \mathcal{O}$, and let $R$ be any point of $E$ with $x(R) = x(sQ)$. Then $x(eR) = x(sP)$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2, $R = \pm sQ$. Then $eR = \pm s(eQ) = \pm sP$, and $x(-T) = x(T)$ for every point $T$ (including $T = \mathcal{O}$ by our convention). $\square$

**Corollary 4 (state recovery).** Within one request, let the $j$-th block be $r_j = x(s_j Q) \bmod 2^{240}$, with $s_j Q \ne \mathcal{O}$. Write $x(s_j Q) = h \cdot 2^{240} + r_j$ with $0 \le h < 2^{16}$. For this $h$, the integer $X = h \cdot 2^{240} + r_j$ is the $x$ coordinate of a curve point $R$, and $x(eR) = s_{j+1}$. Hence the set of candidates computed by the search over all $h \in [0, 2^{16})$ contains $s_{j+1}$, and $s_{j+1}$ determines every later block of the request and the state $x(s_m P)$ left at line L8.

*Proof.* The first claim holds because $X = x(s_j Q)$ and $s_j Q$ is a curve point. Proposition 3 with $s = s_j$ gives $x(eR) = x(s_j P) = s_{j+1}$ by Lemma 1. The later blocks are $x(s_k Q) \bmod 2^{240}$ with $s_{k+1} = x(s_k P)$, computable from $s_{j+1}$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Filtering.* Each wrong candidate survives a check against the next $b$ output bits only if its predicted block agrees on those bits. Heuristically, treating the predicted blocks of wrong candidates as uniform, the expected number of wrong survivors is at most $2^{16 - b}$; with $b = 16$ it is at most 1, and a second check removes the rest. This heuristic is not a theorem; in the experiment on the page exactly one candidate survived 16 bits.
- *Why the theorem does not break the generator by itself.* Computing $e$ from $P$ and $Q$ is a discrete logarithm problem in a group of order about $2^{256}$, for which the best known algorithms need about $2^{128}$ group operations. The proposition says that the security of the generator against a party depends on whether that party knows $e$.
- *Agreement with the standard.* The theorem of Lemma 1 is the generate process of SP 800-90A (2012), Section 10.3.1.4, for P-256, including the final state update; the known-answer tests from the OpenSSL FIPS module confirm the implementation. $\blacksquare$
