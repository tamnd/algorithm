---
title: Radau IIA with s stages has order 2s - 1, stability function equal to the (s - 1, s) Pade approximant, and R(infinity) = 0
proves: [correctness, termination, convergence]
status: full
---

**Setting.** $f : [t_0, T] \times \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$ is $C^{2s}$ with bounded derivatives near the solution and Lipschitz in $y$ with constant $L$. The method is the $s$-stage Runge-Kutta method with Butcher tableau $(A, b, c)$, where $c_1 < \dots < c_s = 1$ are the zeros of $P_s(x) = \frac{d^{s-1}}{dx^{s-1}}\big(x^{s-1}(x - 1)^s\big)$, $a_{ij} = \int_0^{c_i} \ell_j(\tau)\,d\tau$ and $b_j = \int_0^1 \ell_j(\tau)\,d\tau$, with $\ell_j$ the Lagrange basis polynomials for the nodes $c$.

**Step 1: the nodes give a quadrature of order 2s - 1.** $x^{s-1}(x-1)^s$ has zeros of order $s - 1$ at 0 and $s$ at 1, so by Rolle's theorem applied $s - 1$ times, $P_s$ has $s$ distinct zeros in $(0, 1]$, one of them $x = 1$. For any polynomial $q$ of degree at most $s - 2$, integrating by parts $s - 1$ times gives $\int_0^1 P_s(x) q(x)\,dx = 0$, because every boundary term contains a derivative of order below $s - 1$ of $x^{s-1}(x-1)^s$, which vanishes at both ends. Write a polynomial $g$ of degree at most $2s - 2$ as $g = P_s q + r$ with $\deg q \le s - 2$ and $\deg r \le s - 1$. Then $\int_0^1 g = \int_0^1 r = \sum_j b_j r(c_j) = \sum_j b_j g(c_j)$, since the interpolatory rule is exact for degree $s - 1$ and $P_s(c_j) = 0$. So condition $B(2s - 1)$ holds: $\sum_i b_i c_i^{k-1} = 1/k$ for $k \le 2s - 1$. The rule is not exact for $g(x) = (x - 1)\prod_{j<s}(x - c_j)^2$, of degree $2s - 1$: it vanishes at every node, so the rule gives 0, but $g < 0$ on $[0, 1)$ away from the nodes, so its integral is negative. Hence the quadrature order is exactly $2s - 1$.

**Step 2: collocation.** By construction $\sum_j a_{ij} c_j^{k-1} = \int_0^{c_i} \tau^{k-1}d\tau = c_i^k/k$ for $k \le s$ (condition $C(s)$), because interpolation at $s$ nodes reproduces polynomials of degree $s - 1$. Let $u$ be the polynomial of degree at most $s$ with $u(t_n) = y_n$ and $u'(t_n + c_i h) = f(t_n + c_i h, u(t_n + c_i h))$. Writing $u'(t_n + \tau h) = \sum_j k_j \ell_j(\tau)$ with $k_j = f(t_n + c_j h, Y_j)$ and integrating gives $Y_i = u(t_n + c_i h) = y_n + h \sum_j a_{ij} k_j$ and $u(t_n + h) = y_n + h \sum_j b_j k_j$. So one Radau IIA step is collocation, and conversely every solution of the stage equations defines such a $u$. Since $c_s = 1$, $b_j = a_{sj}$ and $y_{n+1} = Y_s$ (line L9).

**Step 3: local error (proved).** Let $y$ be the exact solution through $(t_n, y_n)$ and $\delta(t) = u'(t) - f(t, u(t))$ the defect, which vanishes at the $s$ nodes. By the Alekseev-Grobner formula, $y(t_n + h) - u(t_n + h) = -\int_{t_n}^{t_n+h} \Phi(t_n + h, \sigma, u(\sigma))\,\delta(\sigma)\,d\sigma$, where $\Phi(t, \sigma, v) = \partial y(t; \sigma, v)/\partial v$ is the resolvent of the variational equation. The integrand is $C^{2s-1}$ in $\sigma$ with derivatives bounded independently of $h$ (as $u$ and its derivatives are bounded by the collocation equations for $h$ small). Applying the quadrature of Step 1 on $[t_n, t_n + h]$, the rule gives 0 because $\delta(t_n + c_j h) = 0$, and the quadrature error is $O(h^{2s})$. So the local error is $O(h^{2s})$.

**Step 4: the stage equations are uniquely solvable and the method converges with order 2s - 1 (proved).** The map $Z \mapsto h (A \otimes I) F(Z)$ is a contraction in $\mathbb{R}^{sd}$ when $h L \|A\|_\infty < 1$, so the stages exist and are unique and depend Lipschitz-continuously on $y_n$. The increment function is therefore Lipschitz, and the standard argument for one-step methods turns the local error $O(h^{2s})$ into a global error $\max_n \|y(t_n) - y_n\| = O(h^{2s-1})$ on $[t_0, T]$.

**Step 5: simplified Newton and termination.** Lines L5 to L8 run the iteration $Z \leftarrow Z + (I - \tau A \otimes J)^{-1} G(Z)$ with $G(Z) = -Z + \tau (A \otimes I) F(Z)$ and $J$ the Jacobian at $(t_n, y_n)$. If $f$ is linear, $f = Jy + g(t)$, then $G$ is affine with derivative $-(I - \tau A \otimes J)$ and one iteration gives the exact stages. In general the iteration matrix differs from the true derivative by $\tau (A \otimes I)(\partial F - I \otimes J) = O(\tau^2)$ in the nonstiff regime, so the iteration contracts for $\tau$ small. The loop runs at most max_iter times and either breaks at line L8 or raises an error, so every step terminates, and the method stops after at most $N$ steps.

**Step 6: the stability function.** For $y' = \lambda y$, $z = h\lambda$, the stages solve $(I - zA) Y = y_n \mathbb{1}$, so $y_{n+1} = R(z) y_n$ with $R(z) = 1 + z b^T (I - zA)^{-1} \mathbb{1} = \det(I - zA + z\mathbb{1}b^T)/\det(I - zA)$, a rational function with numerator and denominator of degree at most $s$. Order $2s - 1$ gives $R(z) - e^z = O(z^{2s})$. Collocation at distinct nodes with $c_1 > 0$ makes $A$ invertible (it maps the values $q(c_i)$ of degree $s - 1$ polynomials to the values of their antiderivatives vanishing at 0), and $b^T = e_s^T A$, so $R(\infty) = 1 - b^T A^{-1}\mathbb{1} = 1 - e_s^T \mathbb{1} = 0$. Hence the numerator has degree at most $s - 1$. If $P_1/Q_1$ and $P_2/Q_2$ both have $\deg P \le s - 1$, $\deg Q \le s$, $Q(0) = 1$ and approximate $e^z$ to $O(z^{2s})$, then $P_1 Q_2 - P_2 Q_1 = O(z^{2s})$ has degree at most $2s - 1$, so it is zero. So $R$ is the $(s - 1, s)$ Pade approximant of $e^z$.

**A-stability (cited, not proved here).** The $(s - 1, s)$ subdiagonal Pade approximants satisfy $|R(z)| \le 1$ for $\operatorname{Re} z \le 0$ (Ehle 1969; Hairer and Wanner, Solving ODEs II, Section IV.4). With $R(\infty) = 0$ this makes Radau IIA L-stable. The tests check $|R| \le 1$ on samples of the left half plane and $R(z) = $ Pade exactly for $s \le 2$.
