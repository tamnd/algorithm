---
title: WENO5 terminates, is conservative, reconstructs to fifth order on smooth data and suppresses stencils that cross a jump
proves: [correctness, termination, convergence]
status: full
---

**Setting.** We solve $u_t + f(u)_x = 0$ with cell averages $U_j$ on cells $I_j = [x_{j-1/2}, x_{j+1/2}]$ of width $h = \Delta x$, periodic or transmissive boundaries, the Lax-Friedrichs splitting $f^\pm(u) = \frac{1}{2}(f(u) \pm \alpha u)$ with $\alpha \ge \max|f'|$, the WENO5 reconstruction of lines L8 and L9, and SSP-RK3 in lines L3 to L5. Norms are the max norm over cells unless stated. Which statements are proved here, which are cited and which are only observed is said at each step.

**Termination (proved).** Line L1 returns a finite $N \ge 0$; each of the $N$ passes evaluates the right-hand side $L$ three times, and each evaluation does $\Theta(m)$ work with $m + 6$ flux evaluations. The final step has length $T - t_{N-1} \in (0, \Delta t]$, so the last time is exactly $T$ (up to rounding). For $T = 0$, $N = 0$ and the data are returned unchanged.

**Lemma 1 (conservation, proved).** For periodic boundaries, $\sum_j L(U)_j = -\frac{1}{h}\sum_j(F_{j+1/2} - F_{j-1/2}) = 0$ by telescoping, so each forward Euler stage, and hence each SSP-RK3 step, keeps $\sum_j U_j$. For transmissive boundaries the sum changes by $\frac{\Delta t}{h}$ times the difference of the two boundary fluxes, which for constant states near the boundary equal $f$ of those states.

**Lemma 2 (polynomial exactness, proved).** Let $v$ be a polynomial and $\bar v_i$ its cell averages. The candidate values
$$q_0 = \tfrac{1}{6}(2\bar v_{j-2} - 7\bar v_{j-1} + 11\bar v_j), \quad q_1 = \tfrac{1}{6}(-\bar v_{j-1} + 5\bar v_j + 2\bar v_{j+1}), \quad q_2 = \tfrac{1}{6}(2\bar v_j + 5\bar v_{j+1} - \bar v_{j+2})$$
equal $v(x_{j+1/2})$ when $\deg v \le 2$, and $\sum_k d_k q_k$ with $d = (\frac{1}{10}, \frac{3}{5}, \frac{3}{10})$ equals $v(x_{j+1/2})$ when $\deg v \le 4$. *Proof.* All maps are linear in $v$ and commute with translation, so it suffices to check the monomials $1, x, \dots, x^4$ on the cells $[i, i + 1]$ with the edge at $x = 3$, which is a finite exact computation (done in Fractions by the test `test_polynomial_exactness_of_candidates_and_of_the_linear_combination`, which also confirms failure at degree 3 for $q_k$ and at degree 5 for the combination). $\square$

Hence, for smooth $v$, Taylor's theorem with the exactness gives $q_k - v(x_{j+1/2}) = O(h^3)$ and $\sum_k d_k q_k - v(x_{j+1/2}) = O(h^5)$.

**Lemma 3 (smoothness indicators, proved).** Each $\beta_k$ is a positive semidefinite quadratic form in its three averages that vanishes exactly on constant data: $\beta_k = 0$ forces both $a - 2b + c = 0$ and the first-difference term to vanish, and the two linear equations force $a = b = c$. For smooth $v$, Taylor expansion of the averages about $x_j$ gives
$$\beta_k = h^2 v'^2 + O(h^4), \qquad \beta_k - \beta_l = O(h^4),$$
and at a point with $v' = 0$, $\beta_k = \frac{13}{12}h^4 v''^2 + O(h^5)$ with $\beta_k - \beta_l = O(h^5)$. (The leading coefficients are those of Jiang and Shu, 1996; only the orders are used below.)

**Theorem 1 (fifth order on smooth data, proved for the reconstruction).** The weights satisfy $\omega_k \ge 0$ and $\sum_k \omega_k = 1$. Since $\sum_k(\omega_k - d_k) = 0$,
$$\sum_k \omega_k q_k - v(x_{j+1/2}) = \Big(\sum_k d_k q_k - v(x_{j+1/2})\Big) + \sum_k (\omega_k - d_k)\big(q_k - v(x_{j+1/2})\big) = O(h^5) + O(h^3)\max_k|\omega_k - d_k|.$$
By Lemma 3, $(\varepsilon + \beta_k)/(\varepsilon + \beta_l) = 1 + O(h^4)/(\varepsilon + \beta_l)$. If $v' \ne 0$ this is $1 + O(h^2)$ for every $\varepsilon \ge 0$, so $\omega_k - d_k = O(h^2)$ and the error is $O(h^5)$. If $\varepsilon > 0$ is fixed it is $1 + O(h^4/\varepsilon)$ everywhere. If $\varepsilon = 0$ at a critical point $v' = 0$, $v'' \ne 0$, the ratio is only $1 + O(h)$, so $\omega_k - d_k = O(h)$ and the bound is $O(h^4)$: this is the loss of order found by Henrick, Aslam and Powers (2005), observed in the tests as order 4.0 with $\varepsilon = 10^{-40}$. For WENO-Z, $\alpha_k = d_k(1 + (\tau_5/(\varepsilon + \beta_k))^2)$ with $\tau_5 = |\beta_0 - \beta_2|$, and $\tau_5/\beta_k = O(h^2)$ where $v' \ne 0$ and $O(h)$ at critical points, so $\omega_k - d_k = O(h^4)$ or $O(h^2)$ and the reconstruction is $O(h^5)$ in both cases.

**Order of the scheme (cited and observed).** A reconstruction error $O(h^5)$ at each edge gives a flux difference $O(h^5)/h = O(h^4)$ in general; the full fifth order of $-(F_{j+1/2} - F_{j-1/2})/h$ needs the error to vary smoothly from edge to edge, which holds under $\omega_k - d_k = O(h^3)$ (Henrick, Aslam and Powers 2005) and in practice for the Jiang-Shu weights with $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$. The tests observe order 4.6 to 5.0 for linear advection of a sine with $\Delta t \propto h^{5/3}$, so that the $O(\Delta t^3)$ time error of SSP-RK3 is also $O(h^5)$.

**Theorem 2 (stencils across a jump are switched off, proved).** Suppose stencil $S_0$ lies in a region where $v$ is smooth, so $\beta_0 = O(h^2)$, and stencil $S_l$ contains a jump of size $J \ne 0$ between two of its cells, with $v$ smooth on each side. Write the stencil data as $J\,s + w$ with $s$ a step (0 then 1) and $w$ smooth. Since $\sqrt{\beta_l}$ is a seminorm, $\sqrt{\beta_l(Js + w)} \ge |J|\sqrt{\beta_l(s)} - \sqrt{\beta_l(w)} \ge |J|c - O(h)$ with $c = \min_{\text{step positions}}\sqrt{\beta_l(s)} > 0$, because a step is never constant on the stencil. Then
$$\frac{\omega_l}{\omega_0} = \frac{d_l}{d_0}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon + \beta_0}{\varepsilon + \beta_l}\Big)^2 = O\big((\varepsilon + h^2)^2\big),$$
so the reconstruction uses the jump-free stencil up to a weight $O((\varepsilon + h^2)^2)$. For the data $(0, 0, 0, 1, 1)$ the computed weights of the two crossing stencils are below $10^{-9}$. This explains the essentially non-oscillatory behaviour; it is not a total variation bound, and WENO schemes are not TVD (observed: the total variation after one period of a square wave is 2.0027 against the initial 2).

**Lemma 4 (SSP-RK3, proved).** Lines L3 to L5 are $U^{(1)} = E(U)$, $U^{(2)} = \frac{3}{4}U + \frac{1}{4}E(U^{(1)})$, $U^{n+1} = \frac{1}{3}U + \frac{2}{3}E(U^{(2)})$ with the forward Euler step $E(V) = V + hL(V)$. If a convex functional $\|\cdot\|$ (a norm, the total variation, or a maximum principle bound) satisfies $\|E(V)\| \le \|V\|$ for $h \le h_{FE}$, then by convexity $\|U^{n+1}\| \le \|U^n\|$ for the same $h$. The scheme has Butcher tableau $c = (0, 1, \frac{1}{2})$, $b = (\frac{1}{6}, \frac{1}{6}, \frac{2}{3})$, $a_{21} = 1$, $a_{31} = a_{32} = \frac{1}{4}$; the four order conditions up to order 3 hold exactly and the condition $\sum b_i a_{ij}a_{jk}c_k = \frac{1}{24}$ fails, so the order is exactly 3, and the stability polynomial is $R(z) = 1 + z + \frac{z^2}{2} + \frac{z^3}{6}$ (checked in Fractions by the tests).

**Convergence (cited, not proved).** For linear advection with linear weights the scheme is a linear constant-coefficient scheme and the [von Neumann](/von-neumann-stability-analysis/) test applies; with nonlinear weights no general $\ell^2$ or TV stability theorem is known for WENO5 with SSP-RK3, and convergence to the entropy solution is not proved in general (Shu 2009). For linear advection of a sine on 40 cells up to $T = 5$, Courant numbers $\alpha\Delta t/h$ up to 1.6 show no growth, while 2.0 grows by a factor of about $10^{14}$ (observed), so the limit lies between them.
