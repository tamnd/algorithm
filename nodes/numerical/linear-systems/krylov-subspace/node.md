The Krylov subspace of order $k$ generated by a square matrix $A$ and a vector $b$ is
$$\mathcal K_k(A, b) = \operatorname{span}\{b, A b, A^2 b, \dots, A^{k-1} b\} = \{ p(A) b : \deg p \le k - 1 \}.$$
It is the set of vectors one can build from $b$ with $k - 1$ products by $A$ and linear combinations. Krylov subspace methods for $A x = b$, such as the [conjugate gradient method](/conjugate-gradient-method/), [GMRES](/gmres/), [MINRES](/minres/), [BiCG](/biconjugate-gradient/) and [BiCGSTAB](/bicgstab/), pick their iterate $x_k$ from $x_0 + \mathcal K_k(A, r_0)$ with $r_0 = b - A x_0$, and differ only in which element they pick and how they build a basis. So the residual is $r_k = p_k(A) r_0$ for a polynomial $p_k$ of degree at most $k$ with $p_k(0) = 1$, and convergence questions become questions of polynomial approximation on the spectrum.

## In plain words

If all you can do with a big matrix is multiply it by vectors, then after a few products the only vectors you know are the starting vector, the matrix times it, the matrix times that, and their mixtures. That collection grows by one direction with each product. Krylov methods search for the best approximate answer inside it, so the question of how fast they converge becomes: how quickly does this growing collection come to contain a good answer?

For many matrices from applications, a few dozen directions are enough even when there are millions of unknowns.

## The idea

**Notation.** As in [solving linear systems](/linear-system-solving/): $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, $x_k$ the $k$th iterate, $r_k = b - A x_k$, $\kappa(A)$ the condition number, $u = 2^{-53}$. $\Lambda(A)$ is the spectrum. $\mathcal P_k^0$ is the set of real polynomials $p$ of degree at most $k$ with $p(0) = 1$.

**Nesting and the grade.** $\mathcal K_1 \subseteq \mathcal K_2 \subseteq \cdots$, and the dimensions increase by one until the first index $d$ with $A^d b \in \mathcal K_d$; then $\mathcal K_k = \mathcal K_d$ for all $k \ge d$. The number $d$ is the grade of $b$ with respect to $A$, the degree of the minimal polynomial of $b$ (the monic $q$ of least degree with $q(A) b = 0$), so $d \le$ the degree of the minimal polynomial of $A$ $\le n$ (Saad, Proposition 6.2). The space $\mathcal K_d$ is invariant under $A$.

**The solution lies in a Krylov space.** If $A$ is nonsingular and $q(t) = \sum_{j=0}^d c_j t^j$ is the minimal polynomial of $b$, then $c_0 \ne 0$ (else $A$ would kill a nonzero vector), and $x = A^{-1} b = -\frac{1}{c_0} \sum_{j=1}^{d} c_j A^{j-1} b \in \mathcal K_d(A, b)$. This is why every method above terminates in at most $d \le n$ steps in exact arithmetic, and in far fewer when $A$ has few distinct eigenvalues or $b$ has components along few eigenvectors.

**Residual polynomials.** For $x_k \in x_0 + \mathcal K_k(A, r_0)$, $r_k = p_k(A) r_0$ with $p_k \in \mathcal P_k^0$. The optimal methods minimize a norm of $r_k$ or of the error over this set:

| method | hypothesis on $A$ | minimizes over $x_0 + \mathcal K_k$ | bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| CG | symmetric positive definite | $\|x - x_k\|_A$ | $\le \min_{p \in \mathcal P_k^0} \max_{\lambda \in \Lambda(A)} |p(\lambda)| \cdot \|x - x_0\|_A \le 2 \left(\frac{\sqrt\kappa - 1}{\sqrt\kappa + 1}\right)^k \|x - x_0\|_A$ |
| MINRES | symmetric | $\|r_k\|_2$ | $\le \min_{p} \max_{\lambda \in \Lambda(A)} |p(\lambda)| \cdot \|r_0\|_2$ |
| GMRES | any nonsingular | $\|r_k\|_2$ | $\le \kappa_2(V) \min_{p} \max_{\lambda \in \Lambda(A)} |p(\lambda)| \cdot \|r_0\|_2$ if $A = V \Lambda V^{-1}$ |

These are theorems in exact arithmetic (Greenbaum, Chapter 3); the CG bound with $\kappa = \kappa_2(A)$ comes from the shifted [Chebyshev polynomial](/chebyshev-iteration/) on $[\lambda_{\min}, \lambda_{\max}]$. For nonnormal $A$ the eigenvalues alone do not determine GMRES convergence: Greenbaum, Pták and Strakoš (1996) showed that any nonincreasing residual curve is attained by some matrix with any prescribed eigenvalues.

**Bases.** The power basis $b, A b, A^2 b, \dots$ is useless numerically: its columns converge to the dominant eigenvector, so the basis matrix has condition number growing geometrically in $k$. Krylov methods therefore orthonormalize as they go. The [Arnoldi iteration](/arnoldi-iteration/) builds an orthonormal basis $V_k$ with modified [Gram-Schmidt](/gram-schmidt-process/) and the relation $A V_k = V_{k+1} \bar H_k$, with $\bar H_k$ upper Hessenberg of size $(k+1) \times k$; cost $k$ matvecs plus about $2 n k^2$ flops and $n k$ words of storage. For symmetric $A$ the matrix $\bar H_k$ is tridiagonal and Arnoldi becomes the [Lanczos algorithm](/lanczos-algorithm/), a three-term recurrence with $O(n)$ work and storage per step. For nonsymmetric $A$, two-sided Lanczos builds biorthogonal bases of $\mathcal K_k(A, r_0)$ and $\mathcal K_k(A^T, \tilde r_0)$ with short recurrences, at the price of losing any minimization property; this is the base of BiCG, QMR, CGS and BiCGSTAB.

**Faber-Manteuffel.** A method with an optimal property in a fixed inner product and a short recurrence exists only for matrices that are, in that inner product, essentially Hermitian or of a few special forms (Faber and Manteuffel 1984). So for general nonsymmetric $A$ one must give up either optimality (BiCG and its relatives) or short recurrences (GMRES, whose cost grows with $k$, hence restarting).

**Finite precision.** In floating-point arithmetic the computed Lanczos vectors lose orthogonality once a Ritz value converges (Paige 1971, 1976), so CG and Lanczos do not terminate in $n$ steps and may need many more; but they still converge, as if run in exact arithmetic on a larger matrix with eigenvalues clustered near those of $A$ (Greenbaum 1989). The attainable accuracy of the true residual $b - A x_k$ is limited by the gap between the updated and true residual, of order $u \|A\| \max_j \|x_j\|$ (Greenbaum 1997; see [CG](/conjugate-gradient-method/)). Arnoldi with modified Gram-Schmidt keeps GMRES backward stable (Paige, Rozložník and Strakoš 2006).

**Preconditioning.** With a [preconditioner](/preconditioning/) $M \approx A$, methods search $\mathcal K_k(M^{-1} A, M^{-1} r_0)$; the aim is a spectrum of $M^{-1} A$ that low-degree polynomials can make small, such as a few tight clusters.

## Examples

- **Diagonal matrix with two eigenvalues.** $A = \operatorname{diag}(1, 1, 3, 3)$, $b = (1, 1, 1, 1)$. Then $A b = (1, 1, 3, 3)$, and $A^2 b = (1, 1, 9, 9) = 4 A b - 3 b$, so the grade is $d = 2$ and $\mathcal K_2 = \mathcal K_n$ for all larger $n$. From $(A - I)(A - 3 I) b = 0$ one reads $x = A^{-1} b = (4 b - A b)/3 = (1, 1, 1/3, 1/3)$. CG and GMRES both stop at step 2.
- **Cyclic shift.** Let $A$ be the $n \times n$ cyclic permutation with $A e_j = e_{j+1}$ and $A e_n = e_1$, $b = e_1$ and $x_0 = 0$. Then $\mathcal K_k = \operatorname{span}\{e_1, \dots, e_k\}$ and the grade is $n$. Since $x = A^{-1} e_1 = e_n \notin \mathcal K_{n-1}$, the best residual over $\mathcal K_k$ for $k < n$ is $\|b\|_2$ itself: GMRES makes no progress until step $n$, although $A$ is orthogonal with $\kappa_2(A) = 1$ (Brown 1991; Greenbaum, Section 3.2).
- **Ill-conditioned power basis.** For the discrete 1D Laplacian with $n = 100$ and random $b$, the $100 \times 10$ matrix $[b, A b, \dots, A^9 b]$ has 2-norm condition number of order $10^{6}$ to $10^{7}$ (estimated from the exact Gram matrix), while the Arnoldi basis is orthonormal to about $u$.
- **Hilbert matrix.** For the [Hilbert matrix](/hilbert-matrix/) $H_{10}$ the eigenvalues cluster near $0$, so exact-arithmetic CG would need all ten steps; in floating point CG on $H_{10}$ shows the gap between true and updated residuals (see the CG page tests).

## Where it is used

- Linear systems: CG for SPD, MINRES and SYMMLQ for symmetric indefinite, GMRES, BiCG, QMR, CGS, BiCGSTAB and IDR(s) for nonsymmetric systems, and LSQR for [least squares](/least-squares-problem/).
- Eigenvalue problems: [Lanczos](/lanczos-algorithm/) and [Arnoldi](/arnoldi-iteration/), with implicit restarts (ARPACK).
- Matrix functions: $\exp(A) b$, $A^{1/2} b$ and $f(A) b$ by projection onto $\mathcal K_k$, used for exponential integrators.
- Model order reduction: Padé via Lanczos, moment matching.
- Polynomial methods that use no inner products, such as [Chebyshev iteration](/chebyshev-iteration/), also produce iterates in $x_0 + \mathcal K_k$, chosen from spectral bounds instead of by optimality.
