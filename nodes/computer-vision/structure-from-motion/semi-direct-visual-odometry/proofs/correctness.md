---
title: On exact images the true pose, positions and points are fixed points of every step, no step increases its cost, and the depth filter update matches the posterior moments
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

**Setting.** Poses map world points into the camera, $X_c = R X + t$, and $\Pi$ is the pinhole projection. Line L3 minimizes the mean of the squared residuals $r_q(T) = I_k(\Pi(T X_q)) - I_{k-1}(u_q)$ over the patch pixels $q$ whose sample lies in the image, where $X_q = \Pi^{-1}(u_q, z)$ is the point of pixel $u_q$ in the previous camera. Line L4 minimizes $\sum_{o} (I_k(u' + A o) - I_r(u_r + o) - \beta)^2$ over $(u', \beta)$. Lines L5 and L6 minimize sums of squared reprojection errors $|\Pi(R X + t) - u|^2$.

**Assumptions.** (A1) Exact images: each image is $I_j(u) = L(X_j(u)) + \beta_j$, where $X_j(u)$ is the scene point seen at pixel $u$ in frame $j$, $L$ is the radiance and $\beta_j$ is a constant offset; in L3 the offsets of the two frames are equal. (A2) The map points and the pose of the previous frame are true, and each patch lies on a plane parallel to the image in which its depth is given, so the depth of each patch pixel is the depth of its point. (A3) Every sample used is inside its image. These are the conditions of the exact tests; real images meet them only approximately.

**Lemma 1 (sparse alignment: zero residuals).** Let $T^*$ be the true relative pose. By (A2), $X_q$ is the true point of pixel $u_q$ in the previous camera, so $T^* X_q$ is the same scene point in the current camera, seen at $\Pi(T^* X_q)$. By (A1) with equal offsets, $I_k(\Pi(T^* X_q)) = L(X) + \beta = I_{k-1}(u_q)$, so $r_q(T^*) = 0$ for every $q$. The cost is a mean of squares, so it is $\ge 0$ for every $T$, and $T^*$ attains the value 0. So $T^*$ is a global minimizer.

**Lemma 2 (sparse alignment: fixed point).** The inverse compositional step solves $H \xi = \sum_q J_q^{\mathsf T} r_q$ with fixed $J_q$ and $H$. At $T^*$ every $r_q = 0$ by Lemma 1, so the right-hand side is 0 and $\xi = 0$ (or $H$ is singular and the loop stops). The update $T^* \exp(0)^{-1} = T^*$ gives the same cost, which is not smaller, so the loop stops at $T^*$. The same holds at every pyramid level: each level is an exact image of the same scene with the scaled intrinsics, so Lemma 1 holds there too.

**Lemma 3 (feature alignment: fixed point).** Let $u^*$ be the true position of the point in frame $k$ and assume the warp $A$ is exact on the patch, that is, the pixel $u_r + o$ of keyframe $r$ and the pixel $u^* + A o$ of frame $k$ see the same scene point. This holds when the patch is planar and the warp between the two images is affine on the patch, for example for a fronto-parallel plane under a translation parallel to it, where $A = I$, or a translation along the optical axis, where $A = s I$. Then by (A1) $I_k(u^* + A o) - I_r(u_r + o) = \beta_k - \beta_r$ for every $o$, so $(u^*, \beta_k - \beta_r)$ makes every residual zero. It is a global minimizer of the sum of squares, the right-hand side $-\sum J^{\mathsf T} r$ of the Gauss-Newton system is 0 there, and the loop stops there by the argument of Lemma 2.

**Lemma 4 (refinement: fixed point).** In line L5 let the positions be $u_i = \Pi(R^* X_i + t^*)$ for the true pose and true points. Every reprojection error is zero at $(R^*, t^*)$, so it is a global minimizer of the cost, the gradient $-\sum J^{\mathsf T} e$ is zero, the step is zero, and the loop stops there. Line L6 is the same with the point as the unknown and the poses fixed: if all observations are exact projections of $X^*$, then $X^*$ has zero cost and is a fixed point.

**Lemma 5 (monotone costs).** Each of lines L3, L4, L5 and L6 computes the cost of a candidate step and keeps the step only if the cost is strictly smaller; otherwise it stops. So the costs of the kept iterates are strictly decreasing, and the returned cost is at most the starting cost. This holds for any data. In L3 the cost is the mean over the samples inside the image; the rule holds for that mean as computed.

**Lemma 6 (exact triangulation along the ray).** Let $f_r = (x_r, y_r, 1)$ and $f_c$ be the normalized coordinates of the seed in the reference and of its match in the current camera, and let $(R, t)$ be the relative pose. If the point $z f_r$ of the reference camera is seen along $f_c$, then $z R f_r + t = \lambda f_c$ for some $\lambda$. Taking the cross product with $f_c$ gives $z\, a + c = 0$ with $a = (R f_r) \times f_c$ and $c = t \times f_c$. The least-squares solution over $z$ is $z = -a^{\mathsf T} c / a^{\mathsf T} a$, and when the equation holds exactly with $a \ne 0$ this equals the true $z$. ($a = 0$ means that the two rays are parallel, and then the depth is not defined.) So for an exact match the measurement $x = 1/z$ is the true inverse depth.

**Lemma 7 (depth filter update).** Let the prior be $p(Z, \pi) = N(Z \mid \mu, \sigma^2)\, \mathrm{Beta}(\pi \mid a, b)$ and the likelihood of $x$ be $\pi N(x \mid Z, \tau^2) + (1 - \pi) U$, with the constant density $U$. The exact posterior is proportional to

$$
\pi\, N(x \mid Z, \tau^2)\, N(Z \mid \mu, \sigma^2)\, \mathrm{Beta}(\pi \mid a, b) + (1 - \pi)\, U\, N(Z \mid \mu, \sigma^2)\, \mathrm{Beta}(\pi \mid a, b).
$$

For the first term, the product of the two Gaussians in $Z$ is $N(x \mid \mu, \sigma^2 + \tau^2)\, N(Z \mid m, s^2)$ with $s^2 = (\sigma^{-2} + \tau^{-2})^{-1}$ and $m = s^2(\mu/\sigma^2 + x/\tau^2)$ (the standard identity for a product of Gaussian densities, found by completing the square). Also $\pi\, \mathrm{Beta}(\pi \mid a, b) = \frac{a}{a + b} \mathrm{Beta}(\pi \mid a + 1, b)$ and $(1 - \pi)\, \mathrm{Beta}(\pi \mid a, b) = \frac{b}{a + b} \mathrm{Beta}(\pi \mid a, b + 1)$, because $B(a + 1, b) = B(a, b)\, a/(a + b)$. So the posterior is the mixture

$$
C_1\, N(Z \mid m, s^2)\, \mathrm{Beta}(\pi \mid a + 1, b) + C_2\, N(Z \mid \mu, \sigma^2)\, \mathrm{Beta}(\pi \mid a, b + 1),
$$

with $C_1 \propto \frac{a}{a + b} N(x \mid \mu, \sigma^2 + \tau^2)$, $C_2 \propto \frac{b}{a + b} U$ and $C_1 + C_2 = 1$. These are the weights in the code. The mean of $Z$ under the mixture is $C_1 m + C_2 \mu$, and its second moment is $C_1(s^2 + m^2) + C_2(\sigma^2 + \mu^2)$, so the code's $\mu'$ and $\sigma'^2$ are the exact mean and variance. The first two moments of $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)$ are $\alpha/(\alpha + \beta)$ and $\alpha(\alpha + 1)/((\alpha + \beta)(\alpha + \beta + 1))$, which give the code's $f$ and $e$ for the mixture. Finally, $\mathrm{Beta}(a', b')$ with $a' = (e - f)/(f - e/f) = f(e - f)/(f^2 - e)$ and $b' = a'(1 - f)/f$ has these moments. First, $n = a' + b' = a'/f$, so $a'/n = f$. Second, $n = (e - f)/(f^2 - e)$, so $a' + 1 = (e f - f^2 + f^2 - e)/(f^2 - e) = e(f - 1)/(f^2 - e)$ and $n + 1 = (e - f + f^2 - e)/(f^2 - e) = f(f - 1)/(f^2 - e)$. The second moment is then $\frac{a'(a' + 1)}{n(n + 1)} = f \cdot \frac{e(f - 1)}{f(f - 1)} = e$. (Here $0 < f < 1$ and $e > f^2$, because the variance of $\pi$ is positive, so all the quantities are positive.) So the update replaces the posterior by the product of a Gaussian and a Beta with the same first two moments of $Z$ and of $\pi$. The test checks these moments against a numerical integration of the exact posterior.

**Conclusion.** Under (A1) to (A3), the true relative pose is a global minimizer and a fixed point of the sparse alignment at every level (Lemmas 1 and 2), the true position is a fixed point of the feature alignment when the warp is exact (Lemma 3), and the true pose and points are fixed points of the two refinement steps (Lemma 4). On any data, no kept step increases its cost (Lemma 5). An exact match gives the true inverse depth (Lemma 6), and each depth filter update keeps the first two moments of the exact one-step posterior (Lemma 7). $\blacksquare$

**What is not proved.** Convergence from a far start: all steps are local Gauss-Newton methods on non-convex costs, and the pyramid and the high frame rate are there to keep the start close. The convergence of the depth filter to the true depth is not proved; the Gaussian times Beta form is an approximation that is made again after every update. The keyframe rule and the choice of cells are heuristics. On real images the brightness is not constant, the patches are not planar and parallel to the image, and the estimates drift.
