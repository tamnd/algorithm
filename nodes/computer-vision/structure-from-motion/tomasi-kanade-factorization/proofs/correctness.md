---
title: The centered measurement matrix has rank 3, and the factorization with the metric step recovers motion and shape up to one orthogonal matrix
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

We use the notation of the page. Frame $f$ maps $X_p$ to $u_{fp} = i_f \cdot X_p + a_f$ and $v_{fp} = j_f \cdot X_p + b_f$, where $i_f, j_f$ are orthonormal. $M$ is the $2N \times 3$ matrix with the rows $i_f^{\mathsf T}$ and $j_f^{\mathsf T}$, $\bar X = \frac{1}{n} \sum_p X_p$, and $S$ is the $3 \times n$ matrix with the columns $X_p - \bar X$.

**Claim.** (a) $\tilde W = W - t \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T} = M S$, so $\operatorname{rank} \tilde W \le 3$. (b) If $M$ and $S$ have rank 3, then lines L2 and L3 give $\tilde W = \hat M \hat X$, and there is an invertible $3 \times 3$ matrix $A$ with $M = \hat M A$ and $S = A^{-1} \hat X$. (c) $L^* = A A^{\mathsf T}$ satisfies every equation of line L4, and if the $3N \times 6$ system of line L4 has rank 6, lines L5 to L8 return $M G$ and $G^{\mathsf T} S$ for one orthogonal matrix $G$. (d) The output reproduces $W$ exactly and preserves all distances between the points. (e) For any data, $t$ and the rank 3 factorization of lines L1 to L3 minimize $\|W - M' X' - t' \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\|_F$ over all $2N \times 3$ matrices $M'$, $3 \times n$ matrices $X'$ and vectors $t'$.

**Proof of (a).** Write $c_f$ for the translation entry of row $f$ of $W$, that is $a_f$ or $b_f$, and $m_f$ for the row of $M$. Then $W_{fp} = m_f \cdot X_p + c_f$, so the row mean is $t_f = m_f \cdot \bar X + c_f$, and $\tilde W_{fp} = m_f \cdot (X_p - \bar X) = (M S)_{fp}$. The product of a matrix with 3 columns and a matrix with 3 rows has rank at most 3. $\square$

**Proof of (b).** If $M$ and $S$ have rank 3, then $\tilde W = M S$ has rank exactly 3, because $M$ has full column rank and $S$ has full row rank. So the SVD has exactly 3 nonzero singular values, and $\tilde W = U_3 \Sigma_3 V_3^{\mathsf T} = \hat M \hat X$. The column space of $\tilde W$ is the column space of $M$, and also that of $\hat M = U_3 \Sigma_3^{1/2}$. Both have dimension 3, so each column of $M$ is a combination of the columns of $\hat M$: $M = \hat M A$ for some $3 \times 3$ matrix $A$, and $A$ is invertible because $M$ has rank 3. Then $\hat M A S = \hat M \hat X$, and $\hat M$ has full column rank, so $A S = \hat X$, that is $S = A^{-1} \hat X$. $\square$

**Proof of (c).** Let $\hat a, \hat b$ be the rows of frame $f$ in $\hat M$. The true rows are $i_f^{\mathsf T} = \hat a^{\mathsf T} A$ and $j_f^{\mathsf T} = \hat b^{\mathsf T} A$. So $\hat a^{\mathsf T} L^* \hat a = \|i_f\|^2 = 1$, $\hat b^{\mathsf T} L^* \hat b = \|j_f\|^2 = 1$ and $\hat a^{\mathsf T} L^* \hat b = i_f \cdot j_f = 0$. Each equation is linear in the 6 entries of the symmetric matrix $L$. So $L^*$ solves the system exactly. If the system matrix $G_6$ has rank 6, its least squares solution is unique and equals the exact solution, so line L5 returns $L = L^*$. The matrix $L^* = A A^{\mathsf T}$ is positive definite because $A$ is invertible, so line L6 succeeds and gives $Q$ with $Q Q^{\mathsf T} = A A^{\mathsf T}$. Put $G = A^{-1} Q$. Then $G G^{\mathsf T} = A^{-1} Q Q^{\mathsf T} A^{-\mathsf T} = A^{-1} A A^{\mathsf T} A^{-\mathsf T} = I$, so $G$ is orthogonal. Line L7 gives $\hat M Q = \hat M A G = M G$ and $Q^{-1} \hat X = G^{-1} A^{-1} \hat X = G^{\mathsf T} S$. Line L8 multiplies the motion on the right by $R_0^{\mathsf T}$ and the shape on the left by $R_0$ for a rotation $R_0$, which replaces $G$ by the orthogonal matrix $G R_0^{\mathsf T}$. $\square$

**Proof of (d).** The output gives $M G \cdot G^{\mathsf T} S + t \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T} = M S + t \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T} = W$ by (a). The output points are $G^{\mathsf T} (X_p - \bar X)$, and an orthogonal map preserves distances, so $\|G^{\mathsf T}(X_p - X_q)\| = \|X_p - X_q\|$. Each output frame has the rows $G^{\mathsf T} i_f$ and $G^{\mathsf T} j_f$, which are orthonormal. If $\det G = -1$, the output is the mirror image of the truth, and the data cannot rule it out, because $M G$ and $G^{\mathsf T} S$ explain $W$ exactly. $\square$

**Proof of (e).** For fixed $M'$ and $X'$, the cost $\sum_f \sum_p (W_{fp} - (M' X')_{fp} - t'_f)^2$ separates over the rows, and in row $f$ it is minimized by $t'_f$ equal to the mean of $W_{fp} - (M' X')_{fp}$ over $p$. Replacing $X'$ by $X' - \bar x' \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}$, where $\bar x'$ is the mean column of $X'$, changes $t'$ but not the cost, so we may take $X'$ with zero row means, and then the best $t'$ is $t$, the row means of $W$. What remains is $\min \|\tilde W - M' X'\|_F$ over all matrices of rank at most 3. By the Eckart-Young theorem, the minimum is reached by the truncated SVD $U_3 \Sigma_3 V_3^{\mathsf T} = \hat M \hat X$. The rows of $\hat X$ have zero means, because $\tilde W \mathbf 1 = 0$ gives $V_3^{\mathsf T} \mathbf 1 = \Sigma_3^{-1} U_3^{\mathsf T} \tilde W \mathbf 1 = 0$ when $\Sigma_3$ is invertible. So the triple $(\hat M, \hat X, t)$ is a minimizer. With independent Gaussian noise of equal variance on all entries, this least squares minimizer is the maximum likelihood estimate of the affine motion and shape. $\blacksquare$
