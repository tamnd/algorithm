We use the notation of the page. The input is $n \ge 4$ pairs of 2D points, written as $\mathbf{x}_i = (x_i, y_i, 1)^{\mathsf T}$ and $\mathbf{x}'_i = (x'_i, y'_i, 1)^{\mathsf T}$. A set of points of $\mathbb{P}^2$ is in general position when no three of them lie on one line, which for three points means that their vectors are linearly independent. For a $3 \times 3$ matrix $G$ we write $\mathbf{g} \in \mathbb{R}^9$ for its rows put one after the other, so $\|\mathbf{g}\| = \|G\|_F$. The canonical form of a nonzero matrix $G$ is $G / \|G\|_F$ times the sign of its entry of largest absolute value (on a tie, the first such entry in row order).

**Claim.** (a) The vector $\mathbf{h}$ of line L7 minimizes $\|A\mathbf{h}\|$ over all unit vectors. (b) If there is an invertible $H_0$ with $\mathbf{x}'_i \sim H_0\mathbf{x}_i$ for all $i$, and four of the $\mathbf{x}_i$ are in general position, then DLT-HOMOGRAPHY returns the canonical form of $H_0$.

**Lemma 1 (the two rows).** Let $\mathbf{y}' = (u', v', w')^{\mathsf T}$ with $w' \ne 0$, let $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and let $G$ be a $3 \times 3$ matrix. The rows of lines L5 and L6, applied to $\mathbf{g}$, are 0 if and only if $\mathbf{y}' \times G\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0}$, that is, if and only if $G\mathbf{y}$ is a multiple of $\mathbf{y}'$ (possibly the zero multiple).

*Proof.* Let $\mathbf{z} = G\mathbf{y}$, so $z_j = \mathbf{g}^{j\mathsf T}\mathbf{y}$ with $\mathbf{g}^{j\mathsf T}$ the row $j$ of $G$. The row of L5 applied to $\mathbf{g}$ is $-w' z_2 + v' z_3$, and the row of L6 is $w' z_1 - u' z_3$. These are the first two entries of $\mathbf{y}' \times \mathbf{z}$, whose third entry is $u' z_2 - v' z_1$. If the first two are 0, then $z_1 = u' z_3 / w'$ and $z_2 = v' z_3 / w'$, so the third is $(u' v' z_3 - v' u' z_3)/w' = 0$ and $\mathbf{z} = (z_3 / w')\,\mathbf{y}'$. The converse is clear. $\square$

The points $\mathbf{y}'_i = T'\mathbf{x}'_i$ have last entry 1, because the last row of the similarity $T'$ is $(0, 0, 1)$. So Lemma 1 applies to every pair, and the null space of $A$ is exactly the set of $\mathbf{g}$ with $\mathbf{y}'_i \times G\mathbf{y}_i = \mathbf{0}$ for all $i$.

**Lemma 2 (minimum of the algebraic error).** Let $A$ be an $m \times 9$ real matrix and let $\sigma_{\min} \ge 0$ be its smallest singular value, counted as 0 when $m < 9$. Then $\min_{\|\mathbf{h}\| = 1} \|A\mathbf{h}\| = \sigma_{\min}$, and a right singular vector for $\sigma_{\min}$ attains it.

*Proof.* Let $A^{\mathsf T}A = V \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1^2, \ldots, \sigma_9^2) V^{\mathsf T}$ with $V$ orthogonal, where $\sigma_1 \ge \cdots \ge \sigma_9 = \sigma_{\min}$ are the singular values (with zeros added when $m < 9$). For a unit vector $\mathbf{h}$ put $\mathbf{c} = V^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{h}$, also a unit vector. Then $\|A\mathbf{h}\|^2 = \mathbf{h}^{\mathsf T}A^{\mathsf T}A\mathbf{h} = \sum_j \sigma_j^2 c_j^2 \ge \sigma_9^2 \sum_j c_j^2 = \sigma_9^2$. The last column of $V$, the right singular vector for $\sigma_9$, gives $\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{e}_9$ and equality. $\square$

Part (a) of the claim is Lemma 2 applied to line L7.

**Lemma 3 (four points fix the matrix).** Let $H_0$ be invertible, and let $\mathbf{y}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{y}_4$ be in general position, with $\mathbf{y}'_i \sim H_0\mathbf{y}_i$. If a matrix $G$ satisfies $\mathbf{y}'_i \times G\mathbf{y}_i = \mathbf{0}$ for $i = 1, \ldots, 4$, then $G = \nu H_0$ for some real $\nu$.

*Proof.* Write $H_0\mathbf{y}_i = \kappa_i\mathbf{y}'_i$ with $\kappa_i \ne 0$ (it is not 0, since $H_0$ is invertible and $\mathbf{y}_i \ne \mathbf{0}$), and $G\mathbf{y}_i = \mu_i\mathbf{y}'_i$, which holds by the cross product condition. Let $M = H_0^{-1}G$. Then $M\mathbf{y}_i = (\mu_i / \kappa_i)\,\mathbf{y}_i = \nu_i\mathbf{y}_i$. The vectors $\mathbf{y}_1, \mathbf{y}_2, \mathbf{y}_3$ are a basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$, so $\mathbf{y}_4 = a\mathbf{y}_1 + b\mathbf{y}_2 + c\mathbf{y}_3$. None of $a, b, c$ is 0: if $a = 0$, then $\mathbf{y}_2, \mathbf{y}_3, \mathbf{y}_4$ would be dependent, so three of the points would be on a line. Apply $M$ to both sides:
$$\nu_4 a\mathbf{y}_1 + \nu_4 b\mathbf{y}_2 + \nu_4 c\mathbf{y}_3 = \nu_1 a\mathbf{y}_1 + \nu_2 b\mathbf{y}_2 + \nu_3 c\mathbf{y}_3 .$$
By the independence of $\mathbf{y}_1, \mathbf{y}_2, \mathbf{y}_3$ and since $a, b, c \ne 0$, $\nu_1 = \nu_2 = \nu_3 = \nu_4 =: \nu$. So $M$ acts as $\nu I$ on a basis, $M = \nu I$, and $G = \nu H_0$. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (normalization).** Let $\tilde H_0 = T' H_0 T^{-1}$. Then $\mathbf{y}'_i \sim \tilde H_0\mathbf{y}_i$ for all $i$, $\tilde H_0$ is invertible, and four of the $\mathbf{y}_i$ are in general position. For every nonzero $\lambda$, $T'^{-1}(\lambda\tilde H_0)T = \lambda H_0$.

*Proof.* $\tilde H_0\mathbf{y}_i = T' H_0 T^{-1} T\mathbf{x}_i = T' H_0\mathbf{x}_i \sim T'\mathbf{x}'_i = \mathbf{y}'_i$. It is a product of invertible matrices. The map $\mathbf{x} \mapsto T\mathbf{x}$ is an invertible linear map of $\mathbb{R}^3$, so it keeps linear independence, and the four points stay in general position. The last identity is direct. $\square$

**Proof of part (b).** By Lemma 4 and Lemma 1, $\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_0$ lies in the null space of $A$, so $\sigma_{\min} = 0$. By Lemma 3, applied to $\tilde H_0$ and the four points in general position, and by Lemma 1, every null vector of $A$ is a multiple of $\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_0$. So the null space is the line spanned by $\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_0$, $A$ has rank 8, and its other singular values are positive. By Lemma 2, line L7 returns a unit vector $\mathbf{h}$ with $A\mathbf{h} = \mathbf{0}$, so $\mathbf{h} = \pm\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_0 / \|\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_0\|$ and $\tilde H = \lambda\tilde H_0$ with $\lambda \ne 0$. By Lemma 4, line L9 gives $H = \lambda H_0$. Line L10 divides by $\|H\|_F = |\lambda|\,\|H_0\|_F$ and fixes the sign by a rule that depends only on the matrix up to a positive factor, so the result is the same for every $\lambda \ne 0$: it is the canonical form of $H_0$. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (covariance under similarities).** Let $S$ and $S'$ be similarities of the two images. The normalized points of $S\mathbf{x}_i$ are $Q\mathbf{y}_i$ for a matrix $Q = \begin{pmatrix} R & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0}^{\mathsf T} & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ with $R$ a $2 \times 2$ orthogonal matrix, because a similarity moves the centroid with the points and multiplies all distances by the same factor; likewise $Q'$ for the second image. For orthogonal $Q'$, $Q'\mathbf{a} \times Q'\mathbf{b} = \det(Q')\,Q'(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b})$, and the first two entries of $Q'\mathbf{c}$ are $R'$ times the first two entries of $\mathbf{c}$. So the algebraic error of $G' = Q' G Q^{\mathsf T}$ on the new points equals the algebraic error of $G$ on the old ones, and $G \mapsto Q' G Q^{\mathsf T}$ keeps the Frobenius norm. The minimizer moves with the data, and the output for the moved points is the canonical form of $S' H S^{-1}$ whenever the minimizer is unique. The plain DLT, without normalization, has no such property, because a translation of the pixel frame changes $A$ by more than an orthogonal change of variables.
