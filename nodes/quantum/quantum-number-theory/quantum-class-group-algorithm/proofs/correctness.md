We use the pseudocode of the page. $\Delta < 0$ is a discriminant, $\mathrm{Cl}(\Delta)$ is the group of classes of primitive positive definite forms of discriminant $\Delta$ under composition, and $e$ is the principal form. We use two standard facts (see Cohen, *A Course in Computational Algebraic Number Theory*, Chapter 5): every class contains exactly one reduced form, and reduction and composition take time polynomial in $\log|\Delta|$. We also assume that the forms $g_1, \dots, g_k$ of line L1 generate $\mathrm{Cl}(\Delta)$. This holds for the prime forms of norm at most $\sqrt{|\Delta|/3}$, because every class has a reduced form $(a, b, c)$ with $a \le \sqrt{|\Delta|/3}$, and the class of a form of norm $a$ is a product of prime forms of primes that divide $a$ and their inverses. Under the generalized Riemann hypothesis it also holds for the primes up to Bach's bound $12(\ln|\Delta|)^2$.

**Notation.** Let $r_i$ be the order of $g_i$, $G = \mathbb{Z}_{r_1} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{Z}_{r_k}$, and $\varphi : \mathbb{Z}^k \to \mathrm{Cl}(\Delta)$, $\varphi(x) = g_1^{x_1} \cdots g_k^{x_k}$. The map $\varphi$ is onto, and its kernel $\Lambda$ is the relation lattice. It contains $r_i e_i$ for each $i$. Let $H = \Lambda / (r_1\mathbb{Z} \times \cdots \times r_k\mathbb{Z}) \subseteq G$. For $y \in G$ let $\chi_y(x) = e^{2\pi i \sum_i x_i y_i / r_i}$, which is well defined on $G$ and on $\mathbb{Z}^k$. Let $H^\perp = \{y \in G : \chi_y(h) = 1 \text{ for all } h \in H\}$. Then $|H^\perp| = |G|/|H| = |\mathrm{Cl}(\Delta)| = h$, since $G/H \cong \mathbb{Z}^k/\Lambda \cong \mathrm{Cl}(\Delta)$.

**Lemma 1 (orders).** Line L2 returns $r_i$ exactly.

*Proof.* The order finding routine stops only when $g_i^r = e$, so $r_i \mid r$. It then divides $r$ by a prime $p$ while $g_i^{r/p} = e$. At the end, $r_i \mid r$ and $r_i \nmid r/p$ for every prime $p \mid r$, so $r = r_i$. The analysis of [order finding](/order-finding/) shows that each sample gives a convergent with denominator $r_i/\gcd(j, r_i)$ for a uniform $j$ with constant probability, so the loop stops after an expected $O(\log\log r_i)$ samples. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (the Fourier samples).** One run of lines L5 to L8 returns each $y \in H^\perp$ with probability $1/h$ and never returns $y \notin H^\perp$.

*Proof.* Let $f(x)$ be the reduced form of $\varphi(x)$. Because the reduced form of a class is unique, $f(x) = f(x')$ if and only if $x - x' \in H$. Line L5 gives $|G|^{-1/2}\sum_{x \in G}|x\rangle|0\rangle$ and line L6 gives $|G|^{-1/2}\sum_x |x\rangle|f(x)\rangle$. After line L7 the amplitude of $|y\rangle|f(x_0)\rangle$ is

$$\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h \in H}\chi_y(x_0 + h) = \frac{\chi_y(x_0)}{|G|}\sum_{h \in H}\chi_y(h),$$

and the last sum is $|H|$ if $y \in H^\perp$ and 0 otherwise, since $\chi_y$ is a character of $H$. There are $|G|/|H|$ cosets, so the probability of $y \in H^\perp$ is $\frac{|G|}{|H|}\cdot\frac{|H|^2}{|G|^2} = \frac{|H|}{|G|} = \frac{1}{h}$. These probabilities add up to 1 over $H^\perp$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the lattice of line L9).** Let $L(Y) = \{x \in \mathbb{Z}^k : \chi_y(x) = 1 \text{ for all } y \in Y\}$ for a set $Y \subseteq H^\perp$. Then $\Lambda \subseteq L(Y)$, with equality if and only if $Y$ generates $H^\perp$.

*Proof.* If $x \in \Lambda$, its image lies in $H$, so $\chi_y(x) = 1$ for $y \in H^\perp$. For the equality, $L(Y)$ contains $r_i e_i$ for each $i$ and its image in $G$ is the annihilator $\langle Y\rangle^\perp$ of the subgroup generated by $Y$. For finite abelian groups, $K \mapsto K^\perp$ is an inclusion-reversing bijection between subgroups of $G$ with $(K^\perp)^\perp = K$. So $\langle Y\rangle^\perp = H$ if and only if $\langle Y \rangle = H^\perp$. $\square$

The implementation computes $L(Y)$ as follows. Let $M = \mathrm{lcm}(r_i)$ and let $A$ have the rows $(y_1 M/r_1, \dots, y_k M/r_k)$ for $y \in Y$. Then $x \in L(Y)$ if and only if $Ax + Mz = 0$ for some integer vector $z$. If $U[A \mid MI]W$ is diagonal with unimodular $U$ and $W$, the columns of $W$ at the zero diagonal positions span the integer kernel of $[A \mid MI]$, and their first $k$ coordinates generate $L(Y)$.

**Lemma 4 (the check of line L10).** Line L10 accepts if and only if $L(Y) = \Lambda$.

*Proof.* Line L10 accepts if and only if $\varphi(x) = e$ for every $x$ in a generating set of $L(Y)$, that is, if and only if $L(Y) \subseteq \Lambda$. With Lemma 3 this means $L(Y) = \Lambda$. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (the number of samples).** The expected number of samples before line L10 accepts is at most $2\log_2 h + 1$.

*Proof.* While $\langle Y\rangle \ne H^\perp$, it is a proper subgroup, so it holds at most half of $H^\perp$, and by Lemma 2 the next sample falls outside it with probability at least $1/2$. Each such sample at least doubles $|\langle Y\rangle|$, which can happen at most $\log_2 h$ times. So the number of samples is at most a sum of $\lceil \log_2 h \rceil$ geometric variables with mean at most 2, plus one. By Lemmas 3 and 4, line L10 accepts as soon as $\langle Y \rangle = H^\perp$. $\square$

**Lemma 6 (the Smith normal form).** Let the rows of $A$ generate $\Lambda$, and let $UAW = D$ with $U$, $W$ unimodular and $D$ diagonal with entries $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k$ in the first $k$ rows. Then $\mathbb{Z}^k/\Lambda \cong \mathbb{Z}/d_1 \times \cdots \times \mathbb{Z}/d_k$, and row $i$ of $W^{-1}$ maps to a generator of the factor $\mathbb{Z}/d_i$.

*Proof.* The rows of $A = U^{-1}DW^{-1}$ span the same lattice as the rows of $DW^{-1}$, because $U^{-1}$ is unimodular. In the coordinates $w = xW$, which is a bijection of $\mathbb{Z}^k$, this lattice is $d_1\mathbb{Z} \times \cdots \times d_k\mathbb{Z}$, and the vector $x = e_i W^{-1}$, row $i$ of $W^{-1}$, has $w = e_i$. $\square$

**Theorem.** If the forms of line L1 generate $\mathrm{Cl}(\Delta)$, the algorithm returns invariants $d_i > 1$ with $d_i \mid d_{i+1}$, forms $h_i$, and $h$, such that $\mathrm{Cl}(\Delta) = \langle h_1\rangle \times \cdots$ with $h_i$ of order $d_i$, and $h = |\mathrm{Cl}(\Delta)|$. The expected number of Fourier samples is at most $2\log_2 h + 1$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 1 the orders are exact, so Lemma 2 applies. By Lemmas 4 and 5 the loop ends with $L(Y) = \Lambda$. Then $\varphi$ induces an isomorphism $\mathbb{Z}^k/\Lambda \to \mathrm{Cl}(\Delta)$, and Lemma 6 gives the decomposition with the generators $h_i = \varphi(e_i W^{-1})$. The factors with $d_i = 1$ are trivial and are dropped, and $h = \prod d_i$. $\square$
