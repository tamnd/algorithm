---
title: QUANTUM-WALK-SEARCH returns only marked states and finds one with constant probability
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

We use the notation of the page: a reversible, ergodic chain $P$ on $X$ with stationary distribution $\pi$ and spectral gap $\delta$, $|p_x\rangle = \sum_y \sqrt{P_{xy}}|y\rangle$, $A = \mathrm{span}\{|x\rangle|p_x\rangle\}$, $B = \mathrm{SWAP}(A)$, $W = \mathrm{ref}_B\,\mathrm{ref}_A$, and $D_{xy} = \sqrt{P_{xy}P_{yx}}$. Let $T: \mathbb{C}^X \to \mathbb{C}^X \otimes \mathbb{C}^X$ map $|x\rangle \mapsto |x\rangle|p_x\rangle$, and let $S$ be the swap.

**Claim.** (a) A returned state is always in $M$, and if $M$ is empty the answer is "none". (b) For every eigenvector $v$ of $D$ with eigenvalue $\lambda = \cos\theta$, the vectors $a = Tv$ and $b = STv$ satisfy $Wa = 2\lambda b - a$ and $Wb = (4\lambda^2 - 1)b - 2\lambda a$, so $W$ has the eigenvalues $e^{\pm 2i\theta}$ on their span when $0 < \theta < \pi$; $W|\pi\rangle = |\pi\rangle$ for $|\pi\rangle = T\sqrt{\pi}$. (c) Lines L4 to L6 fix $|\pi\rangle|0\rangle$, and on an eigenvector $|u\rangle$ of $W$ with eigenvalue $e^{i\varphi}$ they give $-|u\rangle|0\rangle$ up to an error of norm $2|\sin(2^{t-1}\varphi)|/(2^t|\sin(\varphi/2)|)$. (d) With exact reflections, $T_1$ rounds give a marked state with probability $\sin^2((2T_1 + 1)\beta)$, where $\sin\beta = \sqrt{\pi(M)}$.

*Proof.* **(a)** Line L7 checks the measured $x$ with the oracle of $M$ and returns it only if it is marked. If $M$ is empty, no $x$ passes, and L8 returns "none".

**(b)** The map $T$ is an isometry, since $\langle x', p_{x'}|x, p_x\rangle = [x = x']\sum_y P_{xy} = [x = x']$. So $\Pi_A = TT^*$, and likewise $\Pi_B = STT^*S$. Also
$$T^*STe_x = T^*|p_x\rangle|x\rangle = \sum_{x'} \langle x', p_{x'}|p_x, x\rangle\, e_{x'} = \sum_{x'} \sqrt{P_{xx'}}\sqrt{P_{x'x}}\, e_{x'} = De_x,$$
so $T^*ST = D$. Since $a \in A$, $\mathrm{ref}_A a = a$, and $\Pi_B a = ST(T^*ST)v = ST Dv = \lambda b$. So $Wa = \mathrm{ref}_B a = 2\lambda b - a$. In the same way $\Pi_A b = TT^*STv = TDv = \lambda a$, so $\mathrm{ref}_A b = 2\lambda a - b$, and
$$Wb = \mathrm{ref}_B(2\lambda a - b) = 2\lambda(2\lambda b - a) - b = (4\lambda^2 - 1)b - 2\lambda a.$$
In the basis $(a, b)$ the matrix of $W$ has trace $-1 + 4\lambda^2 - 1 = 2\cos 2\theta$ and determinant $-(4\lambda^2 - 1) + 4\lambda^2 = 1$, so its eigenvalues are $e^{\pm 2i\theta}$. When $0 < \theta < \pi$, $a$ and $b$ are independent, since $\langle a, b\rangle = v^*Dv = \lambda$ and $|\lambda| < 1$. For a reversible $P$, $D = \Lambda P\Lambda^{-1}$ with $\Lambda = \mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\pi_x})$, so $\sqrt{\pi}$ is an eigenvector of $D$ with $\lambda = 1$. Then $Wa = 2b - a$ with $\|a\| = \|b\| = 1$ and $\langle a, b\rangle = 1$, so $a = b$ and $W|\pi\rangle = |\pi\rangle$.

**Phase gap.** $D$ is symmetric with the eigenvalues of $P$. Since $P$ is ergodic, the eigenvalue 1 is simple, and every other eigenvalue has $|\lambda| \le 1 - \delta$. Its phases $\pm 2\theta$ have angular distance $2\min(\theta, \pi - \theta) \ge 2\arccos(1 - \delta)$ from 0. As $\cos u \ge 1 - u^2/2$, $\arccos(1 - \delta) \ge \sqrt{2\delta}$, so the phase gap is $\Delta \ge 2\sqrt{2\delta}$. The span of all $a$ and $b$ is $A + B$, and on it $|\pi\rangle$ is the only eigenvector of $W$ with eigenvalue 1.

**(c)** Let $|u\rangle$ be an eigenvector of $W$ with eigenvalue $e^{i\varphi}$, and $N = 2^t$. Phase estimation maps $|u\rangle|0\rangle$ to $|u\rangle|\omega\rangle$ with $\omega_0 = N^{-1}\sum_{j<N} e^{ij\varphi}$, so $|\omega_0| = |\sin(N\varphi/2)|/(N|\sin(\varphi/2)|)$ for $\varphi \ne 0$ and $\omega_0 = 1$ for $\varphi = 0$. Line L5 maps $|\omega\rangle$ to $2\omega_0|0\rangle - |\omega\rangle$. Let $E$ be the phase estimation. Line L6 gives $E^{-1}(|u\rangle(2\omega_0|0\rangle - |\omega\rangle)) = 2\omega_0 E^{-1}|u\rangle|0\rangle - |u\rangle|0\rangle$. For $|\pi\rangle$, $\omega = |0\rangle$ and the result is $2|\pi\rangle|0\rangle - |\pi\rangle|0\rangle = |\pi\rangle|0\rangle$, exactly. For any other eigenvector the result differs from $-|u\rangle|0\rangle$ by $2\omega_0 E^{-1}|u\rangle|0\rangle$, of norm $2|\omega_0|$. If $|\varphi| \ge \Delta$ (mod $2\pi$), then $|\omega_0| \le 1/(N\sin(\Delta/2)) \le \pi/(N\Delta)$, which is a small constant for $N \ge c/\Delta$. By linearity the error on any vector of $A + B$ orthogonal to $|\pi\rangle$ is at most this constant times its norm. Theorem 6 of MNRS repeats the estimation $k$ times and flips the sign unless all $k$ estimates are 0, which makes the error at most $2^{1-k}$.

**(d)** Let $|\pi\rangle = \sin\beta\,|\mu\rangle + \cos\beta\,|\nu\rangle$, where $|\mu\rangle$ and $|\nu\rangle$ are the normalized parts of $|\pi\rangle$ with $x \in M$ and $x \notin M$; both lie in $A$. Line L3 is $\mathrm{ref}_\nu$ on this plane: it fixes $|\nu\rangle$ and negates $|\mu\rangle$. With an exact reflection, L4 to L6 are $\mathrm{ref}_\pi = 2|\pi\rangle\langle\pi| - I$, which maps the plane to itself. The product of two reflections about lines at the angle $\beta$ is a rotation by $2\beta$, so after $T_1$ rounds the state is $\sin((2T_1 + 1)\beta)|\mu\rangle + \cos((2T_1 + 1)\beta)|\nu\rangle$, up to a sign. A measurement of $x$ gives a marked state with probability $\sin^2((2T_1 + 1)\beta)$. With $\sin\beta \ge \sqrt{\varepsilon}$ and $T_1 = \lfloor \pi/(4\arcsin\sqrt{\varepsilon}) \rfloor$, if $\pi(M)$ is close to $\varepsilon$ this is $1 - O(\varepsilon)$; for an unknown $\pi(M) \ge \varepsilon$, a random $T_1$ in $[0, 1/\sqrt{\varepsilon}]$ gives a constant success probability, as in BBHT.

**Errors.** With approximate reflections of error $\eta$, each round adds at most $\eta$ to the distance from the ideal state, because all the operations are unitary (or norm 1 on the relevant space). After $T_1$ rounds the success probability is at least $(\sin((2T_1 + 1)\beta) - T_1\eta)^2$. With $k = \lg T_1 + O(1)$ copies, $T_1\eta$ is a small constant, which gives the cost with a $\log(1/\varepsilon)$ factor (Theorem 7 of MNRS). The recursive amplitude amplification of Section 4 of MNRS removes this factor; we cite it and do not repeat it. $\blacksquare$

**Cost.** Line L1 costs $S$, each round costs $C$ for L3 and $2(2^t - 1) = O(1/\Delta) = O(1/\sqrt{\delta})$ walk steps for L4 and L6, each of cost $O(U)$, and L7 costs $C$. So the total is $O(S + T_1(C + U/\sqrt{\delta})) = O(S + (1/\sqrt{\varepsilon})(U/\sqrt{\delta} + C))$ up to the factor discussed above.
