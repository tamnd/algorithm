**Setting.** $p = 4 \ell_1 \cdots \ell_n - 1$ is prime, with distinct odd primes $\ell_i$. Since $\ell_1 \cdots \ell_n$ is odd, $4 \ell_1 \cdots \ell_n \equiv 4 \pmod 8$ and $p \equiv 3 \pmod 8$. $X$ is the set of $A \in \mathbb{F}_p$ with $E_A : y^2 = x^3 + A x^2 + x$ supersingular. $\pi$ is the $p$-power Frobenius map, $\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-p}]$, and $\mathfrak{l}_i = (\ell_i, \pi - 1)$, $\overline{\mathfrak{l}}_i = (\ell_i, \pi + 1)$.

We use four cited facts.

- **(F1)** A supersingular curve $E$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p > 3$ has $\#E(\mathbb{F}_p) = p + 1$, and its Frobenius satisfies $\pi^2 = -p$. Its quadratic twist is supersingular too and also has $p + 1$ points (Silverman, The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves, Theorem V.3.1 gives a trace divisible by $p$, and the Hasse bound of Theorem V.1.1 then forces the trace $t$ to be 0; so $\pi^2 - t \pi + p = \pi^2 + p = 0$, and the twist has trace $-t = 0$).
- **(F2)** For $p \equiv 3 \pmod 8$, every $E_A$ with $A \in X$ has $\mathbb{F}_p$-rational endomorphism ring $\mathcal{O}$, and distinct $A$ give curves that are not isomorphic over $\mathbb{F}_p$. The group $\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{O})$ acts freely and transitively on $X$ by $[\mathfrak{a}] \star E = E / E[\mathfrak{a}]$, where $E[\mathfrak{a}] = \bigcap_{\alpha \in \mathfrak{a}} \ker \alpha$ (Castryck, Lange, Martindale, Panny and Renes, ASIACRYPT 2018, the class group action theorem and the proposition on Montgomery coefficients, from Deuring, Waterhouse and Schoof).
- **(F3)** For a point $K$ of odd prime order $\ell = 2d + 1$ on $E_A$, with $x_j = x([j] K)$, the map $x \mapsto x \prod_{j=1}^{d} \left( \frac{x x_j - 1}{x - x_j} \right)^2$ is the $x$-part of a separable isogeny with kernel $\langle K \rangle$ onto $E_{A'}$, with $A' = \left( \prod_j x_j \right)^2 \left( A - 6 \sum_j (x_j - x_j^{-1}) \right)$ (Costello and Hisil, ASIACRYPT 2017, Theorem 1). If $K$ is fixed or negated by $\pi$, the $x_j$ lie in $\mathbb{F}_p$, so the isogeny and $A'$ are defined over $\mathbb{F}_p$.
- **(F4)** The Montgomery ladder computes $x([k] P)$ from $x(P)$ (Costello and Smith, Montgomery curves and their arithmetic, Journal of Cryptographic Engineering 8, 2018).

**Lemma 1 (the kernels).** Let $A \in X$ and $E = E_A$. Then $E[\mathfrak{l}_i]$ is the set of $P \in E[\ell_i]$ with $\pi(P) = P$, and $E[\overline{\mathfrak{l}}_i]$ is the set of $P \in E[\ell_i]$ with $\pi(P) = -P$. Each is a cyclic group of order $\ell_i$, and $[\overline{\mathfrak{l}}_i] = [\mathfrak{l}_i]^{-1}$ in $\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{O})$.

*Proof.* The ideal $\mathfrak{l}_i$ is generated by $\ell_i$ and $\pi - 1$, so $E[\mathfrak{l}_i] = E[\ell_i] \cap \ker(\pi - 1)$; the same holds for $\overline{\mathfrak{l}}_i$ with $\pi + 1$. Points with $\pi(P) = P$ are the points of $E(\mathbb{F}_p)$, a group of order $p + 1 = 4 \ell_1 \cdots \ell_n$ by (F1). Since $\ell_i$ divides $p + 1$ exactly once, the Sylow $\ell_i$-subgroup of $E(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is cyclic of order $\ell_i$, and it equals $E(\mathbb{F}_p)[\ell_i]$. For $\pi(P) = -P$: such $P = (x, y)$ has $x^p = x$ and $y^p = -y$, so $x \in \mathbb{F}_p$ and $y \sqrt{c}^{-1} \in \mathbb{F}_p$ for a fixed non-square $c$. These points correspond to the $\mathbb{F}_p$-points of the twist $c y^2 = x^3 + A x^2 + x$, which has $p + 1$ points by (F1), and the same count gives a cyclic group of order $\ell_i$. Finally $\mathfrak{l}_i \overline{\mathfrak{l}}_i = (\ell_i^2, \ell_i(\pi + 1), \ell_i(\pi - 1), \pi^2 - 1) = (\ell_i^2, \ell_i (\pi + 1), \ell_i (\pi - 1), -(p + 1))$. This ideal contains $\ell_i^2$ and $p + 1$, and $\gcd(\ell_i^2, p + 1) = \ell_i$; it also contains $2 \ell_i = \ell_i(\pi + 1) - \ell_i(\pi - 1)$. So it contains $\ell_i$, and every generator is a multiple of $\ell_i$. Hence $\mathfrak{l}_i \overline{\mathfrak{l}}_i = (\ell_i)$, a principal ideal, and the classes are inverse. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (one round).** Consider one pass of the body of the loop of line L1, on a curve $E_A$ with $A \in X$ and the vector $e$. Let $\mathfrak{c}$ be the product of $\mathfrak{l}_i^{s}$ over the indices $i$ at which line L11 runs in this pass. At the end of the pass, the curve is $[\mathfrak{c}] \star E_A$, and $e$ has changed by $-s$ exactly at those indices.

*Proof.* Line L2 picks $x \ne 0$. Let $r = x^3 + A x^2 + x$. If $r$ is a nonzero square, $x$ is the $x$-coordinate of a point $P \in E(\mathbb{F}_p)$, so $\pi(P) = P$. If $r$ is a non-square, it is the $x$-coordinate of a point $P$ with $y \notin \mathbb{F}_p$, so $\pi(P) = -P$. If $r = 0$, $P = (x, 0)$ has order 2 and both hold. In every case line L3 sets $s$ so that $\pi(P) = s P$, and $[p + 1] P = \infty$ by (F1) and the proof of Lemma 1.

We claim that at the start of each pass of the inner loop at line L7, the current curve is $E_{A_c} = [\mathfrak{c}_c] \star E_A$, where $\mathfrak{c}_c$ is the product so far, and $Q$ is either $\infty$ or a point on $E_{A_c}$ with $\pi(Q) = s Q$ and $[k] Q = \infty$. At the start, $Q = [(p + 1)/k] P$ by (F4), so $[k] Q = [p + 1] P = \infty$, and $\pi(Q) = [(p + 1)/k] \pi(P) = s Q$. If $Q = \infty$, line L7 stops the loop and nothing changes. Otherwise line L8 computes $K = [k / \ell_i] Q$, which satisfies $[\ell_i] K = [k] Q = \infty$ and $\pi(K) = s K$. If $K = \infty$, line L9 skips $i$, and the claim still holds because $k$ and $Q$ do not change. If $K \ne \infty$, it has order $\ell_i$, so by Lemma 1, $\langle K \rangle = E_{A_c}[\mathfrak{l}_i]$ when $s = 1$ and $\langle K \rangle = E_{A_c}[\overline{\mathfrak{l}}_i]$ when $s = -1$. By (F3), line L10 computes $E_{A_c} / \langle K \rangle$, defined over $\mathbb{F}_p$; by (F2) it is $[\mathfrak{l}_i] \star E_{A_c}$ or, with Lemma 1, $[\mathfrak{l}_i]^{-1} \star E_{A_c}$, that is, $[\mathfrak{l}_i^{s}] \star E_{A_c}$. Since the action keeps $X$, the new $A_c$ is in $X$, and since the codomain is $\mathbb{F}_p$-isomorphic to exactly one Montgomery curve by (F2), the coefficient $A'$ of (F3) is that coefficient. Line L11 then subtracts $s$ from $e_i$, matching the factor $\mathfrak{l}_i^{s}$. Let $\varphi$ be the isogeny. It is defined over $\mathbb{F}_p$, so it commutes with $\pi$, and $\pi(\varphi(Q)) = s \varphi(Q)$. Also $[k/\ell_i] \varphi(Q) = \varphi(K) = \infty$, so after line L11 sets $k \leftarrow k / \ell_i$, the claim holds again. Each $i \in S$ is visited once, so each $e_i$ changes at most once. $\square$

**Theorem 1 (GROUP-ACTION).** If $A \in X$ and GROUP-ACTION$(A, e)$ returns, it returns $[\mathfrak{l}_1^{e_1} \cdots \mathfrak{l}_n^{e_n}] \star E_A$. It returns with probability 1.

*Proof.* Let $e^{(0)}$ be the input vector. By Lemma 2 and the commutativity of $\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{O})$, the invariant "the current curve is $[\prod_i \mathfrak{l}_i^{e^{(0)}_i - e_i}] \star E_A$" holds at line L1 before each round: it holds at the start with $e = e^{(0)}$, and a round that subtracts $s$ from $e_i$ multiplies the class by $\mathfrak{l}_i^{s}$. When the loop exits, $e = 0$, so the curve is $[\prod_i \mathfrak{l}_i^{e^{(0)}_i}] \star E_A$, and line L12 returns its coefficient.

For termination: line L4 picks only $i$ with $s e_i > 0$, so each update $e_i \leftarrow e_i - s$ moves $e_i$ toward 0, and $\sum_i |e_i|$ never grows. Take an index $i$ with $e_i \ne 0$. About half of the nonzero $x$ give each sign, so the sign of $e_i$ comes up with probability close to $1/2$. Given that sign, $P$ is uniform in a cyclic group of order $\ell_i$ times a coprime part, after the factor $[(p+1)/k]$, and the $\ell_i$-part of $Q$ survives the earlier steps of the inner loop, since those apply isogenies of other prime degrees, which are injective on points of order $\ell_i$. So $K \ne \infty$ with probability $1 - 1/\ell_i \ge 2/3$. Hence each round lowers $\sum_i |e_i|$ with probability bounded below by a constant, and the loop ends with probability 1. $\square$

**Theorem 2 (the shared key).** Let $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{b}$ be the classes of Alice's and Bob's exponent vectors, $A = [\mathfrak{a}] \star E_0$ and $B = [\mathfrak{b}] \star E_0$. Then Alice's key GROUP-ACTION$(B, e_A)$ and Bob's key GROUP-ACTION$(A, e_B)$ are equal.

*Proof.* $E_0 : y^2 = x^3 + x$ is supersingular for $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, so $0 \in X$, and by Theorem 1 both public keys are in $X$; so both pass the check before the shared key. By Theorem 1 again, Alice gets $[\mathfrak{a}] \star ([\mathfrak{b}] \star E_0) = [\mathfrak{a} \mathfrak{b}] \star E_0$ and Bob gets $[\mathfrak{b}] \star ([\mathfrak{a}] \star E_0) = [\mathfrak{b} \mathfrak{a}] \star E_0$, by the action axioms. $\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{O})$ is abelian, so $[\mathfrak{a} \mathfrak{b}] = [\mathfrak{b} \mathfrak{a}]$, and the two curves are the same element of $X$. By (F2), the same element of $X$ has one Montgomery coefficient, so the two returned values are equal. $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the twist.** If $E_A = [\mathfrak{a}] \star E_0$, then $[\mathfrak{a}]^{-1} \star E_0 = E_{-A}$. The map $(x, y) \mapsto (-x, \sqrt{-1}\, y)$ takes $E_A$ to $E_{-A}$, and since $-1$ is not a square mod $p$, it is defined only over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$, so $E_{-A}$ is the quadratic twist of $E_A$; it swaps the roles of $\pi$ and $-\pi$, hence of $\mathfrak{l}_i$ and $\overline{\mathfrak{l}}_i$. The tests check this on the toy prime.
