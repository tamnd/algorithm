---
title: The Arora-Raghavan-Rao scheme returns a (1 + ε)-approximate k-median in expectation
proves: [correctness, time.worst]
status: sketch
---

This is a proof outline. Parts 1 to 3 are complete. Part 4, the correctness of the dynamic program with rounded distances, is sketched; the full argument with the error bound for each level is in Arora, Raghavan and Rao (1998).

**Setup.** After line L1 the points and the candidate centres are centres of unit cells of an $L \times L$ grid, with $L$ a power of 2, and each part of the instance has $L = O(n^2/\varepsilon)$. With the shift $(a, b)$, the root square is $[-a, 2L - a) \times [-b, 2L - b)$, and $D = \log_2(2L)$ is the number of levels. A grid line has level $i$ if it is a side of squares of depth $i$, of side $2L/2^i$, but of no larger square. It carries portals at gap $2L/(2^i m)$, with $m$ a power of 2.

**Part 1 (the cost of snapping).** Let $C^*$ be an optimal set of centres and $c(p)$ the centre of $C^*$ nearest to $p$. Serve each $p$ along the segment from $p$ to $c(p)$, but at each crossing of a grid line walk along the line to the nearest portal and back. Over the random shift, the expected total length of these paths is at most $(1 + \sqrt 2\, D/m)\,\mathrm{OPT}$.

*Proof.* The segment from $p$ to $c(p)$ crosses $|\Delta_x| + |\Delta_y| \le \sqrt 2\, \lVert p - c(p) \rVert$ grid lines, since both ends are cell centres. A crossing of a line of level $i$ is at most half a gap from a portal, so its detour is at most $2L/(2^i m)$. The level of a vertical line $x = c$ is $i$ only if $c + a$ is a multiple of $2L/2^i$; among the $L$ values of $a$ this happens for at most $2^{i-1}$ of them. So the expected detour of one crossing is at most $\sum_{i=1}^{D} (2^{i-1}/L)(2L/(2^i m)) = D/m$, for every $b$; the same holds for horizontal lines. By linearity of expectation the expected total detour is at most $(D/m)\sqrt 2\,\mathrm{OPT}$. $\square$

With $m \ge 2\sqrt 2\, D/\varepsilon$, rounded up to a power of 2, the expected cost of this *portal-respecting* solution is at most $(1 + \varepsilon/2)\,\mathrm{OPT}$. Unlike for the TSP, no bound on the number of crossings per side is needed: each path is a single segment, and many paths may share a portal.

**Part 2 (smooth profiles).** For a square $Q$ of side $s$, list its $4m$ portals in order around the boundary, so that consecutive portals are $g = s/m$ apart. For a set $C$ of centres, let $\phi_j = \lfloor \delta_j / g \rfloor$, where $\delta_j$ is the distance from portal $j$ to the nearest centre of $C$. Then $|\phi_j - \phi_{j+1}| \le 1$ for all $j$, cyclically, and the number of such profiles with $\phi - \min \phi$ in $\{0, \ldots, R\}$ is at most $(R + 1)\, 3^{4m - 1}$.

*Proof.* By the triangle inequality $|\delta_j - \delta_{j+1}| \le g$. If $\delta_j \le \delta_{j+1} \le \delta_j + g$, then $\lfloor \delta_j/g \rfloor \le \lfloor \delta_{j+1}/g \rfloor \le \lfloor \delta_j/g \rfloor + 1$. A profile relative to its least value is fixed by its first value, at most $R + 1$ choices, and the $4m - 1$ steps that follow, each in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. $\square$

Any two portals of $Q$ are at most $\sqrt 2\, s = \sqrt 2\, m g$ apart, so $R \le 2m$ for the relative profile. The least value itself is one number per square, of size polynomial in $n/\varepsilon$ after the rounding below.

**Part 3 (time).** A square of the dynamic program has a state $(j, \phi^{\mathrm{in}}, \phi^{\mathrm{out}})$: the number $j \le k$ of centres inside, and the rounded profiles of distances to the nearest centre inside and outside the square. By Part 2 there are at most $(k + 1) \cdot (\mathrm{poly}(n/\varepsilon) \cdot 3^{4m})^2$ states. With $m = O(D/\varepsilon)$ and $D = O(\log(n/\varepsilon))$, this is $n^{O(1/\varepsilon)}$ for fixed $\varepsilon$ less than 1. The quadtree has $O(nD)$ squares, and a parent combines the states of its four children in $n^{O(1/\varepsilon)}$ time. The total time is $n^{O(1/\varepsilon)}$.

**Part 4 (the dynamic program, sketch).** For a state of $Q$, the stored value is the least cost of the points in $Q$, where a point is served either by a centre inside $Q$ along a portal-respecting path inside $Q$, or by a portal $j$ of $Q$ at the cost of the path to the portal plus $\phi^{\mathrm{out}}_j g$. A state of $Q$ is built from states of its children: the counts $j$ add up, the inner profile of $Q$ is the least over the children of the inner profiles carried to the portals of $Q$, and the outer profile of each child is implied by the outer profile of $Q$ and the inner profiles of its siblings. Each check uses portal-to-portal distances, so it is local to $Q$. The portal-respecting solution of Part 1 is consistent with one state of each square, so the root value is at most its cost plus the rounding errors. Arora, Raghavan and Rao choose the unit of rounding at each level so that the errors over the $D$ levels add up to at most $(\varepsilon/2)\,\mathrm{OPT}$. Conversely, every root value is the cost of an actual set of at most $k$ centres plus non-negative detours, so the returned solution costs at most the root value. Together with Part 1, the expected cost is at most $(1 + \varepsilon)\,\mathrm{OPT}$, and trying all $L^2$ shifts gives a deterministic bound. $\blacksquare$
