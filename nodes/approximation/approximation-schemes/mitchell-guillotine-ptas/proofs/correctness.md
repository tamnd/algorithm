**Setting.** $P$ is a set of $n$ points with no two on a common horizontal or vertical line. A *network* $E$ is a finite set of straight edges between points of $P$, of total length $L$. The *grid* is the set of lines $x = x_i$ and $y = y_i$ through the points, and a *window* is a rectangle with sides on the grid. For a window $W$ and a grid line $\ell$ through its inside, the *parts* of $\ell$ are the distinct points of $\ell \cap \operatorname{int}(W) \cap \bigcup E$, in order $\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_k$. The m-span $\sigma_m(\ell)$ is empty if $k \le 2(m - 1)$, and otherwise it is the piece of $\ell$ from $\xi_m$ to $\xi_{k - m + 1}$. No edge lies on a grid line, by the general position, so parts are points.

A *cut tree* of $W$ is either a leaf, if no point of $P$ lies strictly inside $W$, or a grid line $\ell$ through the inside of $W$ with cut trees of the two windows $W_1$ and $W_2$ into which $\ell$ cuts $W$. Its *bridge cost* is $\sum |\sigma_m(\ell)|$ over its cuts, where each span is measured in the window that the cut splits. Every window with a point strictly inside has a grid line through that point, so every window has a cut tree.

**Lemma 1 (the table).** For each window $W$, the value that line L3 stores for $W$ is the least bridge cost $B(W)$ of a cut tree of $W$.

*Proof.* Windows are handled in order of their number of grid gaps, and both parts of a cut have fewer gaps, so their values are ready. Induction on this number. A leaf has cost 0, and a window with no point strictly inside has no other cut tree that the definition allows. Otherwise the first cut of a best tree is some $\ell$, and the subtrees are cut trees of $W_1$ and $W_2$, so $B(W) \ge |\sigma_m(\ell)| + B(W_1) + B(W_2)$, and line L3 takes the minimum of the right side over all $\ell$. Conversely, each value that line L3 compares is the cost of a cut tree. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (bridges stay apart).** In a cut tree, each bridge $\sigma_m(\ell)$ of a window $W$ lies in $\operatorname{int}(W)$, its two ends are points of $\bigcup E$, and two bridges of the tree do not meet.

*Proof.* The ends are the parts $\xi_m$ and $\xi_{k-m+1}$, which are points of $\bigcup E$ in $\operatorname{int}(W)$, and the span lies between them on $\ell$. Two cuts of the tree are either in disjoint windows, whose insides are disjoint, or one window $W'$ lies in a part $W_1$ of the other. In the second case the bridge of $W'$ lies in $\operatorname{int}(W') \subseteq \operatorname{int}(W_1)$, and the bridge of $W$ lies on the cut line, which is on the boundary of $W_1$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the repair).** Let $E$ be a tour and $\mathcal B$ the bridges of a cut tree. Split each edge at the points where bridges meet it, and add each piece of each bridge between two consecutive parts twice. The result is a connected multigraph with all degrees even, of length $L + 2\,|\mathcal B|$, and shortcutting an Euler circuit of it gives a tour of length at most $L + 2\,|\mathcal B|$.

*Proof.* Splitting an edge at a point gives that point degree 2. A part of a bridge is a point of the network, and each bridge piece at it adds 2 to its degree, so all degrees stay even; a vertex of the tour has degree 2 and gains an even number. Lemma 2 says that bridges do not meet, so no other degrees change. The tour is connected and contains every part, so the graph is connected. A connected multigraph with even degrees has an Euler circuit. The circuit visits every point of $P$; keeping the first visit of each point replaces sub-walks by straight edges, which by the triangle inequality are no longer. $\square$

Lines L4 and L5 are Lemma 3, and lines L1 to L3 are Lemma 1.

**Lemma 4 (the length theorem, sketch).** Mitchell (1999) proves that every network of length $L$ in the plane has an m-guillotine supergraph whose added segments have length at most $\frac{\sqrt 2}{m} L$. For axis-parallel segments with the factor 1 in place of $\sqrt 2$, a full proof is on the [guillotine cut technique](/guillotine-cut-technique/) page: some cut has span at most its dark length, by counting the area of the dark region in two ways, and each unit of span is charged to the $m$ nearest edges on each side, with each point of an edge charged at most once from each side. For edges in general directions, a vertical cut charges an edge by at most $\frac{1}{m}$ of its $y$ extent and a horizontal cut by $\frac{1}{m}$ of its $x$ extent, and $|\Delta x| + |\Delta y| \le \sqrt 2$ times the length. That cuts can be taken on the grid, as in Lemma 1, is not proved here; the tests check the bound $B \le \frac{\sqrt 2}{m} L$ for the least cut tree on random inputs.

**Theorem (sketch).** Let $T^*$ be an optimal tour. Granting Lemma 4 with cuts on the grid, $B(T^*) \le \frac{\sqrt 2}{m}\,\mathrm{OPT}$, and Lemma 3 gives a tour of length at most $(1 + \frac{2\sqrt 2}{m})\,\mathrm{OPT}$.

The full scheme does not know $T^*$. It keeps, for each window and each *boundary state*, the least length of a network inside the window that is consistent with the state, plus its bridges counted twice. A state lists, on each of the four sides, the parts of the network on that side outside the span, at most $m - 1$ at each end, the two parts that end the span, and a pattern of how these pieces are joined inside the window. The network $T^*$ with its cut tree is one of the networks the table considers, so the value at the bounding box, over the states that close to a connected graph with even degrees, is at most $\mathrm{OPT} + 2\,B(T^*)$. Lemma 3 then turns that graph into a tour. The details of the states and of the agreement test at a cut are in Mitchell (1999); this part is a sketch.

**Running time (sketch).** There are $\binom{n}{2}^2 = O(n^4)$ windows, and each has at most $2n - 4$ grid lines through its inside. A part on a side is one edge, or a point with two of its edges, so there are $O(n^3)$ choices for each part, and a side keeps at most $2m$ parts: $n^{O(m)}$ choices per side. The joining pattern is a partition of $O(m)$ pieces, so $m^{O(m)}$ choices. Each window has $n^{O(m)}$ states and each transition pairs two states of the parts, so the total time is $n^{O(m)}$. The code runs only the table of Lemma 1 for a fixed network, in $O(n^4 \cdot n \cdot n) = O(n^6)$ time: $O(n^4)$ windows, $O(n)$ cuts each, and $O(n)$ edges per span.
