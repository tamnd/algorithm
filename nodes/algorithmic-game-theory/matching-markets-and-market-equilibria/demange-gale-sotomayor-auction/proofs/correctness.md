---
title: The exact auction stops at the minimum Walrasian prices with a Walrasian assignment
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

Fix $n$ bidders, $m$ items and nonnegative integer values $v_{ij}$. At a price vector $p \in \mathbb{R}^m_{\ge 0}$, bidder $i$ has utility $v_{ij} - p_j$ for item $j$ and $0$ for the null option $\varnothing$. Her demand set $D_i(p)$ is the set of options of maximum utility; $\varnothing \in D_i(p)$ exactly when that maximum is at most $0$. Call $i$ *required at $p$* when $\varnothing \notin D_i(p)$. A set $S$ of items is *overdemanded at $p$* when more than $|S|$ bidders have $D_i(p) \subseteq S$; such bidders are required, because $\varnothing \notin S$.

A *Walrasian equilibrium* is a pair $(p, \mu)$ of prices and an assignment ($\mu(i)$ is an item or $\varnothing$, no item twice) such that $\mu(i) \in D_i(p)$ for every $i$ and $p_j = 0$ for every item that $\mu$ leaves unsold. Then $p$ is a *Walrasian price vector*.

**Fact (Shapley and Shubik 1971; Leonard 1983).** Walrasian price vectors exist, and there is a componentwise minimum one, $p^*$. Each $p^*_j$ is the VCG payment of the buyer of $j$ (and $0$ if $j$ is unsold), a difference of two optimal assignment values, so with integer values $p^*$ is an integer vector. We use this fact; the tests check it on random instances.

**Claim 1 (Hall).** Let $R$ be the required bidders at $p$ and $S$ a set of items. Some subset of $S$ is overdemanded at $p$ if and only if the bidders $\{i \in R : D_i(p) \subseteq S\}$ cannot all be matched to distinct demanded items of $S$. In particular, no set is overdemanded if and only if all of $R$ can be matched to distinct demanded items. *Proof.* If $S' \subseteq S$ is overdemanded, the bidders with $D_i \subseteq S'$ are more than $|S'|$ and all their edges go into $S'$, so they cannot all be matched. Conversely, if they cannot, Hall's theorem gives a set $X$ of them with fewer than $|X|$ neighbors; the neighbor set $S' = N(X) \subseteq S$ has every bidder of $X$ demanding only inside $S'$, so $S'$ is overdemanded. $\square$

**Claim 2 (lines L6 and L7 find a minimal overdemanded set).** If line L5 does not stop, line L7 ends with a set $S$ that is overdemanded and has no overdemanded proper subset. *Proof.* Let $b$ be the unmatched required bidder of line L6, and $T$, $Y$ the items and bidders reached from $b$ by alternating paths (demand edges out of bidders, matching edges out of items). Every item of $T$ is matched, because otherwise the path to it would augment the maximum matching $M$. So $|Y| = |T| + 1$, and every bidder of $Y$ demands only items of $T$; hence $T$ is overdemanded. Line L7 keeps the invariant "$S$ contains an overdemanded subset", tested by Claim 1. At the end, let $S' \subseteq S$ be overdemanded. If some $j \in S \setminus S'$ existed, then when line L7 examined $j$ the current set (a superset of the final $S$) minus $j$ contained $S'$, so $j$ would have been removed. Hence $S' = S$, and every proper subset of $S$ lies in some $S \setminus \{j\}$, which contains no overdemanded subset. $\square$

**Claim 3 (no overshoot).** If $p \le p^*$ and $S$ is a minimal overdemanded set at $p$, then $p_j < p^*_j$ for every $j \in S$. *Proof.* Let $S_0 = \{j \in S : p_j = p^*_j\}$ and suppose $S_0 \ne \varnothing$. Let $B = \{i : D_i(p) \subseteq S\}$, so $|B| > |S|$, and let $X = \{i \in B : D_i(p) \cap S_0 \ne \varnothing\}$. For $i \in X$, the items of $D_i(p) \cap S_0$ keep their price at $p^*$ and all other prices weakly rise, so the maximum utility of $i$ is the same at $p^*$, it is positive, and every item that reaches it at $p^*$ reached it at $p$ with an unchanged price. So $D_i(p^*) \subseteq D_i(p) \cap S_0 \subseteq S_0$. In a Walrasian equilibrium at $p^*$ the bidders of $X$ get distinct items of $S_0$, so $|X| \le |S_0|$. The bidders of $B \setminus X$ demand only items of $S \setminus S_0$ at $p$, and $|B \setminus X| > |S| - |S_0| = |S \setminus S_0|$. If $S_0 = S$ this says that a nonempty set of bidders demands only items of the empty set, which is impossible. Otherwise $S \setminus S_0$ is an overdemanded proper subset of $S$, against minimality. $\square$

**Claim 4 (progress).** If $p \le p^*$ are integer vectors and $p \ne p^*$, then $S = \{j : p_j < p^*_j\}$ is overdemanded at $p$. *Proof.* Fix $0 < \varepsilon < 1$ and let $p' = p^* - \varepsilon \mathbf{1}_S$. Since all values and $p^*$ are integers, every utility gap at $p^*$ is an integer, so lowering the prices of $S$ by $\varepsilon$ keeps all strict preferences of $p^*$: $D_i(p') = D_i(p^*) \cap S$ when this intersection is nonempty (and then $\varnothing \notin D_i(p')$, because those items now give utility at least $\varepsilon$), and $D_i(p') = D_i(p^*)$ otherwise. Let $Z = \{i : D_i(p^*) \cap S \ne \varnothing\}$ and let $\mu^*$ be a Walrasian assignment at $p^*$. Each $j \in S$ has $p^*_j \ge p_j + 1 \ge 1$, so $\mu^*$ sells it, to a bidder who demands it, that is, to a bidder of $Z$. Hence $|Z| \ge |S|$. If $|Z| = |S|$, then $\mu^*$ maps $Z$ onto $S$, every $i \in Z$ gets $\mu^*(i) \in D_i(p^*) \cap S = D_i(p')$, every other bidder gets $\mu^*(i) \in D_i(p^*) = D_i(p')$, and every item with $p'_j > 0$ has $p^*_j > 0$ and is sold. So $(p', \mu^*)$ is a Walrasian equilibrium with $p' \le p^*$ and $p' \ne p^*$, against the minimality of $p^*$. Hence $|Z| > |S|$. Finally, $p$ differs from $p'$ only by lower prices on $S$, so for $i \in Z$, whose demand at $p'$ lies in $S$ and excludes $\varnothing$, the demand at $p$ still lies in $S$. So more than $|S|$ bidders have $D_i(p) \subseteq S$. $\square$

**Termination and the final prices.** By induction on the rounds, $p \le p^*$ always: it holds for $p = 0$, and by Claims 2 and 3 a round raises only prices with $p_j < p^*_j$, by 1, and $p^*_j$ is an integer. Each round raises the integer $\sum_j p_j$ by at least 1, and $\sum_j p_j \le \sum_j p^*_j$, so there are at most $\sum_j p^*_j$ rounds. The loop stops at line L5 only when no set is overdemanded (Claim 1). By Claim 4 this cannot happen while $p \ne p^*$; and at $p = p^*$ no set is overdemanded, because in a Walrasian equilibrium the bidders who demand only items of $S$ get distinct items of $S$. So the loop stops exactly at $p = p^*$.

**The final assignment (lines L9 and L10).** At $p = p^*$, line L4 gave a matching $M$ of all required bidders to demanded items. Let $j_0$ be an item with $p_{j_0} > 0$ that $M$ leaves unsold, and let $\mu^*$ be a Walrasian assignment at $p^*$, seen as a matching in the graph of demand edges (the bidders who get $\varnothing$ are unmatched). In the symmetric difference of $M$ and $\mu^*$, the component of $j_0$ is a path that starts at $j_0$ with an edge of $\mu^*$ (since $\mu^*$ sells $j_0$) and alternates. It ends at a bidder that $M$ leaves unmatched, or at an item that $\mu^*$ leaves unsold, which has price 0. So the search of line L9 finds an alternating path of that kind. Flipping it sells $j_0$, keeps every matched bidder matched to a demanded item, and either matches a bidder with $\varnothing \in D_i(p)$ to a demanded item or unsells an item of price 0. The number of unsold items of positive price drops by one. After line L9, every bidder has a demanded option (the required ones are matched; the others are matched to a demanded item or take $\varnothing \in D_i(p)$), and every unsold item has price 0. So line L10 returns a Walrasian equilibrium at the minimum Walrasian prices. $\blacksquare$
