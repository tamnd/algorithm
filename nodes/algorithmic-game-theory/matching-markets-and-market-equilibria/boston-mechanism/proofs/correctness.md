---
title: The Boston mechanism is feasible, Pareto efficient for the reported lists, and equal to deferred acceptance with adjusted priorities
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

Fix an input: students $I$ with $|I| = n$, schools $S$ with $|S| = m$, capacities $q_s \ge 0$, strict priorities $\succ_s$ over $I$, and reported strict lists $P_i$ without repeats, each of length at most $m$. Write $\text{pl}_i(s)$ for the place of $s$ on $P_i$ (1 for the top) and set $\text{pl}_i(\text{none}) = \infty$; student $i$ prefers $s$ to $t$ when $\text{pl}_i(s) < \text{pl}_i(t)$. Let $a$ be the output of BOSTON, and say that student $i$ is *accepted in round $k$* when line L6 assigns her in the pass of line L2 with that $k$.

**Termination.** The loop of line L2 makes exactly $m$ passes, and each pass touches finitely many students and schools. So the algorithm stops.

**Claim 1 (feasibility).** Every student gets at most one school, that school is on her list, and school $s$ gets at most $q_s$ students. *Proof.* Line L6 removes each accepted student from $U$, and line L3 only looks at students in $U$, so a student is accepted at most once. A student in $A_s$ has $s$ on her list. The invariant $\text{left}[s] = q_s - |\{i : a(i) = s \text{ so far}\}| \ge 0$ holds after line L1 and is kept by lines L5 and L6, because $|T| \le \text{left}[s]$. $\square$

**Claim 2 (full after a rejection).** If student $i$ applies to $s$ in round $k$ and is not accepted, then at the end of round $k$ school $s$ has no seat left, and every student assigned to $s$ was accepted in a round $j \le k$. *Proof.* Line L5 takes $\min(\text{left}[s], |A_s|)$ students. If $i$ is not among them, then $|A_s| > \text{left}[s]$ and the minimum is $\text{left}[s]$, so after line L6 $\text{left}[s] = 0$. Nothing is assigned to $s$ later, because line L5 then takes nobody. $\square$

**Claim 3 (Pareto efficiency for the reported lists).** There is no feasible assignment $b$ with $\text{pl}_i(b(i)) \le \text{pl}_i(a(i))$ for all $i$ and strict inequality for some $i$. *Proof.* Let $b$ be feasible with $\text{pl}_i(b(i)) \le \text{pl}_i(a(i))$ for all $i$. We show $b = a$. We prove by induction on $k = 1, \ldots, m$ that every student accepted in round $k$ has $b(i) = a(i)$.

Let $i$ be accepted in round $k$ at $s = a(i)$, so $s$ is at place $k$ of $P_i$ and $i$ was in $U$ in every round $j < k$. Since $\text{pl}_i(b(i)) \le k$, $b(i)$ is one of the first $k$ schools on $P_i$. Let $t$ be at place $j < k$. In round $j$ student $i$ applied to $t$ and was not accepted, so by Claim 2 school $t$ is full in $a$ with students accepted in rounds $\le j < k$. By the induction hypothesis all of them have $b = t$ as well, so $b$ gives $t$ its $q_t$ students already and has no seat for $i$. So $b(i) \ne t$, and hence $b(i) = s$.

Now let $i$ be unassigned in $a$. She applied to every school on her list, in rounds $1, \ldots, |P_i|$, and was rejected each time. By Claim 2 and the induction (which now covers all rounds), every school on her list is full in $b$ with students other than $i$, so $b(i) = \text{none}$.

So $b = a$, and no $b$ can be strictly better for some student. $\square$

**Claim 4 (most first choices).** Let $f_s$ be the number of students with $s$ at place 1. Every feasible assignment gives at most $\sum_s \min(q_s, f_s)$ students their first choice, and $a$ gives exactly that many. *Proof.* In any feasible assignment, the students who get their first choice $s$ are among the $f_s$ students who list $s$ first and are at most $q_s$. In round 1 of BOSTON, $A_s$ is exactly these $f_s$ students and $\text{left}[s] = q_s$, so line L5 accepts $\min(q_s, f_s)$ of them at $s$. $\square$

**Claim 5 (deferred acceptance with adjusted priorities).** For each school $s$ define the strict order $\succ'_s$ on $I$: $i \succ'_s j$ if $\text{pl}_i(s) < \text{pl}_j(s)$, or $\text{pl}_i(s) = \text{pl}_j(s)$ and $i \succ_s j$. Then student-proposing deferred acceptance with the lists $P_i$, the priorities $\succ'_s$ and the capacities $q_s$ outputs $a$. *Proof.* Deferred acceptance gives the same output for every order of proposals, because its output is the student-optimal stable matching (see the proof on the Gale-Shapley page; the many-to-one case reduces to one-to-one by splitting each school into $q_s$ copies with the same priority). So we may run it in rounds: in round $k$, every student who is not held and has a list of length at least $k$ proposes to the school at place $k$ of her list, and each school keeps the $q_s$ best proposers by $\succ'_s$ among those it holds and the new ones, and rejects the rest.

We prove by induction on $k$: at the end of round $k$ of this run, the students held at each school $s$ are exactly the students that BOSTON has assigned to $s$ in rounds $1, \ldots, k$, and the students not held are exactly $U$ after round $k$ of BOSTON, each of whom has made exactly $k$ proposals or used up her list. For $k = 0$ nobody is held, and $U$ is all students. In round $k$, the new proposers to $s$ are the students of $U$ with $s$ at place $k$, which is the set $A_s$ of line L3. Each student held at $s$ from an earlier round $j < k$ lists $s$ at place $j < k$, so she is above every new proposer in $\succ'_s$, and the held students are never rejected. Among the new proposers, who all list $s$ at place $k$, $\succ'_s$ agrees with $\succ_s$. So $s$ keeps its old students and takes the $\min(\text{left}[s], |A_s|)$ new proposers of highest priority $\succ_s$, which is the set $T$ of line L5. This is the step of BOSTON, and the induction goes through. After round $m$ every list is used up or its owner is held, so the run of deferred acceptance has ended, with the output $a$. $\blacksquare$
