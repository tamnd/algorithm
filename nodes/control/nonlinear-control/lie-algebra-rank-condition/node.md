The Lie algebra rank condition (LARC) is the rank test for nonlinear systems that takes the place of the [Kalman rank condition](/kalman-rank-condition/). It asks whether the system fields and all their iterated [Lie brackets](/lie-bracket/) span all directions at a point. If they do, the set of states reachable from the point has nonempty interior: the system can reach a full-dimensional set, not only a thin surface. This property is called local accessibility. It is weaker than [controllability](/controllability/): a system can satisfy the condition and still be unable to return to its start, because the drift pushes in one direction only. For analytic systems the condition is also necessary for accessibility. For systems without drift, such as the [unicycle](/unicycle-model/), the theorem of Chow and Rashevsky turns the same condition into controllability. These results are due to Sussmann and Jurdjevic (1972) and Hermann and Krener (1977), with Chow (1939) and Rashevsky (1938) for the driftless case.

## Statement

**Setting.** The system is $\dot x = f(x) + \sum_{i=1}^m g_i(x) u_i$ on an open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, with smooth ($C^\infty$) fields. The inputs are piecewise constant with values in a set $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$. For each constant $u \in \Omega$, the field $X_u = f + \sum_i g_i u_i$ is smooth, so its solutions exist and are unique by the [Picard-Lindelöf theorem](/picard-lindelof-theorem/). Let $\mathcal F = \{X_u : u \in \Omega\}$. The **accessibility algebra** $\mathcal L$ is the smallest set of vector fields that contains $\mathcal F$ and is closed under linear combinations and Lie brackets. If $\Omega$ contains $0$ and the unit vectors, $\mathcal L$ is also the Lie algebra generated by $f, g_1, \dots, g_m$. Write $\mathcal L(x) = \{X(x) : X \in \mathcal L\}$.

The system satisfies the **Lie algebra rank condition at $x_0$** if $\dim \mathcal L(x_0) = n$.

The **reachable set** $\mathcal R(x_0, \le T)$ is the set of states $x(t)$ with $0 \le t \le T$ over all such inputs and all solutions from $x(0) = x_0$ that stay in $U$. The system is **locally accessible** from $x_0$ if $\mathcal R(x_0, \le T)$ has nonempty interior for every $T > 0$.

**Theorem.**

1. (Sufficiency.) If the Lie algebra rank condition holds at $x_0$, the system is locally accessible from $x_0$.
2. (Necessity for analytic systems.) If $f$ and the $g_i$ are real analytic and $\mathcal R(x_0, \le T)$ has nonempty interior for some $T > 0$, then the condition holds at $x_0$ (Sussmann and Jurdjevic 1972).
3. (Systems without drift.) If $f = 0$, $\Omega$ is $\mathbb{R}^m$ or contains a neighborhood of $0$, $U$ is connected, and the condition holds at every point of $U$, then every point of $U$ can be reached from every other point (Chow 1939, Rashevsky 1938).

Part 1 is proved below. Parts 2 and 3 are stated with their sources.

## In plain words

Each input pushes the state in some direction. Switching between inputs creates new directions, just as a car parks sideways by going forward and backward while turning. The condition collects all directions that can be made this way, by switching quickly and in combinations. If they cover every direction at the starting point, then the states you can reach from there fill a solid region, not just a curve or a surface. That does not mean you can reach every point near the start. A system that always drifts forward can fill a region ahead of it and still never go back. When there is no drift, so that every motion can be reversed, filling every direction does mean you can go anywhere.

## Proof

We prove part 1. Two facts are used. (F1) The condition is open: if $X_1, \dots, X_n \in \mathcal L$ have independent values at $x_0$, they are independent on a neighborhood $V$ of $x_0$, so $\dim \mathcal L(x) = n$ for $x \in V$. (F2) If $N$ is a $k$-dimensional embedded submanifold and the fields $X$, $Y$ are tangent to $N$ at every point of $N$, then $[X, Y]$ is tangent to $N$ too. In coordinates where $N = \{x_{k+1} = \dots = x_n = 0\}$, for $j > k$ the component $Y_j$ vanishes on $N$, so its derivatives in $x_1, \dots, x_k$ vanish on $N$, and $X_i = 0$ on $N$ for $i > k$. So $[X, Y]_j = \sum_i \left( \frac{\partial Y_j}{\partial x_i} X_i - \frac{\partial X_j}{\partial x_i} Y_i \right) = 0$ on $N$.

Fix $T > 0$, and let $\Phi^X_t$ be the flow of $X$. For $X^1, \dots, X^k \in \mathcal F$ and times $t = (t_1, \dots, t_k)$ with all $t_i > 0$, let

$$F_k(t) = \Phi^{X^k}_{t_k} \circ \dots \circ \Phi^{X^1}_{t_1}(x_0).$$

Each $F_k(t)$ is in $\mathcal R(x_0, \le T)$ if $t_1 + \dots + t_k \le T$: use the input $u^1$ for time $t_1$, then $u^2$ for time $t_2$, and so on. We show by induction on $k = 1, \dots, n$: there are fields $X^1, \dots, X^k \in \mathcal F$ and a nonempty open set $W_k$ of times $t$ with all $t_i > 0$ and $\sum t_i < T$, such that $F_k(W_k) \subseteq V$ and the derivative $DF_k(t)$ has rank $k$ for every $t \in W_k$.

*Step $k = 1$.* If every $X \in \mathcal F$ had $X(x_0) = 0$, then every bracket of them would vanish at $x_0$, because $[X, Y](x_0) = \frac{\partial Y}{\partial x} X(x_0) - \frac{\partial X}{\partial x} Y(x_0) = 0$. Then $\mathcal L(x_0) = 0$, which contradicts the condition. So some $X^1 \in \mathcal F$ has $X^1(x_0) \neq 0$. Then $DF_1(t) = X^1(F_1(t)) \neq 0$ for small $t > 0$, and $F_1(t) \in V$ for small $t$. Take $W_1$ an interval $(0, \delta)$.

*Step $k$ to $k + 1$, for $k < n$.* Pick $t^* \in W_k$. By the constant rank theorem, there is an open $W' \subseteq W_k$ around $t^*$ whose image $N = F_k(W')$ is a $k$-dimensional embedded submanifold, and $N \subseteq V$. Suppose that every $X \in \mathcal F$ is tangent to $N$ at every point of $N$. By (F2) and induction on the number of brackets, every field in $\mathcal L$ is tangent to $N$. Then $\dim \mathcal L(p) \le k < n$ for $p \in N$, which contradicts (F1). So there are $X^{k+1} \in \mathcal F$ and $s \in W'$ such that $X^{k+1}(p)$, with $p = F_k(s)$, is not in the tangent space $DF_k(s)\, \mathbb{R}^k$. Let $F_{k+1}(t, \tau) = \Phi^{X^{k+1}}_\tau(F_k(t))$. At $(s, 0)$, its derivative has the $k$ independent columns of $DF_k(s)$ and the column $X^{k+1}(p)$, so it has rank $k + 1$. Rank $k + 1$ is an open condition, $F_{k+1}(s, \tau) \to p \in V$ as $\tau \to 0$, and $\sum s_i < T$. So for a small $\tau_0 > 0$, the point $(s, \tau_0)$ has rank $k + 1$, image in $V$ and total time below $T$. A small open ball $W_{k+1}$ around $(s, \tau_0)$ keeps these three properties and $\tau > 0$.

*Conclusion.* For $k = n$, $DF_n(t)$ is invertible at a point $t \in W_n$. By the inverse function theorem, $F_n$ maps a neighborhood of $t$ onto an open set. This open set is in $\mathcal R(x_0, \le T)$. $\blacksquare$

## Consequences

- **Linear systems.** For $f(x) = A x$ and $g_i = b_i$, the brackets are $[A x, b_i] = -A b_i$, $[A x, A b_i] = -A^2 b_i$, and so on, and brackets of constant fields are zero. So $\mathcal L(0) = \mathrm{span}\{A^k b_i\}$, and the condition at $0$ is the [Kalman rank condition](/kalman-rank-condition/). For linear systems it is equivalent to controllability.
- **Systems without drift.** The nonholonomic integrator $\dot x_1 = u_1$, $\dot x_2 = u_2$, $\dot x_3 = x_1 u_2 - x_2 u_1$ has $g_1 = (1, 0, -x_2)$, $g_2 = (0, 1, x_1)$ and $[g_1, g_2] = (0, 0, 2)$. These span $\mathbb{R}^3$ at every point, so by part 3 the system is controllable. The [unicycle](/unicycle-model/) and other systems with [nonholonomic constraints](/nonholonomic-constraint/) are controllable for the same reason.
- **Stabilization is a different question.** The nonholonomic integrator is controllable, but by [Brockett's condition](/brockett-condition/) no continuous feedback $u(x)$ makes the origin asymptotically stable.
- **Feedback linearizable systems.** The rank part of the [conditions for feedback linearizability](/feedback-linearizability-conditions/) implies the condition here, so those systems are locally accessible.
- **Observability.** The dual condition, on the span of the differentials of the output and its Lie derivatives, gives local observability (Hermann and Krener 1977).

## Tightness

- **Accessibility is not controllability.** For $\dot x_1 = x_2^2$, $\dot x_2 = u$, the fields are $f = (x_2^2, 0)$ and $g = (0, 1)$. Then $[f, g] = (-2 x_2, 0)$ and $[g, [f, g]] = (-2, 0)$, so $\mathcal L(0)$ is all of $\mathbb{R}^2$. But $\dot x_1 \ge 0$ always, so no state with $x_1 < 0$ can be reached from $0$. The system is locally accessible and not locally controllable.
- **A linear system off the origin.** For $\dot x = x + e_1 u$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$, the pair $(A, b) = (I, e_1)$ is not controllable. But at $x_0 = (0, 1)$, $\mathcal L(x_0)$ contains $b = (1, 0)$ and $f(x_0) = (0, 1)$, so the condition holds there. The reachable set has nonempty interior, while $x_2(t) = e^t x_2(0)$ can only grow.
- **Necessity fails for smooth systems that are not analytic.** Let $\varphi(s) = e^{-1/s^2}$ for $s \neq 0$ and $\varphi(0) = 0$, and take $\dot x_1 = u$, $\dot x_2 = \varphi(x_1)$. The field $f = (0, \varphi(x_1))$ and all brackets of $f$ and $g = (1, 0)$ have the form $(0, c\, \varphi^{(k)}(x_1))$ with a constant $c$, and they vanish at $x_1 = 0$. So $\dim \mathcal L(0) = 1$. But with $u = 1$ for a time $a$ and then $u = 0$ for a time $b$, the end state has $x_1 = a$ and $x_2$ increasing in $b$ at the rate $\varphi(a) > 0$, and these states fill an open set. So part 2 needs analyticity.
- **Accessibility at a fixed time** needs a different algebra, the ideal generated by the brackets with at least one $g_i$; the condition here allows any time up to $T$.

## History

Chow (1939) and Rashevsky (1938) proved independently that a distribution whose brackets span every direction connects any two points by curves tangent to it, which is part 3 in the language of control. Sussmann and Jurdjevic (1972) proved accessibility for systems with drift from the rank of the Lie algebra, and the converse for analytic systems. Hermann and Krener (1977) set out the local notions of controllability and observability and their rank conditions in the form used in control texts today.
