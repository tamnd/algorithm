The additive group modulo $n$, written $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}_n$ or $(\mathbb{Z}_n, +)$, is the set of residues $\{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$ with addition modulo $n$ as the operation. It is the simplest [finite group](/finite-group/) with $n$ elements. It is a [cyclic group](/cyclic-group/): every element is a sum of copies of $1$, and every cyclic group with $n$ elements has the same structure. Its generators are the residues [coprime](/coprime-integers/) to $n$, so there are $\varphi(n)$ of them, where $\varphi$ is [Euler's totient](/euler-totient/). It is the model of unsigned machine arithmetic with $w$-bit words, which is arithmetic in $\mathbb{Z}/2^w\mathbb{Z}$.

## In plain words

Think of a clock with a fixed number of hours. To add two times, move the hand forward and wrap around past the top. That is addition modulo the number of hours.

These remainders with this wrap-around addition form a group. There is a zero, every element has an opposite that brings it back to zero, and the order of the additions does not matter.

Starting from one and adding one again and again visits every hour, so a single element creates the whole group. Some other steps also visit every hour, and some do not. On a twelve-hour clock, a step of five visits all twelve hours, but a step of eight only visits three of them.

## The idea

**The group.** Fix $n \ge 1$. On the residues $\{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$ define $a \oplus b = (a + b) \bmod n$. Since [congruence](/congruence/) respects addition, this is the same as adding residue classes, $[a] + [b] = [a + b]$. The group axioms hold:

- associativity and commutativity come from those of integer addition;
- the identity is $0$;
- the inverse of $a$ is $-a \bmod n$, which is $n - a$ for $a \ne 0$ and $0$ for $a = 0$.

So $(\mathbb{Z}_n, +)$ is an abelian group of order $n$. For $n = 1$ it is the trivial group $\{0\}$.

**Multiples instead of powers.** In additive notation, the "power" $a^k$ of a multiplicative group becomes the multiple $k \cdot a = a + a + \cdots + a$ ($k$ terms), reduced modulo $n$. The **order** of $a$ is the least $t \ge 1$ with $t a \equiv 0 \pmod n$.

**Order of an element.** The order of $a$ in $\mathbb{Z}_n$ is $n / \gcd(a, n)$. Let $g = \gcd(a, n)$, $a = g a'$ and $n = g n'$, so $\gcd(a', n') = 1$. Then $n \mid t a$ if and only if $n' \mid t a'$, if and only if $n' \mid t$, by [Euclid's lemma](/euclids-lemma/). The least such $t \ge 1$ is $n' = n/g$.

**Generators.** The element $a$ generates the group, that is, its multiples are all of $\mathbb{Z}_n$, exactly when its order is $n$, that is, when $\gcd(a, n) = 1$. So $1$ always generates $\mathbb{Z}_n$, and the number of generators is $\varphi(n)$. If $\gcd(a, n) = 1$, the map $x \mapsto a x \bmod n$ is a permutation of $\mathbb{Z}_n$, and its inverse is multiplication by the [modular inverse](/modular-inverse/) of $a$.

**Subgroups.** For each positive divisor $d$ of $n$ there is exactly one [subgroup](/subgroup/) of order $d$, namely the multiples of $n/d$:

$$\langle n/d \rangle = \{0, \, n/d, \, 2n/d, \, \ldots, \, (d-1) n/d\}.$$

There are no other subgroups. Let $H \ne \{0\}$ be a subgroup, and let $m$ be its least element in $1$ to $n - 1$. For $h \in H$, write $h = qm + r$ with $0 \le r < m$; then $r = h - qm$ is in $H$, so $r = 0$ by the choice of $m$. Write also $n = qm + r$ with $0 \le r < m$; then $r \equiv -qm \pmod n$ is in $H$, so again $r = 0$ and $m \mid n$. Hence $H = \langle m \rangle$ with $m \mid n$, and it has $n/m$ elements. This is the [Lagrange's theorem](/lagranges-theorem-group-theory/) picture in its simplest form: subgroup orders are exactly the divisors of $n$.

**Counting by order.** Each element of order $d$ generates the subgroup of order $d$, and that subgroup is cyclic of order $d$, so it has $\varphi(d)$ generators. Every element has some order $d \mid n$, so

$$\sum_{d \mid n} \varphi(d) = n,$$

which is the [totient divisor sum](/totient-divisor-sum/) identity.

**Universality.** Every cyclic group $G = \langle g \rangle$ of order $n$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_n$ by $k \mapsto g^k$. So facts about $\mathbb{Z}_n$ hold for every cyclic group, including the [multiplicative group modulo a prime](/multiplicative-group-modulo-n/), which is cyclic of order $p - 1$.

**Products.** By the [Chinese remainder theorem](/chinese-remainder-theorem/), $\mathbb{Z}_{mn} \cong \mathbb{Z}_m \times \mathbb{Z}_n$ when $\gcd(m, n) = 1$. When $\gcd(m, n) > 1$ they differ: $\mathbb{Z}_4$ has an element of order $4$, while each element of $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ has order at most $2$.

## Examples

- **$\mathbb{Z}_{12}$.** The orders of the elements $0, 1, \ldots, 11$ are $1, 12, 6, 4, 3, 12, 2, 12, 3, 4, 6, 12$. The generators are $1, 5, 7, 11$, which are the $\varphi(12) = 4$ residues coprime to $12$. The element $8$ has order $12 / \gcd(8, 12) = 3$, and its multiples are $\{0, 8, 4\}$. The subgroups are $\{0\}$, $\{0, 6\}$, $\{0, 4, 8\}$, $\{0, 3, 6, 9\}$, $\{0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10\}$ and $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$, one for each divisor $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$ of $12$.
- **$\mathbb{Z}_2$.** The group $\{0, 1\}$ with $1 + 1 = 0$ is the exclusive or of one bit. The group $\mathbb{Z}_2^k$ is the set of $k$-bit strings under bitwise exclusive or, which is not cyclic for $k \ge 2$.
- **Machine words.** Unsigned addition of $w$-bit words in C, Java and most hardware is addition in $\mathbb{Z}_{2^w}$: the carry out of the top bit is dropped. Signed [two's complement](/twos-complement/) arithmetic uses the same group with the representatives $-2^{w-1}, \ldots, 2^{w-1} - 1$. The generators of $\mathbb{Z}_{2^w}$ are the odd residues.
- **Non-example.** The residues $\{0, \ldots, n-1\}$ with multiplication modulo $n$ are not a group for $n \ge 2$, since $0$ has no inverse. The invertible residues form the [multiplicative group modulo n](/multiplicative-group-modulo-n/) instead.

## Where it is used

- **Cyclic structure in algorithms.** A [circular buffer](/circular-buffer/) moves its indices in $\mathbb{Z}_n$. The [Josephus problem](/josephus-problem/) counts around a circle in $\mathbb{Z}_n$. The [Caesar cipher](/caesar-cipher/) adds a fixed key in $\mathbb{Z}_{26}$.
- **Visiting all slots.** A step $s$ with $\gcd(s, n) = 1$ visits every residue before it returns to the start. This is used in hashing with a fixed probe step in a [hash table](/hash-table/), and in the full-period condition of the [linear congruential generator](/linear-congruential-generator/).
- **Discrete logarithms are easy here.** Solving $k a \equiv b \pmod n$ for $k$ is a [linear congruence](/linear-congruence/), solved by the [extended Euclidean algorithm](/extended-euclidean-algorithm/). The same task in a multiplicative group is the [discrete logarithm problem](/discrete-logarithm-problem/), which is believed hard in some groups. The two groups can be isomorphic, but the isomorphism is the discrete logarithm itself.
- **Fourier analysis.** The [discrete Fourier transform](/discrete-fourier-transform/) of length $n$ is the Fourier transform on the group $\mathbb{Z}_n$, and cyclic convolution is convolution in $\mathbb{Z}_n$.
