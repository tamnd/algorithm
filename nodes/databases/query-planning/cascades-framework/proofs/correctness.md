---
title: The task-based Cascades search on the join model explores each needed group completely and returns a cheapest physical plan for the required sort order
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

**Setting.** The model is the one of the code. The relations $R_0, \ldots, R_{n-1}$ share one join attribute, a group is a set of relations, a join expression of a group $S$ is an ordered split $(L, R)$ of $S$, and the leaf group $\{i\}$ holds the expression GET$(R_i)$. The rules are commutativity and associativity, as on the Volcano page. A *goal* is a pair $(S, p)$ of a group and a property in $\{\text{any}, \text{sorted}\}$. For a goal, the moves are the algorithms of the code on each expression of $S$ and, if $p$ is sorted, the enforcer Sort with the input goal $(S, \text{any})$. A physical plan for a goal is a move with a plan for each input goal; its cost is the local cost plus the costs of the input plans, and $\mathrm{opt}(S, p)$ is the least cost of a plan. All local costs are nonnegative. The tasks are kept on a stack, and the main loop of lines L1 to L3 pops and runs the top task until the stack is empty. A task T is a *descendant* of a task U if U pushed T, or pushed a task of which T is a descendant.

**Lemma 1 (stack order).** Let a task U run. Every descendant of U runs before every task that was on the stack below U when U was popped.

*Proof.* The tasks below U stay below every task that U or its descendants push, and the stack is last in, first out. $\square$

So the goal O_GROUP$(G, q, \lambda)$ that an O_INPUTS task pushes at line L14, with all its descendants, runs before the copy of O_INPUTS that line L14 pushed under it.

**Lemma 2 (descendants stay inside).** The descendants of E_GROUP$(S)$ change only the groups that are subsets of $S$, and they push E_GROUP only for proper subsets of $S$. The descendants of O_GROUP$(S, p)$ push O_GROUP only for goals $(G, q)$ with $G \subsetneq S$, or for $(S, \text{any})$ when $p$ is sorted, and the descendants of O_GROUP$(S, \text{any})$ never push O_GROUP$(S, \cdot)$.

*Proof.* An E_EXPR or APPLY_RULE task of a group $T$ adds expressions to $T$ and to $B \cup R \subsetneq T$, and pushes E_GROUP only for the left group $L \subsetneq T$ of an expression of $T$. A join move of $T$ asks for goals of its two sides, which are proper subsets of $T$; a scan asks for nothing; the Sort move of $(T, \text{sorted})$ asks for $(T, \text{any})$. Induction over the descendants gives the claim. $\square$

By Lemmas 1 and 2, when a task asks for E_GROUP$(L)$ or O_GROUP$(G, q)$, the group $L$ is not being explored and the goal $(G, q)$ is not being optimized by a task below. So the assertions of the code hold, and E_GROUP returns early at line L7 only for an explored group.

**Lemma 3 (exploration).** When MARK_EXPLORED$(S)$ runs at line L17, the group $S$ holds every ordered split of $S$. Later, no new expression is added to $S$. Each rule is applied to each expression at most once.

*Proof.* By induction on $|S|$. The rule marks of line L8 are set before the rule is applied, so each rule runs at most once on each expression. Line L7 pushes E_EXPR for every expression that $S$ has when its exploration starts. During the exploration, by Lemma 2, only APPLY_RULE tasks of $S$ add expressions to $S$, and line L9 pushes E_EXPR for each new one. By Lemma 1 all of these tasks run before MARK_EXPLORED$(S)$. So at that time both rules were applied to every expression of $S$. For associativity on $(L, R)$, line L8 pushes E_GROUP$(L)$ above the APPLY_RULE task, so $L$ is explored when the rule runs, and by induction $L$ holds all its ordered splits. So the expressions of $S$ are closed under commutativity and under associativity with all splits of the left side. The argument of Lemma 2 on the Volcano page then gives every ordered split: from any expression, associativity and commutativity give $(\{a\}, S - \{a\})$ and $(S - \{a\}, \{a\})$, and associativity on the second with all splits of $S - \{a\}$, followed by commutativity, gives the rest. A later addition to $S$ is an ordered split of $S$ by soundness of the rules, so it is already there. $\square$

Line L5 explores a group before its first optimization, so every group that is optimized holds all its ordered splits, and the moves of a goal are all moves of the model.

**Lemma 4 (goals).** A winner of a goal always costs $\mathrm{opt}$ of the goal, and a failure stored with the bound $b$ means $\mathrm{opt} \ge b$. After O_GROUP$(S, p, \lambda)$ and its descendants have run, the goal has a winner, or it has a failure with a bound at least $\lambda$.

*Proof.* By induction over the goals, smaller groups first and $(S, \text{any})$ before $(S, \text{sorted})$, which by Lemma 2 is the order of the requests. If line L4 returns, the claim holds by the invariant. Otherwise line L6 makes a context with the limit $\ell = \lambda$ (or $\infty$ without pruning) and pushes FINISH below the move tasks, so FINISH runs after all of them.

Fix a move $m$ with input goals $(G_1, q_1), \ldots, (G_r, q_r)$ and let $c_m = \mathrm{local}(m) + \sum_i \mathrm{opt}(G_i, q_i)$. The O_INPUTS tasks of $m$ form a chain: each one either ends or pushes its next copy under a child goal. The limit $\ell$ of the context changes only at line L15 of a move of this context, and by Lemmas 1 and 2 no such move runs while the chain of $m$ waits for a child goal. So $\ell$ is constant along the chain. Let $t$ be the total before input $i$, with $t < \ell$. If the goal $(G_i, q_i)$ failed with a bound at least $\ell - t$, then $c_m \ge t + \mathrm{opt}(G_i, q_i) \ge \ell$, so dropping $m$ at line L12 loses nothing. If it has a winner, line L13 adds $\mathrm{opt}(G_i, q_i)$. Otherwise line L14 asks for the goal with the limit $\ell - t$; by Lemma 1 it ends first, and by induction the goal then has a winner or a failure with a bound at least $\ell - t$, which the next copy handles at lines L12 and L13. So $m$ reaches line L15 with the total $c_m$ exactly when $c_m < \ell$, and then it becomes the best move of the context and $\ell$ becomes $c_m$. Over all moves, the best move at FINISH is one with the least $c_m$ if that is below $\lambda$, and there is none otherwise. Since the moves are all moves of the model, the least $c_m$ is $\mathrm{opt}(S, p)$. Line L16 stores a winner of cost $\mathrm{opt}(S, p)$, or a failure with the bound $\lambda$ and $\mathrm{opt}(S, p) \ge \lambda$. A winner is never replaced, because line L4 returns for a goal with a winner. $\square$

**Termination.** Each group is explored once, and each exploration adds finitely many expressions. A goal is optimized again only with a limit above its stored failure bound. By induction over the goal order, only finitely many limits can reach a goal, because each limit is a limit of a parent goal minus a sum of local costs and winner costs. So the main loop ends.

**Theorem.** CASCADES$(p)$ returns a physical plan of the join of all relations that gives the order $p$ and costs $\mathrm{opt}(\text{all relations}, p)$, the least cost over all bushy join trees, algorithms, scans and placements of Sort.

*Proof.* Line L1 asks for the goal of the query with the limit $\infty$. A plan always exists, so $\mathrm{opt} < \infty$ and by Lemma 4 the goal has a winner of cost $\mathrm{opt}$. Each winner is a move whose input goals had winners when it was accepted, and winners never change, so line L3 can read the plan from the winners. Each move gives the order that it promises, so the plan gives $p$. $\blacksquare$

**Work.** By Lemma 3 each of the $3^n - 2^{n+1} + 1$ join expressions gets each of the two rules once. Associativity on $(L, R)$ reads the $2^{|L|} - 2$ expressions of $L$, so exploration costs $O(\sum_{S} 3^{|S|}) = O(4^n)$ set operations. Without pruning every limit is $\infty$, so each goal is optimized once, with $O(2^{|S|})$ moves and $O(1)$ tasks for each move and input, and the search costs $O(3^n)$ tasks. With pruning a goal can be optimized again with a higher limit, so the number of tasks depends on the costs.
