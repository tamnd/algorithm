---
title: The Volcano search on the join model explores every join tree and returns a cheapest physical plan for the required sort order
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

**Setting.** The relations $R_0, \ldots, R_{n-1}$ share one join attribute $k$, so a join of any two disjoint sets of relations is an equijoin on $k$, and every bushy join tree over all relations is a valid logical plan. A group is a set of relations, and a join expression of a group $S$ is an ordered pair $(L, R)$ of disjoint nonempty groups with $L \cup R = S$, an *ordered split*. The transformation rules are commutativity, $(L, R) \to (R, L)$, and associativity, which for an expression $(L, R)$ of $S$ and an expression $(A, B)$ of $L$ adds $(B, R)$ to the group $B \cup R$ and $(A, B \cup R)$ to $S$. The required property is $p \in \{\text{any}, \text{sorted}\}$. The moves for $(S, p)$ are the ones listed in the code: the scans for a single relation, the three join algorithms for each expression of $S$ with their required input properties, and the Sort enforcer over $(S, \text{any})$ when $p$ is sorted. Every local cost is a nonnegative number that depends only on the groups. A physical plan for $(S, p)$ is a move for $(S, p)$ together with a physical plan for each of its inputs $(G_i, p_i)$, and its cost is the local cost plus the costs of the input plans. $\mathrm{opt}(S, p)$ is the least cost of such a plan.

**Lemma 1 (the rules are sound).** Every expression that the rules add to a group $S$ is an ordered split of $S$, and it computes the same bag of rows as every other expression of $S$.

*Proof.* Commutativity keeps the two sides. Associativity on $(L, R)$ with $L = A \cup B$ makes $(B, R)$, a split of $B \cup R$, and $(A, B \cup R)$, a split of $S$. The natural join on $k$ is commutative and associative on bags, also with nulls, since a row of the result is a choice of one row from each relation with equal non-null values of $k$, whatever the order of the joins. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (the rules are complete).** When EXPLORE$(S)$ returns, $S$ holds every ordered split of $S$, and every nonempty proper subset of $S$ is a group of the memo.

*Proof.* By induction on $|S|$; for $|S| = 1$ there is nothing to show. A group always holds at least one expression, because it is created with one. Lines L3 to L7 repeat until a pass adds nothing, so at the end the set $E$ of expressions of $S$ is closed under the two rules, where associativity on $(L, R)$ uses the expressions of $L$, which hold all ordered splits of $L$ by the induction hypothesis, because line L6 explores $L$ first. By Lemma 1 later additions to an explored group are splits that it holds already, so explored groups stay complete.

Let $(L, R) \in E$. If $|S| = 2$, commutativity gives both splits. Otherwise one side has two or more relations, and by commutativity we may take $|L| \ge 2$. Pick $a \in L$. Associativity with the split $(\{a\}, L - \{a\})$ of $L$ gives $(\{a\}, S - \{a\}) \in E$, and commutativity gives $(S - \{a\}, \{a\}) \in E$. Associativity on this expression with every split $(A, B)$ of $S - \{a\}$ gives $(A, S - A) \in E$ for every nonempty proper subset $A$ of $S - \{a\}$, and $(S - \{a\}, \{a\})$ is there too. So $E$ holds $(X, S - X)$ for every nonempty $X$ with $a \notin X$, and by commutativity for every $X \ne S$ with $a \in X$. These are all ordered splits. Each side of an expression is a group, so every nonempty proper subset of $S$ is a group. $\square$

EXPLORE-ALL explores the query group and then every group that is not explored yet, so by Lemma 2 the memo holds every nonempty subset of the relations, each with all its ordered splits. So the logical space is the set of all bushy join trees.

**Lemma 3 (the search).** Let FIND-BEST-PLAN$(S, p, \ell)$ be called. If $\mathrm{opt}(S, p) < \ell$, it returns a plan for $(S, p)$ of cost $\mathrm{opt}(S, p)$, and otherwise it returns failure. Afterwards the table holds either ("plan", $\mathrm{opt}(S, p)$) or ("fail", $\ell'$) with $\mathrm{opt}(S, p) \ge \ell'$.

*Proof.* The calls form a recursion on the pairs $(S, p)$: a join move calls strictly smaller groups, and the Sort move for $(S, \text{sorted})$ calls $(S, \text{any})$, whose moves do not call $S$ again. So we can use induction in the order where smaller groups come first and $(S, \text{any})$ comes before $(S, \text{sorted})$, and inside one pair on the order of the calls.

*Lookup, line L8.* If the table holds ("plan", $c$) for $(S, p)$, then $c = \mathrm{opt}(S, p)$ by the induction hypothesis for the earlier call, and returning the plan when $c < \ell$ and failure otherwise is correct. If it holds ("fail", $\ell'$), then $\mathrm{opt}(S, p) \ge \ell'$, so failure is correct when $\ell \le \ell'$; otherwise the search runs again.

*Search, lines L9 to L15.* For a move $m$ with inputs $(G_1, p_1), \ldots, (G_r, p_r)$, let $c_m = \mathrm{local}(m) + \sum_i \mathrm{opt}(G_i, p_i)$, so $\mathrm{opt}(S, p) = \min_m c_m$. Let $\lambda$ be the value of the limit when the loop reaches $m$. We claim that $m$ is accepted at line L15 exactly when $c_m < \lambda$, and then with the total $c_m$. Suppose $c_m < \lambda$. Before input $i$, the total is $\mathrm{local}(m) + \sum_{j < i} \mathrm{opt}(G_j, p_j) \le c_m < \lambda$, so the loop does not stop, and $\mathrm{opt}(G_i, p_i) \le c_m - \text{total} < \lambda - \text{total}$, so by induction the call of line L13 returns a plan of cost $\mathrm{opt}(G_i, p_i)$. Hence the total ends at $c_m$ and the move is accepted. Conversely, every input plan that a call returns has the optimal cost of its input, so an accepted total equals $c_m$, and it is accepted only if it is below $\lambda$. After an acceptance the limit becomes $c_m$. So after the loop, if some $c_m$ is below the first limit $\ell$, the loop has accepted a move with the smallest $c_m$, and $\mathrm{best}$ costs $\mathrm{opt}(S, p)$. If every $c_m \ge \ell$, no move is accepted and the result is failure, with $\mathrm{opt}(S, p) \ge \ell$, which line L16 stores. Without pruning, the limit passed to the inputs is $\infty$ and the same argument holds with $\lambda$ the cost of the best move so far. $\square$

**Theorem.** VOLCANO$(p)$ returns a physical plan for the join of all relations that gives the order $p$, of cost $\mathrm{opt}(\text{all relations}, p)$. This is the least cost over all bushy join trees, all choices of join algorithms and scans, and all placements of Sort, except a Sort at the top of a plan that needs no order, which is never cheaper than the plan below it.

*Proof.* Line L2 calls FIND-BEST-PLAN with the limit $\infty$, so by Lemma 3 it returns a plan of cost $\mathrm{opt}(\text{all}, p)$; such a plan exists, since a table scan of each relation, hash joins and a Sort make one. By Lemma 2 the expressions of each group are all its ordered splits, so the moves range over all join trees. Each move gives the property that it promises: a merge join of sorted inputs is sorted on $k$, a nested loop join keeps the order of its outer input, and a Sort sorts. A plan with a Sort on top of a plan that gives no required order costs at least as much as the plan below, because the Sort has a nonnegative cost. $\blacksquare$

**Work.** By Lemmas 1 and 2 the memo holds $\sum_{S} (2^{|S|} - 2) = 3^n - 2^{n+1} + 1$ join expressions. The proof of Lemma 2 uses a chain of at most five rule steps from the first expression, and each pass of lines L3 to L7 does at least one more step of the chain, so a group reaches all its splits within five passes, and a sixth pass finds nothing new. One pass over a group with $k$ relations costs $O(2^k + 3^k)$ set operations, one for each expression and each expression of its left side, so the exploration costs $O(\sum_S 3^{|S|}) = O(4^n)$ set operations. Without pruning, each pair $(S, p)$ is searched once, with $O(2^{|S|})$ moves, which is $O(3^n)$ in all. With pruning, a pair that failed can be searched again with a larger limit, so the number of searches depends on the costs.
