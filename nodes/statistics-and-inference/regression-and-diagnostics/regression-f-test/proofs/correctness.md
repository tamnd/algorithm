---
title: The nested-model F statistic has the F distribution with q and n - p degrees of freedom under normal errors
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

We prove that the algorithm computes $\mathrm{RSS}_1$ and $\mathrm{RSS}_0 - \mathrm{RSS}_1$ from one QR factorization, that the statistic has the $F_{q, n-p}$ law under the null hypothesis with normal errors, that the p-value is uniform under the null, so the size is exactly $\alpha$, and that the Wald form for $Rb = r$ gives the same number. The model is $y = Xb + \varepsilon$ with $X$ a fixed $n \times p$ matrix of rank $p$, $n > p$, whose first $p_0$ columns form $X_0$ ($0 \le p_0 < p$), and $\varepsilon \sim N(0, \sigma^2 I_n)$. The null hypothesis is $H_0$: the last $q = p - p_0$ coefficients are 0, that is, $\mathbb{E}[y] \in \operatorname{col}(X_0)$.

**Lemma 1 (what the loop computes).** After lines L1 to L4, the transformed matrix is $Q^\top X = \binom{R}{0}$ with $R$ upper triangular $p \times p$ and nonsingular, and the transformed $y$ is $c = Q^\top y$, where $Q = P_1 P_2 \cdots P_p$ is orthogonal. For each $k \le p$, the first $k$ columns of $Q$ are an orthonormal basis of the span of the first $k$ columns of $X$.

*Proof.* Step $j$ applies the Householder reflection $P_j = I - 2vv^\top/(v^\top v)$, which is symmetric and orthogonal ($P_j^2 = I$). The vector $v$ is 0 in its first $j - 1$ entries, so $P_j$ leaves rows $1, \dots, j - 1$ alone, and the choice $v = a - \alpha e_j$ with $|\alpha| = \|a\|$, where $a$ is the part of column $j$ in rows $j..n$, maps $a$ to $\alpha e_j$. The sign of $\alpha$ is opposite to $a_j$, so $v_j = a_j - \alpha$ has no cancellation and $v^\top v > 0$ whenever $a \ne 0$. By induction, after step $j$ columns $1..j$ are 0 below the diagonal, so after $p$ steps $P_p \cdots P_1 X = \binom{R}{0}$, and $Q^\top = P_p \cdots P_1$. Since $X$ has rank $p$, $a \ne 0$ at every step: otherwise column $j$ of the current matrix would be a combination of the earlier columns, and so would column $j$ of $X$. So $|R_{jj}| = \|a\| > 0$. Write $Q = (Q_1, Q_2)$ with $Q_1$ the first $p$ columns. From $X = Q_1 R$ with $R$ upper triangular, column $k$ of $X$ is a combination of the first $k$ columns of $Q_1$, and since $R$ is nonsingular the converse holds too. $\square$

The loop runs $p$ times, each step over finitely many entries, so the algorithm terminates. Lines L5 to L9 are finite sums and one call of the tail function.

**Lemma 2 (the two sums of squares).** With $c = Q^\top y$, $\mathrm{RSS}_1 = \sum_{i > p} c_i^2$ and $\mathrm{RSS}_0 - \mathrm{RSS}_1 = \sum_{p_0 < i \le p} c_i^2$, where $\mathrm{RSS}_m = \min_\beta \|y - X_m \beta\|^2$ for the null ($m = 0$) and full ($m = 1$) models.

*Proof.* $Q^\top$ keeps lengths, so for any $\beta \in \mathbb{R}^p$, $\|y - X\beta\|^2 = \|c - \binom{R\beta}{0}\|^2 = \|c_{1..p} - R\beta\|^2 + \sum_{i > p} c_i^2$. Since $R$ is nonsingular, $\beta = R^{-1}c_{1..p}$ makes the first term 0, so the minimum is $\sum_{i > p} c_i^2$. For the null model, $X_0 = Q_1 R_{\cdot, 1..p_0}$, and the first $p_0$ columns of $R$ are 0 below row $p_0$. With $R_{00}$ the leading $p_0 \times p_0$ block (nonsingular), $\|y - X_0\gamma\|^2 = \|c_{1..p_0} - R_{00}\gamma\|^2 + \sum_{i > p_0} c_i^2$, and the minimum is $\sum_{i > p_0} c_i^2$. Subtract. For $p_0 = 0$ the null model is $\mathbb{E}[y] = 0$ and $\mathrm{RSS}_0 = \|y\|^2 = \|c\|^2$, the same formula. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the null law).** Under the model, $c = Q^\top y \sim N(Q^\top X b, \sigma^2 I_n)$, so $c_1, \dots, c_n$ are independent with variance $\sigma^2$. The means are $\mathbb{E}[c_i] = 0$ for $i > p$, and, under $H_0$, also for $p_0 < i \le p$. So under $H_0$, $U = (\mathrm{RSS}_0 - \mathrm{RSS}_1)/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_q$ and $V = \mathrm{RSS}_1/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_{n-p}$, independent, and

$$F = \frac{(\mathrm{RSS}_0 - \mathrm{RSS}_1)/q}{\mathrm{RSS}_1/(n - p)} = \frac{U/q}{V/(n - p)} \sim F_{q, n-p}.$$

*Proof.* A linear map of a normal vector is normal, with $\operatorname{Cov}(Q^\top y) = \sigma^2 Q^\top Q = \sigma^2 I$. For a normal vector, uncorrelated coordinates are independent. $\mathbb{E}[c] = Q^\top X b = \binom{Rb}{0}$, which is 0 below row $p$. Under $H_0$, $Xb = X_0 b_0$, and $Q^\top X_0 b_0$ is 0 below row $p_0$ (the first $p_0$ columns of $\binom{R}{0}$ are). So $c_i/\sigma$ for $p_0 < i \le n$ are iid $N(0, 1)$. A sum of $k$ squared iid standard normals is $\chi^2_k$, which gives $U$ and $V$; they use disjoint sets of coordinates, so they are independent. The ratio of independent chi-square variables, each divided by its degrees of freedom, is $F_{q, n-p}$ by definition. $\sigma$ cancels, so the law does not depend on $\sigma$ or on the null coefficients $b_0$. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (the p-value and the size).** Line L9 returns $\bar G(F)$, where $G$ is the CDF of $F_{q, n-p}$ and $\bar G = 1 - G$. Under $H_0$, $P(\bar G(F) \le u) = u$ for $u \in [0, 1]$, so rejecting when the p-value is at most $\alpha$ has size exactly $\alpha$.

*Proof.* $G$ is continuous and strictly increasing on $(0, \infty)$, since the F law has a positive density there. Then $\{\bar G(F) \le u\} = \{F \ge G^{-1}(1 - u)\}$, of probability $u$. The helper computes $\bar G(f) = I_{\nu/(\nu + q f)}(\nu/2, q/2)$ with $\nu = n - p$, the standard expression of the F tail through the regularized incomplete beta function: if $F = (U/q)/(V/\nu)$ then $V/(V + U) = \nu/(\nu + qF)$ has the Beta$(\nu/2, q/2)$ law, and $F > f$ exactly when this ratio is below $\nu/(\nu + q f)$. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (the Wald form).** Let $R$ be a $q \times p$ matrix of rank $q$, $r \in \mathbb{R}^q$, $\hat b$ the least squares estimate and $s^2 = \mathrm{RSS}_1/(n - p)$. The minimum of $\|y - X\beta\|^2$ over $\{\beta : R\beta = r\}$ is $\mathrm{RSS}_1 + (R\hat b - r)^\top [R (X^\top X)^{-1} R^\top]^{-1} (R\hat b - r)$. So the Wald statistic $(R\hat b - r)^\top [R(X^\top X)^{-1}R^\top]^{-1}(R\hat b - r)/(q s^2)$ equals the nested F statistic of the restricted model, and has the $F_{q, n-p}$ law under $H_0: Rb = r$.

*Proof.* $\|y - X\beta\|^2 = \mathrm{RSS}_1 + (\beta - \hat b)^\top X^\top X (\beta - \hat b)$, since $X^\top(y - X\hat b) = 0$. Write $A = X^\top X$ (positive definite) and $d = R\hat b - r$. Minimize $(\beta - \hat b)^\top A(\beta - \hat b)$ subject to $R\beta = r$: with a Lagrange multiplier, $A(\beta - \hat b) = R^\top \lambda$, so $\beta - \hat b = A^{-1}R^\top\lambda$ and $R(\beta - \hat b) = -d$ gives $\lambda = -(RA^{-1}R^\top)^{-1}d$; the value is $\lambda^\top R A^{-1} R^\top \lambda = d^\top (RA^{-1}R^\top)^{-1} d$. The matrix $RA^{-1}R^\top$ is positive definite because $R$ has full row rank. This value is the minimum because the objective is convex. The restricted set is a translate of a linear subspace of dimension $p - q$, so the restricted model is a nested linear model after a change of variables: pick $\beta_r$ with $R\beta_r = r$, replace $y$ by $y - X\beta_r$, and choose a basis of the column space of $X$ whose first $p - q$ vectors span $X\ker R$. Lemmas 1 to 4 then apply with $p_0 = p - q$. $\square$

**Theorem.** For data from the model, the algorithm returns $F = ((\mathrm{RSS}_0 - \mathrm{RSS}_1)/q)/(\mathrm{RSS}_1/(n - p))$, which has the $F_{q, n-p}$ law under $H_0$, and a p-value that is uniform on $[0, 1]$ under $H_0$. With one restriction ($q = 1$, the last column), $F = t^2$ for the t statistic $t = \hat b_p/\operatorname{se}(\hat b_p)$.

*Proof.* Lemmas 1 to 4 give the first two claims. For $q = 1$, take $R = e_p^\top$ in Lemma 5: $F = \hat b_p^2/(s^2 [(X^\top X)^{-1}]_{pp}) = t^2$. $\square$

**What is not proved.** Without normality the law of $F$ is not $F_{q, n-p}$ in finite samples. If the errors are iid with mean 0 and finite variance and $\max_i h_{ii} \to 0$, then $qF \to \chi^2_q$ in distribution, so the size tends to $\alpha$. This follows from the Lindeberg-Feller central limit theorem applied to the projection of $\varepsilon$ on the tested directions, together with $s^2 \to \sigma^2$ in probability; we do not prove it here. The measured sizes on the page are only measurements. Under heteroscedasticity the statistic has neither law, and the test is not valid even asymptotically. $\blacksquare$
