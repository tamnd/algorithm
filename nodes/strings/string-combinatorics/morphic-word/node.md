A morphic word is an infinite word generated by iterating a [string morphism](/string-morphism/): if $h(a)$ starts with the letter $a$, the words $a, h(a), h^2(a), \ldots$ are prefixes of each other and converge to the fixed point $h^\omega(a)$, a *pure morphic word*. Applying a letter-to-letter map (a coding) to such a fixed point gives a *morphic word* in general. The [Thue-Morse word](/thue-morse-word/), the [Fibonacci word](/fibonacci-word/) and the square-free word of [Thue's theorem](/thue-theorem/) are pure morphic, and the codings of fixed points of uniform morphisms are exactly the automatic sequences. Morphic words are finitely described yet aperiodic in general, which makes them the standard source of examples and counterexamples in combinatorics on words.

## In plain words

Start with one letter and a rewriting table that says, for each letter, which short word replaces it. Rewrite every letter at once, then do it again with the result, and again. If the word for the first letter starts with that same letter, the front of the word never changes: each round only adds letters at the end. Running forever gives one endless word, and that word is unchanged by one more round of rewriting.

Such endless words are easy to describe, by a small table, yet they can avoid repeats, never settle into a cycle, and have rich structure. If you then rename some letters to the same symbol, you get an even larger family, the morphic words.

## The idea

Let $\Sigma$ and $\Delta$ be finite [alphabets](/alphabet/); only equality of letters is used. Infinite words are indexed from 0: $x = x[0]\,x[1]\,x[2] \cdots$.

**Pure morphic words.** Let $h : \Sigma^* \to \Sigma^*$ be a morphism and $a \in \Sigma$ with $h(a) = a\,u$ and $h^k(u) \ne \varepsilon$ for all $k$ ($h$ is *prolongable* on $a$). Then $h^k(a)$ is a proper prefix of $h^{k+1}(a)$ for every $k$, and
$$ h^\omega(a) = \lim_{k \to \infty} h^k(a) = a\,u\,h(u)\,h^2(u) \cdots $$
is the unique infinite word that starts with $a$ and is fixed by $h$. The proof is on the [string morphism](/string-morphism/) page. A word of this form is *pure morphic*.

**Morphic words.** A word $x \in \Delta^\omega$ is *morphic* if $x = \tau(h^\omega(a))$ for a pure morphic word $h^\omega(a)$ and a coding $\tau : \Sigma \to \Delta$, a morphism that maps letters to letters. Allowing $h$ to erase some letters does not enlarge the class, a theorem of Cobham and of Pansiot.

**Automatic sequences.** If $h$ is $k$-uniform, $|h(b)| = k$ for every letter, then $h^\omega(a)[i]$ is found by reading the base-$k$ digits of $i$: writing $i = k q + r$ with $0 \le r < k$, the letter at $i$ is the letter at position $r$ of $h(\text{letter at } q)$. Cobham (1972) showed that the codings of fixed points of $k$-uniform morphisms are exactly the $k$-automatic sequences, the sequences whose $i$-th term is computed by a finite automaton reading $i$ in base $k$.

**Growth and frequencies.** With the incidence matrix $M_{bc} = |h(c)|_b$, the [Parikh vector](/parikh-vector/) of $h^k(a)$ is $M^k e_a$, so the lengths $|h^k(a)|$ grow polynomially or exponentially, according to the eigenvalues of $M$. If $h$ is *primitive*, that is $M^k$ has only positive entries for some $k$, then the Perron-Frobenius theorem gives frequencies of all letters in $h^\omega(a)$, and the word is uniformly recurrent: every factor occurs with bounded gaps.

**Complexity.** Pansiot (1984) showed that the [subword complexity](/subword-complexity/) $p(n)$ of a pure morphic word is, up to constant factors, one of $1$, $n$, $n \log \log n$, $n \log n$ and $n^2$. Fixed points of primitive morphisms have linear complexity, and so do all automatic sequences. In particular the entropy of every morphic word is 0.

**Decision problems.** Given $h$ and $a$, it is decidable whether $h^\omega(a)$ is ultimately periodic (Harju and Linna; Pansiot, 1986), and this extends to morphic words (Durand; Mitrofanov, 2013). By the [Morse-Hedlund theorem](/morse-hedlund-theorem/) this is the question whether $p(n) \le n$ for some $n$.

## Examples

| morphism | start | word | type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0 \mapsto 01$, $1 \mapsto 10$ | 0 | Thue-Morse $0110100110010110\cdots$ | 2-uniform, overlap-free |
| $0 \mapsto 01$, $1 \mapsto 0$ | 0 | Fibonacci $0100101001001\cdots$ | primitive, Sturmian |
| $a \mapsto abc$, $b \mapsto ac$, $c \mapsto b$ | $a$ | $abcacbabcbac\cdots$ | square-free |
| $a \mapsto ab$, $b \mapsto aa$ | $a$ | period-doubling $abaaabababaaabaa\cdots$ | 2-uniform |
| $a \mapsto aab$, $b \mapsto b$ | $a$ | $aabaabbaabaabbbaab\cdots$ | quadratic complexity |
| $a \mapsto ab$, $b \mapsto b$ | $a$ | $abbbb\cdots$ | ultimately periodic |

- **Period-doubling.** In $abaaabab\cdots$ the letter at index $i$ is $b$ exactly when the exponent of 2 in $i + 1$ is odd; this was checked for the first 4096 letters.
- **Rudin-Shapiro.** The fixed point of $a \mapsto ab$, $b \mapsto ac$, $c \mapsto db$, $d \mapsto dc$ followed by the coding $a, b \mapsto +1$ and $c, d \mapsto -1$ gives $+ + + - + + - + + + + - - - + - \cdots$, whose $i$-th term is $(-1)$ raised to the number of occurrences of `11` in the binary expansion of $i$. It is morphic and 2-automatic, obtained through a coding of a fixed point on four letters.
- **Squares.** The fixed point of $a \mapsto ab$, $b \mapsto ccb$, $c \mapsto c$, coded by $a, b \mapsto 1$ and $c \mapsto 0$, is $1100100001\cdots$, with a 1 exactly at the perfect squares, as shown on the [string morphism](/string-morphism/) page. It is morphic but not automatic.
- **Quadratic complexity.** For $a \mapsto aab$, $b \mapsto b$ the blocks of $b$ grow by one at each level, and the complexity is $p(1), \ldots, p(12) = 2, 4, 6, 9, 13, 17, 22, 28, 35, 43, 51, 60$, with differences that grow linearly.

## Where it is used

- **Avoiding repetitions.** Infinite words that avoid squares, overlaps, cubes or fractional powers are built as fixed points of morphisms that preserve the property; see [square-free words](/square-free-word/), [Thue's theorem](/thue-theorem/) and the [repetition threshold](/repetition-threshold/).
- **Examples in combinatorics on words.** [Sturmian words](/sturmian-word/) such as the Fibonacci word, the Thue-Morse word and many extremal words for complexity, balance and recurrence are morphic.
- **Automatic sequences and number theory.** Digits of algebraic power series over finite fields (Christol's theorem), paperfolding sequences and the Rudin-Shapiro sequence are automatic; their transcendence and normality are studied through the morphism.
- **Lindenmayer systems.** D0L systems iterate a morphism on a start word to model the growth of plants and cells; the limit words are pure morphic.
- **Grammar compression.** A prefix of length $N$ of a pure morphic word with exponential growth has a [straight-line program](/straight-line-program-grammar/) of size $O(\log N)$, so these words are standard test cases for compressed string algorithms.
- **Computing letters.** For a uniform morphism the letter at index $i$ takes $O(\log i)$ steps, one per base-$k$ digit; for a nonuniform morphism the lengths $|h^k(b)|$ allow the same descent from $h^k(a)$ to the position $i$.
