---
title: Each pass keeps exactly the candidates that meet the selection rule
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Claim.** Run GENERATE (lines G1 to G14) on a list of hyphenated words and a list of levels. Fix a level $\ell$ with parameters $(\gamma, \delta, \theta)$ (good weight, bad weight, threshold), a length $k$ and a dot position $d \in \{0, \ldots, k\}$. Let $\Pi$ be the patterns before this pass, and for a gap $g$ of a word let "now" mean that the value of $g$ under $\Pi$ by Liang's rule is odd. Call a counted gap $g$ of a word *good* if it is a correct hyphen and not now (odd $\ell$), or not a correct hyphen and now (even $\ell$); call it *bad* if it is not a correct hyphen and not now (odd $\ell$), or a correct hyphen and now (even $\ell$). For a string $q$ of $k$ letters, let $\mathrm{good}(q)$ and $\mathrm{bad}(q)$ be the number of good and bad counted gaps $g$ of the words such that $q$ occurs in the word with "." at both ends with its gap $d$ at $g$. Then the pass adds the value $\ell$ at the gap $d$ of $q$ exactly for the strings $q$ with $\mathrm{good}(q) + \mathrm{bad}(q) > 0$ and $\gamma \cdot \mathrm{good}(q) - \delta \cdot \mathrm{bad}(q) \ge \theta$, and for no other gap or string. (When $\theta \ge 1$, as in every level of patgen, the first condition follows from the second.) The run stops after $\sum_{\ell} \sum_{k = k_{\min}}^{k_{\max}} (k + 1)$ passes.

**Setup.** A word $w$ of $n$ letters gives $u = .w.$ of $n + 2$ letters. Gap $g$ of $w$ (before $w_g$) is gap $g + 1$ of $u$. A string $q$ of $k$ letters that starts at position $s$ of $u$ has its gap $d$ at gap $s + d$ of $u$, which is gap $s + d - 1$ of $w$. So the occurrence of $q$ with its gap $d$ at gap $g$ of $w$, if it exists, is the one at $s = g + 1 - d$, and it exists exactly when $0 \le s$, $s + k \le n + 2$ and $u_s \cdots u_{s+k-1} = q$. For each gap there is at most one such string.

**Step 1 (the values are right).** Line G7 computes the gap values of $w$ under $\Pi$. The function `gap_values` is Liang's rule written out: for each start $i$ of $u$ and each length of a pattern, it looks up the substring of that length at $i$ and raises the covered gaps to the values of the pattern. This is the definition of the value of a gap, the maximum over all patterns that cover it, as proved on the page on Liang's method. The test `test_final_patterns_find_every_hyphen_and_no_bad_hyphen` also checks it against a brute force. So "now" in line G8 is the "now" of the claim. The patterns of this pass are not used in this computation: line G13 adds them only after all words are counted (lines G11 to G13 come after the loop of line G6), so every gap is judged under $\Pi$ alone.

**Step 2 (the counts are right).** Line G8 visits each counted gap $g$ of each word once. The two tests before the count give exactly the good and the bad of the claim, and a gap cannot be both, because the two conditions differ in "correct hyphen" (odd $\ell$) or in "now" (even $\ell$). A gap that is neither is skipped. By the setup, line G9 takes the only string $q$ of $k$ letters that has its gap $d$ at $g$, or none when it would run past the marks. Line G10 adds one to the good or to the bad count of that $q$. So after the loop of line G6, $\mathrm{count}[q] = (\mathrm{good}(q), \mathrm{bad}(q))$ for every $q$ with a nonzero count, and the strings that are not in the dict have both counts 0.

**Step 3 (the selection).** By Step 2 the dict holds exactly the strings with $\mathrm{good}(q) + \mathrm{bad}(q) > 0$, and lines G11 to G13 add the value $\ell$ at gap $d$ of $q$ exactly for those with $\gamma \cdot \mathrm{good}(q) - \delta \cdot \mathrm{bad}(q) \ge \theta$. The function `add_pattern` stores the larger of the old and the new value at the gap $d$, which is the value of the merged pattern by Liang's rule, since the maximum over the patterns is what counts. It changes no other gap.

**Termination and the number of passes.** Each level runs the lengths $k_{\min}, \ldots, k_{\max}$ (line G3) and the $k + 1$ dots of each length (line G4), and each pass reads each counted gap of each word once, so it stops. This gives $\sum_{k} (k + 1)$ passes per level, the number Liang gives for patgen and the one the test checks. $\blacksquare$

**What the proof does not say.** The rule is a heuristic. A level can add a pattern that makes a wrong hyphen elsewhere, and the next level of the other parity may or may not remove it. The claim is only that the program does what the rule says; whether the final patterns hyphenate every word of the list correctly depends on the parameters, as the tests of the page show.
