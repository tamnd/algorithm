---
title: The script and fraction rules give the boxes and clearances of TeX
proves: [correctness, termination, complexity]
status: full
---

We use the formulas, the styles and the pseudocode of the page. The styles are numbered $D = 0$, $D' = 1$, $T = 2$, $T' = 3$, $S = 4$, $S' = 5$, $SS = 6$, $SS' = 7$, so a style is cramped exactly when its number is odd. Write $\xi_8$ for the default rule thickness, $\sigma_5$ for the x-height and $\sigma_{22}$ for the axis height of the current size. A box has a width $w$, a height $h$ above the baseline and a depth $d$ below it. A box raised by $u$ has its top at $h + u$ and its bottom at $u - d$; a box lowered by $v$ has its top at $h - v$ and its bottom at $-(d + v)$.

**Lemma 1 (the styles).** The formulas of the page, $\mathrm{sup}(s) = 2 \lfloor s / 4 \rfloor + 4 + (s \bmod 2)$, $\mathrm{sub}(s) = 2 \lfloor s / 4 \rfloor + 5$, $\mathrm{num}(s) = s + 2 - 2 \lfloor s / 6 \rfloor$ and $\mathrm{denom}(s) = 2 \lfloor s / 2 \rfloor + 3 - 2 \lfloor s / 6 \rfloor$, give the table of The TeXbook: a superscript of $D$ or $T$ is set in $S$, of $S$ or $SS$ in $SS$, cramped when the style is cramped; a subscript is set in the cramped form of the same style as the superscript; a numerator of $D$ is in $T$, of $T$ in $S$, of $S$ or $SS$ in $SS$, cramped when the style is; a denominator is the cramped form of the numerator style.

*Proof.* There are eight styles, and the four formulas can be evaluated on each. For $s \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, $\lfloor s/4 \rfloor = 0$, so $\mathrm{sup}(s) = 4 + (s \bmod 2)$, which is $S$ or $S'$, and $\mathrm{sub}(s) = 5 = S'$. For $s \in \{4, \ldots, 7\}$, $\mathrm{sup}(s) = 6 + (s \bmod 2)$ and $\mathrm{sub}(s) = 7$. For $s < 6$, $\mathrm{num}(s) = s + 2$ keeps the parity and moves one size class down; for $s \in \{6, 7\}$ it is $s$. $\mathrm{denom}(s)$ is $\mathrm{num}(s)$ with the parity bit set, since $2 \lfloor s/2 \rfloor + 1$ is $s$ with the parity bit set. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (the sizes of the result).** For scripts with both parts, the result is the nucleus followed by a box of width $\max(w_x, w_y)$, height $h_x + u$ and depth $d_y + v$; with only a superscript, the box $x$ raised by $u$; with only a subscript, the box $x$ lowered by $v$. For a fraction, the result has height $\max(h_x + u, \sigma_{22})$, depth $\max(d_z + v, -\sigma_{22})$ and width $\max(w_x, w_z) + 2n$, where $n$ is the null delimiter space. These are the boxes that TeX builds.

*Proof.* With both scripts, TeX stacks $x$, a kern $k = (u - d_x) - (h_y - v)$ and $y$, and lowers the stack by $v$. The stack has height $h_x + d_x + k + h_y$ and depth $d_y$, so after the shift its height is $h_x + d_x + (u - d_x) - (h_y - v) + h_y - v = h_x + u$ and its depth is $d_y + v$. For a fraction, TeX sets the height of the stack to $u + h_x$ and its depth to $d_z + v$ directly. Each null delimiter is an empty box of width $n$, height 0 and depth 0, moved so that its center is on the axis, which is a shift up by $\sigma_{22}$; it reaches the height $\sigma_{22}$ and the depth $-\sigma_{22}$. The width and the other cases are sums and maxima of the parts. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the clearances).** Let $x$ be the superscript and $y$ the subscript, both set. After line L11, the gap $(u - d_x) - (h_y - v)$ between the bottom of the superscript and the top of the subscript is at least $4 \xi_8$. For a fraction with a rule of thickness $\theta > 0$ and $c = 3\theta$ in display styles, $c = \theta$ otherwise, after lines L18 and L19 the gap between the bottom of the numerator and the top of the rule, $(u - d_x) - (\sigma_{22} + \mathrm{half}(\theta))$, and the gap between the bottom of the rule and the top of the denominator, $(\sigma_{22} - \mathrm{half}(\theta)) - (h_z - v)$, are both at least $c$. For $\theta = 0$, after line L16 the gap $(u - d_x) - (h_z - v)$ is at least $c = 7 \xi_8$ in display styles and $3 \xi_8$ otherwise.

*Proof.* Scripts: let $g$ be the gap before line L10 and $e = 4 \xi_8 - g$. If $e \le 0$, nothing changes and $g \ge 4 \xi_8$. If $e > 0$, line L11 adds $e$ to $v$, which adds $e$ to the gap, so it becomes $4 \xi_8$. The second step adds the same amount to $u$ and subtracts it from $v$, which leaves the gap the same. Rule: line L18 adds $\delta_1$ to $u$ when $\delta_1 > 0$, and the first gap is $c - \delta_1$ before it, so it is $\ge c$ after it. Line L19 changes only $v$ and so does not undo this, and the same argument gives the second gap. No rule: let $g$ be the gap and $\delta = \mathrm{half}(c - g)$. If $\delta \le 0$ then $c - g \le 0$, since $\mathrm{half}(x) \ge x / 2$, so $g \ge c$. If $\delta > 0$, both shifts grow by $\delta$, the gap grows by $2\delta \ge c - g$, and so it is $\ge c$. Here $\mathrm{half}(x) = (x + 1) \operatorname{div} 2$ for odd $x$ and $x \operatorname{div} 2$ for even $x$, so $2\, \mathrm{half}(x) \ge x$ for all integers $x$. $\square$

**Theorem.** The layout terminates on every formula. Its boxes have the sizes of Lemma 2, its scripts and fractions use the styles of Lemma 1, and they satisfy the clearances of Lemma 3. With the same font parameters, these are the sizes and shifts that rules 15 and 18 of The TeXbook, appendix G, give for formulas with these parts.

*Proof.* Each call of the layout on a formula calls itself only on its proper subformulas (the parts of a row, the numerator and the denominator, the nucleus and the scripts), so the recursion depth is at most the height of the formula tree, and it terminates. The rest follows from Lemmas 1 to 3, since each line of the pseudocode is the step of rule 15 or rule 18 with the same name (15a to 15d and 18a to 18f) and computes the same shifts from the same parameters of the current size, and of the script size for the drops. $\square$

**Complexity.** Each node of the formula is laid out once, by its parent, and each rule does $O(1)$ work besides the calls on its parts, or $O(k)$ for a row of $k$ parts. So the time is $O(N)$ for a formula with $N$ nodes, and the extra space is the recursion depth, $O(N)$ in the worst case.
