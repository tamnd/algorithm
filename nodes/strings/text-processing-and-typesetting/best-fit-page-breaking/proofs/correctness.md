---
title: The page builder returns the pages of the TeX rule
proves: [correctness, termination, complexity]
status: full
---

We use the model and the pseudocode of the page. The items are $x_0, \ldots, x_{m-1}$, boxes have height $h \ge 0$ and depth $d \ge 0$, glue has width $w \ge 0$, stretch $y \ge 0$ of some order and finite shrink $z \ge 0$, and $x_{m-1}$ is a forced penalty. For a page that starts at the box $s$ and a legal break $b > s$, let $T(s, b)$, $Y(s, b)$ and $Z(s, b)$ be the page total, the stretch by order and the shrink, as defined on the page: the heights of the boxes in $x_s, \ldots, x_{b-1}$, the depths of these boxes except the depth of the last box $x_k$ when no glue lies between $x_k$ and $x_b$, the part of that last depth above the maximum depth $D$, and the widths, stretches and shrinks of the glue in $x_s, \ldots, x_{b-1}$. Let $c(s, b)$ be the cost of the break $b$ for this page, computed from $T$, $Y$, $Z$, the page goal and the penalty of $x_b$ by the rules of the page.

**The TeX rule.** For a page that starts at the box $s$, let $b_1 < b_2 < \cdots$ be the legal breaks after $s$, and let $e$ be the first of them with $c(s, e) = \infty$ or with $x_e$ a forced penalty. The page ends at the last $b_i \le e$ with the least value of $c(s, b_i)$ among $b_1, \ldots, e$. The next page starts at the first box after that break. This is the rule of TeX: The Program, part 45, for a vertical list with boxes, glue and penalties only.

**Lemma 1 (the page total).** For every page start $s$ and legal break $b > s$, the value that line L8 computes is $(T(s, b), Y(s, b), Z(s, b))$, and it is equal to the running totals that TeX holds when it reaches $x_b$.

*Proof.* The prefix sums give the sum of $h + d$ over the boxes and of $w$, $y$ and $z$ over the glue in $x_s, \ldots, x_{b-1}$. Line L8 then removes the depth $d$ of the last box $x_k$ when no glue lies between $x_k$ and $x_b$, and adds back $d - D$ when $d > D$. This is $T(s, b)$ by definition. TeX keeps a running total $t$ and a separate page depth $\delta$. On a box, it sets $t \leftarrow t + \delta + h$ and $\delta \leftarrow d$, and if $\delta > D$ it moves $\delta - D$ into $t$ and sets $\delta \leftarrow D$. On a glue, it sets $t \leftarrow t + \delta + w$ and $\delta \leftarrow 0$. A penalty changes neither. So a box adds its full depth to $t$ once a glue or another box follows it: the part above $D$ at once, the rest later. The last box adds only the part above $D$ when only penalties follow it. Its height counts at once in both cases. This is $T(s, b)$. The stretch and shrink sums agree, since TeX adds them on each glue. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (one page).** Let the loop of line L2 start a page after the break $a$. Line L3 sets $s$ to the first box after $a$. If $s < m$, then lines L5 to L12 end with $best$ equal to the break that the TeX rule chooses for the page that starts at $s$, and with $best > a$.

*Proof.* TeX discards glue and penalties at the top of a page, so the page starts at the first box after $a$, which is $s$. Since $x_s$ is a box, $s < m - 1$, and the forced penalty $x_{m-1}$ is a legal break after $s$. So $e$ exists. Line L6 visits $b = s + 1, s + 2, \ldots$ in order, and line L7 skips the items that are not legal breaks, so lines L8 to L12 run for $b_1, b_2, \ldots$ in order. By Lemma 1 and line L9, $c$ is $c(s, b_i)$. Line L12 stops after the first $b_i$ with $c = \infty$ or a forced penalty, which is $e$. So exactly $b_1, \ldots, e$ are compared. Line L5 starts with $least = \infty$, and lines L10 and L11 replace $(least, best)$ whenever $c \le least$. By induction on $i$, after $b_i$ the value $least$ is the least cost among $b_1, \ldots, b_i$ and $best$ is the last break that has it, because a later break with an equal cost replaces an earlier one. At the first break, $c \le \infty = least$ holds, so $best$ is set, and $best \ge b_1 > s > a$. $\square$

**Theorem.** The page builder terminates. It returns the breaks of the TeX rule: the first page starts at the first box, each page ends at the break that the rule chooses, and the next page starts at the first box after it. The list ends when no box is left.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2, each pass of the loop of line L2 with $s < m$ appends the break of the rule (line L13) and moves $a$ to it (line L14), and the new $a$ is larger than the old one. There are at most $m$ legal breaks, so after at most $m$ passes line L3 finds no box after $a$, and line L4 ends the loop. When the loop ends, every box lies on a page, since a pass starts at the first box after the last break. Glue and penalties after the last break, including the final forced penalty when the last page ends before it, are discarded, as TeX discards them at the top of a page. $\square$

**Complexity.** The prefix sums, the arrays of the last box and the last glue, and the array of the next box take $O(m)$ time and space. Each pass of line L6 takes $O(1)$ time, and line L3 takes $O(1)$ time. Let $k$ be the largest value of $e - s$ over the pages, the number of items that line L6 scans for one page. The number of pages $P$ is at most the number of boxes, which is at most $m$. So the time is $O(m + P k)$, which is $O(m k)$ in the worst case. Items after the chosen break and up to $e$ are scanned again by the next page, as in TeX, where they go back to the list of contributions; this is why the bound is not $O(m)$. When each page holds about $k$ legal breaks, $P k$ is $O(m)$ and the time is linear. The extra space is $O(m)$.
