---
title: First fit uses the fewest lines when spaces cannot stretch
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Claim.** Let $x_0, \ldots, x_{m-1}$ be a paragraph of boxes, glue and penalties whose last item is a forced break. Assume that every box width and every penalty width is at least 0, and that every glue has $w_i - z_i \ge 0$ with shrink of finite order. Then GREEDY-BREAK (lines L1 to L10) stops. If the lengths $l_0, l_1, \ldots$ of the lines are all equal to $l$ and some breaking has no overfull line, then no line of the greedy breaking is overfull, and the greedy breaking has the fewest lines among all breakings with no overfull line. For any lengths $l_0, l_1, \ldots$, the greedy breaking has at most as many lines as any breaking with no overfull line. Stretch plays no part in this claim: a line counts as good when it is not overfull, whatever its badness.

**Setup.** A breaking is a list of legal breaks $b_1 < \cdots < b_k = m - 1$ that holds every forced break. Put $b_0 = -1$. Line $t$ (for $t = 1, \ldots, k$) runs from just after $b_{t-1}$ to $b_t$, and its length is $l_{t-1}$. For a break $a$ (or $a = -1$) and a later legal break $b$, let $s(a, b)$ be the first box after $a$, or $b$ if there is none before $b$; the items from $a + 1$ to $s(a, b) - 1$ are discarded, as in TeX. The content of the line $(a, b)$ is the items $s(a, b), \ldots, b - 1$, plus the penalty $x_b$ if $x_b$ is a penalty. Glue and penalties that are not at the break inside the content count as in the model: glue adds $w_i$, $y_i$ and $z_i$, a penalty that is not the break adds nothing. Let $M(a, b) = L - Z$ be the width of the line $(a, b)$ when its glue shrinks fully. The line is overfull at length $l$ exactly when $M(a, b) > l$; this is the test `fits` of line L5. Each item of the content adds a value of at least 0 to $M$, by the hypothesis on the widths.

**Lemma 1 (a shorter start fits).** If $a \le a' < b$ and the line $(a, b)$ fits at length $l$, then the line $(a', b)$ fits at length $l$.

*Proof.* The first box after $a'$ is not before the first box after $a$, so $s(a', b) \ge s(a, b)$. Hence the content of $(a', b)$ is a subset of the content of $(a, b)$: it is a final part of the items from $s(a, b)$ to $b - 1$, with the same item at the break. Every item adds at least 0 to $M$, so $M(a', b) \le M(a, b) \le l$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (the scan finds the farthest fitting break).** Fix a break $a$ and a length $l$. Let $b^*$ be the largest legal break $b > a$ such that no forced break lies strictly between $a$ and $b$ and the line $(a, b)$ fits, if there is one. Then the loop of lines L4 to L7 sets $best = b^*$. If there is none, line L8 sets $best$ to the first legal break after $a$.

*Proof.* The loop visits the legal breaks after $a$ in increasing order. Line L6 records each one that fits, so at the exit $best$ is the largest visited break that fits. It remains to show that no unvisited break qualifies. The loop stops at a break $b$ for one of two reasons (line L7). If $x_b$ is a forced break, every later break $b'$ has the forced break $b$ strictly between $a$ and $b'$, so it does not qualify. Otherwise $\mu(b) > l$, where $\mu(b)$ is the width at full shrink of the items $s(a, b), \ldots, b - 1$ without $x_b$. Since $\mu(b) > l \ge 0$, some box lies between $a$ and $b$, so $s(a, b)$ is the first box after $a$ and $s(a, b') = s(a, b)$ for every later legal break $b'$. Then the content of $(a, b')$ holds the items $s(a, b), \ldots, b - 1$, and all its other items add at least 0. So $M(a, b') \ge \mu(b) > l$ and $(a, b')$ is overfull. If the loop ends because the legal breaks run out, every break was visited. In each case $best$ is $b^*$, or nothing fits and line L8 takes the first legal break. $\square$

**Termination.** Each pass of the outer loop (line L2) appends a break $best > a$ and sets $a$ to it. There are finitely many legal breaks and the last item $m - 1$ is a forced break, which the scan never passes (line L7). So the breaks grow strictly and the loop stops when $a = m - 1$. The greedy list $g_1 < \cdots < g_K = m - 1$ holds every forced break: a forced break $f > a$ stops the scan, so $g_{t+1} \le f$, and the breaks keep moving until they reach $f$. Hence the output is a breaking.

**Theorem (greedy stays ahead).** Let $o_1 < \cdots < o_k = m - 1$ be any breaking with no overfull line, and let $g_1 < \cdots < g_K$ be the greedy breaking, for any lengths $l_0, l_1, \ldots$. Put $g_t = m - 1$ for $t > K$. Then $g_t \ge o_t$ for $t = 0, \ldots, k$. In particular $K \le k$.

*Proof.* By induction on $t$. For $t = 0$ both are $-1$. Assume $g_t \ge o_t$ with $t < k$. If $g_t = m - 1$, then $g_{t+1} = m - 1 \ge o_{t+1}$. If $o_{t+1} \le g_t < m - 1$, then $g_{t+1} > g_t \ge o_{t+1}$. Otherwise $o_t \le g_t < o_{t+1}$. The line $(o_t, o_{t+1})$ fits at length $l_t$, so by Lemma 1 the line $(g_t, o_{t+1})$ fits at length $l_t$. No forced break lies strictly between $g_t$ and $o_{t+1}$, because the breaking $o$ holds every forced break and has none strictly between $o_t$ and $o_{t+1}$. So $o_{t+1}$ qualifies in Lemma 2 for the greedy line number $t + 1$, which has length $l_t$, and $g_{t+1} = b^* \ge o_{t+1}$. This ends the induction. For $t = k$ we get $g_k \ge o_k = m - 1$, so the greedy breaking has ended after at most $k$ lines. $\square$

**Theorem (no overfull line with one length).** If $l_t = l$ for all $t$ and some breaking $o$ has no overfull line, then line L8 never uses its fallback, so no greedy line is overfull.

*Proof.* Take a greedy break $g_t < m - 1$. Let $u$ be the least index with $o_u > g_t$; it exists because $o_k = m - 1$. Then $o_{u-1} \le g_t < o_u$. The line $(o_{u-1}, o_u)$ fits at length $l$, so the line $(g_t, o_u)$ fits at length $l$ by Lemma 1, and no forced break lies strictly between $g_t$ and $o_u$. By Lemma 2 the scan finds a fitting break. $\square$

**Conclusion.** With one length $l$, the greedy breaking has no overfull line and at most as many lines as any breaking with no overfull line, so it has the fewest. With other lengths, it still has at most as many lines as any breaking with no overfull line, but its own lines can be overfull: greedy is ahead, so the line of $o$ that covers the start of the greedy line $t$ can have a later index and a larger length than $l_{t-1}$. Only the test "not overfull" enters the proofs. When the test is "badness at most a tolerance", Lemma 1 fails: a later start makes the line shorter and its stretch larger, so a line that was good can become underfull. The page gives an example. $\blacksquare$
