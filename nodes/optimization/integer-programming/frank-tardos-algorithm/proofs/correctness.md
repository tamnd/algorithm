---
title: The Frank-Tardos vector keeps every sign on short integer vectors
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

**Statement.** Let $w \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ and let $N \ge 2$ be an integer. FRANK-TARDOS$(w, N)$ stops after at most $n$ rounds and returns $\bar{w} \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ with $\operatorname{sign}(w^\top b) = \operatorname{sign}(\bar{w}^\top b)$ for every $b \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ with $\|b\|_1 \le N - 1$. Moreover $\|\bar{w}\|_\infty \le (2^k N^{n+1})^n$ with $k = \lceil n(n+1)/4 \rceil$.

**Lemma 1 (approximation).** Let $v \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ and $0 < \varepsilon < 1$. Let $\delta = 2^{-k} \varepsilon^{n+1}$, let $L$ be the lattice generated by the rows $r_j = (e_j, 0)$ for $j = 1, \dots, n$ and $r_{n+1} = (-v, \delta)$, and let $f$ be the first vector of an LLL-reduced basis of $L$ (with $\delta_{\mathrm{LLL}} = 3/4$). Write $f = (p - q v, q \delta)$, after a change of sign of $f$ if needed so that $q \ge 0$. Then $p \in \mathbb{Z}^n$, $1 \le q \le 2^k \varepsilon^{-n}$ and $\|q v - p\|_\infty \le \varepsilon$.

*Proof.* Every vector of $L$ is $\sum_j p_j r_j + q r_{n+1} = (p - q v, q \delta)$ with integers $p_j$ and $q$, so $p$ and $q$ are integers. The rows form an upper triangular matrix with diagonal $1, \dots, 1, \delta$, so $\det L = \delta$. The first vector of an LLL-reduced basis of a lattice of dimension $n + 1$ satisfies $\|f\| \le 2^{n/4} (\det L)^{1/(n+1)}$ (LLL 1982, Proposition 1.6). Since $k \ge n(n+1)/4$,
$$\|f\| \le 2^{n/4} \, 2^{-k/(n+1)} \varepsilon \le 2^{n/4} \, 2^{-n/4} \varepsilon = \varepsilon.$$
Hence $\|q v - p\|_\infty \le \|f\| \le \varepsilon$ and $q \delta \le \varepsilon$, so $q \le \varepsilon / \delta = 2^k \varepsilon^{-n}$. If $q = 0$, then $f = (p, 0)$ with $p \ne 0$ an integer vector, so $\|f\| \ge 1 > \varepsilon$, a contradiction. So $q \ge 1$. $\square$

Number the rounds $i = 1, \dots, t$. Let $d_1 = w$, and in round $i$ let $v_i = d_i / \|d_i\|_\infty$, let $(q_i, p_i)$ be the result of line L4, and let $d_{i+1} = q_i v_i - p_i$. By Lemma 1 with $\varepsilon = 1/N$, $q_i \ge 1$ and $\|d_{i+1}\|_\infty \le 1/N$.

**Lemma 2 (termination).** If $d_{i, j} = 0$ then $d_{i+1, j} = 0$, and $d_{i+1}$ has at least one more zero entry than $d_i$. So $t \le n$.

*Proof.* If $d_{i,j} = 0$, then $v_{i,j} = 0$ and $|p_{i,j}| = |d_{i+1,j}| \le 1/N < 1$, so $p_{i,j} = 0$ and $d_{i+1,j} = 0$. Let $j$ be an index with $|d_{i,j}| = \|d_i\|_\infty$, so that $v_{i,j} = \pm 1$ and $d_{i,j} \ne 0$. Then $q_i v_{i,j}$ is an integer, and $p_{i,j}$ is an integer at distance at most $1/N < 1$ from it, so they are equal and $d_{i+1,j} = 0$. Each round adds a zero entry, and $d$ has $n$ entries, so after at most $n$ rounds $d = 0$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (lexicographic test).** Let $b \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ with $\|b\|_1 \le N - 1$. Then $\operatorname{sign}(w^\top b)$ is the sign of the first nonzero number in the list $p_1^\top b, \dots, p_t^\top b$, and it is $0$ if all of them are $0$.

*Proof.* We show by downward induction on $i$ that $\operatorname{sign}(d_i^\top b)$ is the sign of the first nonzero number among $p_i^\top b, \dots, p_t^\top b$, or $0$ if there is none. For $i = t + 1$, $d_{t+1} = 0$ and the list is empty. For $i \le t$, multiply $d_i$ by the positive number $q_i / \|d_i\|_\infty$:
$$\frac{q_i}{\|d_i\|_\infty} \, d_i^\top b = q_i v_i^\top b = p_i^\top b + d_{i+1}^\top b.$$
Here $|d_{i+1}^\top b| \le \|d_{i+1}\|_\infty \|b\|_1 \le (N-1)/N < 1$. If $p_i^\top b \ne 0$, it is an integer with $|p_i^\top b| \ge 1$, so the right side has the sign of $p_i^\top b$. If $p_i^\top b = 0$, the right side is $d_{i+1}^\top b$, and the induction hypothesis gives its sign. The case $i = 1$ is the claim, because $d_1 = w$. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (packing).** Let $x_1, \dots, x_t$ be integers with $|x_i| \le M - 1$, where $M \ge 2$. Then $\sum_{i=1}^t M^{t-i} x_i$ has the sign of the first nonzero $x_i$, and it is $0$ if all are $0$.

*Proof.* Let $x_j$ be the first nonzero number. Then $|M^{t-j} x_j| \ge M^{t-j}$, and
$$\Big|\sum_{i > j} M^{t-i} x_i\Big| \le (M - 1) \sum_{i > j} M^{t-i} = M^{t-j} - 1 < M^{t-j}.$$
So the sum has the sign of $x_j$. If all $x_i$ are $0$, the sum is $0$. $\square$

**Proof of the statement.** If $w = 0$, the loop does not run, the output is $0$, and both signs are $0$. Otherwise $t \ge 1$ by Lemma 2. The value $M$ of line L7 satisfies $M \ge 2$, because $p_1 \ne 0$ by the proof of Lemma 2. For $\|b\|_1 \le N - 1$, let $x_i = p_i^\top b$. Then $|x_i| \le \|p_i\|_\infty \|b\|_1 \le (N-1) \max_i \|p_i\|_\infty = M - 1$. Line L8 gives $\bar{w}^\top b = \sum_i M^{t-i} x_i$. By Lemma 4 its sign is that of the first nonzero $x_i$, and by Lemma 3 this is the sign of $w^\top b$.

For the size, $|v_{i,j}| \le 1$ and $|q_i v_{i,j} - p_{i,j}| < 1$, so $|p_{i,j}| < q_i + 1$, and since both are integers $|p_{i,j}| \le q_i \le 2^k N^n$ by Lemma 1. So $M \le (N-1) 2^k N^n + 1 \le 2^k N^{n+1}$, and
$$\|\bar{w}\|_\infty \le \sum_{i=1}^t M^{t-i} \|p_i\|_\infty \le (M - 1) \sum_{i=1}^t M^{t-i} = M^t - 1 < M^t \le (2^k N^{n+1})^n,$$
using $\|p_i\|_\infty \le M - 1$ and $t \le n$. $\blacksquare$
