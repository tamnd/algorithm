## Definition

Notation: $K$ is a field of characteristic $0$, $K[x]$ is the [polynomial ring](/polynomial-ring/) in one variable, $K(x)$ its field of fractions, and $K[[x]]$ the ring of formal power series. $D = d/dx$ is the derivative, and $S$ is the shift $u_n \mapsto u_{n+1}$ on sequences.

A power series $f \in K[[x]]$ is **D-finite**, or **holonomic**, if it satisfies a linear differential equation

$$p_r(x)\, f^{(r)}(x) + p_{r-1}(x)\, f^{(r-1)}(x) + \cdots + p_0(x)\, f(x) = 0$$

with polynomial coefficients $p_0, \ldots, p_r \in K[x]$ and $p_r \ne 0$. Equivalently, the derivatives $f, f', f'', \ldots$ span a vector space of finite dimension over $K(x)$. The least such $r$ is the *order* of $f$, and the largest degree of the $p_i$ is the *degree* of the equation. The same definition applies to analytic functions on a domain of $\mathbb{C}$.

A sequence $(u_n)_{n \ge 0}$ in $K$ is **P-recursive**, or a **holonomic sequence**, if it satisfies a linear [recurrence](/recurrence-relation/)

$$q_r(n)\, u_{n+r} + q_{r-1}(n)\, u_{n+r-1} + \cdots + q_0(n)\, u_n = 0 \quad \text{for all } n \ge 0$$

with $q_0, \ldots, q_r \in K[n]$ and $q_r \ne 0$.

**The two notions match (Stanley 1980).** $f = \sum_n u_n x^n$ is D-finite if and only if $(u_n)$ is P-recursive. The translation is mechanical: the coefficient of $x^n$ in $x^j f^{(i)}$ is $(n - j + 1)(n - j + 2) \cdots (n - j + i)\, u_{n - j + i}$, so a differential equation of order $r$ and degree $d$ becomes a recurrence of order at most $r + d$, and a recurrence of order $r$ and degree $d$ becomes a differential equation of order at most $d + 1$ (the extra order clears the polynomial that the initial terms leave on the right-hand side).

**Finite description.** A D-finite function is given exactly by finite data: its equation, as an element of an [Ore algebra](/ore-algebra/) $K[x]\langle D \rangle$ or $K[n]\langle S \rangle$, plus enough initial values. For a recurrence, the values $u_0, \ldots, u_{r-1}$ and the values $u_{m + r}$ at the nonnegative integer roots $m$ of $q_r$ fix the whole sequence. This is why D-finite functions are a data structure for computer algebra: equality is decidable, by the closure under subtraction below and a comparison of finitely many initial values.

**Several variables.** For $f(x_1, \ldots, x_m)$, *D-finite* means that all partial derivatives span a finite-dimensional space over $K(x_1, \ldots, x_m)$. Bernstein's *holonomic* means that the module generated by $f$ over the Weyl algebra has the minimal possible dimension $m$. Holonomic implies D-finite, and Zeilberger (1990) used holonomy because it is preserved by definite summation and integration. For functions the two classes are known to coincide.

## Examples and non-examples

**Examples.** Each line gives a D-finite function or a P-recursive sequence, with one equation.

| function or sequence | equation |
|---|---|
| $e^x$, coefficients $u_n = 1/n!$ | $f' - f = 0$, and $(n + 1) u_{n+1} - u_n = 0$ |
| $\sin x$, $\cos x$ | $f'' + f = 0$ |
| $\log(1 + x)$ | $(1 + x) f'' + f' = 0$ |
| Bessel $J_0(x)$ | $x f'' + f' + x f = 0$, and $(n + 1)^2 u_{n+1} + u_{n-1} = 0$ |
| Catalan numbers $C_n = \frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}$ | $(n + 2) C_{n+1} - 2(2n + 1) C_n = 0$ |
| Fibonacci numbers $F_n$ | $F_{n+2} - F_{n+1} - F_n = 0$ |
| harmonic numbers $H_n$ | $(n + 2) H_{n+2} - (2n + 3) H_{n+1} + (n + 1) H_n = 0$ |
| Legendre polynomials $P_n(x)$, in $n$ | $(n + 2) P_{n+2} - (2n + 3)\, x P_{n+1} + (n + 1) P_n = 0$ |
| every polynomial and rational function | $p f' - p' f = 0$ for $f = p$, and similar |
| every [hypergeometric term](/hypergeometric-term/) | an order $1$ recurrence |
| every sequence that satisfies a [linear recurrence with constant coefficients](/linear-recurrences-with-constant-coefficients/) | that recurrence |

**A worked conversion.** Let $f = e^x/(1 - x)$. Then $f' = f \cdot \frac{2 - x}{1 - x}$, so $(1 - x) f' - (2 - x) f = 0$. Taking the coefficient of $x^n$ gives $(n + 1) u_{n+1} - n u_n - 2 u_n + u_{n-1} = 0$, that is

$$(n + 1)\, u_{n+1} - (n + 2)\, u_n + u_{n-1} = 0,$$

and indeed $u_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} 1/k!$, the partial sums of $e$, satisfy it: $(n + 1)(u_{n+1} - u_n) = 1/n! = u_n - u_{n-1}$. Here the product of the D-finite series $e^x$ and $1/(1 - x)$ is D-finite, as the closure properties promise.

**Closure properties.** If $f$ and $g$ are D-finite of orders $r$ and $s$, then so are $f + g$ (order at most $r + s$), $fg$ (order at most $rs$), $f'$ and $\int f$, the Hadamard product $\sum u_n v_n x^n$, and $f(a(x))$ for an algebraic function $a$ with $a(0) = 0$. Every algebraic power series is D-finite. Sums and products of P-recursive sequences are P-recursive, and so is $\sum_{k=0}^{n} u_k$. Each closure is effective: an equation for the result comes from linear algebra over $K(x)$ on the finite-dimensional space of derivatives. For example, $n!\, F_n$ satisfies $u_{n+2} = (n + 2) u_{n+1} + (n + 2)(n + 1) u_n$.

**Non-examples.**

- **$\tan x$** and $1/\cos x$. A solution of a linear equation with leading coefficient $p_r$ is analytic wherever $p_r \ne 0$, so a D-finite function has only finitely many singularities, among the roots of $p_r$. $\tan x$ has poles at all $\pi/2 + k\pi$.
- **$2^{n^2}$.** If $q_r(n) \ne 0$ for $n \ge n_0$, the recurrence gives $|u_{n+r}| \le C n^c \max_{i < r} |u_{n+i}|$ for constants $C$ and $c$, so a P-recursive sequence grows at most like $(n!)^c A^n$. The sequence $2^{n^2}$ grows faster.
- **$e^{e^x}$.** Its derivatives are $e^{e^x}$ times polynomials in $e^x$ of growing degree, which are independent over $K(x)$, so they span an infinite-dimensional space.
- **The sequence of primes.** It is not P-recursive (Flajolet, Gerhold and Salvy, 2005), although it is computable.

## Related definitions

- **Hypergeometric term.** A P-recursive sequence of order $1$. [Hyper](/petkovsek-hyper-algorithm/) finds the hypergeometric solutions of a P-recursive equation, and [Kovacic's algorithm](/kovacic-algorithm/) finds the Liouvillian solutions of a D-finite equation of order $2$.
- **C-finite sequence.** A P-recursive sequence with constant coefficients, whose generating function is rational.
- **Ore algebra.** The operators $\sum p_i(x) D^i$ and $\sum q_i(n) S^i$ form [Ore algebras](/ore-algebra/), and a D-finite function is a function whose annihilator in the Ore algebra is a nonzero left ideal.
- **Creative telescoping.** For a holonomic $F(n, k)$, the definite sum $\sum_k F(n, k)$ is holonomic in $n$, and [creative telescoping](/creative-telescoping/), with [Zeilberger's algorithm](/zeilberger-algorithm/) for hypergeometric $F$, computes a recurrence for it.
- **Liouvillian and closed-form solutions.** Solving a D-finite equation in closed form is the subject of [closed-form ODE solving](/closed-form-ode-solving/) and [differential Galois theory](/differential-galois-theory/).
