## Definition

Let $K \subseteq L$ be fields and let $\alpha \in L$. The set

$$I_\alpha = \{ f \in K[x] : f(\alpha) = 0 \}$$

is an ideal of $K[x]$, the kernel of the evaluation map $K[x] \to L$, $f \mapsto f(\alpha)$. The element $\alpha$ is *algebraic* over $K$ if $I_\alpha \ne \{0\}$, and *transcendental* otherwise.

If $\alpha$ is algebraic, the **minimal polynomial** of $\alpha$ over $K$, written $\mu_\alpha$ or $\mu_{\alpha, K}$, is the unique monic generator of $I_\alpha$. It exists because $K[x]$ is a [Euclidean domain](/euclidean-domain/), so every ideal is generated by one element, and that element is unique once it is made monic. Equivalently, $\mu_\alpha$ is the monic polynomial of least degree in $K[x]$ that vanishes at $\alpha$.

Its basic properties follow from this description:

- **Divisibility.** For $f \in K[x]$, $f(\alpha) = 0$ if and only if $\mu_\alpha \mid f$.
- **Irreducibility.** $\mu_\alpha$ is [irreducible](/irreducible-polynomial/) over $K$. If $\mu_\alpha = gh$ with both factors of lower degree, then $g(\alpha) h(\alpha) = 0$ in the field $L$, so one of them vanishes at $\alpha$, against minimality. Conversely, a monic irreducible polynomial that vanishes at $\alpha$ is $\mu_\alpha$.
- **Degree.** $K[x]/\langle \mu_\alpha \rangle \cong K(\alpha)$, so $\deg \mu_\alpha = [K(\alpha) : K]$, and $1, \alpha, \ldots, \alpha^{n-1}$ is a basis of $K(\alpha)$ over $K$ for $n = \deg \mu_\alpha$. This is how computer algebra systems represent elements of $K(\alpha)$: as polynomials of degree less than $n$, multiplied modulo $\mu_\alpha$.
- **Conjugates.** The roots of $\mu_\alpha$ in an algebraic closure are the *conjugates* of $\alpha$. In characteristic $0$ they are distinct, because an irreducible polynomial is square-free there.
- **Dependence on $K$.** If $K \subseteq K'$ then $\mu_{\alpha, K'}$ divides $\mu_{\alpha, K}$.

## Examples and non-examples

**Examples.**

- $\sqrt{2}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$: $x^2 - 2$, irreducible by the [Eisenstein criterion](/eisenstein-criterion/) at $2$. Over $\mathbb{R}$, the minimal polynomial is $x - \sqrt{2}$.
- $\sqrt[3]{2}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$: $x^3 - 2$. So $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}) : \mathbb{Q}] = 3$.
- The golden ratio $\varphi = (1 + \sqrt{5})/2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$: $x^2 - x - 1$.
- A primitive $n$th [root of unity](/roots-of-unity/) $\zeta_n$ over $\mathbb{Q}$: the cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_n$, of degree $\varphi(n)$ (Euler's totient). For $n = 5$ it is $x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + x + 1$.
- $\alpha = \sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$: $x^4 - 10x^2 + 1$. A [resultant](/resultant/) produces it: $\operatorname{res}_y\bigl(y^2 - 2, (x - y)^2 - 3\bigr) = (x^2 - 2\sqrt{2} x - 1)(x^2 + 2\sqrt{2} x - 1) = x^4 - 10x^2 + 1$. Its roots are $\pm \sqrt{2} \pm \sqrt{3}$, and it is irreducible because none of them generates a field of degree less than $4$.
- The class $\alpha$ of $x$ in $\mathbb{F}_4 = \mathbb{F}_2[x]/\langle x^2 + x + 1 \rangle$, over $\mathbb{F}_2$: $x^2 + x + 1$. Every element of a finite field is algebraic over its prime field.

**Non-examples.**

- $\pi$ and $e$ are transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$ (Lindemann 1882, Hermite 1873), so they have no minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$. Over $\mathbb{Q}(\pi)$, the minimal polynomial of $\pi$ is $x - \pi$.
- $(x^2 - 2)(x^2 - 3)$ vanishes at $\sqrt{2}$, but it is reducible, so it is a multiple of $\mu_{\sqrt{2}}$, not $\mu_{\sqrt{2}}$ itself.
- $2x^2 - 4$ has least degree among the polynomials that vanish at $\sqrt{2}$ but is not monic. Over $\mathbb{Z}$ one often uses the primitive integer multiple of $\mu_\alpha$ instead. Then $\alpha$ is an *algebraic integer* exactly when $\mu_\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, by Gauss's lemma.
- The minimal polynomial of a *matrix* $A$ is a different object: the monic generator of $\{ f : f(A) = 0 \}$, which need not be irreducible. See [matrix minimal polynomial](/matrix-minimal-polynomial/). The two meet in one place: $\mu_\alpha$ is the matrix minimal polynomial of multiplication by $\alpha$ on $K(\alpha)$.

## Related definitions

- **Characteristic polynomial of an element.** For a finite extension $L \supseteq K$ and $\alpha \in L$, the [characteristic polynomial](/characteristic-polynomial/) of the $K$-linear map $y \mapsto \alpha y$ on $L$ is $\mu_\alpha^{[L : K(\alpha)]}$. So $\mu_\alpha$ is its square-free part in characteristic $0$, and the norm and trace of $\alpha$ come from its coefficients.
- **Computing a minimal polynomial.** By linear algebra: find the first linear dependence among $1, \alpha, \alpha^2, \ldots$ in a basis of $L$ over $K$. By resultants: for $\alpha$ and $\beta$ with known minimal polynomials, $\operatorname{res}_y(\mu_\alpha(y), \mu_\beta(x - y))$ vanishes at $\alpha + \beta$, and factoring it isolates $\mu_{\alpha + \beta}$. From a numerical approximation: the [LLL algorithm](/lll-algorithm/) finds a small integer relation among $1, \alpha, \ldots, \alpha^n$, as in Kannan, Lenstra and Lovász (1988). See [minimal polynomial computation](/minimal-polynomial-computation/).
- **Primitive element.** In characteristic $0$, every finite extension $K(\alpha, \beta)$ is $K(\alpha + c\beta)$ for all but finitely many $c \in K$, so one minimal polynomial describes the whole extension.
- **Use in integration.** The constants of the logarithmic part of a rational integral are given by the minimal polynomials of the residues, the irreducible factors of the [Rothstein-Trager](/rothstein-trager/) resultant.
