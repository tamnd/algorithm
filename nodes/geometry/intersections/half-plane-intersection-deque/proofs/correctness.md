---
title: The sort-and-deque method returns the vertices of the intersection of the half-planes
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Setting.** A half-plane $h = (p_h, d_h)$ is the closed set $\{x : \varphi_h(x) \ge 0\}$ with $\varphi_h(x) = \operatorname{cross}(d_h, x - p_h)$; "strictly inside" means $\varphi_h(x) > 0$. Let $R$ be the intersection of all the input half-planes. The precondition is that $R$ is bounded (an empty $R$ is bounded). The angle of $h$ is the angle of $d_h$ in $[0, 2\pi)$. For two half-planes $a, b$, the gap $a \to b$ is the counterclockwise angle from $d_a$ to $d_b$, in $[0, 2\pi)$. If the gap $a \to b$ is in $(0, \pi)$ then $\operatorname{cross}(d_a, d_b) > 0$ and the lines meet in one point $\operatorname{meet}(a, b)$. A half-plane $e$ is *essential* if $R \cap \partial e$ is a segment of positive length (an edge of $R$).

**Claim.** If $R$ has a non-empty interior, the output is the list of the vertices of $R$ in counterclockwise order, each once and with no collinear vertices. Otherwise the output is empty.

**Fact 1 (the edge of an essential half-plane).** Let $R$ have a non-empty interior and let $e$ be essential with edge $s$. For every input half-plane $h$ whose boundary line is not the line of $e$, the relative interior of $s$ is strictly inside $h$. *Proof.* The line of $h$ meets the line of $e$ in at most one point. If it passed through a relative interior point of $s$, then $s$ would have points on both sides of it, but $s \subseteq h$. $\square$

**Lemma 1 (three half-planes).** Let $a, b, c$ be half-planes with gaps $a \to b$ and $b \to c$ in $(0, \pi)$, and let $x = \operatorname{meet}(a, b)$ be not strictly inside $c$.
(c) Without the hypothesis on $x$: $\operatorname{meet}(a, b)$ is strictly inside $c$ if and only if $\operatorname{meet}(b, c)$ is strictly inside $a$.
(a) Then $a \cap c \cap \partial b \subseteq \{x\}$, so $b$ is not essential when $R \subseteq a \cap c$.
(b) If the gap $a \to c$ is also in $(0, \pi)$, then $a \cap c \subseteq b$.

*Proof.* (c) On $\partial b$, written $z_0 + t d_b$, the half-plane $a$ keeps the points with $t \ge t_a$, where $\operatorname{meet}(a, b)$ has parameter $t_a$, because $\operatorname{cross}(d_a, d_b) > 0$; and $c$ keeps the points with $t \le t_c$, where $\operatorname{meet}(b, c)$ has parameter $t_c$, because $\operatorname{cross}(d_b, d_c) > 0$. Both conditions say $t_a < t_c$. (a) Points of $\partial b$ are $x + t d_b$. Then $\varphi_a(x + t d_b) = t \operatorname{cross}(d_a, d_b)$, which is $\ge 0$ exactly for $t \ge 0$. And $\varphi_c(x + t d_b) = \varphi_c(x) - t \operatorname{cross}(d_b, d_c)$ with $\varphi_c(x) \le 0$ and $\operatorname{cross}(d_b, d_c) > 0$, which is $\ge 0$ only for $t \le 0$. So only $t = 0$ is left. (b) Let $w = \operatorname{meet}(a, c) = x + s d_a$. From $\varphi_c(x) + s \operatorname{cross}(d_c, d_a) = 0$ and $\operatorname{cross}(d_c, d_a) < 0$ we get $s \le 0$, so $\varphi_b(w) = s \operatorname{cross}(d_b, d_a) = -s \operatorname{cross}(d_a, d_b) \ge 0$. The set $a \cap c$ is the cone $\{w + \alpha d_c - \beta d_a : \alpha, \beta \ge 0\}$, and $\varphi_b$ grows along both generators, because $\operatorname{cross}(d_b, d_c) > 0$ and $-\operatorname{cross}(d_b, d_a) > 0$. So $\varphi_b \ge 0$ on $a \cap c$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (a closed chain is a convex polygon).** Let $D = (D_1, \dots, D_m)$, $m \ge 3$, be half-planes in strictly increasing angle order, with every cyclic gap $D_i \to D_{i+1}$ (indices mod $m$) in $(0, \pi)$, and with $v_i = \operatorname{meet}(D_i, D_{i+1})$ strictly inside $D_{i+2}$ for every $i$. Then $v_1, \dots, v_m$ are the vertices, in counterclockwise order, of a convex polygon $P$ with a non-empty interior, $P = \bigcap D$, and the edge $v_{i-1} v_i$ of $P$ lies on $\partial D_i$ and has positive length.

*Proof.* On $\partial D_{i+1}$, written $v_i + t d_{i+1}$, the half-plane $D_{i+2}$ keeps the points with $t \le t^*$, where $v_{i+1}$ has parameter $t^*$, and $v_i$ strictly inside $D_{i+2}$ means $t^* > 0$. So $v_{i+1} - v_i = \lambda_{i+1} d_{i+1}$ with $\lambda_{i+1} > 0$. The closed polygon $v_1 v_2 \cdots v_m$ has edge directions $d_2, \dots, d_m, d_1$, which turn left at every vertex by an angle in $(0, \pi)$, and the turns add up to exactly $2\pi$ because the angles are sorted in $[0, 2\pi)$. A closed polygon whose edges turn left at every vertex with a total turn of $2\pi$ is simple and convex. A convex polygon is the intersection of the left half-planes of its edges, which are the $D_i$. $\square$

**The loop invariant.** Let $h_1, \dots, h_r$ be the half-planes after line L1 and the removal of repeated directions. During the loop of lines L3 to L7: (I1) the deque is in increasing angle order, and each gap between neighbors is in $(0, \pi)$; (I2) for each three neighbors $a, b, c$ of the deque, $\operatorname{meet}(a, b)$ is strictly inside $c$; (I3) after $h_i$ is appended, the intersection of the deque is $h_1 \cap \dots \cap h_i$.

The removal of repeated directions keeps, for each direction, the half-plane that is contained in all the others with this direction, so it does not change $R$. Line L7 appends $h$ after the loops of lines L4 and L5 stop, so the last two elements meet strictly inside $h$ (I2) and line L6 makes the new gap less than $\pi$ (I1). Pops remove only end elements and make no new triple. In a deque with (I2), the vertices $\operatorname{meet}$ of neighbors form a chain whose edge from $\operatorname{meet}(a, b)$ to $\operatorname{meet}(b, c)$ is a positive multiple of $d_b$, as in the proof of Lemma 2. We now check the pops for (I3).

*Front pops.* Line L5 pops the front $e$, with second element $f$, only if the deque has at least 3 elements, because with 2 elements the test is the same as the one of line L4, which has passed. Let $l$ be the back. The chain from $\operatorname{meet}(e, f)$ to the last vertex has edges in directions strictly between $d_e$ and $d_h$, and the last vertex is strictly inside $h$. If $h - e \le \pi$ in angle, then every edge direction $d$ of the chain satisfies $\operatorname{cross}(d_h, d) < 0$, so $\varphi_h$ falls strictly along the chain and $\operatorname{meet}(e, f)$ is strictly inside $h$, so there is no pop. Also $h - f > \pi$: otherwise the edge from $\operatorname{meet}(e, f)$ to $\operatorname{meet}(f, f')$, where $f'$ is the element after $f$, in direction $d_f$, does not raise $\varphi_h$, and the rest of the chain, with directions strictly between $d_f$ and $d_h$, makes $\varphi_h$ fall strictly, so $\varphi_h(\operatorname{meet}(e, f)) \ge \varphi_h(\text{last vertex}) > 0$. So the gaps $h \to e$, $e \to f$ and $h \to f$ are all in $(0, \pi)$, By Lemma 1(c) with $a = h$, $b = e$, $c = f$, the point $\operatorname{meet}(h, e)$ is not strictly inside $f$, and Lemma 1(b) gives $h \cap f \subseteq e$. The pop does not change the intersection of the deque with $h$.

*Back pops.* Let $b$ be the back when line L4 stops. If line L6 does not return, the gap $b \to h$ is less than $\pi$. Every element $e$ popped at line L4 lies between $b$ and $h$ in angle, and so does its predecessor $g$, so all three gaps $g \to e$, $e \to h$ and $g \to h$ are in $(0, \pi)$, and Lemma 1(b) gives $g \cap h \subseteq e$. Since $g$ is either kept or popped later with the same property, the intersection of the deque with $h$ does not change. This proves (I3).

**Lemma 3 (non-empty interior).** Let $R$ have a non-empty interior. Then line L6 never returns, and no essential half-plane is ever popped.

*Proof.* As $R$ is bounded with a non-empty interior, every gap between neighbors in the sorted list $h_1, \dots, h_r$, including the gap $h_r \to h_1$, is less than $\pi$. Otherwise all directions lie in a closed half-turn ending at some $d$, every $h_j$ contains $x + t d$ for $x \in R$ and $t \ge 0$, and $R$ is unbounded. The back of the deque, when $h$ arrives, is the half-plane just before $h$ in this list. Let $q_1, q_2, \dots$ be the elements popped at line L4 in order, and $b$ the back after the pops. Suppose that some $q_j$, or $b$, is at angle $\pi$ or more before $h$; take the first one, $q_j$. Then $j \ge 2$, and $q_{j-1}$ was popped because $y = \operatorname{meet}(q_j, q_{j-1})$ is not strictly inside $h$. The set $q_j \cap q_{j-1}$ is the cone $\{y + \alpha d_{q_{j-1}} - \beta d_{q_j}\}$, and on it $\varphi_h = \varphi_h(y) + \alpha \operatorname{cross}(d_h, d_{q_{j-1}}) - \beta \operatorname{cross}(d_h, d_{q_j}) \le 0$, because the gap $q_{j-1} \to h$ is in $(0, \pi)$ and the gap $h \to q_j$ is in $(0, \pi]$. So $R$ has no point strictly inside $h$, which is false. Hence every gap from a popped element or from $b$ to $h$ is less than $\pi$, and line L6 does not return. Each popped $e$ at line L4 has gaps $g \to e$ and $e \to h$ in $(0, \pi)$, so Lemma 1(a) shows that $e$ is not essential. For a front pop at line L5, the gaps $h \to e$ and $e \to f$ are in $(0, \pi)$ (see the front pops above), and Lemma 1(c) and 1(a) with $a = h$, $b = e$, $c = f$ show that $e$ is not essential.

For the loop of lines L8 and L9, the deque contains all essential half-planes and they have pairwise gaps less than $\pi$ around the circle; so the back $l_2$ and the front $l_0$ have a gap $l_2 \to l_0$ less than $\pi$, at most the gap from the last essential half-plane to the first. A back pop at line L8 has gaps $l_1 \to l_2$ and $l_2 \to l_0$ in $(0, \pi)$, and Lemma 1(a) shows that $l_2$ is not essential. A front pop at line L9 is the mirror case, with Lemma 1(c). $\square$

**Proof of the claim.** *Non-empty interior.* By Lemma 3 the final deque $D$ contains the set $E$ of essential half-planes, $|E| \ge 3$, and the test of line L10 passes because the gap from the back to the front is less than $\pi$. When the loop of lines L8 and L9 stops, the two triples that pass through the end are good, and the other triples are good by (I2). By Lemma 2 the output is the vertex list of $P = \bigcap D$, and each $D_i$ carries an edge of positive length of $P$. Now $R \subseteq P$ since $D$ is a subset of the input, and $P \subseteq \bigcap E = R$, since a convex polygon is the intersection of its edge half-planes. So $P = R$, every element of $D$ is essential, $D = E$, and the output is the vertex list of $R$.

*Empty interior.* Suppose the output is not empty. Then line L6 never returned, so by (I3) the deque after the main loop has intersection $R$. The final deque $D$ passes line L10, so Lemma 2 applies and $P = \bigcap D$ has a non-empty interior. Every element popped at line L8 or L9 lies in the angle range from the final back to the final front, which is less than $\pi$; so at each such pop all three gaps are in $(0, \pi)$ and Lemma 1(b), with Lemma 1(c) for the pops at line L9, shows that the popped half-plane contains the intersection of two half-planes that are still in the deque at that moment. By induction backwards over the pops, every popped half-plane contains $P$. So $P \subseteq R$, and $R$ has a non-empty interior, which is false. So the output is empty.

**Termination.** Each half-plane is appended at most once and popped at most once, so the loops of lines L3 to L9 do $O(r)$ steps in total, and line L1 sorts in $O(n \log n)$ time. $\blacksquare$
