---
title: BUILD gives the doubly connected edge list of the arrangement clipped to the box
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Notation.** $L = \{\ell_1, \dots, \ell_n\}$ is a set of distinct lines, $\ell_i = \{a_i x + b_i y = c_i\}$ with integers and $(a_i, b_i) \ne (0, 0)$. The arrangement $\mathcal{A}(L)$ has as vertices the points on two or more lines, as edges the connected parts of $\ell_i$ minus the vertices, and as faces the connected parts of $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \bigcup L$. For a vertex $v$, $m_v$ is the number of lines through $v$. $B = [-M, M]^2$ is the box of line L2 and $G$ is the plane graph formed by the segments of lines L3 and L4. "Left of a half-edge" means strictly left of its directed line.

**Lemma 1 (the box).** Every vertex of $\mathcal{A}(L)$ lies in the interior of $B$, and every line of $L$ meets the interior of $B$ and meets $\partial B$ in exactly two points.

*Proof.* $M$ exceeds the absolute value of every coordinate of every crossing, so each vertex is in the open box. The point of $\ell_i$ next to the origin is $c_i (a_i, b_i) / (a_i^2 + b_i^2)$, whose coordinates have absolute value at most $|c_i| \max(|a_i|, |b_i|) / (a_i^2 + b_i^2) \le |c_i| < M$. A line through an interior point of a convex compact set with nonempty interior meets its boundary in exactly two points. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (a plane graph).** Two segments of $G$ meet at most in a common end point, the segments on $\ell_i$ cover $\ell_i \cap B$, and $G$ is connected.

*Proof.* On $\ell_i$, line L3 sorts the crossings and the two hits along the line; by Lemma 1 the hits are the two ends of $\ell_i \cap B$ and the crossings lie between them. So the segments of $\ell_i$ cover $\ell_i \cap B$ and two of them share at most an end point. A segment of $\ell_i$ and a segment of $\ell_j$, $i \ne j$, can only share the point $\ell_i \cap \ell_j$, which is a split point of both lines, so it is an end point of both. A box segment meets a line segment only on $\partial B$, where the line segment has its hit as an end point, and that hit is a split point of $\partial B$ in line L4. The box segments form one cycle, and every line chain ends on it, so $G$ is connected. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the face rule).** After line L7, $\mathrm{next}$ is a permutation of the half-edges with inverse $\mathrm{prev}$, and its cycles are the boundaries of the faces of $G$, each traversed with its face on the left.

*Proof.* Let $h_0, \dots, h_{d-1}$ be the half-edges that leave a vertex $v$, in counterclockwise order after line L6, with indices mod $d$. The half-edges that end at $v$ are their twins, and line L7 sets $\mathrm{next}(\mathrm{twin}(h_k)) = h_{k-1}$. This is a bijection from the half-edges that end at $v$ to those that leave $v$, so $\mathrm{next}$ is a permutation and line L7 sets $\mathrm{prev}$ to its inverse. Since two segments meet only at end points (Lemma 2), the open wedge $W_k$ from $h_{k-1}$ counterclockwise to $h_k$ contains no edge near $v$, so the points of $W_k$ near $v$ lie in one face $\phi$ of $G$. Near $v$, the left side of $\mathrm{twin}(h_k)$ is $W_k$, and the left side of $h_{k-1}$ is $W_k$ too. So $e$ and $\mathrm{next}(e)$ have the same face on their left, for every $e$, and each cycle of $\mathrm{next}$ lies on the boundary of one face. Conversely, the boundary of a face of a connected plane graph is one closed walk, and that walk turns at each vertex from the incoming edge to the next edge clockwise from the way back, which is the rule of line L7. So each face has exactly one cycle. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (faces of $G$ and of $\mathcal{A}(L)$).** The map $\phi \mapsto \phi \cap \operatorname{int} B$ is a bijection from the faces of $\mathcal{A}(L)$ to the faces of $G$ other than the outer face. Each such face is a strictly convex polygon, its cycle has positive area, and the outer cycle, along $\partial B$, has negative area.

*Proof.* A face $\phi$ of $\mathcal{A}(L)$ is the set of points with a fixed nonzero sign for each $a_i x + b_i y - c_i$, an intersection of open half-planes, so it is open and convex. Then $\phi \cap \operatorname{int} B$ is open and convex, and it does not meet $G$, which lies in $\bigcup L \cup \partial B$ by Lemma 2; its boundary lies in $G$. So, if it is nonempty, it is one face of $G$. It is nonempty: if the closure of $\phi$ holds a vertex $v$, then $v \in \operatorname{int} B$ by Lemma 1 and the points of $\phi$ near $v$ are in $B$; otherwise the boundary of $\phi$ contains a whole line $\ell_i$ with no vertex, and $\ell_i$ meets $\operatorname{int} B$ at some point $q$ (Lemma 1), and the points of $\phi$ near $q$ are in $B$. Two faces of $\mathcal{A}(L)$ are disjoint, so the map is injective. Every point of $\operatorname{int} B$ off the lines lies in some face of $\mathcal{A}(L)$, so the map is onto the faces of $G$ inside $B$; the only other face of $G$ is the outside of $B$.

The face $\phi \cap \operatorname{int} B$ is convex, its boundary is a polygon, and by Lemma 3 its cycle has it on the left, so the cycle is counterclockwise and has positive area. Two consecutive half-edges of the cycle meet at a vertex $v$. If $v \in \operatorname{int} B$, the outgoing half-edges at $v$ are the two rays of each of the $m_v \ge 2$ lines through $v$; the two rays of one line are opposite, and each open half-plane of that line holds at least one ray of another line, so two rays that are neighbours in the angular order lie on different lines and are not collinear. If $v \in \partial B$, the two half-edges are a box edge and a line edge, or two box edges at a corner, and they are not collinear since every line meets $\operatorname{int} B$. So each corner of the polygon turns strictly left. The outer face is on the left of the box edges directed clockwise, so its cycle has negative area. $\square$

**Theorem.** BUILD terminates, and its result satisfies the four invariants of the representation and describes $\mathcal{A}(L)$: the vertices inside $B$ are the vertices of $\mathcal{A}(L)$, the line edges are its edges, with each unbounded edge cut at $\partial B$, and the faces $1, 2, \dots$ are its faces cut by $B$. Moreover $E = n + \sum_v m_v$ and $F = 1 + n + \sum_v (m_v - 1)$ for $\mathcal{A}(L)$, $n \ge 1$.

*Proof.* Termination: every loop of lines L1 to L7 runs over a finite list, and the walks of line L8 follow the permutation $\mathrm{next}$ (Lemma 3) and stop when they come back to a half-edge with a face, so each half-edge is visited once.

Invariant 1 holds by line L5 (twins) and Lemma 3. For invariant 2, a vertex $v \in \operatorname{int} B$ is on $m_v$ lines and, by Lemma 1, is not an end of any line chain, so each of these lines gives two segments at $v$, and no box segment ends at $v$. Invariant 3 is Lemma 4. Invariant 4 is Euler's formula for the connected plane graph $G$ (Lemma 2), whose faces are the cycles (Lemma 3). The description of $\mathcal{A}(L)$ follows from Lemmas 2 and 4.

For the counts, let $k_i$ be the number of vertices on $\ell_i$. The vertices cut $\ell_i$ into $k_i + 1$ edges, so $E = \sum_i (k_i + 1) = n + \sum_v m_v$, since each vertex is counted once for each line through it. For $F$, insert the lines in the order $\ell_1, \dots, \ell_n$. The first line gives $2$ faces. Let $\ell = \ell_j$ meet $\ell_1, \dots, \ell_{j-1}$ in $k$ distinct points. These points cut $\ell$ into $k + 1$ open pieces. A piece meets no earlier line, so it lies in one face $\psi$ of the earlier arrangement, and since $\psi$ is convex and the ends of the piece lie on earlier lines or at infinity, the piece is the whole of $\ell \cap \psi$ and splits $\psi$ into two faces. Distinct pieces lie in distinct faces, because a convex face meets $\ell$ in one interval. So the number of faces grows by $k + 1$. A vertex $v$ is counted as such a point once for each line through it except the first, so the sum of the $k$ over all insertions is $\sum_v (m_v - 1)$, and $F = 2 + (n - 1) + \sum_v (m_v - 1)$.

Finally, FACE-BOUNDARY returns the cycle of $f$, and LOCATE is correct: a point $p \in \operatorname{int} B$ off the lines lies in exactly one face $f \ge 1$ of $G$, and since $f$ is a strictly convex polygon traversed counterclockwise, $p$ lies in it if and only if $p$ is left of every half-edge of its cycle. $\blacksquare$
