title: Each INSERT adds one line to the clipped arrangement, so EOS builds the whole arrangement
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full

**Notation.** $L_m = \{\ell_1, \dots, \ell_m\}$ are the lines inserted so far. $B = [-M, M]^2$ is the box of line L1; every crossing of two input lines lies in the open box, and every input line meets the open box and meets $\partial B$ in exactly two points (Lemma 1 on the [line arrangement](/line-arrangement/) correctness page). $G_m$ is the plane graph whose edges are the pieces of the lines of $L_m$ inside $B$ between consecutive crossings and hits, and the pieces of $\partial B$ between consecutive hits and corners. A half-edge has its face on its left. For the line $\ell : a x + b y = c$ being inserted, $d = (b, -a)$ and $s(x) = a x_1 + b x_2 - c = \operatorname{cross}(d, x - q)$ for any $q \in \ell$; a point $x$ is left of $\ell$ if $s(x) > 0$ and right if $s(x) < 0$.

**Invariant.** Before the insertion of $\ell_{m+1}$, the structure $A$ is a doubly connected edge list of $G_m$: twins and next pointers are consistent, the cycles of $\mathrm{next}$ are the boundaries of the faces of $G_m$ with each face on the left, each vertex has a valid outgoing half-edge, and every face inside $B$ is a strictly convex polygon. For $m = 0$ this holds for the box built in line L1.

**Lemma 1 (entry).** Lines L4 and L5 end with a vertex $u$ at the entry point $p$ of $\ell$, and $A$ is then an edge list of $G_m$ with $p$ added as a vertex of $\partial B$.

*Proof.* The walk of line L4 follows the outer cycle, which runs once around $\partial B$, so it reaches the vertex or the edge that holds $p$. Splitting an edge at an inner point $q$ replaces $a \to b$ by $a \to q \to b$ on both sides and keeps all cycles, so the result is an edge list of the subdivided graph. Every inner face stays convex, and only the face that has $p$ on its boundary gets a straight angle at $p$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (the wedge).** Let $u \in \ell$ be a vertex of $A$ with $u$ before the exit point of $\ell$ on $\ell \cap B$. Then $d$ lies strictly inside exactly one wedge between two neighbouring half-edges $h, h'$ out of $u$, with $h'$ counterclockwise from $h$; this wedge has angle at most $\pi$; the head of $h$ is strictly right of $\ell$ and the head of $h'$ is strictly left; and $\phi = \mathrm{face}(h)$ is the face that holds $u + t d$ for all small $t > 0$.

*Proof.* No edge at $u$ lies along $\ell$: edges lie on other lines, which meet $\ell$ in one point at most, or on $\partial B$, which does not contain $\ell$. So $d$ is not the direction of any half-edge out of $u$, and it lies strictly inside exactly one of the wedges. If $u \in \operatorname{int} B$, then $u$ is on a line $\ell' \ne \ell$ of $L_m$, whose two rays leave $u$ in opposite directions, so each wedge lies in a closed half-plane of $\ell'$ and has angle at most $\pi$. If $u \in \partial B$, the wedge that contains the inward direction $d$ lies inside $B$, which near $u$ is a half-plane or a quadrant. So the angle from $h$ to $d$ and the angle from $d$ to $h'$ are both in $(0, \pi)$, which gives $\operatorname{cross}(d, h) < 0 < \operatorname{cross}(d, h')$: the head of $h$ is right of $\ell$ and the head of $h'$ is left. The open wedge meets no edge near $u$, so its points near $u$ lie in one face, which is on the left of $h$; the points $u + t d$ are among them. Line L7 tests exactly this wedge condition with exact angle comparisons. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the exit).** With $u, h, \phi$ as in Lemma 2, $\ell$ meets the closure of $\phi$ in a segment $[u, w]$ with $w \ne u$, the walk of line L8 stops at the half-edge $g$ whose closed edge holds $w$ with $w$ not equal to the tail of $g$, and line L9 makes $w$ a vertex.

*Proof.* $\phi$ is a convex polygon and $\ell$ meets its interior near $u$, so $\ell \cap \overline{\phi}$ is a segment $[u, w]$ with $w \ne u$ on $\partial \phi$. No edge of $\phi$ lies on $\ell$, so $\partial \phi \cap \ell = \{u, w\}$, and $\partial \phi \setminus \{u, w\}$ is two open arcs, one strictly right and one strictly left of $\ell$. The counterclockwise boundary walk from $u$ starts with $h$, whose head is right of $\ell$ (Lemma 2), so it runs through the right arc to $w$ and then through the left arc back to $u$. Line L8 moves on while the head is strictly right, so it stops at the first half-edge $g$ whose head is on $\ell$ or left of it. Its tail is $u$ or a point of the right arc, so $s$ is not positive at the tail. If the head is on $\ell$, it is $w$. If the head is strictly left, the edge goes from the right arc to the left arc and holds $w$ in its interior, and line L9 splits it at its crossing with $\ell$, which is $w$. The walk stops at the latest at $\mathrm{prev}(h)$, whose head is $u$, but it stops earlier because $w$ comes first. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (the split of the face).** Line L10 replaces $\phi$ by the two faces $\phi_R = \phi \cap \{s < 0\}$ and $\phi_L = \phi \cap \{s > 0\}$, gives the right part the old name and the left part a new one, and keeps the edge list consistent. The corners of $\phi_R$ and $\phi_L$ at $u$ and $w$ are strictly convex.

*Proof.* The chord $uw$ cuts the convex region $\phi$ into the two convex regions $\phi_R$ and $\phi_L$. Let $x$ be $u \to w$ and $y$ be $w \to u$. Around $u$, $x$ goes between $h$ and $h'$, so the rule "next is the half-edge just clockwise of the twin" gives $\mathrm{next}(\mathrm{prev}(h)) = x$, because $\mathrm{prev}(h) = \mathrm{twin}(h')$, and $\mathrm{next}(y) = h$. Around $w$, the face $\phi$ fills the wedge from $\mathrm{next}(g)$ counterclockwise to $\mathrm{twin}(g)$, and the direction $-d$ of $y$ lies in it, so $\mathrm{next}(g) = y$ and $\mathrm{next}(x)$ is the old $\mathrm{next}(g)$. These are the four assignments of line L10. The cycle $h, \dots, g, y$ runs through the right arc, so it bounds $\phi_R$ and keeps the name $\mathrm{face}(h)$; the cycle $x, \dots, \mathrm{prev}(h)$ runs through the left arc, so it bounds $\phi_L$, and line L10 writes the new name on each of its half-edges. At $u$ the new corners are the angles from $h$ to $d$ and from $d$ to $h'$, both in $(0, \pi)$ by Lemma 2. At $w$ the corners split the angle of $\phi$ at $w$, which is less than $\pi$ if $w$ was an old vertex and equal to $\pi$ if it was made by a split, into two parts by the direction $-d$, which is not along an edge; both parts are in $(0, \pi)$. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (the next face).** If $w \in \operatorname{int} B$, then $\ell$ goes on from $w$ into the face $\phi'$ that the next iteration finds in line L7, and if $w$ was made by a split of an edge, $\phi'$ is the face on the other side of that edge, which is the only face with a straight angle at $w$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 3, $\ell$ leaves $\overline{\phi}$ at $w$; the points $w + t d$ for small $t > 0$ are not in $\overline{\phi}$, and Lemma 2 at $w$ gives the face that holds them. If $w$ is inside an edge $e$, the line $\ell$ crosses $e$ there, so these points lie in the face on the other side of $e$. The split of line L9 gave that face a straight angle at $w$, and the next execution of line L10 cuts this angle by Lemma 4. $\square$

**Theorem.** INSERT terminates and, if the invariant holds for $L_m$, it holds for $L_{m+1} = L_m \cup \{\ell\}$. Hence EOS terminates and returns the edge list of $G_n$, which is the arrangement of the $n$ lines clipped to $B$.

*Proof.* Each iteration of lines L6 to L12 moves $u$ to a point $w$ on $\ell$ further in the direction $d$ (Lemma 3). Every such $w$ is a crossing of $\ell$ with an earlier line, or a point of $\partial B$: a vertex of $A$ on $\ell$ is a crossing, a box corner or a hit of another line, and hits of other lines are not on $\ell$ because crossings are not on $\partial B$; an edge that line L9 splits lies on another line or on $\partial B$. There are finitely many crossings on $\ell$, and $\ell \cap \partial B$ is the entry and the exit point. So the loop stops, at the exit point, after at most $m + 1$ iterations. Each walk of line L8 and each renaming of line L10 is finite because it follows a cycle of $\mathrm{next}$.

After the loop, the points of $\ell$ that are vertices are the entry point, all crossings of $\ell$ with $L_m$ in order (each one is passed, because $[u, w] \cap$ the earlier lines $= \{u, w\}$ by Lemma 3) and the exit point, and the edges added in line L10 are the pieces of $\ell$ between them. Every edge of an earlier line that $\ell$ crosses was split at the crossing, and the box edges were split at the two hits. So the graph is $G_{m+1}$. By Lemmas 1 to 5 the edge list stays consistent, the faces of $G_{m+1}$ are the old faces that $\ell$ misses and the two parts of each face that it crosses, and every inner face is strictly convex: the straight angles made by the splits at the entry point and in line L9 are cut by the next execution of line L10, and the one made at the exit point lies on the outer face. So the invariant holds for $L_{m+1}$.

By induction, after line L2 the structure is the edge list of $G_n$, and by the correctness proof on the [line arrangement](/line-arrangement/) page, the faces of $G_n$ inside $B$ correspond one to one to the faces of the arrangement of $L_n$. $\blacksquare$
