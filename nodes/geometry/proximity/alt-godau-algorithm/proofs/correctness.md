---
title: The free-space decision procedure is exact, and the Fréchet distance is the smallest critical value it accepts
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Setting.** $P = (P_0, \ldots, P_p)$ and $Q = (Q_0, \ldots, Q_q)$ are polygonal curves in the plane with $p, q \ge 1$ segments and rational vertices, parametrized by $P(s)$ for $s \in [0, p]$ (linear on each $[i, i+1]$) and $Q(t)$ for $t \in [0, q]$. For $\varepsilon \ge 0$ the free space is $F_\varepsilon = \{(s, t) \in [0, p] \times [0, q] : \|P(s) - Q(t)\| \le \varepsilon\}$. The cell $(i, j)$ is $[i, i+1] \times [j, j+1]$. On the vertical edge $\{i\} \times [j, j+1]$ the free set is $\{i\} \times (j + L_{ij})$ and on the horizontal edge $[i, i+1] \times \{j\}$ it is $(i + B_{ij}) \times \{j\}$, where $L_{ij}, B_{ij} \subseteq [0, 1]$. A path is monotone when both coordinates are nondecreasing along it. We use the standard characterization of Alt and Godau: $d_F(P, Q) \le \varepsilon$ if and only if $F_\varepsilon$ contains a monotone path from $(0, 0)$ to $(p, q)$. (A pair of nondecreasing surjective reparametrizations with leash at most $\varepsilon$ is exactly such a path, and the infimum over homeomorphisms in the definition of $d_F$ equals the minimum over nondecreasing reparametrizations.)

**Lemma 1 (exact signs).** `sign_one_root(a, b, x)` returns the sign of $a + b\sqrt{x}$ and `sign_two_roots(a, b, x, c, y)` the sign of $a + b\sqrt{x} + c\sqrt{y}$, for rationals with $x, y \ge 0$.

*Proof.* For one root: if $b = 0$ or $x = 0$ the value is $a$. Otherwise $b\sqrt{x} \ne 0$. If $a \ge 0$ and $b > 0$ the sum is positive, and if $a \le 0$ and $b < 0$ it is negative. Otherwise $a$ and $b\sqrt{x}$ have strictly opposite signs and the sum has the sign of the term with the larger absolute value, decided by comparing $a^2$ with $b^2 x$; if $a > 0$ that sign is $\text{sgn}(a^2 - b^2 x)$, and if $a < 0$ it is the opposite. For two roots let $L = a + b\sqrt{x}$ and $R = -c\sqrt{y}$, so the value is $L - R$. If $L \ge 0 \ge R$ the difference is $\ge 0$, and $0$ exactly when both are $0$; if $L \le 0 \le R$ it is $\le 0$ and the function returns $-1$, which is right because the case $L = R = 0$ was handled first. Otherwise $L$ and $R$ are nonzero with the same sign, and $L - R$ has the sign of $L^2 - R^2 = (a^2 + b^2 x - c^2 y) + 2ab\sqrt{x}$ if both are positive and the opposite sign if both are negative; this is again a one-root sign. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (free intervals).** `free_interval(x, a, b, eps2)` returns exactly $\{t \in [0, 1] : \|a + t(b - a) - x\|^2 \le \varepsilon^2\}$, as a closed interval of surds or `None` when it is empty.

*Proof.* With $d = b - a$ and $w = a - x$, $\|a + td - x\|^2 - \varepsilon^2 = \alpha t^2 + 2\beta t + \gamma$ with $\alpha = d \cdot d$, $\beta = d \cdot w$, $\gamma = w \cdot w - \varepsilon^2$. If $\alpha = 0$ the segment is a point and the set is $[0, 1]$ or empty according to the sign of $\gamma$. If $\alpha > 0$ the quadratic is convex, so its sublevel set is the closed interval between the roots $(-\beta \mp \sqrt{\Delta})/\alpha$ with $\Delta = \beta^2 - \alpha\gamma$, and empty if $\Delta < 0$. Intersecting with $[0, 1]$ clamps the ends to $0$ and $1$, and the result is empty exactly when the clamped lower end exceeds the clamped upper end. All comparisons use Lemma 1, so they are exact. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (convex cells).** $F_\varepsilon \cap (i, j)$ is convex. Hence if $u$ and $v$ are free points of the same cell with $u \le v$ in both coordinates, the segment $uv$ is a monotone path in $F_\varepsilon$.

*Proof.* On the cell, $(s, t) \mapsto P(s) - Q(t)$ is affine, and $F_\varepsilon \cap (i, j)$ is the preimage of the closed disk of radius $\varepsilon$ under it intersected with the square; preimages of convex sets under affine maps are convex. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (reachability).** Call a point reachable if a monotone path in $F_\varepsilon$ leads from $(0, 0)$ to it. After `frechet_decide` has passed line L1 and processed cell $(i, j)$, $LR[i+1][j]$ is the set of reachable points of the edge $\{i + 1\} \times [j, j+1]$ and $BR[i][j+1]$ that of $[i, i+1] \times \{j + 1\}$, in local coordinates. The initial values of line L3 are the reachable parts of the edges on the lines $s = 0$ and $t = 0$.

*Proof.* Boundary. A monotone path from $(0, 0)$ to a point $(0, t)$ stays on the line $s = 0$, so $(0, t)$ is reachable if and only if all of $\{0\} \times [0, t]$ is free. Since $(0, 0)$ is free after line L1, $L_{00}$ contains $0$, and being an interval it is entirely reachable. Inductively, $LR[0][j]$ is all of $L_{0j}$ if the reachable part of the previous edge contains its top end $1$ (then $(0, j)$ is free, so $0 \in L_{0j}$ and the whole interval connects), and empty otherwise, because the line $s = 0$ is then blocked below $j$. This is the code; the bottom row is symmetric.

Inner cells, in the order of the loops, so that $LR[i][j]$ and $BR[i][j]$ are already correct. Let $v$ be a free point of the right edge of cell $(i, j)$. If $v$ is reachable by a path $\pi$, let $u$ be the last point of $\pi$ with $s \le i$ or $t \le j$; it exists since $\pi$ starts at $(0, 0)$. After $u$ the path has $s > i$ and $t > j$, and by monotonicity $s \le i + 1$ and $t \le t(v) \le j + 1$, so $u$ lies on the left or bottom edge of cell $(i, j)$, and it is reachable. If $u$ is on the bottom edge, $BR[i][j]$ is nonempty. If $u$ is on the left edge, $t(v) \ge t(u) \ge \min LR[i][j]$. Conversely, if $BR[i][j]$ contains some $u$, then $u \le v$ coordinatewise for every point $v$ of the right edge (its $s$ is at most $i + 1$ and its $t$ is $j$), and by Lemma 3 the segment $uv$ extends the path to $u$, so every free point of the right edge is reachable. If only $LR[i][j]$ is nonempty, the free points $v$ of the right edge with $t(v) \ge \min LR[i][j]$ are reached the same way from the lowest point of $LR[i][j]$, and no others by the first part. This is `_reach(L[i+1][j], BR[i][j], LR[i][j])` in line L6, whose comparisons are exact by Lemma 1. Line L7 is the same argument with the roles of $s$ and $t$ exchanged. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (the decision).** `frechet_decide(P, Q, eps2)` returns true if and only if $d_F(P, Q) \le \varepsilon$.

*Proof.* If an endpoint pair is farther than $\varepsilon$, no path can start or end, and line L1 returns false correctly. Otherwise $(p, q)$ is reachable if and only if it is a reachable point of the right edge of cell $(p - 1, q - 1)$ or of its top edge, that is, the top end $1$ of $LR[p][q-1]$ or of $BR[p-1][q]$. By Lemma 4 these sets are the reachable parts, each an interval whose upper end is the upper end of the free interval, which is $1$ because $(p, q)$ is free. Line L8 tests exactly this. By the characterization in the setting, reachability of $(p, q)$ is equivalent to $d_F \le \varepsilon$. If one curve is a single point, a leash from that point to every point of the other curve is needed, and the distance from a fixed point to a segment is largest at an endpoint, so comparing with all vertices decides it. $\square$

**Lemma 6 (critical values).** $d_F(P, Q)^2$ belongs to the set $C$ computed by `critical_values`: the squared distances of the start points and of the end points (type a), the squared distances from every vertex of one curve to every segment of the other (type b), and, for every segment of one curve and every two vertices $U_k, U_l$ of the other, the squared distance from $U_k$ to the point where the perpendicular bisector of $U_k U_l$ crosses the segment (type c). Moreover the set of $\varepsilon$ with $d_F \le \varepsilon$ is $[d_F, \infty)$.

*Proof.* A monotone path from $(0, 0)$ to $(p, q)$ passes through a staircase sequence of cells and crosses the edges between consecutive cells. Group the crossings into maximal runs of vertical edges within one row and of horizontal edges within one column. For a run of vertical edges $\{k\} \times [j, j+1]$, $k = a, \ldots, b$, the crossing heights $y_a \le \cdots \le y_b$ must be nondecreasing with $y_k \in L_{kj}$; between runs, a crossing of a horizontal edge followed by one of a vertical edge, or the start and end corners, impose no order constraint, by Lemma 3. Such heights exist if and only if every $L_{kj}$ in the run is nonempty and $\min L_{kj} \le \max L_{lj}$ for all $a \le k < l \le b$: take $y_k = \max_{k' \le k} \min L_{k'j}$. So, by Lemma 3 again, a monotone free path exists if and only if, for some staircase of cells, the following conditions hold: (i) the start and end points are free; (ii) every crossed edge has a nonempty free interval; (iii) the pairwise conditions within each run.

Each condition holds on a closed half-line $[c, \infty)$ of $\varepsilon$, since free sets grow with $\varepsilon$, and its threshold $c$ is a critical value. For (i), $c$ is the endpoint distance, type a. For (ii), $L_{kj}$ is nonempty exactly when the distance from $P_k$ to the segment $Q_j Q_{j+1}$ is at most $\varepsilon$, type b. For (iii), $\min L_{kj}$ is nonincreasing and $\max L_{lj}$ nondecreasing in $\varepsilon$, both continuous where defined. If at the threshold one of them is clamped to $0$ or $1$, the inequality holds as soon as both intervals are nonempty, so the threshold is of type b. Otherwise at the threshold they are equal unclamped roots: there is a point $y$ on $Q_j Q_{j+1}$ with $\|y - P_k\| = \varepsilon = \|y - P_l\|$, so $y$ is on the bisector of $P_k P_l$, and $\varepsilon^2$ is a type c value. The same holds for horizontal runs with the roles of the curves exchanged. A conjunction of conditions holds on $[\max c, \infty)$ and the disjunction over finitely many staircases on $[\min \max c, \infty)$, whose left end is in $C$. That left end is $d_F$. $\square$

**Lemma 7 (the search).** `frechet_distance2` returns $d_F(P, Q)^2$.

*Proof.* If a curve is a single point, the answer is the largest squared distance to a vertex of the other curve, as in Lemma 5. Otherwise, after lines L9 and L10, $C$ is sorted. By Lemma 6, the predicate "`frechet_decide` accepts $c$" is false below $d_F^2$ and true from $d_F^2$ on, and $d_F^2 \in C$, so the largest element of $C$ is accepted. The binary search of line L11 keeps the invariant that the smallest accepted element of $C$ has index in $[lo, hi]$, and stops when $lo = hi$; that element is $d_F^2$. $\square$

**Termination.** `frechet_decide` runs two nested loops with $p$ and $q$ iterations after $O(pq)$ free intervals are computed; each step is a constant number of rational operations on numbers that are fixed polynomials of the input, never results of earlier steps fed back, so their sizes stay bounded. `critical_values` has $O(p^2 q + p q^2)$ iterations, and the binary search halves $hi - lo + 1$ in each of its $O(\log(pq))$ rounds.

**Conclusion.** By Lemmas 1 to 5 the decision procedure answers exactly whether $d_F(P, Q) \le \varepsilon$, and by Lemmas 6 and 7 the search returns the exact squared Fréchet distance; both terminate. $\blacksquare$
