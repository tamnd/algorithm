---
title: The partition tree answers closed polygonal range queries exactly, and its query cost is governed by the crossing number
proves: [correctness, termination, complexity]
status: full
---

**Setting.** $P$ is a multiset of $n$ points in the plane with exact coordinates, $r \ge 2$ the fan-out and $b \ge 1$ the leaf size. BUILD is lines L1 to L11 of the page; a query range is $\Delta = \bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell} \{x : a_i \cdot x \le c_i\}$, closed. For a node $v$, $S(v)$ is its multiset of points and $C(v) = \mathrm{conv}\, S(v)$ its cell, stored as the lower chain $L$ and the upper chain $U$, both from the lexicographically smallest to the largest vertex. For a vector $u = (a, b)$ write $f_u(x) = a x_1 + b x_2$.

**Claim.** (a) BUILD terminates, and the tree satisfies: the children of an internal node partition its multiset into at least $2$ and at most $\max(r, 2)$ nonempty classes, each smaller than the parent, and for $r \ge 4$ each of at most $2 \lceil m/r \rceil - 1$ points, where $m$ is the parent's size; leaves hold at most $b$ points; the blocks of the point array match the subtrees. (b) EXTREME$(C(v), u)$ returns $\max_{x \in C(v)} f_u(x)$. (c) QUERY returns $|P \cap \Delta|$, and REPORT lists $P \cap \Delta$ with multiplicity. (d) Let $r \ge 4$. If every internal node's partition has crossing number at most $\kappa$ for each of the $\ell$ boundary lines of $\Delta$, the number of visited nodes $V(m)$ in a subtree with $m$ points satisfies $V(m) \le 1 + r + \ell \kappa\, V(2 \lceil m / r \rceil - 1)$ for $m > b$, and $V(m) = 1$ for $m \le b$. (e) The space and build time are as in the table of the page.

**Step 1 (one node splits).** Let $m = |S| > b \ge 1$, so $m \ge 2$. Line L5 sets $s = \lceil m/r \rceil$ and $q = \lfloor m/s \rfloor$; if $q < 2$ it sets $s = \lfloor m/2 \rfloor \ge 1$ and $q = 2$. In both cases $1 \le s$, $2 \le q \le \max(r, 2)$ (since $\lfloor m / \lceil m/r \rceil \rfloor \le r$) and $(q - 1) s \le m - s$. The loop at line L6 makes $q - 1$ classes of exactly $s$ points each, and line L10 makes the last class of the remaining $m - (q - 1) s \ge s$ points. So there are $q$ nonempty classes, disjoint and covering $S$, because each round removes its class from the remaining list, and each has at most $m - s < m$ points. Without the lowering, the last class has $s + (m \bmod s) \le 2s - 1$ points. The lowering happens only if $m < 2 \lceil m/r \rceil$, and for $r \ge 4$ and $m \ge 2$ we have $2 \lceil m/r \rceil \le 2 \lceil m/4 \rceil \le m$, so for $r \ge 4$ every class has at most $2 \lceil m/r \rceil - 1$ points.

The inner loop at line L7 terminates: before each round at least $m - (q - 2) s \ge 2s$ points remain; if no group has $s$ points, $t$ is halved, and at $t = 0$ no line is drawn, so all remaining points form one group.

**Step 2 (termination and the shape).** Every class pushed at line L11 is smaller than its parent by Step 1, so along any path from the root the sizes strictly decrease, and every path ends at a leaf with at most $b$ points. The tree is finite, so the stack at line L2 empties. The root's block is $[0, n)$, and line L11 gives the children consecutive blocks of the sizes of their classes, starting at the parent's start; so the blocks of the children partition the block of the parent, and a leaf writes its points into its own block. By induction from the leaves up, the block of every node holds exactly $S(v)$. This proves (a).

**Step 3 (extreme values).** Let $C = \mathrm{conv}\, S$ with chains $L$ and $U$ computed by the monotone chain method, which removes non-strict turns, so the chains have no three consecutive collinear vertices. The maximum of the linear function $f_u$ over $C$ is attained at a vertex. If $b = 0$, $f_u = a x_1$ is maximized at a point of smallest or largest $x_1$, and $L$ starts and ends at such points. Let $b > 0$. The maximum of $f_u$ over $C$ is attained on the upper chain: for any point $x$ of $C$, the point of the upper chain above $x$ (same $x_1$, largest $x_2$) has $f_u$ at least as large, since $b > 0$. Walk $U$ from left to right and let $\theta_j$ be the direction angle of edge $j$. The chain turns clockwise at each vertex, and its edges point rightward or vertically, so $\theta_0 > \theta_1 > \dots$ within $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$; a vertical edge can only be the first edge (upward, $\theta = \pi/2$), because the monotone chain pass removes a downward vertical edge at the right end. The increment of $f_u$ along edge $j$ has the sign of $\cos(\theta_j - \varphi)$, where $\varphi \in (0, \pi)$ is the angle of $u$. As $\theta$ decreases from at most $\pi/2$, the difference $\theta - \varphi$ starts below $\pi/2$ and, since $\varphi > 0$ and $\theta \ge -\pi/2$, stays above $-\pi/2 - \varphi > -3\pi/2$; on this interval the cosine is positive above $-\pi/2$ and at most zero below it. So the increments are positive on an initial run of edges and at most zero after it. The binary search finds the first edge with increment at most zero, whose start vertex maximizes $f_u$ on $U$ (or the last vertex if no such edge exists). The case $b < 0$ is the mirror image on $L$, which turns counterclockwise. When $C$ is a point or a segment, both chains are the one or two extreme points and the same argument applies. The minimum is $-\max f_{-u}$. This proves (b), and each call costs $O(\log |S|)$.

**Step 4 (the tests at lines L12 and L13).** Let $h_i = \{x : a_i \cdot x \le c_i\}$. If $\min_{C(v)} f_{a_i} > c_i$, then every point of $C(v)$, and so every point of $S(v) \subseteq C(v)$, is outside $h_i$ and outside $\Delta$; line L12 returns $0$ correctly. If $\max_{C(v)} f_{a_i} \le c_i$ for every $i$, then $C(v) \subseteq \Delta$, so $S(v) \subseteq \Delta$, and line L13 returns $|S(v)|$ correctly; REPORT lists the block of $v$, which is $S(v)$ by Step 2. By Step 3 these extremes are computed exactly.

**Step 5 (exactness of the query).** By induction on the height of $v$, QUERY$(v, \Delta) = |S(v) \cap \Delta|$. At a node where lines L12 and L13 decide, Step 4 applies. At a leaf, line L14 tests each point directly with exact arithmetic and the closed inequalities. At an internal node, the children's multisets partition $S(v)$ by Step 2, so $|S(v) \cap \Delta| = \sum_w |S(w) \cap \Delta|$, and line L15 returns this sum by the induction hypothesis. REPORT follows the same recursion and lists each point once, because the blocks of the reported nodes are disjoint: a node is reported whole only if no ancestor was, since its ancestors were opened at line L15. At the root this gives (c).

**Step 6 (visited nodes).** Suppose a node $v$ is opened at line L15. Then for some $i$ the maximum of $f_{a_i}$ over $C(v)$ exceeds $c_i$, and for the same $i$ the minimum does not, since line L12 did not return. So $C(v)$ has points on both closed sides of the line $\partial h_i$ and is not contained in it: $\partial h_i$ crosses $C(v)$. Now let $v$ be opened with $m$ points. All its children are visited, at most $r$ of them by Step 1. A child $w$ that is opened in turn has a cell crossed by one of the $\ell$ lines $\partial h_i$, and each line crosses at most $\kappa$ children's cells, so at most $\ell \kappa$ children are opened. Each child has at most $2 \lceil m/r \rceil - 1$ points by Step 1. Counting $v$, its children, and the visits below the opened children gives $V(m) \le 1 + r + \ell \kappa\, V(2 \lceil m/r \rceil - 1)$, and a leaf costs one visit. This proves (d).

**Step 7 (solving the recurrence).** For $r \ge 4$ and $m > b$, $2 \lceil m/r \rceil - 1 \le 2m/r + 1$, so after $j$ levels the size is at most $(2/r)^j n + \sum_{i < j} (2/r)^i \le (2/r)^j n + 2$, and the recursion reaches a leaf after $D \le \log_{r/2}(n / b) + O(1)$ levels. Unfolding, $V(n) \le (1 + r) \sum_{j < D} (\ell \kappa)^j + (\ell \kappa)^D = O(r (\ell \kappa)^D)$ for $\ell \kappa \ge 2$, and $(\ell \kappa)^{\log_{r/2} (n/b)} = (n/b)^{\log(\ell \kappa) / \log(r/2)}$. Each visit costs $O(\ell \log n)$ by Step 3, and each opened leaf scans at most $b$ points. With $\kappa \le C \sqrt{r}$, as the [partition theorem](/partition-theorem/) guarantees for suitable cells, the exponent is $\log(\ell C \sqrt{r}) / \log(r/2)$, which is less than $1/2 + \varepsilon$ once $r$ is a large enough constant.

**Step 8 (space and build time).** By Step 1 every internal node has at least $2$ children and every leaf holds a point, so there are at most $2n - 1$ nodes, each with $O(1)$ words besides its chains. At one depth the multisets $S(v)$ are disjoint, so the chains at that depth have at most $2n$ vertices together; with depth $O(\log_{r/2} n)$ the chains take $O(n \log_r n)$ words for fixed $r \ge 4$, and points in convex position show that every level can be this large. At a node with $m$ points, $s \ge m/r$, so the sample size is $t \le \lfloor \sqrt{m/s} \rfloor \le \sqrt{r}$. One try at line L7 costs $O(t^2 + m t)$ for the vertices of the drawn lines and the grouping (each comparison is an exact sign evaluation; the bisection adds $O(\log t)$ per point), and there are at most $\log_2 t + 1$ tries. Line L8 handles disjoint candidate groups: sorting them by distance and computing the hulls costs $O(m \log m)$, and testing each hull of at most $s$ vertices against the $\lvert T \rvert$ lines costs $O(m \lvert T \rvert)$ in all. Line L9 costs $O(s \lvert T \rvert)$. With at most $q \le r$ rounds, a node costs $O(r m (\lvert T \rvert + \log m + \sqrt{r} \log^2 r))$. The sizes at one depth sum to at most $n$ and there are $O(\log_{r/2} n)$ depths, so BUILD takes $O(r n \log_r n \, (\lvert T \rvert + \log n))$ time for fixed $r \ge 4$, in the worst case and for every seed, since the random draws change only which classes are chosen. This proves (e). $\blacksquare$
