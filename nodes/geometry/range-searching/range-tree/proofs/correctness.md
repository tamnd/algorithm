---
title: The range tree answers closed box queries correctly, with O(log^d n + k) query time and O(n log^(d-1) n) space
proves: [correctness, complexity]
status: full
---

**Setting.** $P = (p_1, \dots, p_n)$ is a list of points in $\mathbb{R}^d$, repeats allowed. For a coordinate $j$, the key of point $i$ is $(p_{i,j}, i)$, compared lexicographically, so the keys of distinct indices differ. $Q = [a_1, b_1] \times \dots \times [a_d, b_d]$ is a closed box. For a list $L$ sorted by the key of coordinate $j$, write $Q_j(L)$ for the points $p$ of $L$ with $a_l \le p_l \le b_l$ for all $l \ge j$.

**Claim.** QUERY$(S, j, Q)$, where $S$ is BUILD$(L, j)$, reports each index of $Q_j(L)$ exactly once and no other. With $m = |L|$ and $d \ge 2$, the structure stores $O(m \log^{d-1} m)$ entries and is built in $O(m \log^{d-1} m)$ time from $L$, and the query takes $O(\log^{d - j + 1} m + |Q_j(L)|)$ time.

**Step 1 (the block of line L7).** $L$ is sorted by $(p_j, \text{index})$, so the coordinates $p_j$ along $L$ do not decrease. Hence the positions with $a_j \le p_j \le b_j$ form one contiguous block, from the first position with $p_j \ge a_j$ (the lower bound of $a_j$) up to just before the first position with $p_j > b_j$ (the upper bound of $b_j$). Line L7 computes exactly $[lo, hi)$, which is empty if $a_j > b_j$.

**Step 2 (canonical nodes).** Let $T$ be the tree of line L2 and $[lo, hi)$ a block. Call a node canonical if its block lies inside $[lo, hi)$ and its parent's block does not (or it is the root). (a) The blocks of the canonical nodes are disjoint: two nodes of $T$ have disjoint blocks or one block contains the other, and if a canonical block contained another canonical node's block, that node's parent block would also lie inside $[lo, hi)$. (b) Their union is $[lo, hi)$: each position $t \in [lo, hi)$ lies in the leaf block $[t, t+1) \subseteq [lo, hi)$, and the highest ancestor of the leaf whose block lies inside $[lo, hi)$ is canonical. (c) At each depth there are at most two canonical nodes. The blocks of the nodes at one depth are disjoint intervals. Suppose three canonical nodes at depth $t$ have blocks $B_1 < B_2 < B_3$ in left-to-right order. The sibling of the node of $B_2$ has a block $C$ at depth $t$ that is adjacent to $B_2$; say $C$ follows $B_2$ (the other case is symmetric). $B_3$ is disjoint from $C$ and lies to the right of $B_2$, so it lies to the right of $C$. Hence $B_2 \cup C$ lies between the start of $B_1$ and the end of $B_3$, inside the interval $[lo, hi)$. But $B_2 \cup C$ is the block of the parent of $B_2$, which contradicts the canonicity of $B_2$. The tree has height $\lceil \log_2 m \rceil$, so there are at most $2\lceil \log_2 m \rceil$ canonical nodes (and at most one if $m = 1$). The descent that finds them visits only nodes whose blocks meet $[lo, hi)$ without lying inside it, at most two per depth by the same argument, and their children, so $O(\log m)$ nodes.

**Step 3 (correctness, by induction on $d - j$).** If $j = d$, line L8 reports the block $[lo, hi)$ of $L$, which by Step 1 is exactly $Q_d(L)$, each index once. If $j < d$, by Step 2 the points of $L$ with $a_j \le p_j \le b_j$ are the disjoint union of the canonical subsets $P(v)$. By the invariant of BUILD, $\mathrm{assoc}(v)$ is BUILD$(Y(v), j + 1)$, where $Y(v)$ is the list of points of $P(v)$ sorted by the key of coordinate $j + 1$ (line L4 merges two lists sorted by that key, which gives a sorted list, by induction over the post-order). By induction, QUERY$(\mathrm{assoc}(v), j + 1, Q)$ reports each index of $Q_{j+1}(Y(v))$ once. A point of $L$ is in $Q_j(L)$ if and only if it lies in some $P(v)$, necessarily one, and satisfies the constraints $l \ge j + 1$. So the union of the reports is $Q_j(L)$, each index once.

**Step 4 (space).** Let $S_j(m)$ be the largest number of entries of BUILD$(L, j)$ over lists with $|L| \le m$, counting one entry per element of every array, and let $g(m) = \lceil \log_2 m \rceil + 2$. Then $S_d(m) \le m$. For $j < d$, a point of $L$ lies in $Y(v)$ exactly for the nodes $v$ on the path from the root to its leaf, at most $\lceil \log_2 m \rceil + 1$ nodes, so $\sum_v |Y(v)| \le m (g(m) - 1)$. Suppose $S_{j+1}(m') \le m' g(m)^{d-j-1}$ for all $m' \le m$, which holds for $j + 1 = d$. Then
$$S_j(m) \le m + \sum_v S_{j+1}(|Y(v)|) \le m + \sum_v |Y(v)|\, g(m)^{d-j-1} \le m + m (g(m) - 1) g(m)^{d-j-1} \le m\, g(m)^{d-j},$$
and since $g$ does not decrease, the bound holds for all $m' \le m$ as well. So the whole structure has at most $n (\lceil \log_2 n \rceil + 2)^{d-1} = O(n \log^{d-1} n)$ entries. In the plane it has at most $n(\lceil \log_2 n \rceil + 2)$ entries, with equality when $n$ is a power of two.

**Step 5 (build time).** One call BUILD$(L, j)$ with $j < d$ performs merges whose output lengths add up to $\sum_v |Y(v)| \le m (g(m) - 1)$, so they take $O(m \log m)$ time, and copying the keys takes $O(m)$. With $B_d(m) = O(m)$, the recurrence $B_j(m) \le O(m \log m) + \sum_v B_{j+1}(|Y(v)|)$ is solved exactly as in Step 4 and gives $B_j(m) = O(m\, g(m)^{d-j})$. The initial sort costs $O(n \log n)$. So the build takes $O(n \log^{d-1} n)$ time for $d \ge 2$.

**Step 6 (query time).** Let $R_j(m)$ be the time of QUERY at level $j$ without the output. $R_d(m) = O(\log m)$ for the two binary searches. For $j < d$, the call does two binary searches and the canonical descent in $O(\log m)$ time, and makes at most $2\lceil \log_2 m \rceil$ calls at level $j + 1$, each on at most $m$ points. So $R_j(m) \le O(\log m) + 2\lceil \log_2 m \rceil R_{j+1}(m)$, which gives $R_1(n) = O(\log^{d} n)$. Each reported point costs $O(1)$ at line L8, and each is reported once by Step 3, so REPORT takes $O(\log^{d} n + k)$ time and COUNT, which adds $hi - lo$ at line L8, takes $O(\log^{d} n)$ time. $\blacksquare$
