---
title: The partition tree query counts and reports the points of a closed polygonal range exactly
proves: [correctness, termination, complexity]
status: full
---

**Setting.** $T$ is a partition tree on a multiset $P$ of $n$ points in the plane, built as on the [partition tree](/partition-tree/) page, with fan-out $r$ and leaf size $b$. Its properties, proved there, are: (T1) the children of an internal node $v$ partition the multiset $S(v)$ into at least two nonempty classes, each smaller than $S(v)$, and for $r \ge 4$ each of at most $2 \lceil |S(v)|/r \rceil - 1$ points; (T2) the block of the point array assigned to $v$ holds exactly $S(v)$, and the blocks of the children partition it; (T3) the cell $C(v)$ is the convex hull of $S(v)$, stored as its lower chain $L$ and upper chain $U$ from the lexicographically smallest to the largest vertex, with no three consecutive vertices collinear. The range is $\Delta = \bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell} h_i$ with $h_i = \{x : a_i \cdot x \le c_i\}$, closed. For $u = (a, b)$ let $f_u(x) = a x_1 + b x_2$.

**Claim.** (a) EXTREME$(C, u)$ returns $\max_{x \in C} f_u(x)$. (b) PARTITION-TREE-QUERY terminates and returns $|P \cap \Delta|$ and the multiset $P \cap \Delta$. (c) Every node popped and decided at line L6 or L7 has a cell crossed by some boundary line $\partial h_i$ (it meets the cell and does not contain it). (d) If $r \ge 4$ and every line crosses at most $\kappa$ of the cells of the children of any node, the number of popped nodes is at most $V(n)$ with $V(m) = 1$ for $m \le b$ and $V(m) \le 1 + r + \ell \kappa\, V(2 \lceil m/r \rceil - 1)$ for $m > b$; each costs $O(\ell \log n)$, plus $O(b \ell)$ at a scanned leaf and the output.

**Step 1 (EXTREME).** The maximum of a linear function over a convex polygon is attained at a vertex. If $b = 0$, $f_u = a x_1$, which is largest at a vertex of smallest or largest $x_1$; the lower chain starts and ends at such vertices, which is line L9. Let $b > 0$. For any point $x$ of $C$, the point of the upper boundary with the same $x_1$ has an $x_2$ at least as large, hence an $f_u$ at least as large; so the maximum is attained on $U$. Let $\theta_0, \theta_1, \dots$ be the direction angles of the edges of $U$, from left to right. $U$ turns clockwise at every vertex and its edges point to the right, except possibly the first edge, which may be vertical and upward; so $\pi/2 \ge \theta_0 > \theta_1 > \dots > -\pi/2$. The increment of $f_u$ along edge $j$ is its length times $\cos(\theta_j - \varphi)$, where $\varphi \in (0, \pi)$ is the angle of $u$. The differences $\theta_j - \varphi$ decrease and lie in $(-3\pi/2, \pi/2)$, where the cosine is positive exactly on $(-\pi/2, \pi/2)$. So the increments are positive on an initial run of edges and at most zero on the rest. Hence $f_u$ increases strictly along $U$ up to the start of the first edge with increment at most zero and does not increase after it, and that vertex, or the last vertex if every increment is positive, is a maximum. Line L11 finds this edge by binary search on a monotone predicate, and line L12 returns the value. For $b < 0$ the same argument applies to $L$, which turns counterclockwise, by reflecting in the $x_1$-axis. When $C$ is a point or a segment the chains are its one or two extreme points, and the argument still holds. This proves (a); the search takes $O(\log |S(v)|)$ steps.

**Step 2 (the bounds at line L3).** By Step 1, $\mathrm{hi}_i = \max_{C(v)} f_{a_i}$ and $\mathrm{lo}_i = -\max_{C(v)} f_{-a_i} = \min_{C(v)} f_{a_i}$.

**Step 3 (decisions).** If $\mathrm{lo}_i > c_i$ for some $i$, every point $x \in C(v)$ has $f_{a_i}(x) > c_i$, so no point of $S(v) \subseteq C(v)$ lies in $h_i$, and $S(v) \cap \Delta = \emptyset$: line L4 is right to add nothing. If $\mathrm{hi}_i \le c_i$ for every $i$, then $C(v) \subseteq \Delta$, so $S(v) \subseteq \Delta$: line L5 adds $|S(v)|$ and, by (T2), the block of $v$, which is $S(v)$. A leaf that reaches line L6 is tested point by point with the closed inequalities in exact arithmetic.

**Step 4 (every point is counted exactly once).** Let $x$ be a copy of a point of $P$, stored at leaf $\lambda$, and let $\pi$ be the path from the root to $\lambda$. The walk pops the root; whenever it pops a node $v$ on $\pi$ and neither line L4 nor line L5 decides $v$, either $v = \lambda$ and line L6 tests $x$, or line L7 pushes the children of $v$, among them the next node of $\pi$, which is popped later because the loop runs until the stack is empty. So the walk follows $\pi$ until the first node $v$ of $\pi$ that is decided at line L4 or L5, or until it tests $x$ at $\lambda$. In the first case $x$ is counted if and only if $v$ is decided at L5, which by Step 3 happens only if $x \in \Delta$, and $v$ is decided at L4 only if $x \notin \Delta$. In the second case $x$ is counted if and only if $x \in \Delta$. No node below the first decided node is pushed, and each node is pushed at most once, by its parent. So $x$ contributes exactly once if $x \in \Delta$ and never otherwise. Summing over the copies gives (b) for the count; the reported multiset is built from the same contributions.

**Step 5 (termination).** Each node is pushed at most once, by its parent, and the tree is finite, so the loop of line L2 pops finitely many nodes; each pop does finite work.

**Step 6 (opened nodes are crossed).** Suppose $v$ reaches line L6 or L7. Line L5 did not fire, so $\mathrm{hi}_i > c_i$ for some $i$; line L4 did not fire, so for that $i$, $\mathrm{lo}_i \le c_i$. The cell is convex and connected, so it has a point with $f_{a_i} = c_i$, meaning it meets the line $\partial h_i$, and it has a point with $f_{a_i} > c_i$, so it is not contained in the line. This proves (c).

**Step 7 (counting visits).** Let $v$ with $m > b$ points be opened at line L7. All its children are pushed and later popped, at most $r$ of them by (T1). By Step 6 every child that is opened or scanned in turn has a cell crossed by one of the $\ell$ lines $\partial h_i$, and each line crosses at most $\kappa$ of these cells, so at most $\ell \kappa$ children are opened or scanned; each has at most $2 \lceil m/r \rceil - 1$ points by (T1). The other children are popped once and decided at line L4 or L5. Counting $v$, all children, and the nodes popped below the opened children, $V(m) \le 1 + r + \ell \kappa\, V(2 \lceil m/r \rceil - 1)$, and a leaf is popped once. Line L3 costs $O(\ell \log n)$ by Step 1, a scanned leaf costs $O(b \ell)$, and line L5 adds $O(1)$ for counting or the size of the block for reporting; the reported blocks are disjoint by Step 4, so reporting adds $O(k)$ in total. This proves (d).

**Step 8 (the stack).** The stack holds, for each node on the current path from the root, at most $r - 1$ siblings still waiting, so at most $(r - 1) D + 1$ entries for depth $D = O(\log_{r/2} n)$ when $r \ge 4$. $\blacksquare$
