---
title: Insertion keeps the trapezoidal map and its search DAG correct
proves: [correctness, invariant, termination]
status: full
---

**Setting.** All geometry is in the sheared plane of the symbolic shear $(x, y) \mapsto (x + \varepsilon y, y)$, with $\varepsilon > 0$ smaller than any value at which the order of the finitely many points involved changes. Then the sheared $x$-order of points is the lexicographic order $<$, no segment is vertical, and $\mathrm{orient}$ is unchanged (the shear has determinant $1$). $S$ is the set of inserted segments, stored as $(a, b)$ with $a < b$, any two meeting at most in a common end point, and $P$ is their set of end points. For a point $v \in P$, the *wall* of $v$ is the maximal vertical segment through $v$ whose relative interior meets no segment of $S$; it ends at the first segment above and below $v$ (or goes to infinity). The *trapezoidal map* $T(S)$ is the subdivision of the plane by the segments and the walls. Its faces are open sets.

**Claim.** After every sequence of insertions: (I1) the set of trapezoid records equals the set of faces of $T(S)$, and each record has the right top, bottom, leftp and rightp, and neighbor pointers that follow the slot rules of the page; (I2) for every point $q$ that is not in $P$ and lies on no segment, TM-QUERY ends at the leaf of the face of $T(S)$ that contains $q$; for $q \in P$ it returns the vertex $q$, and for $q$ in the relative interior of a segment $s$ it returns $s$. (I3) The number of faces is $1 + |S| + |P|$. Each insertion and each query terminates.

**Step 1 (shape of a face).** Let $F$ be a face of $T(S)$. No wall and no segment crosses $F$, so for every vertical line $\ell$ meeting $F$, $\ell \cap F$ is an open vertical interval bounded by the first segments above and below it (or infinity). Two such lines that meet $F$ see the same bounding segments, since the segment directly above can only change at a sheared $x$ where a segment starts or ends, that is at a point of $P$, and there a wall separates the two lines. So $F$ has one top and one bottom (possibly none), and its left and right sides lie on the walls of two points of $P$ (leftp and rightp) or at infinity. Conversely, given leftp $u$, the face is determined by $u$ and the side of the segments at $u$ on which it lies.

**Step 2 (the count I3).** Every face except the leftmost one (the face with no leftp, which contains all points left of the smallest end point) has a leftp. Fix $v \in P$ and a small disk around $v$. Just to the right of the wall of $v$, the segments that start at $v$ cut the right half of the disk into $\mathrm{starts}(v) + 1$ sectors. Each sector lies in a face with leftp $v$, distinct sectors lie in distinct faces (a segment separates them), and every face with leftp $v$ contains such a sector (its left side touches the wall of $v$ at $v$, by Step 1, as $v$ is on its closure). So exactly $\mathrm{starts}(v) + 1$ faces have leftp $v$, and the total is $1 + \sum_{v} (\mathrm{starts}(v) + 1) = 1 + |S| + |P|$, since each segment starts at exactly one point.

**Step 3 (the start of the walk).** Let $s = (p, r)$ be the new segment, and assume (I1) and (I2) for $S$. Let $q_t = p + t(r - p)$ for small $t > 0$. For small enough $t$, $q_t$ is not in $P$ and lies on no segment of $S$ (only finitely many points of $s$ are on other segments, and $s$ meets them only at end points). So by (I2) the search for $q_t$ ends at the face $\Delta_0$ that contains the start of $s$. Line L1 simulates this search without choosing $t$: at an X-node with key $w \ne p$, $q_t < w$ if and only if $p < w$ for small $t$; at key $w = p$, $q_t > p$, so it goes right; at a Y-node with segment $t' = (a, b)$, the sign of $\mathrm{orient}(a, b, q_t)$ is that of $\mathrm{orient}(a, b, p)$ when it is not zero, and when it is zero, $p$ lies on $t'$, so $p$ is an end point of $t'$; as the node is reached only by points strictly inside the sheared $x$-range of $t'$ (Step 7), $p = a$, and then $\mathrm{orient}(a, b, q_t) = t \cdot \mathrm{orient}(a, b, r)$ has the sign of $\mathrm{orient}(a, b, r)$.

**Step 4 (the walk finds the crossed faces).** The faces that $s$ meets, in the order of $s$, are $\Delta_0, \ldots, \Delta_k$: $s$ is connected and passes from one face to the next only through a wall, since it crosses no segment. It leaves $\Delta_j$ through the right wall at $v = $ rightp$(\Delta_j)$, unless $v \ge r$, in which case $r$ lies in the closure of $\Delta_j$ and the walk stops. Since $v$ is not on $s$ (it is a point of $P$ strictly inside the $x$-range of $s$), either $v$ is above $s$, and then $s$ crosses the wall below $v$, into the right neighbor whose bottom is bottom$(\Delta_j)$, which is lr$(\Delta_j)$ by the slot rule; or $v$ is below $s$, and $s$ enters ur$(\Delta_j)$. These are the choices of line L3. The walk terminates because rightp strictly increases.

**Step 5 (the new faces).** In $T(S \cup \{s\})$, the faces that do not meet $s$ are unchanged, and also their fields, except neighbor pointers to faces that meet $s$: no wall outside $\bigcup_j \overline{\Delta_j}$ changes, because a wall changes only if $s$ cuts it, which happens only on the walls of the rightp values between the $\Delta_j$, or if it is the wall of a new end point. Inside $\bigcup_j \overline{\Delta_j}$:

- The wall of $v = $ rightp$(\Delta_j)$, $j < k$, is cut by $s$. If $v$ is above $s$, its part below $s$ disappears and its part above stays; so the parts of $\Delta_j$ and $\Delta_{j+1}$ above $s$ remain separated, and the parts below $s$ merge. If $v$ is below $s$, it is the opposite. This is line L4: the upper pieces form maximal runs between cuts at rightp values above $s$, with top equal to the top of the $\Delta_j$ of the run (constant along a run: crossing a wall below rightp into ur keeps the top, by the slot rule) and bottom $s$; the lower chain is symmetric.
- If $p$ is new, its wall goes from $p$ up to top$(\Delta_0)$ and down to bottom$(\Delta_0)$, and cuts off the part $A$ of $\Delta_0$ left of $p$, with the fields of $\Delta_0$ except rightp $= p$. If $p$ is not new, leftp$(\Delta_0) = p$: $p$ is on the closure of $\Delta_0$, so on its left wall, and the left wall contains only one point of $P$. Then no piece $A$ exists. The case of $r$ is symmetric. This is line L5.

By Step 2, the number of faces grows by $1 + [p \text{ new}] + [r \text{ new}]$, and the code creates (number of upper pieces) $+$ (number of lower pieces) $+ [p\text{ new}] + [r\text{ new}] = (k + 2) + [p\text{ new}] + [r\text{ new}]$ records while removing $k + 1$, which agrees.

**Step 6 (the neighbor pointers).** We check each rule against the slot conventions.

- *A cut of the upper chain at $v$ above $s$* between $U_i$ (left) and $U_{i+1}$ (right). The right side of $U_i$ is the part of the wall of $v$ from $s$ up to top$(U_i)$. Its bottom $s$ does not end at $v$. If top$(U_i)$ ends at $v$, $U_i$ has one right neighbor, below $v$, which is $U_{i+1}$, in slot lr; and ur$(\Delta_j)$ is none by the same rule for $\Delta_j$. Otherwise the part of the wall above $v$ borders the old upper-right neighbor ur$(\Delta_j)$, which has the same top and does not meet $s$, and the part below $v$ borders $U_{i+1}$. So $\mathrm{lr}(U_i) = U_{i+1}$ and $\mathrm{ur}(U_i) = \mathrm{ur}(\Delta_j)$, and the left pointer of ur$(\Delta_j)$ that pointed to $\Delta_j$ now points to $U_i$. The left side of $U_{i+1}$ is symmetric: $\mathrm{ll}(U_{i+1}) = U_i$ and $\mathrm{ul}(U_{i+1}) = \mathrm{ul}(\Delta_{j+1})$, which is none exactly when top$(\Delta_{j+1})$ starts at $v$.
- *A cut of the lower chain* is the mirror image, with $\mathrm{ur}(L_i) = L_{i+1}$, $\mathrm{lr}(L_i) = \mathrm{lr}(\Delta_j)$, $\mathrm{ul}(L_{i+1}) = L_i$ and $\mathrm{ll}(L_{i+1}) = \mathrm{ll}(\Delta_{j+1})$.
- *The left end.* $U_0$ has bottom $s$, which starts at $p$, so its one left neighbor is in ul; $L_0$ has top $s$, so its one left neighbor is in ll. If $p$ is new, both are $A$, and $A$ has two right neighbors ($p$ is the end point of no segment of $S$), $\mathrm{ur}(A) = U_0$ and $\mathrm{lr}(A) = L_0$, and takes the left neighbors of $\Delta_0$. If $p$ is not new, the part of the wall of $p$ above $p$ borders ul$(\Delta_0)$ (none if top$(\Delta_0)$ starts at $p$), and that part borders $U_0$ now; so $\mathrm{ul}(U_0) = \mathrm{ul}(\Delta_0)$, and $\mathrm{ll}(L_0) = \mathrm{ll}(\Delta_0)$.
- *The right end* is symmetric.
- In each case, the old neighbor's pointer to the replaced face is redirected to the new face (the functions that fix back pointers). A neighbor that is itself crossed by $s$ is never used in these rules: at a cut above $s$, the crossed neighbor of $\Delta_j$ is lr$(\Delta_j)$ and the rule uses ur$(\Delta_j)$, and so on.

This proves (I1) after the insertion.

**Step 7 (the DAG, I2).** Assign to each node $\nu$ of $D$ its region $R(\nu)$: the root has the whole plane; an X-node with key $w$ sends $R \cap \{q < w\}$ left and $R \cap \{q > w\}$ right; a Y-node with segment $t = (a, b)$ sends $R \cap \{\mathrm{orient}(a, b, q) > 0\}$ to the above child and $R \cap \{\mathrm{orient}(a, b, q) < 0\}$ to the below child. We show the invariant: the region of the leaf of a face $F$ is $F$ (as open sets; a node can have several parents, and then its region is the union over the paths, which is still $F$). Before any insertion, the one leaf has the whole plane, the one face. At an insertion, the leaf of $\Delta_j$ has region $\Delta_j$ by hypothesis and becomes an inner node. For $0 < j < k$, $\Delta_j$ lies strictly inside the sheared $x$-range of $s$, so the Y-node splits it into its parts above and below $s$, which are contained in the upper and lower pieces of $\Delta_j$; the region of a merged piece is the union of the parts from all the $\Delta_j$ of its run, which is the piece. For $j = 0$ with $p$ new, the X-node of $p$ first separates $A$ from the part right of $p$, and the Y-node then works on a region strictly inside the $x$-range of $s$; with $p$ not new, leftp$(\Delta_0) = p$ already. The case $j = k$ is symmetric. So each new leaf has its face as region, and other leaves are unchanged. This also shows the remark used in Step 3: a Y-node of $t$ has a region strictly inside the sheared $x$-range of $t$.

Now let $q$ be a query point. If $q \notin P$ and $q$ is on no segment, then $q$ lies on no wall (a wall meets the plane at one sheared $x$, which is that of a point of $P$, different from that of $q$), so $q$ is in a face $F$. At every node on its path, the test is strict, because $q$ is not an X-key and not on a segment, and the path follows the regions that contain $q$; it ends at a leaf whose region, a face, contains $q$, which is $F$. If $q$ is in $P$ or on a segment, it lies in no open face. Follow the path while the tests are strict: $q$ stays in the regions of the nodes, and it cannot reach a leaf, since leaf regions are open faces. So some test is not strict. At an X-node this means $q = w$, and line L8 returns the vertex. At a Y-node with segment $t$, $\mathrm{orient} = 0$ and $q$ is strictly inside the $x$-range of $t$ (it is in the open region of the node), so $q$ is in the relative interior of $t$. Since segments meet only at end points, this does not happen for $q \in P$, and for $q$ on a segment $s$ it gives $t = s$. Line L9 returns it. Each step of the query goes from a node to a child, and $D$ is acyclic, since new nodes only point to new leaves, so the query terminates. $\blacksquare$
