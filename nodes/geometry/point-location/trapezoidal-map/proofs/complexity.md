---
title: Expected size, query time and build time of the randomized trapezoidal map
proves: [space.map, space.dag, time.query, time.insert, time.build]
status: full
---

**Setting.** $S$ is a set of $n$ segments that meet only at shared end points, and $s_1, \ldots, s_n$ is a uniformly random order of $S$. $S_i = \{s_1, \ldots, s_i\}$, $T_i = T(S_i)$ its trapezoidal map, $D_i$ the DAG after $i$ insertions, and $m_i$ the number of distinct end points of $S_i$. All costs are on the real RAM, where an orientation test and a comparison of two points take $O(1)$. The probabilities are over the random order only; the input and the query point are fixed.

**Claim.** (a) $T_i$ has exactly $1 + i + m_i \le 3i + 1$ faces, in the worst case. (b) The expected number of trapezoids created in step $i$ is at most $12 + 4/i$, so the expected number ever created is at most $12n + 4H_n$, and the expected size of $D_n$ is $O(n)$. (c) For every fixed point $q$, the expected length of the search path of $q$ in $D_n$ is at most $12 H_n = O(\log n)$. (d) The expected total build time is $O(n \log n)$.

**Step 1 (count).** (a) is Step 2 of the correctness proof: each point $v$ of $P$ is the leftp of $\mathrm{starts}(v) + 1$ faces and one face has no leftp. Since $m_i \le 2i$, the count is at most $3i + 1$.

**Step 2 (defining segments).** Say that a face $\Delta$ of $T(R)$ *depends on* a segment $s \in R$ if $\Delta$ is not a face of $T(R \setminus \{s\})$. We show that $\Delta$ depends on at most four segments of $R$. Suppose $s$ is not top$(\Delta)$ nor bottom$(\Delta)$, and that leftp$(\Delta)$ and rightp$(\Delta)$ (when they exist) are still end points of segments of $R \setminus \{s\}$. Then $s$ does not meet $\Delta$ or its top and bottom sides, and the boundary of $\Delta$ consists of parts of top, bottom and the walls of leftp and rightp between them. All these parts are still present in $T(R \setminus \{s\})$: top and bottom are still segments, and a wall of a remaining end point can only become longer when a segment is removed. No new wall or segment enters $\Delta$, since removing $s$ only removes boundaries. So $\Delta$ is still a face. Hence $\Delta$ depends on $s$ only if $s$ is top$(\Delta)$, bottom$(\Delta)$, the only segment of $R$ with end point leftp$(\Delta)$, or the only segment of $R$ with end point rightp$(\Delta)$: at most four segments.

**Step 3 (backwards analysis of step $i$).** The faces created in step $i$ are exactly the faces of $T_i$ that are not faces of $T_{i-1}$, that is, the faces of $T_i$ that depend on $s_i$. Condition on the set $S_i$ (a fixed set $R$ of $i$ segments). Then $s_i$ is uniform in $R$, and

$$\mathbb{E}[\#\text{created in step } i \mid S_i = R] = \frac{1}{i} \sum_{s \in R} \#\{\Delta \in T(R) : \Delta \text{ depends on } s\} \le \frac{1}{i} \cdot 4 \cdot (3i + 1) = 12 + \frac{4}{i},$$

by exchanging the sums and Step 2 with (a). The bound does not depend on $R$, so it holds unconditionally. Summing over $i$ gives at most $12n + 4H_n$ trapezoids ever created.

**Step 4 (size of $D$).** Step $i$ removes the $k_i + 1$ crossed faces and creates $k_i + 2 + [p \text{ new}] + [r \text{ new}] \ge k_i + 2$ faces, so $k_i + 1$ is at most the number created. It adds one leaf per created face, $k_i + 1$ Y-nodes and at most two X-nodes (the old leaves are reused as inner nodes). So $D_n$ has $O(\text{number of created faces} + n)$ nodes, and its expected size is $O(n)$ by Step 3. This proves (b).

**Step 5 (query path).** Fix $q$. First let $q$ be a point that is not an end point and lies on no segment (a generic point). In the sheared plane, with $\varepsilon$ chosen after $q$, no point other than $v$ has the sheared $x$ of an end point $v$, so a generic point lies on no wall and is inside a face of every $T_i$. Let $\Delta_q(i)$ be the face of $T_i$ that contains $q$. In step $i$, the path of $q$ changes only if the leaf of $\Delta_q(i-1)$ is replaced, that is, if $\Delta_q(i-1)$ is crossed by $s_i$; then $\Delta_q(i)$ is a new face, and the path grows by at most three nodes, since the replacement subtree of a leaf has depth at most three (X$(p)$, X$(r)$, Y$(s_i)$). So the path length is $\sum_i 3 X_i$ with $X_i = 1$ if $\Delta_q(i)$ depends on $s_i$. Condition on $S_i = R$: the face $\Delta_q(i)$ is then fixed, depends on at most four segments of $R$, and $s_i$ is uniform in $R$, so $\Pr[X_i = 1] \le 4/i$. Hence the expected path length is at most $\sum_{i=1}^{n} 12/i = 12 H_n$.

If $q$ is an end point or lies on a segment, choose a generic point $q'$ so close to $q$ that every strict test made on the path of $q$ has the same outcome for $q'$ (finitely many tests, each an open condition). Then the path of $q$ is a prefix of the path of $q'$, up to the first test that is not strict for $q$, where the query of $q$ stops. So the bound $12 H_n$ holds for every fixed $q$. This proves (c).

**Step 6 (build time).** Step $i$ costs the search of line L1 plus $O(k_i + 1 + \#\text{created})$ for the walk, the new trapezoids and the DAG update, which is $O(\#\text{created in step } i)$ by Step 4. The search is the search of a point $q_t$ just right of $p_i$ on $s_i$ in $D_{i-1}$. Condition on $s_i = s$: then $q_t$ is a fixed point (for a fixed small $t$), and $s_1, \ldots, s_{i-1}$ is a uniformly random order of a uniformly random $(i-1)$-subset of $S \setminus \{s\}$. The argument of Step 5 applies to this process step by step (in step $j \le i - 1$, given the set of the first $j$ segments, the $j$-th is uniform in it), and gives an expected search path of at most $12 H_{i-1}$. Summing, the expected build time is $O(\sum_i (H_{i-1} + 1)) = O(n \log n)$. This proves (d). $\blacksquare$

**Remarks.** The bounds are expectations for a fixed query point. The bound that holds for all query points at once with high probability is in de Berg et al., Section 6.4, and is discussed on the page of [Seidel's algorithm](/seidel-point-location/). For a bad fixed order, the DAG can have $\Theta(n^2)$ size and a query can take $\Theta(n)$ steps; the upper bounds $O(n^2)$ and $O(n)$ follow from (a), since step $i$ crosses at most $3i + 1$ faces and adds at most $3$ nodes to any path.
