---
title: Kirkpatrick's hierarchy has logarithmic depth, linear size and a linear build
proves: [time.build, time.query, space.extra]
status: full
---

**Setting.** $T_0$ is a triangulation of a triangle $ABC$ with $n$ vertices, as in [the correctness proof](../correctness/), and $T_r$ is the triangulation after $r$ rounds, with $N_r$ vertices, $N_0 = n$. $R$ is the number of rounds, so $T_R = \{ABC\}$ and $N_R = 3$. The degree bound of line L2 is $8$. Costs are on the real RAM, with vertices and triangles stored as records with pointers, so a degree lookup, a mark and an unmark take $O(1)$ time.

**Claim.** (a) A triangulation of $ABC$ with $N$ vertices has $3N - 6$ edges and $2N - 5$ triangles, and at least $(N - 3)/2$ of its inner vertices have degree at most $8$. (b) Round $r$ removes at least $(N_r - 3)/18$ vertices, and $R \le \log_{18/17}(n - 3) + 1$ for $n \ge 4$. (c) The hierarchy has at most $36(n - 3) + R + 1$ triangle records, each with at most $8$ children, so it takes $O(n)$ space in the worst case. (d) The build takes $O(n)$ time in the worst case. (e) A query makes at most $8R + 1$ closed-triangle tests and takes $O(\log n)$ time in the worst case.

**Step 1 (counting, a).** The vertices and edges of a triangulation of $ABC$ form a connected plane graph whose bounded faces are the $T$ triangles and whose outer face is bounded by the three edges $AB$, $BC$, $CA$: no other vertex lies on the boundary of $ABC$, so each side is a single edge. Every edge borders two faces, so $2E = 3T + 3$. By [Euler's formula](/euler-formula/), $N - E + (T + 1) = 2$. Solving gives $E = 3N - 6$ and $T = 2N - 5$. The degrees sum to $2E = 6N - 12$. Each corner is adjacent to the other two, so the corners have degree at least $2$ and the inner degrees sum to at most $6N - 18$. By Step 1 of the correctness proof, every inner vertex has degree at least $3$. If $h$ inner vertices have degree at least $9$, then $9h + 3(N - 3 - h) \le 6N - 18$, so $h \le (N - 3)/2$, and at least $(N - 3) - h \ge (N - 3)/2$ inner vertices have degree at most $8$.

**Step 2 (rounds, b).** Let $L$ be the set of inner vertices of $T_r$ of degree at most $8$, and $I$ the set picked in line L2. A vertex of $L$ is skipped only if it is blocked, and a vertex is blocked only if it is picked or is a neighbor of a picked vertex. A picked vertex has at most $8$ neighbors, so $|L| \le 9|I|$, and by Step 1, $|I| \ge (N_r - 3)/18$. So $N_{r+1} - 3 = N_r - 3 - |I| \le \frac{17}{18}(N_r - 3)$, and by induction $N_r - 3 \le (17/18)^r (n - 3)$. Round $r$ runs only if $T_r$ has more than one triangle, that is, $N_r \ge 4$, so $1 \le (17/18)^r (n - 3)$ and $r \le \log_{18/17}(n - 3)$. The rounds are numbered $0, \ldots, R - 1$, so $R \le \log_{18/17}(n - 3) + 1$. For $n = 3$ there is no round.

**Step 3 (space, c).** A triangle that survives a round is the same record in both levels, so the number of records is at most $\sum_{r=0}^{R} |T_r| = \sum_{r=0}^{R} (2N_r - 5) = \sum_{r=0}^{R} \big(2(N_r - 3) + 1\big)$. By Step 2, $\sum_{r} (N_r - 3) \le (n - 3) \sum_{r \ge 0} (17/18)^r = 18(n - 3)$, so the number of records is at most $36(n - 3) + R + 1$. The children of a new triangle in the hole of $v$ are triangles of the star of $v$, so there are at most $\deg(v) \le 8$ of them. Each record stores three points, an index and at most $8$ pointers, so the space is $O(n)$ in the worst case. The vertex lists used during the build hold $O(N_r)$ entries at round $r$.

**Step 4 (build time, d).** Building the star lists of $T_0$ takes $O(n)$ time, since $T_0$ has $2n - 5$ triangles. In round $r$, filtering the vertex order and scanning it in line L2 take $O(N_{r-1})$ time for $r \ge 1$ and $O(n)$ for $r = 0$; a pick marks the corners of at most $8$ triangles. For each removed vertex $v$, with $d = \deg(v) \le 8$, building the link polygon takes $O(d)$, ear clipping takes $O(d^3)$ (at most $d$ clips, each scanning $d$ candidates against $d$ vertices), the overlap tests take $O(d^2)$, and updating the star lists of the at most $8$ neighbors takes $O(d)$. All of these are $O(1)$. A round removes at most $N_r$ vertices, so round $r$ costs $O(N_{r-1} + N_r)$, and the build costs $O(n + \sum_r N_r) = O(n + 18(n - 3) + 3(R + 1)) = O(n)$, using $R = O(\log n)$ from Step 2. This holds for any fixed order of the scan. The code sorts the vertices once, in $O(n \log n)$ time, so that the hierarchy does not depend on the input order; with the input order instead, the code is linear apart from hashing. Its star lists are Python sets, which take $O(1)$ expected time per operation; with vertex indices and arrays, as on the real RAM, the same operations take $O(1)$ worst-case time.

**Step 5 (query time, e).** Line L6 is one closed-triangle test. By Step 6 of the correctness proof, each step of the loop of lines L7 and L8 goes down at least one level, so there are at most $R$ steps, and each step tests at most the $8$ children of the current triangle. So a query makes at most $8R + 1$ tests, each of three orientation tests of $O(1)$ time, and line L9 makes at most six more comparisons. By Step 2, $R = O(\log n)$, so the query takes $O(\log n)$ time in the worst case.
