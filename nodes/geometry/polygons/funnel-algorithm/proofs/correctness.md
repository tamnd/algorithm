---
title: The funnel algorithm returns the shortest path and stops after a linear number of steps
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

We use the pseudocode of the page. $P$ is a simple polygon with a triangulation $T$, and $s, t$ are points of the closed polygon. For points $x, y$ of $P$, $\pi(x, y)$ is the shortest path from $x$ to $y$ in $P$ and $d(x, y)$ is its length. We use two standard facts about a simple polygon $Q$.

- **(A) Uniqueness.** Between two points of $Q$ there is exactly one shortest path. It is a polygonal path whose inner points are reflex vertices of $Q$.
- **(B) Taut paths are shortest.** A polygonal path in $Q$ is *taut* if no short piece of it around an inner point $p$ can be replaced by a shorter path in $Q$. This means that $p$ is a vertex of $Q$, and the path bends at $p$ around the vertex, with $Q$ locally on the outer side of the bend. In a simply connected domain all paths between two points are homotopic, and a taut path is the shortest path of its homotopy class (Hershberger and Snoeyink). So in $Q$, a taut path is the shortest path.

**Claim.** The loop of lines L6 to L9 runs $m + 2$ times, its inner loops run $O(m)$ times in total, and line L10 returns $\pi(s, t)$, with no repeated and no straight inner points.

**Step 1 (the sleeve).** If $s = t$ the answer $[s]$ is correct. Otherwise the dual graph of a triangulation of a simple polygon is a tree, so the triangles $T_0, \ldots, T_m$ of line L2 form its unique path from $T_s$ to $T_t$. Let $e_i = \ell_i r_i$ be the diagonal between $T_{i-1}$ and $T_i$. A diagonal splits $P$ into two simple polygons. Let $P_i$ be the closed part that contains $T_{i-1}$, and let $S_i = T_0 \cup \cdots \cup T_{i-1}$. The triangles of $P_i$ outside $S_i$ form pockets. Each pocket is attached to $S_i$ along one diagonal that is not a portal, because the dual tree has no cycles. A path from a point of $S_i$ to another point of $S_i$ that enters a pocket must leave it through the same diagonal. Replacing that excursion by the segment of the diagonal does not make it longer. The same holds for an excursion beyond $e_i$. By (A), $\pi(x, y)$ lies in $S_i$ for all $x, y \in S_i$. In particular every shortest path used below lies in the sleeve.

**Step 2 (the portals).** If $u \to w$ is the counterclockwise edge of $T_{i-1}$ that it shares with $T_i$, then $T_{i-1}$ lies to the left of $u \to w$. Crossing it from $T_{i-1}$ to $T_i$ means moving to the right of $u \to w$, so $w$ is on the walker's left. Hence line L3 lists $(\ell_i, r_i) = (w, u)$. For $i \ge 2$, the portals $e_{i-1}$ and $e_i$ are two sides of the triangle $T_{i-1}$, so they share exactly one endpoint. The third vertex of $T_{i-1}$ is the new endpoint. It is new on the left if $r_i = r_{i-1}$ and new on the right if $\ell_i = \ell_{i-1}$, as line L4 decides. So the events of line L4 list $\ell_1$, $r_1$, the new endpoint of each later portal and finally $t$, each with its side.

**Step 3 (the invariant).** For the chains $L = (a = u_0, u_1, \ldots, u_p)$ and $R = (a = w_0, w_1, \ldots, w_q)$, with the finished list $\textit{path}$, the invariant $\mathcal{I}_i$ after the events of portal $i$ states:

1. $\textit{path} \cdot L = \pi(s, \ell_i)$ and $\textit{path} \cdot R = \pi(s, r_i)$, where $\cdot$ joins lists. In particular $\textit{path} \cdot (a) = \pi(s, a)$, and $u_p = \ell_i$, $w_q = r_i$.
2. $L$ turns strictly left at $u_1, \ldots, u_{p-1}$, and $R$ turns strictly right at $w_1, \ldots, w_{q-1}$.
3. Let $F_i$ be the closed region bounded by $L$, the portal $e_i$ and $R$. It is a simple polygon, possibly with empty interior when $a$ lies on $e_i$, and it is contained in $S_i \cup e_i$. For every point $x$ of $e_i$, $\pi(s, x) = \pi(s, a) \cdot \pi_{F_i}(a, x)$, where $\pi_{F_i}(a, x)$ follows one of the chains from $a$ to some point $z$ and then runs straight from $z$ to $x$.

Part 3 follows from parts 1 and 2. Going around $F_i$ counterclockwise, from $a$ along $R$, along the portal and back along $L$, every inner chain point is a right turn and thus a reflex corner of $F_i$. So $F_i$ is a *funnel*: its only convex corners are $a$, $\ell_i$ and $r_i$. In such a polygon the shortest path from $a$ to a point $x$ of $e_i$ follows a chain to the last point $z$ that blocks the view and then goes straight to $x$. By Step 1 the shortest path from $s$ to $x$ stays in $S_i$, where every path to $e_i$ near $x$ passes the funnel, and it shares the part $\pi(s, a)$ with the paths to $\ell_i$ and $r_i$ by (A).

**Step 4 (the first portal).** Line L5 sets $L = R = (s)$ and $\textit{path}$ empty. The first two events push $\ell_1$ onto $L$ and $r_1$ onto $R$. Line L7 does nothing, as each chain has one point. For the second event, line L8 tests $\operatorname{orient}(s, \ell_1, r_1) > 0$, which fails, because $s \in T_0$ lies to the right of $\ell_1 \to r_1$ or on it. The segments $s \ell_1$ and $s r_1$ lie in the convex triangle $T_0$ and are the shortest paths, so $\mathcal{I}_1$ holds with the apex $s$. If $m = 0$, the only event is $t$, and $R = (s, t)$ is the segment $st$ in the triangle $T_0$, which is correct.

**Step 5 (a new point on the right).** Assume $\mathcal{I}_i$ and let $v$ be the new right endpoint, so $e_{i+1} = \ell_i v$ and $T_i = \ell_i r_i v$. The case of a new point on the left is the mirror image, and the tests with $\sigma = -1$ are the mirrored tests. The final event $t$ is treated in Step 6.

*The target.* Any path from $s$ to $v$ in $S_{i+1}$ crosses $e_i$, and the part after the last crossing point $x$ can be replaced by the segment $xv$, which lies in the convex triangle $T_i$. So $d(s, v) = \min_{x \in e_i} \bigl( d(s, x) + |xv| \bigr)$. By part 3 of $\mathcal{I}_i$, $\pi(s, v) = \pi(s, a) \cdot \pi_G(a, v)$, where $G = F_i \cup T_i$ is a simple polygon bounded by $R$, the edge $r_i v$, the edge $v \ell_i$ and $L$.

*Line L7.* While $q \ge 1$ and $\operatorname{orient}(w_{q-1}, w_q, v) \ge 0$, the point $v$ lies on or to the left of the line through the last edge of $R$. Then $w_q$ does not block the segment from $w_{q-1}$ to $v$ inside $G$. Either the segment passes $w_q$ on its inner side, or it passes through $w_q$ when the points are collinear. So $w_q$ is not a bend of $\pi_G(a, v)$ and is popped. The loop stops at the first $j$ with $j = 0$, or with $\operatorname{orient}(w_{j-1}, w_j, v) < 0$. In the second case the path $w_0, \ldots, w_j, v$ turns strictly right at every inner point, and the segment $w_j v$ lies in $G$. Its points beyond $e_i$ lie in $T_i$, and before $e_i$ they lie in $F_i$ by the concavity of $R$. Every bend is at a reflex corner of $G$ with $G$ on the outer side, so the path is taut. By (B) it is $\pi_G(a, v)$. The new chain $R' = (w_0, \ldots, w_j, v)$ satisfies part 2.

*Line L8.* If $R$ was reduced to the apex, the string from $a$ to $v$ may still be blocked by the left chain. If $p \ge 1$ and $\operatorname{orient}(u_0, u_1, v) > 0$, then $v$ lies strictly left of the line through the first left edge. Then the segment $u_0 v$ leaves $G$ across $L$ near $u_1$, so $\pi_G(a, v)$ must pass around $u_1$. Because $L$ turns left at $u_1$ and $v$ is beyond the portal, it actually starts with the segment $u_0 u_1$. So $\pi(s, v)$ starts with $\pi(s, u_1)$. Moving $u_0$ to $\textit{path}$ and making $u_1$ the apex keeps $\textit{path} \cdot L$ unchanged as a list, so part 1 still holds for $\ell_i$, and the test repeats from the new apex. The loop stops when $p = 0$ or $\operatorname{orient}(u_0, u_1, v) \le 0$. Then the segment from the apex $u_0$ to $v$ does not cross $L$ or $R$, and lies in $G$, and the path $\textit{path} \cdot (u_0, v)$ is taut. At $u_0$ it bends around the left chain, which turns left there. If the orientation is $0$, the segment passes straight through $u_1$, which is allowed, because paths may touch the boundary. So $\pi(s, v) = \textit{path} \cdot (u_0, v)$, and line L9 sets $R' = (u_0, v)$.

In both cases, after line L9, $\textit{path} \cdot R' = \pi(s, v)$ and $\textit{path} \cdot L = \pi(s, \ell_i) = \pi(s, \ell_{i+1})$, and the chains satisfy part 2. Part 3 follows as in Step 3, so $\mathcal{I}_{i+1}$ holds.

**Step 6 (the goal).** After the events of portal $m$, the point $t$ lies in $T_m$, which is on the far side of $e_m$ or on $e_m$ itself. If $t$ lies on $e_m$, then $\pi(s, t)$ is given by part 3 directly. In all cases the argument of Step 5 applies with $T_m$ in place of $T_i$ and $t$ in place of $v$. The convex triangle $T_m$ contains every segment from a point of $e_m$ to $t$. So after the last event $\textit{path} \cdot R = \pi(s, t)$, and line L10 returns it.

**Step 7 (the output form).** Every inner point of the returned list is either an old apex moved by line L8 or a point of $R$ kept by line L7. By the strict tests in lines L7 and L8, the path really bends at each of them, so no inner point is straight. A point can occur twice in a row only when $s$ coincides with $\ell_1$ or $r_1$, or $t$ with a point of the funnel. These repeats have length zero, and the implementation removes them.

**Step 8 (termination and time).** There are $m + 2$ events by Step 2, and each pushes one point in line L9. Each iteration of the loop in line L7 pops a point from a chain. Each iteration of the loop in line L8 removes a point from the front of a chain, moves it to the finished path and never puts it back. Each such point was put into a chain earlier, by line L9 or as one of the two copies of $s$ in line L5, so the total number of inner iterations is at most $m + 4$. Hence the loop terminates after $O(m)$ steps, and $m \le n - 3$. $\blacksquare$
