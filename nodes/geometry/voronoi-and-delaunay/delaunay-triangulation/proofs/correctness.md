title: The incremental Delaunay triangulation with a history DAG is correct
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full

**Claim.** Let $S'$ be the set of distinct points inserted so far. If $S'$ lies on one line, there is no triangle. Otherwise the live real triangles are counterclockwise and form a Delaunay triangulation of $S'$, LOCATE$(q)$ returns a live triangle whose region holds $q$, and `find_triangle(q)` returns a closed triangle that holds $q$ exactly when $q$ is in $\operatorname{conv} S'$. Every call ends. No general position is assumed.

**Notation.** As on the [Bowyer-Watson proof](/bowyer-watson-algorithm/), $|q|^2 = q_x^2 + q_y^2$, and for a counterclockwise real triangle $t$, $f_t$ is the affine function equal to $|\cdot|^2$ at the vertices of $t$, so $\mathrm{incircle}(t, q) > 0$ exactly when $|q|^2 < f_t(q)$. We use the facts (F1) and (F2) and Lemma 1 (neighbours) of that proof, and its invariants: (I1) the real triangles are counterclockwise and tile $\operatorname{conv} S'$ edge to edge with vertex set $S'$; (I2) no point of $S'$ is strictly inside the circle of a real triangle; (I3) each boundary edge $v \to u$ has one ghost $(u, v, G)$, and each directed edge of a triangle has its reverse in exactly one other triangle. Conflict is as defined on the page. For a point $z$ write $z' = z - o$, and $\mathrm{cr}(a', b') = a'_x b'_y - a'_y b'_x$. For points $b, a$ with $\mathrm{cr}(b', a') > 0$, let

$$W(b, a) = \{o + \lambda b' + \mu a' : \lambda, \mu \ge 0\}, \qquad R(b, a) = \{o + \lambda b' + \mu a' : \lambda, \mu \ge 0,\ \lambda + \mu > 1\},$$

the closed wedge from $o$ and the part of it strictly beyond the segment $[b, a]$; let $R(b, a) = \varnothing$ when $\mathrm{cr}(b', a') = 0$. $T(x, y, z)$ is the closed triangle.

## Part 1: an insertion is a Bowyer-Watson step

Fix one call of line L5 for a new point $q$, with (I1) to (I3) true before it, and let $K$ be the set of live triangles in conflict with $q$. The **Bowyer-Watson step** replaces $K$ by the fan of triangles $(x, y, q)$, one for each border edge $(x, y)$ of $K$ (an edge of a triangle of $K$ whose reverse is in a triangle not in $K$). The Bowyer-Watson proof shows: $K$ is not empty and is connected through shared edges (its Lemma 2), and after the step (I1), (I2) and (I3) hold for $S' \cup \{q\}$ (its Lemmas 5 and 7 and the [local Delaunay criterion](/local-delaunay-criterion/)).

**Lemma 1 (the conflict set is a tree).** Let $b$ be the number of border edges of $K$. Then $b = |K| + 2$, and the graph on $K$ whose links are the edges shared by two triangles of $K$ is a tree.

*Proof.* By (I1), (I3) and the counts on the page, a structure with $i$ points not on a line has $2i - 2 - h$ real triangles and $h$ ghosts, so $2i - 2$ triangles in all. The Bowyer-Watson step removes $|K|$ triangles and adds $b$, and goes from $2i - 2$ to $2(i + 1) - 2$ triangles, so $b = |K| + 2$. By (I3), every edge of a triangle of $K$ is in one or two triangles of $K$, so the number of shared edges is $(3|K| - b) / 2 = |K| - 1$. A connected graph with $|K|$ nodes and $|K| - 1$ links is a tree. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (a flip makes a convex pair).** Let $(x, y, q)$ and $(y, x, r)$ be counterclockwise real triangles with $\mathrm{incircle}(y, x, r, q) > 0$. Then the segment $qr$ crosses the open segment $xy$, the triangles $(q, x, r)$ and $(q, r, y)$ are counterclockwise, and $T(x, y, q) \cup T(y, x, r) = T(q, x, r) \cup T(q, r, y)$.

*Proof.* The points of the segment $qr$ other than $r$ are strictly inside the circle $C$ of $(y, x, r)$, because $q$ is strictly inside, $r$ is on $C$ and the disk is convex. The points $q$ and $r$ are strictly on opposite sides of the line $xy$, so the segment $qr$ meets this line at one point other than $r$, which is strictly inside $C$. The line $xy$ meets the open disk in the open segment $xy$. So $x, r, y, q$ is a strictly convex quadrilateral in counterclockwise order. Any three of its vertices in this cyclic order are counterclockwise, and each diagonal cuts it into two triangles. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the flip loop).** Let $C$ be the set of old triangles removed so far. After line L6 or L7, and after each pass of lines L8 to L11, the following hold.

- (J1) $C \subseteq K$ and $C$ is connected.
- (J2) The live triangles are the old triangles not in $C$ and, for each border edge $(x, y)$ of $C$, one triangle $(x, y, q)$. Each real one is counterclockwise.
- (J3) For each border edge $(x, y)$ of $C$, the edge $(x, y)$ is on the stack, or the old triangle that holds $(y, x)$ is not in $K$.

*Proof.* *Start, line L6, real $t$.* LOCATE gives a live triangle whose region holds $q$ (Part 2). If $t$ is real and $q$ is on no line of a side of $t$, then $q$ is strictly inside $t$, so $t \in K$ by (F1). $C = \{t\}$, the three new triangles are counterclockwise, and the three edges of $t$ are pushed.

*Start, line L6, ghost $t = (u, v, G)$.* The region of $t$ is inside $\mathrm{orient}(u, v, q) > 0$, so $t \in K$. The new triangles are $(u, v, q)$, which is counterclockwise, and the ghosts on $(v, G)$ and $(G, u)$. The three edges of $t$ are pushed.

*Start, line L7.* Here $t = (x, y, z)$ is real and $q$ is on the side $xy$, so strictly between $x$ and $y$, since $q$ is not a vertex. Then $t \in K$ by (F1). Let $t_2 = (y, x, r)$. If it is real, $t_2 \in K$ by (F1); if it is a ghost, $t_2 \in K$ by the segment rule. $C = \{t, t_2\}$ has the border edges $(y, z), (z, x), (x, r), (r, y)$, and these are the four new triangles and the four pushed edges. The real ones, such as $(x, q, z)$, are counterclockwise because $q$ is strictly inside a side of a counterclockwise triangle.

*A pass.* Line L8 pops $(x, y)$. An edge is pushed only as a border edge of $C$ with its triangle $(x, y, q)$. A border edge stops being one only when it is flipped, and then line L10 deletes $(x, y)$ from `edge` for ever, because new triangles contain $q$. So the test of line L8 passes exactly when $(x, y)$ is still a border edge, and then $t_1 = (x, y, q)$ and $t_2 = (y, x, r)$ is the old triangle across it, not in $C$. If $t_2 \notin K$, nothing changes and (J3) holds for $(x, y)$ for ever, since old triangles do not change. If $t_2 \in K$, let $C' = C \cup \{t_2\}$; it is connected and in $K$. By Lemma 1, $t_2$ shares no edge other than $xy$ with a triangle of $C$, else the tree would have a cycle. So the border of $C'$ is that of $C$ without $(x, y)$ and with $(x, r)$ and $(r, y)$, and line L10 makes the triangles $(x, r, q)$ and $(r, y, q)$ for them, and line L11 pushes them. For the orientation: if $t_1$ and $t_2$ are real, use Lemma 2. If $t_1$ is real and $t_2 = (y, x, G)$ is a ghost, then $\mathrm{orient}(y, x, q) < 0$, so $t_2$ is not in conflict and this case does not occur. If $t_1$ is a ghost, its base edge contains $G$, so $t_2$ is an old ghost $(x, r, G)$ or $(r, y, G)$. Ghosts at $q$ come from line L6 or line L7. After line L7, $q$ is inside a hull edge, so it is not strictly beyond the line of a hull edge, nor strictly inside another hull edge, and $t_2$ is not in conflict. After line L6, $q$ is in the region of a hull ghost, so strictly beyond the line of a hull edge and outside the old hull; so $t_2$ is in conflict only when $q$ is strictly beyond its real edge, and the new real triangle $(q, x, r)$ or $(q, r, y)$ is counterclockwise. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (end of the loop).** The loop of line L8 ends after at most $|K|$ flips, and then the live triangles are those of the Bowyer-Watson step.

*Proof.* Each flip adds one triangle of $K$ to $C$, so there are at most $|K|$ flips, and at most $4 + 2|K|$ pops. When the stack is empty, (J3) says that no border edge of $C$ has a triangle of $K$ on its other side. As $K$ is connected and $C \subseteq K$ is not empty, $C = K$. By (J2) the live triangles are the old ones not in $K$ and the fan over the border of $K$. $\square$

So after each insertion (I1) to (I3) hold again, and the live real triangles form a Delaunay triangulation of $S' \cup \{q\}$.

## Part 2: the history DAG

**Lemma 5 (the code computes these regions).** For a real node, the test of `_region_contains` is the closed triangle (the node is counterclockwise by Lemma 3). For a ghost node $(a, b, G)$ with $\mathrm{cr}(b', a') > 0$, the test is $R(b, a)$. For a ghost node with $a' = s b'$ for some $s > 0$, the test is empty.

*Proof.* The test is $\mathrm{orient}(a, b, z) > 0$, $\mathrm{cr}(b', z') \ge 0$ and $\mathrm{cr}(a', z') \le 0$. If $\mathrm{cr}(b', a') > 0$, write $z' = \lambda b' + \mu a'$ with $\lambda = \mathrm{cr}(z', a') / \mathrm{cr}(b', a')$ and $\mu = \mathrm{cr}(b', z') / \mathrm{cr}(b', a')$. The last two tests say $\lambda, \mu \ge 0$. Then $\mathrm{orient}(a, b, z)$ has the sign of $(\lambda + \mu - 1)\,\mathrm{cr}(b', a')$, so the first test says $\lambda + \mu > 1$. If $a' = s b'$ with $s > 0$, the last two tests give $\mathrm{cr}(b', z') = 0$, so $z$ is on the line through $o$, $a$ and $b$, and $\mathrm{orient}(a, b, z) = 0$. $\square$

**Lemma 6 (ghost nodes).** For every ghost node $(a, b, G)$, either $\mathrm{cr}(b', a') > 0$, or $a' = s b'$ for some $s > 0$. For a live ghost after an insertion, $\mathrm{cr}(b', a') > 0$.

*Proof.* After an insertion, a live ghost $(a, b, G)$ belongs to a boundary edge $b \to a$ of the hull (Part 1). The point $o$, the centroid of the first triangle, is strictly inside the hull, so $\mathrm{orient}(b, a, o) > 0$, which is $\mathrm{cr}(b', a') > 0$. This covers the three roots too. A new ghost is made in three ways.

- *Line L6 splits a ghost $(u, v, G)$* with $q \in R(v, u)$, so $q' = \lambda v' + \mu u'$ with $\lambda, \mu \ge 0$ and $\lambda + \mu > 1$. The new ghosts are $(q, v, G)$ and $(u, q, G)$, and $\mathrm{cr}(v', q') = \mu\,\mathrm{cr}(v', u') \ge 0$ and $\mathrm{cr}(q', u') = \lambda\,\mathrm{cr}(v', u') \ge 0$. If the first is zero then $\mu = 0$ and $q' = \lambda v'$ with $\lambda > 1$; the second case is the same.
- *Line L7 with a ghost $t_2 = (y, x, G)$.* The point $q$ is strictly inside the hull edge $x \to y$, so $q' = (1 - \theta) x' + \theta y'$ with $0 < \theta < 1$, and the new ghosts $(q, x, G)$ and $(y, q, G)$ have $\mathrm{cr}(x', q') = \theta\,\mathrm{cr}(x', y') > 0$ and $\mathrm{cr}(q', y') = (1 - \theta)\,\mathrm{cr}(x', y') > 0$.
- *Line L10 with a ghost $t_1 = (q, x, G)$* and the old live ghost $t_2 = (x, r, G)$, with $\mathrm{orient}(x, r, q) > 0$ (the other side is the mirror image). The new ghost is $(q, r, G)$. By induction $\mathrm{cr}(x', q') \ge 0$, and $\mathrm{cr}(r', x') > 0$. Write $q' = \lambda r' + \mu x'$, with $\lambda = \mathrm{cr}(q', x') / \mathrm{cr}(r', x') \le 0$ and $\mu = \mathrm{cr}(r', q') / \mathrm{cr}(r', x')$. If $\mathrm{cr}(r', q') \le 0$, then $\mu \le 0$ and $\lambda + \mu \le 0 < 1$. The line $rx$ is the set where $\lambda + \mu = 1$, and $o$ has $\lambda + \mu = 0$, so this puts $q$ on the closed side of the line $rx$ that holds $o$, against $\mathrm{orient}(x, r, q) > 0$. So $\mathrm{cr}(r', q') > 0$. $\square$

**Lemma 7 (wedges).** Let $b, m, a$ be points not on one line with $\mathrm{cr}(b', a') > 0$, $\mathrm{cr}(b', m') \ge 0$ and $\mathrm{cr}(m', a') \ge 0$. Then $R(b, a) \subseteq T(b, m, a) \cup R(b, m) \cup R(m, a)$ and $R(b, m) \cup R(m, a) \subseteq T(b, m, a) \cup R(b, a)$.

*Proof.* All these sets lie in $W(b, a) = W(b, m) \cup W(m, a)$ (a wedge of zero width is read as a ray) and do not contain $o$. Take a point $z$ of one of them and the ray $\rho$ from $o$ through $z$. If $\mathrm{cr}(b', m') = 0$, then $W(m, a) = W(b, a)$ holds $\rho$; so we may take $\rho$ in a wedge of positive width, say $W(b, m)$ (the other is the same with $a$ in place of $b$). On $\rho$, $R(b, a)$ is the open part beyond the point $P_1$ where $\rho$ crosses $[b, a]$, and $R(b, m)$ is the open part beyond the point $P_2$ where it crosses $[b, m]$. In $W(b, m)$, the triangle $T(b, m, a)$ is bounded by the sides $[b, a]$ and $[b, m]$, as its third side meets this wedge only at $m$; so $\rho \cap T(b, m, a)$ is the closed segment between $P_1$ and $P_2$. $R(m, a)$ meets $\rho$ only if $\rho$ goes through $m$, and then in the part beyond $m = P_2$. So on $\rho$, each side of each inclusion is a ray beyond $P_1$ or $P_2$, and the segment between them fills the gap. $\square$

**Lemma 8 (children cover their parent).** When a node is removed, its region is contained in the union of the regions of its children.

*Proof.* By Lemma 5, regions are closed triangles or the sets $R$. *Line L6, real $t$:* $q$ is strictly inside $t$, and the three closed triangles with apex $q$ cover $t$. *Line L6, ghost $(u, v, G)$:* the children are $T(v, q, u)$, $R(v, q)$ and $R(q, u)$, and $q$ is not on the line $uv$; Lemma 7 with $(b, m, a) = (v, q, u)$ gives the first inclusion. *Line L7:* a real triangle with $q$ on a side is the union of its two closed halves; for a ghost $t_2 = (y, x, G)$, a ray from $o$ in $W(x, q)$ or $W(q, y)$ crosses $[x, q]$ or $[q, y]$ where it crosses $[x, y]$, so $R(x, y) \subseteq R(x, q) \cup R(q, y)$. *Line L10, real pair:* Lemma 2. *Line L10, ghost pair* $(q, x, G)$ and $(x, r, G)$ with children $T(q, x, r)$ and $R(r, q)$: the points $r, x, q$ are not on one line, Lemma 6 gives the sign conditions, and Lemma 7 with $(b, m, a) = (r, x, q)$ gives the second inclusion. The mirror case is the same. $\square$

**Lemma 9 (LOCATE).** LOCATE$(q)$ ends and returns a live node whose region holds $q$.

*Proof.* The roots are the first triangle $abc$ and the ghosts with regions $R(a, b)$, $R(b, c)$, $R(c, a)$ for its counterclockwise sides. The three wedges from $o$ cover the plane, and each wedge is the closed triangle with apex $o$, which lies in $T(a, b, c)$, together with an $R$; so the roots cover the plane and line L1 succeeds. By Lemma 8, line L3 always finds a child. Children are made after their parents, so the walk ends, at a node without children, which is live. $\square$

**Proof of the claim.** While all points are on one line they wait and no triangle exists. When the first point $q$ off the line of the first two waiting points arrives (all waiting points are on this line), `_start` makes a counterclockwise first triangle from these two points and $q$, with three ghosts; (I1) to (I3) hold. The other waiting points are then inserted one by one. Each insertion keeps (I1) to (I3) by Lemmas 3 and 4 and needs a correct LOCATE, which is Lemma 9. A point already present returns False. For `find_triangle(q)`: a live real node holds $q$ in its closed triangle; a live ghost region $R(b, a)$ of a hull edge $b \to a$ is strictly beyond the line of that edge, so it does not meet the hull, and the hull is covered by the real triangles. So the answer is a real triangle exactly when $q \in \operatorname{conv} S'$. Every loop is finite by Lemmas 4 and 9. $\blacksquare$
