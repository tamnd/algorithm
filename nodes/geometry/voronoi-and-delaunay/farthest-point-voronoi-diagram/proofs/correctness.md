---
title: The farthest-point Voronoi diagram built by reversed flips is correct
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

**Claim.** Let $P$ be a set of $n \ge 1$ distinct sites in the plane, and let $H$ be the set of corners of its convex hull, $h = |H|$. Then FARTHEST-BUILD stops after at most $h(h - 3)/2$ flips, it returns exactly the vertices of the farthest-point Voronoi diagram, and for each pair $\{p, q\}$ whose cells $F_p$ and $F_q$ share a piece of positive length it records exactly that piece, with its ends. $\mathrm{farthest}(q)$ returns a site at the largest distance from $q$. For $h \ge 3$ the diagram is a tree with at most $h - 2$ vertices and $2h - 3$ edges.

No general position is assumed. For a point $x$, let $r_x = \max_{s \in P} |x - s|$ and let $Q_x$ be the set of sites at distance $r_x$ from $x$, the farthest sites of $x$. Then $x \in F_p$ exactly when $p \in Q_x$.

**Step 1 (only corners are farthest).** Fix $x$. The function $\phi(y) = |y - x|^2$ is strictly convex, so on a segment it is smaller at every inner point than at one of the two ends. Let $s \in Q_x$ and suppose $s$ is not a corner of the hull $K = \mathrm{conv}(P)$. Then $s = (1 - \lambda) a + \lambda b$ for two different points $a, b \in K$ and some $0 < \lambda < 1$, so $\phi(s) < \max(\phi(a), \phi(b))$. A convex function on the polygon $K$ is at most its largest value at the corners, so $\phi(s) < \max_{c \in H} \phi(c) \le r_x^2$, a contradiction. So $Q_x \subseteq H$ for every $x$. Hence the scan in $\mathrm{farthest}(q)$ over $H$ finds the largest distance over all of $P$, sites outside $H$ have empty cells, and the diagram of $P$ is the diagram of $H$.

Conversely, let $p \in H$. As $p$ is a corner, there is a unit vector $u$ with $u \cdot (p - s) > 0$ for all $s \in P \setminus \{p\}$. For $x = p - t u$, $|x - s|^2 = |p - s|^2 - 2 t \, u \cdot (p - s) + t^2 < t^2 = |x - p|^2$ as soon as $2 t \, u \cdot (p - s) > |p - s|^2$ for all $s \ne p$. So $x$ has $p$ as its only farthest site for all large $t$, and $F_p$ is nonempty and unbounded.

**Small cases.** If $h = 1$, then $n = 1$ and the single cell is the plane; the code returns no vertex and no edge. If $h = 2$, all sites lie on the segment between the two corners $a$ and $b$, by Step 1 every point has $a$ or $b$ as a farthest site, and the diagram is the bisector of $a$ and $b$, a line with no vertex; the code stores it with both ends at infinity. From now on $h \ge 3$. Then the corners are in strictly convex position: no three are on a line, and every quadrilateral of corners is convex.

**Lifting.** For a point $y = (y_1, y_2)$ write $\hat{y} = (y_1, y_2, |y|^2)$. For a circle with center $c$ and radius $\rho$, the affine function $\pi(y) = 2 c \cdot y - |c|^2 + \rho^2$ satisfies

$$|y|^2 - \pi(y) = |y - c|^2 - \rho^2,$$

so $y$ is inside the circle or on it exactly when $|y|^2 \le \pi(y)$. For a triangle $t = abc$ of corners, let $f_t$ be the affine function equal to $|y|^2$ at $a$, $b$ and $c$; it is $\pi$ for the circumcircle of $t$, because the three corners are on that circle. For counterclockwise $a, b, c$, $\mathrm{incircle}(a, b, c, d) \ge 0$ exactly when $|d|^2 \le f_t(d)$.

For a triangulation $T$ of the polygon $K$ with vertex set $H$, let $F_T$ be the function on $K$ that equals $f_t$ on each triangle $t$. It is continuous and equals $|s|^2$ at every corner $s$. Let $U(y) = \max \{\sum_i \lambda_i |s_i|^2 : \lambda_i \ge 0, \sum_i \lambda_i = 1, \sum_i \lambda_i s_i = y\}$ over convex combinations of corners, the upper hull of the lifted corners. It is concave and $U(s) = |s|^2$ at each corner, since a corner is a convex combination of corners only in the trivial way.

An inner edge $ab$ of $T$, with triangles $t = abc$ and $t' = bad$, is **good** if $|d|^2 \le f_t(d)$. The function $f_{t'} - f_t$ is affine and zero on the line $ab$, and $c$ and $d$ are on different sides of that line; $f_{t'}(d) - f_t(d) = |d|^2 - f_t(d)$ and $f_t(c) - f_{t'}(c) = |c|^2 - f_{t'}(c)$, so the condition is the same from both sides of the edge. Good means that $f_{t'} \le f_t$ on the side of $d$, that is, $F_T$ bends down across $ab$. Line L5 flips $ab$ exactly when it is not good.

**Step 2 (flips raise the surface).** Let line L6 flip the edge $ab$ with triangles $t = abc$ and $t' = bad$, where $|d|^2 > f_t(d)$. The four corners are in convex position, in the counterclockwise order $a, d, b, c$, so the flip is legal and the new triangles $adc$ and $dbc$ are counterclockwise. On the quadrilateral $R$ the old function is $\max(f_t, f_{t'})$, because $f_{t'} > f_t$ strictly on the side of $d$ and $f_{t'} < f_t$ strictly on the side of $c$; it is convex on $R$. A convex function is at most the affine function that interpolates it at the corners of a triangle, so the old function is at most the new one on $adc$ and on $dbc$. At the crossing point $y$ of $ab$ and $cd$ the old function restricted to the segment $cd$ has a strict bend, so it is strictly below the chord, which is the new value: the new function is larger near $y$. Outside $R$ nothing changes. So $\int_K F_T$ strictly grows with each flip.

Moreover, the removed edge $ab$ never comes back. After the flip, $F_T$ is strictly above the chord of $\hat{a}$ and $\hat{b}$ at $y$, and $F_T$ never goes down; if $ab$ were again an edge, $F_T$ would equal that chord on $ab$. So each of the $h(h - 3)/2$ diagonals of the polygon is removed at most once, and there are at most $h(h - 3)/2$ flips. The new edge $cd$ is good: on the segment $ab$, the new function equals $|a|^2$ and $|b|^2$ at the ends and is strictly above the chord at $y$, so it bends down across $cd$.

**Step 3 (the loop ends with a farthest-point Delaunay triangulation).** Invariant: every inner edge of $T$ that is not good is on the stack $S$. Line L3 puts all inner edges on $S$. A pop of an edge that no longer exists, or of a good edge, keeps the invariant. A flip changes the triangles next to $ab$ only: $ab$ disappears, $cd$ is good by Step 2, the four sides $ad$, $db$, $bc$ and $ca$ are pushed at line L6, and every other edge keeps its two triangles. Each loop step pops one edge and pushes four only after a flip, so by Step 2 the loop makes at most $(h - 3) + 4 \cdot h(h - 3)/2$ steps. When $S$ is empty, every inner edge is good.

Then $F_T$ is concave. Take a segment in $K$ that passes through no corner. Along it, $F_T$ is piecewise linear, and at each crossing of an inner edge the slope does not go up, because the edge is good. So $F_T$ is concave on such segments, and these segments are dense among all segments, so by continuity $F_T$ is concave on $K$.

A concave $F_T$ equals $U$. For a convex combination $y = \sum_i \lambda_i s_i$, concavity gives $F_T(y) \ge \sum_i \lambda_i |s_i|^2$, so $F_T \ge U$. On a triangle, $F_T(y)$ is itself such a combination, with the barycentric weights of $y$, so $F_T \le U$.

**Step 4 (vertices).** For a point $x$, let $\pi_x(y) = 2 x \cdot y - |x|^2 + r_x^2$, the function of the circle $C_x$ around $x$ through $Q_x$. All sites are in $C_x$, so $|s|^2 \le \pi_x(s)$ for every corner, with equality exactly for $s \in Q_x$. Hence $\pi_x \ge U$ on $K$, by applying the inequality to each convex combination. And $U = \pi_x$ exactly on $\mathrm{conv}(Q_x)$: if $U(y) = \sum_i \lambda_i |s_i|^2$ is attained by a combination and equals $\pi_x(y) = \sum_i \lambda_i \pi_x(s_i)$, then every $s_i$ with $\lambda_i > 0$ is in $Q_x$; conversely on $\mathrm{conv}(Q_x)$ we have $U \ge \pi_x$.

Every triangle $t$ of $T$ has all sites on or inside its circumcircle. Indeed, $F_T$ is concave by Step 3, so it lies below the affine function $f_t$ that agrees with it on the open triangle $t$, and $|s|^2 = F_T(s) \le f_t(s)$ for every corner $s$; the disc is convex and holds the corners, so it holds all sites. So the circumcenter $c(t)$ has its three corners as farthest sites, and $c(t)$ is a vertex.

Conversely, let $x$ be a vertex, so $|Q_x| \ge 3$. The points of $Q_x$ lie on a circle, so $\mathrm{conv}(Q_x)$ has interior points. The triangles of $T$ cover $K$, so some triangle $t$ meets the interior of $\mathrm{conv}(Q_x)$ in an open set $W$ inside the open triangle. On $t$ the affine function $\pi_x - f_t = \pi_x - U$ is $\ge 0$, and it is $0$ on $W$, so it is $0$ on the whole plane. So the corners $s$ of $t$ satisfy $|s|^2 = \pi_x(s)$, they are in $Q_x$, the circumcircle of $t$ is $C_x$, and $c(t) = x$. Line L7 thus lists exactly the vertices, each once.

**Step 5 (edges).** Fix two corners $p \ne q$, their midpoint $m$, and the unit vector $\nu$ perpendicular to $q - p$ that points to the left of the direction from $p$ to $q$. The bisector is $z(\tau) = m + \tau \nu$, and the circle around $z(\tau)$ through $p$ and $q$ has $\rho(\tau)^2 = |p - m|^2 + \tau^2$. For a site $w$,

$$|w - z(\tau)|^2 - \rho(\tau)^2 = |w - m|^2 - |p - m|^2 - 2 \tau \, \nu \cdot (w - m),$$

which is affine in $\tau$. If $w$ is strictly left of the line $pq$, then $\nu \cdot (w - m) > 0$ and $w$ is in the circle exactly for $\tau \ge \tau_w$, for one threshold $\tau_w$. If $w$ is strictly right of it, $w$ is in the circle exactly for $\tau \le \tau_w$. No other corner is on the line $pq$. So the set $E_{pq} = \{\tau : p, q \in Q_{z(\tau)}\}$, which is the closure of the common edge of $F_p$ and $F_q$ together with its ends, is the interval $[L, R]$ with $L = \max \tau_w$ over the left corners and $R = \min \tau_w$ over the right corners, an empty maximum being $-\infty$ and an empty minimum $+\infty$.

- *Inner edge of $T$.* Let $t = pqc$ be on its left and $t' = qpd$ on its right. The circle of $t$ holds all sites and has $c$ on it, so $c(t) = z(\tau_c)$, and $\tau_c \ge \tau_w$ for every left corner $w$: so $\tau_c = L$. In the same way $c(t')$ gives $R$. So $E_{pq}$ is the segment from $c(t)$ to $c(t')$, and it has positive length exactly when $c(t) \ne c(t')$. Line L8 records it in that case and skips it otherwise.
- *Hull edge.* Let $pq$ be a hull edge, directed counterclockwise, with $t = pqc$ on its left. All other corners are on the left, so $R = +\infty$ and, as before, $L$ is given by $c(t)$. So $E_{pq}$ is the ray from $c(t)$ in the direction $\nu$, which points from the edge toward the inside of the hull. Line L8 records it.
- *Not an edge of $T$.* Then $pq$ is a diagonal, its open segment lies in the interior of $K$ and contains no corner, so it meets the interior of some triangle $t$ of $T$. Suppose $E_{pq}$ has positive length. It contains only finitely many vertices, so it contains a point $x$ with $Q_x = \{p, q\}$. By Step 4, $U = \pi_x$ exactly on the segment $pq$. On $t$, $\pi_x - f_t = \pi_x - U \ge 0$ is affine and is $0$ at an interior point of $t$, so it is $0$ on $t$, and $U = \pi_x$ on all of $t$, which is not inside the segment $pq$. This contradiction shows that $p$ and $q$ share no edge.

Each undirected inner edge is recorded once, because the code records it only for the direction $u < v$, and a hull edge has only one direction in the map.

**Shape.** For each vertex $x$, the triangles of $T$ with circumcenter $x$ are those with all corners in $Q_x$, by Step 4, and they cover $\mathrm{conv}(Q_x)$: every point of the interior of $\mathrm{conv}(Q_x)$ that is inside a triangle $t$ has $c(t) = x$ by the argument of Step 4, these points are dense in $\mathrm{conv}(Q_x)$, and a finite union of triangles is closed. So the polygons $\mathrm{conv}(Q_x)$ cut $K$ along diagonals into convex faces, and by Step 5 the segments of the diagram join the vertices of two faces with a common side. A diagonal cuts a convex polygon into two parts, so removing any segment disconnects this graph, and the graph is connected because $K$ is: it is a tree. Its number of vertices is the number of faces, at most the $h - 2$ triangles of $T$, and it has $h$ rays and at most $h - 3$ segments, one for each inner edge of $T$, so at most $2h - 3$ edges. Both bounds are reached exactly when every face is a triangle, that is, when no four corners lie on a circle that holds all sites. $\blacksquare$
