---
title: Fortune's sweep returns the Voronoi diagram
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

**Claim.** Let $S$ be the set of distinct sites, $|S| = n \ge 1$, and assume general position (G):

- (G1) no two sites have the same $y$;
- (G2) no four sites lie on one circle;
- (G3) the *event heights*, which are the $y$ of the sites and the lowest $y$ of the circles through three sites not on a line, are pairwise distinct, except that a circle through three sites counts once.

Then FORTUNE terminates, its vertex list is the set of Voronoi vertices of $S$, and for each pair of sites whose Voronoi cells share an edge of positive length it records that pair once, with the two ends of the edge (None for an end at infinity), and it records no other pair.

**Notation.** The sweep line is $y = \ell$ and moves down. $S_\ell$ is the set of sites with $p_y > \ell$. For $p \in S_\ell$,
$$\beta_p(x) = \frac{(x - p_x)^2 + p_y^2 - \ell^2}{2 (p_y - \ell)}$$
is the height of the center of the circle through $p$ that touches the line $y = \ell$ at $(x, \ell)$ from above. Write $D_\ell(x, y)$ for the open disc with center $(x, y)$ and radius $y - \ell$; its circle touches the sweep line at $(x, \ell)$, which we call its *foot*. The function $\psi_p(x, y) = |(x, y) - p|^2 - (y - \ell)^2$ is strictly decreasing in $y$ and is zero at $y = \beta_p(x)$, so $p$ is strictly outside $D_\ell(x, y)$ exactly when $y < \beta_p(x)$. The *beach line* is $b_\ell(x) = \min_{p \in S_\ell} \beta_p(x)$. A site $p$ is *active* at $x$ if $\beta_p(x) = b_\ell(x)$. An *arc* is a maximal open interval of $x$ on which one site is the only active site; a *breakpoint* is a point where two or more sites are active. The *structure* at $\ell$ is the list of the sites of the arcs from left to right. For a circle $C$, its *bottom* is its lowest point $z$, and $\varphi_C(p)$ is the angle of $p \in C$ measured counterclockwise from $z$ around the center, in $(0, 2\pi)$ for $p \ne z$.

**Lemma 1 (parabolas).** Let $p \ne r$ be in $S_\ell$. Then $\beta_p - \beta_r$ has exactly two zeros, both simple. The two circles through $p$ and $r$ that touch the sweep line have their feet on opposite sides of the line $pr$.

*Proof.* $\beta_p - \beta_r$ is a quadratic polynomial in $x$ with leading coefficient $\frac{1}{2(p_y - \ell)} - \frac{1}{2(r_y - \ell)} \ne 0$ by (G1). Say $p_y < r_y$. Then $\beta_p$ has the larger leading coefficient, so it is larger for large $|x|$, and at $x = p_x$, $\beta_p(p_x) = (p_y + \ell)/2$, while $\beta_r(p_x)$ is the center height of a circle through $r$ that touches the line at $(p_x, \ell)$; $p$ is strictly inside that circle, since $p$ lies on its vertical diameter strictly between the foot $(p_x, \ell)$ and the top point, which is at least as high as $r_y > p_y$; so $\beta_p(p_x) < \beta_r(p_x)$. A quadratic that is negative at one point and positive for large $|x|$ has two simple zeros. For the feet: the line $pr$ is not horizontal, so it meets the sweep line at a point $m$, and $m \ne p, r$. The power of $m$ with respect to a circle through $p$ and $r$ is $|m - p| \cdot |m - r|$, and for a circle with foot $z$ it is also $|m - z|^2$. So both feet are at distance $(|m - p| |m - r|)^{1/2} > 0$ from $m$ on the sweep line, and as the circles differ, the feet differ and lie on the two sides of $m$, which are the two sides of the line $pr$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (empty discs).** For every $x$, the disc $D_\ell(x, b_\ell(x))$ holds no site. Its circle holds the sites active at $x$ and no other site of $S_\ell$. If two sites $p$ and $r$ are active at $x$, then the center $(x, b_\ell(x))$ is a point of the Voronoi diagram that is equidistant from $p$ and $r$ with no site closer. Conversely, if $q$ is a point equidistant from $p$ and $r$ with no site closer, and $\ell = q_y - |q - p| < \min(p_y, r_y)$, then $p$ and $r$ are both active at $q_x$ at height $\ell$ and $b_\ell(q_x) = q_y$.

*Proof.* A site of $S_\ell$ strictly inside the disc would have $\beta_p(x) < b_\ell(x)$. A site with $p_y \le \ell$ is not in the open disc, which lies strictly above the line. For the second part, the disc with center $q$ and radius $|q - p|$ touches the line $y = \ell$ at its bottom and holds no site, so every site $s \in S_\ell$ has $\beta_s(q_x) \ge q_y$, with equality for $p$ and $r$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (local picture).** Let $C$ be a circle through three sites with center $v$ and bottom height $\ell^*$, with no site in its open disc and no site at its bottom. Name its three sites $u_1, u_2, u_3$ so that $\varphi_C(u_1) > \varphi_C(u_2) > \varphi_C(u_3)$; then $\mathrm{orient}(u_1, u_2, u_3) < 0$. There are $\varepsilon > 0$ and an open interval $X \ni v_x$ such that for $\ell \in (\ell^*, \ell^* + \varepsilon)$ the structure over $X$ is $u_1 u_2 u_3$, and for $\ell \in (\ell^* - \varepsilon, \ell^*)$ it is $u_1 u_3$.

*Proof.* The points $u_1, u_2, u_3$ appear in this order when we walk clockwise around $C$ from the bottom, so the triangle is clockwise. At $(x, \ell) = (v_x, \ell^*)$ all three $\beta$ equal $v_y$, and every other site of $S_{\ell^*}$ has $\beta > v_y$ there; by continuity, for $(x, \ell)$ near $(v_x, \ell^*)$ only $u_1, u_2, u_3$ can be active. The slope is $\beta_p'(x) = (x - p_x)/(p_y - \ell)$. Write $p = v + \rho(\sin\varphi, -\cos\varphi)$ with $\varphi = \varphi_C(p)$; then at $(v_x, \ell^*)$ the slope is $-\sin\varphi / (1 - \cos\varphi) = -\cot(\varphi/2)$, which increases with $\varphi$ on $(0, 2\pi)$. So the slopes satisfy $\sigma_1 > \sigma_2 > \sigma_3$. Choose $X$ and $\varepsilon$ so that on $X \times (\ell^* - \varepsilon, \ell^* + \varepsilon)$ the derivatives of $\beta_{u_1} - \beta_{u_2}$, $\beta_{u_2} - \beta_{u_3}$ and $\beta_{u_1} - \beta_{u_3}$ stay positive, and each of these differences has exactly one zero in $X$ (the simple zero near $v_x$; Lemma 1 and continuity); call the zeros $x_{12}, x_{23}, x_{13}$. Then $u_1$ is below $u_2$ left of $x_{12}$ and above it to the right, and so on.

Let $\ell \ne \ell^*$ be in the range, and let $C'$ be the circle through $u_1, u_3$ with foot $(x_{13}, \ell)$. Then $u_2 \notin C'$, since the only circle through the three sites is $C$, whose bottom is at $\ell^*$. The bottom $z$ of $C$ and the site $u_2$ lie on opposite sides of the line $u_1 u_3$, as $z$ is not on the arc from $u_1$ to $u_3$ through $u_2$. The function $f_{C'} - f_C$, with $f_C(q) = 2 c \cdot q + \rho^2 - |c|^2$ for center $c$ and radius $\rho$, is affine and zero on the line $u_1 u_3$.

- If $\ell < \ell^*$, the foot of $C'$ is below the line $y = \ell^*$, so strictly outside $C$, and it is near $z$, on the side of $z$. There $f_{C'} - f_C > 0$, so on the side of $u_2$ it is negative, and $u_2$ is strictly outside $C'$: $\beta_{u_2}(x_{13}) > \beta_{u_1}(x_{13})$. If $u_2$ were the lowest at some $x \in X$, then $x_{12} < x < x_{23}$; but at $x_{13}$, $u_2$ above $u_1$ means $x_{13} < x_{12}$ and $u_2$ above $u_3$ means $x_{13} > x_{23}$, so $x_{23} < x_{12}$, a contradiction. So the structure over $X$ is $u_1 u_3$.
- If $\ell > \ell^*$, the point $z$ is below the sweep line, so strictly outside $C'$, and the same computation with the roles of $C$ and $C'$ exchanged shows that $u_2$ is strictly inside $C'$: $\beta_{u_2}(x_{13})$ is below the other two. Then $x_{12} < x_{13} < x_{23}$, and the structure over $X$ is $u_1 u_2 u_3$. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (changes).** Let $J$ be an open interval of heights that holds no site height. If the structure is not constant on $J$, then at some $\ell_0 \in J$ three sites are active at one $x$, and $\ell_0$ is then the bottom height of a circle through three sites with no site in its open disc. In particular the structure is constant on every open interval with no event height.

*Proof.* $S_\ell$ is constant on $J$. By Lemma 1 every breakpoint is a simple zero of some $\beta_p - \beta_r$, so it moves continuously with $\ell$, and the structure can change only when two neighbouring breakpoints meet or when a site starts or stops being active somewhere. At such a height $\ell_0$ and such a point $x$, if only one other site $r$ were active at $x$, the site $p$ would touch the envelope without crossing it, so $\beta_p - \beta_r$ would have a zero of even order, against Lemma 1. So three sites are active at $x$, and by Lemma 2 the disc $D_{\ell_0}(x, b_{\ell_0}(x))$ is empty, has them on its circle, and has its bottom at height $\ell_0$. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (breakpoint side).** If $p$ is the arc just left of a breakpoint and $r$ the arc just right of it, then the foot $z$ of the empty disc at that breakpoint is strictly right of the directed line $p \to r$.

*Proof.* Left of the breakpoint $\beta_p < \beta_r$, and the zero is simple, so $\beta_p' > \beta_r'$ at the breakpoint. The slope formula in the proof of Lemma 3 holds for any circle that touches the sweep line from above, so $\varphi(p) > \varphi(r)$ on the circle of the disc. Walking counterclockwise from $z$ we meet $r$ before $p$, so $(z, r, p)$ is in counterclockwise order and $(p, r, z)$ is in clockwise order: $z$ is strictly right of $p \to r$. $\square$

**Lemma 6 (clockwise triples).** Let $a, b, c$ be consecutive arcs, in this order, for all $\ell$ in an open interval $J$ with no event height, with $\mathrm{orient}(a, b, c) < 0$, and let $C$ be their circle, with bottom $z$ at height $\ell^*$ and center $v$. Then $\ell^* < \ell$ for all $\ell \in J$. If moreover the three arcs are consecutive for all $\ell \in (\ell^*, \sup J)$, then $C$ holds no site in its open disc, $z$ is strictly right of $a \to b$ and of $b \to c$, and the arc of $b$ shrinks to the point $v_x$ at height $\ell^*$ and is gone just below.

*Proof.* Take $\ell \in J$ and let $D$ be the empty disc of the breakpoint between $a$ and $b$, with foot $z_D$ strictly right of $a \to b$ (Lemma 5). The site $c$ is strictly right of $a \to b$ and on $C$. If $z_D$ were strictly outside $C$, the affine function $f_D - f_C$, which is zero on the line $ab$, would be positive at $z_D$ and so on the whole open right side of $a \to b$; then $f_D(c) > f_C(c) = |c|^2$ and $c$ would be strictly inside $D$, which is not possible. So $z_D$, which is on the sweep line, is in the closed disc of $C$, and $\ell \ge \ell^*$. Equality is excluded, as $\ell$ is not an event height.

For the second part, let $\ell$ decrease to $\ell^*$. The breakpoint moves continuously (Lemma 1), so the discs $D$ tend to a disc $D^*$ through $a$ and $b$ that touches the line $y = \ell^*$; a site strictly inside $D^*$ would be strictly inside $D$ for $\ell$ near $\ell^*$, so $D^*$ is empty. Its foot is on the right of $a \to b$ or on the line $ab$, and not on the line, because by Lemma 1 the feet are at positive distance from the point where the line $ab$ meets the sweep line. If $z$ were strictly left of $a \to b$, then $D^* \ne C$, the foot of $D^*$ would be a point of the line $y = \ell^*$ other than $z$, so strictly outside $C$, and as above $c$ would be strictly inside $D^*$. So $z$ is strictly right of $a \to b$; by Lemma 1 the two circles through $a, b$ that touch the line have feet on opposite sides of the line $ab$, so $D^* = C$. In the same way $z$ is strictly right of $b \to c$ and the disc of the breakpoint between $b$ and $c$ tends to $C$. So $C$ is empty, and both breakpoints tend to $v_x$. The right side of $a \to b$ meets $C$ in the arc from $a$ to $b$ that holds $c$, and the right side of $b \to c$ in the arc from $b$ to $c$ that holds $a$; their common part is the arc from $c$ to $a$ without $b$, which holds $z$. Walking clockwise from $z$ we meet $a$, $b$, $c$ in this order, and Lemma 3 with $u_2 = b$ shows that the arc of $b$ is gone just below $\ell^*$. $\square$

**The invariant.** The algorithm processes the valid events one by one. Let $\ell_e$ be the height of the event just processed (at the start, the height of the top site) and $\ell_n$ the height of the next valid event in the queue ($-\infty$ if there is none). The invariant is:

- (I1) for $\ell$ just below $\ell_e$, the list of arcs is the structure at $\ell$;
- (I2) the valid events in the queue are exactly a site event for each site with $y < \ell_e$, and, for each three consecutive arcs $a, b, c$ of the list with $\mathrm{orient}(a, b, c) < 0$, one circle event at the bottom of their circle, attached to $b$;
- (I3) each breakpoint of the list has a trace that starts at the point of the Voronoi diagram where this breakpoint appeared.

Under (G1) the top site is alone on its row, so at the start the list is its single arc, all other sites are in the queue, and there is no triple. This is the invariant at the start.

**Lemma 7 (no hidden change).** Under the invariant, the structure is the same for all $\ell \in (\ell_n, \ell_e)$, and every valid circle event lies strictly below $\ell_e$.

*Proof.* The interval holds no site height, by (I2). Suppose the structure is not constant on it. By Lemma 4 the heights in the interval where three sites are active at one $x$ are bottoms of circles through three sites, so there are finitely many; let $\ell_0$ be the highest. By Lemma 4 again the structure is constant on $(\ell_0, \ell_e)$, so by (I1) it is the list there. The empty circle at $\ell_0$ has no site at its bottom by (G3), so by Lemma 3 its sites $u_1, u_2, u_3$ are consecutive arcs just above $\ell_0$, with $\mathrm{orient}(u_1, u_2, u_3) < 0$. By (I2) the queue holds a valid circle event at height $\ell_0 > \ell_n$, against the choice of $\ell_n$. For the second statement, a valid circle event belongs to a clockwise triple of consecutive arcs on an interval just below $\ell_e$, and Lemma 6 puts its height strictly below that interval. $\square$

So the queue, which takes the highest event first and compares heights exactly, processes the events in the order of their heights.

**Lemma 8 (site event).** The invariant holds again after lines L4 to L8 process the site event of $s$, at height $\ell_e' = s_y$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 7 the list is the structure on $(\ell_e', \ell_e)$. By (G3), $\ell_e'$ is not the bottom of a circle through three sites, so no three sites are active at one $x$ at $\ell_e'$, and the breakpoints at $\ell_e'$ are the limits of those above. At $x = s_x$ only one site $p$ is active; if two were, their disc would have $s$ at its bottom. Line L5 compares $s_x$ with the breakpoints at height $s_y$ exactly and stops at the arc of $p$. For $\ell$ just below $s_y$, $\beta_s(s_x) = (s_y + \ell)/2$ is below $\beta_p(s_x)$, and $\beta_s$ is very large away from $s_x$; by Lemma 1, $\beta_s - \beta_p$ has exactly two zeros, both near $s_x$, and the other sites stay above near $s_x$. So the arc of $p$ splits into $p, s, p$, which is what line L7 does. The new breakpoints start at $(s_x, \beta_p(s_x))$, the center of the empty disc through $p$ with foot $s$, which is a point of the Voronoi edge of $p$ and $s$; line L7 starts two twin traces there. The triples whose sites change are the ones with middle arc $p$ (left part), $s$, and $p$ (right part). Line L6 invalidates the old event of $p$; the triple $(p, s, p)$ is on a line and gets no event; line L8 checks the two others. All other triples keep their sites and their events. $\square$

**Lemma 9 (circle event).** The invariant holds again after lines L9 to L13 process a valid circle event of the triple $a, b, c$ at height $\ell^*$, and the point $v$ of line L10 is a Voronoi vertex.

*Proof.* By (I2) and Lemma 7 the triple is consecutive and clockwise on $(\ell^*, \ell_e)$, and it has been consecutive since its event was made. By Lemma 6 its circle $C$ is empty and the arc of $b$ vanishes at $v_x$ at height $\ell^*$; $v$ is equidistant from $a, b, c$ with no site closer, so it is a Voronoi vertex. By (G2) and (G3), $C$ is the only circle with three sites and bottom at $\ell^*$, so no other change happens at $\ell^*$ (Lemma 4), and the structure just below is the list without $b$, which line L12 makes. The traces of the breakpoints between $a, b$ and between $b, c$ end at $v$ (line L10), and the new breakpoint between $a$ and $c$ starts at $v$ (line L11). The triples that change are the ones with middle arc $a$ and with middle arc $c$; line L12 drops their old events and line L13 checks the new ones. $\square$

**Every vertex.** Let $v$ be a Voronoi vertex. By (G2) its empty circle holds exactly three sites, and by (G3) none at its bottom. By Lemma 3 these three sites are consecutive arcs just above the bottom height, in clockwise order, so by (I1) and (I2) their circle event is valid when the sweep reaches that height, and line L10 records $v$. With Lemma 9, the vertex list is the set of Voronoi vertices.

**Every edge.** Let $e$ be the Voronoi edge of $p$ and $r$: the centers of empty discs through $p$ and $r$, a segment, a ray or a line in their bisector. For a point $q$ of the bisector let $\tau(q) = q_y - |q - p|$, the bottom height of the circle with center $q$ through $p$. Along the bisector, $\tau$ is strictly concave, tends to $-\infty$ at both ends, and takes its maximum $\min(p_y, r_y)$ at the one point $q_0$ whose circle has the lower of $p$ and $r$ at its bottom. By Lemma 2, at each height $\ell < \min(p_y, r_y)$ the breakpoints between $p$ and $r$ are exactly the points $q \in e$ with $\tau(q) = \ell$; there are at most two, one on each side of $q_0$. By Lemmas 8 and 9, a breakpoint appears only at a site event, at the point $q_0$ of its pair, as two twin traces that move away from $q_0$, or at a circle event, at a Voronoi vertex; and it disappears only at a circle event, at a Voronoi vertex $w$. Such a $w$ is an end of $e$: the third site $u$ at $w$ is not on the line $pr$, so the bisector of $p$ and $u$ crosses the bisector of $p$ and $r$ at $w$, and beyond $w$ the site $u$ is strictly closer than $p$. So if $q_0 \in e$, the pair has two twin traces from $q_0$, each running to the end of $e$ on its side or to infinity; if $q_0 \notin e$, it has one trace from the end of $e$ nearer to $q_0$ to the other end or to infinity. Line L14 joins twins and reads off the ends, so the pair is recorded once with the two ends of $e$. A pair with no Voronoi edge of positive length has no breakpoint at any height, by Lemma 2 and (G2), and is not recorded.

**Termination.** Each site gives one site event. Each processed circle event removes one arc and each site event adds two, so at most $2n$ circle events are processed; each processed event pushes at most two events. So the loop of line L3 ends after $O(n)$ steps. $\blacksquare$

*Degenerate input.* The code also handles sites with equal $y$ (the top row starts as several arcs separated by vertical edges from infinity, and line L5 has an exact test for two sites at the same height), four or more cocircular sites (several circle events at one point; line L14 drops edges of length zero) and sites at the bottom of a circle (ties in the queue are broken by $x$ and then by kind). These cases are checked against a brute-force diagram on grids and on 25 points of one circle, but they are not covered by the proof above.
