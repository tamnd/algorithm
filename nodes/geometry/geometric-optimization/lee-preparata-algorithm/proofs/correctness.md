---
title: The Lee-Preparata algorithm returns the shortest path between two points of a simple polygon
proves: [correctness, termination]
status: full
---

We use the pseudocode of the page. $P$ is a closed simple polygon, $s, t \in P$, and the triangulation of line L1 has triangles of positive area. For a closed region $Q$ and points $x, y \in Q$, $d_Q(x, y)$ is the length of a shortest path from $x$ to $y$ in $Q$, and $\pi_Q(x, y)$ is that path when it is unique; we drop the index for $Q = P$. A *region* below is always a closed set bounded by a closed polygonal curve, and it is simply connected. We use two standard facts about such a region $Q$.

- **(F1) Uniqueness.** Between two points of $Q$ there is exactly one shortest path. It is a polygonal path whose bends are at reflex corners of $Q$. Hence every subpath of a shortest path is the shortest path between its ends, and two shortest paths from the same point $x$ that pass through a common point $y$ agree from $x$ to $y$.
- **(F2) Taut paths are shortest.** Call a polygonal path in $Q$ *taut* if at each bend $b$ the path turns around a reflex corner of $Q$ at $b$, that is, the outside of $Q$ near $b$ lies on the inner side of the turn. A taut path is locally shortest, and in a simply connected region a locally shortest path is the shortest path (Hershberger and Snoeyink 1994: it is the shortest path of its homotopy class, and all paths with the same ends are homotopic in $Q$).

**Step 1 (the sleeve).** If $s = t$, line L1 returns $[s]$, which is correct. Otherwise the dual graph of a triangulation of a simple polygon is a tree: removing a diagonal splits $P$ into two parts, so no cycle of triangles exists. Hence line L4 finds the unique chain $T_0, \ldots, T_m$ from $T_s$ to $T_t$. Let $S_j = T_0 \cup \cdots \cup T_{j-1}$ for $1 \le j \le m + 1$. Each component of the closure of $P \setminus S_j$ is a part of $P$ attached to $S_j$ along one diagonal $g$ only, again because the dual graph is a tree. A path between two points of $S_j$ that enters such a part through $g$ must leave it through $g$. Replacing the excursion by the piece of the segment $g$ between the entry and the exit does not make the path longer. So $d(x, y) = d_{S_j}(x, y)$ for $x, y \in S_j$, and by (F1) $\pi(x, y)$ lies in $S_j$. $S_j$ is itself a simple polygon (triangles glued along a path of the dual tree), so (F1) and (F2) apply in it.

**Step 2 (the portals and the events).** Let $e_i = \ell_i r_i$ be the diagonal of $T_{i-1}$ and $T_i$. $T_{i-1}$ is counterclockwise, so it lies to the left of its edge $u \to w$. Crossing from $T_{i-1}$ into $T_i$ moves to the right of $u \to w$, and a walker moving that way has $w$ on the left. So line L5 sets $(\ell_i, r_i) = (w, u)$ correctly. For $i \ge 2$, $e_{i-1}$ and $e_i$ are two sides of $T_{i-1}$, so they share one endpoint, and the third vertex of $T_{i-1}$ is the new endpoint, on the left if $r_i = r_{i-1}$ and on the right if $\ell_i = \ell_{i-1}$. So line L6 lists $\ell_1$, $r_1$, each later new endpoint with its side, and $t$.

**Step 3 (the invariant).** Write $L = (a = u_0, u_1, \ldots, u_p)$ and $R = (a = w_0, w_1, \ldots, w_q)$, and let $F_i$ be the closed region bounded by $L$, $e_i$ and $R$ (its interior may be empty). After the events of portal $i$, the invariant $\mathcal{I}_i$ is:

1. $\textit{done} \cdot L = \pi(s, \ell_i)$ and $\textit{done} \cdot R = \pi(s, r_i)$, where $\cdot$ joins lists; so $\textit{done} \cdot (a) = \pi(s, a)$, $u_p = \ell_i$ and $w_q = r_i$.
2. $L$ turns strictly left at $u_1, \ldots, u_{p-1}$ and $R$ turns strictly right at $w_1, \ldots, w_{q-1}$.
3. $F_i \subseteq S_{i+1}$, and $d(s, x) = d(s, a) + d_{F_i}(a, x)$ for every point $x$ of $e_i$.

By part 2, walking around $F_i$ counterclockwise (out along $R$, across $e_i$, back along $L$), every inner chain point is a right turn, so it is a reflex corner of $F_i$. A simple polygon has total turning $2\pi$, and $F_i$ has at most three convex corners, $a$, $\ell_i$ and $r_i$, each with turning less than $\pi$. So the reflex corners of $F_i$ turn by less than $\pi$ in total: each chain is a convex chain whose direction changes by less than $\pi$. In such a chain, all its points lie weakly on its outer side of the line through any of its edges.

**Step 4 (the first portal).** Lines L7 to L11 on the events $\ell_1$ and $r_1$ give $L = (s, \ell_1)$ and $R = (s, r_1)$. Line L10 for the event $r_1$ tests $\operatorname{orient}(s, \ell_1, r_1) > 0$, which is false because $s \in T_0$ lies to the right of $\ell_1 \to r_1$ or on it. The segments $s\ell_1$ and $sr_1$ lie in the convex triangle $T_0$, so they are the shortest paths. $F_1$ is the triangle $s \ell_1 r_1$, which lies in $T_0$, and it satisfies part 3, because for $x \in e_1$ the segment $sx$ lies in $F_1$. So $\mathcal{I}_1$ holds. If $m = 0$, the only event is $t$, and $R$ becomes $(s, t)$: the segment $st$ in the convex triangle $T_0$ is the shortest path.

**Step 5 (a new endpoint on the right).** Assume $\mathcal{I}_i$ and let $v$ be the new right endpoint, so $T_i = \ell_i r_i v$ and $e_{i+1} = \ell_i v$. The left case is the mirror image, which is what the sign $\sigma$ does. Let $G = F_i \cup T_i$, the region bounded by $R$, the edge $r_i v$, the edge $v \ell_i$ and $L$.

*(a) Reduction to $G$.* A shortest path from $s$ to a point $y \in T_i$ lies in $S_{i+1}$ by Step 1. If $y \notin e_i$ it crosses $e_i$; let $x$ be its last point on $e_i$. Its part after $x$ can be replaced by the segment $xy$, which lies in the convex $T_i$. With part 3,

$$d(s, y) = \min_{x \in e_i} \bigl(d(s, x) + |xy|\bigr) = d(s, a) + \min_{x \in e_i} \bigl(d_{F_i}(a, x) + |xy|\bigr) \ge d(s, a) + d_G(a, y).$$

Conversely $\pi(s, a) \cdot \pi_G(a, y)$ is a path in $P$, so equality holds, and by (F1) $\pi(s, y) = \pi(s, a) \cdot \pi_G(a, y)$.

*(b) The shape of $\pi_G(a, v)$.* By (F1) in $G$, $\pi_G(a, v)$ bends only at reflex corners of $G$, which are inner points of $L$ or of $R$. Suppose it bends at a point $w_k$ of $R$. Its part from $a$ to $w_k$ is $\pi_G(a, w_k)$ by (F1). By part 1, the part of $R$ from $a$ to $w_k$ is a subpath of $\pi(s, r_i)$, so it is $\pi(a, w_k)$, and it lies in $F_i \subseteq G$, so it is also $\pi_G(a, w_k)$. Hence $\pi_G(a, v)$ follows $R$ from $a$ to $w_k$ exactly; the same holds for a bend on $L$. A path cannot follow both chains, because they share only $a$. So $\pi_G(a, v)$ is $C_R(j) = (w_0, \ldots, w_j, v)$ for some $j \ge 0$, or $C_L(k) = (u_0, \ldots, u_k, v)$ for some $k \ge 1$, and its last segment lies in $G$.

*(c) The algorithm builds a taut candidate.* Line L9 pops $w_q, w_{q-1}, \ldots$ while $\operatorname{orient}(w_{k-1}, w_k, v) \ge 0$, that is, while $v$ lies on the inner side of the line through the last edge, or on it. Then the string from $w_{k-1}$ to $v$ does not wrap around $w_k$.

- If it stops at $w_j$ with $j \ge 1$, then $\operatorname{orient}(w_{j-1}, w_j, v) < 0$: the candidate $C_R(j)$ turns strictly right at $w_j$, as $R$ does at its earlier points, so it turns around reflex corners of $G$ at every bend. Its last segment starts at $w_j$ into $G$: $v$ is weakly left of $w_j w_{j+1}$ (that edge was popped, or $w_j = r_i$ and $w_j v$ is an edge of $T_i$). The popped points $w_{j+1}, \ldots, w_q$ lie weakly right of the segment $w_j v$, because $v$ is weakly left of each popped edge and the chain turns right by less than $\pi$ (Step 3). $L$ lies on the other side: $L$ and the popped part of $R$ are joined only through $a$ and across $e_i$, and the segment $w_j v$ starts on $R$ and crosses $e_i$ before entering $T_i$. So the segment lies in $G$, and $C_R(j)$ is a taut path in $G$.
- If $R$ shrinks to the apex, line L10 moves the apex along $L$ while $\operatorname{orient}(u_{h-1}, u_h, v) > 0$, that is, while $v$ lies strictly on the outer side of the line through the next edge of $L$, so that the segment from $u_{h-1}$ to $v$ would leave $G$ across $L$ near $u_h$. It stops at $u_k$ when $k = p$ or $\operatorname{orient}(u_k, u_{k+1}, v) \le 0$. The candidate $C_L(k)$ turns strictly left at $u_1, \ldots, u_k$: at $u_h$ with $h < k$ because $L$ does, and at $u_k$ because the test at $u_k$ passed. By the same argument with the roles of the chains exchanged, its last segment lies in $G$; when the orientation is 0 the segment passes straight through $u_{k+1}$, which a path may do. If $k = 0$ the candidate is the segment $a v$.

In both cases the candidate is taut in $G$, so by (F2) it is $\pi_G(a, v)$, and by (a) $\textit{done} \cdot (\text{the candidate}) = \pi(s, v)$. Line L10 moved $u_0, \ldots, u_{k-1}$ into $\textit{done}$, and line L11 sets $R = (w_0, \ldots, w_j, v)$ or $R = (u_k, v)$ with apex $u_k$. In both cases $\textit{done} \cdot R = \pi(s, v)$ and $\textit{done} \cdot L$ is unchanged as a list, so it is still $\pi(s, \ell_{i+1})$ with $\ell_{i+1} = \ell_i$. This is part 1 of $\mathcal{I}_{i+1}$, and part 2 holds because every kept bend is strict.

*(d) Part 3 for the new funnel.* $F_{i+1}$ is bounded by the two new chains and $e_{i+1}$; it lies in $G \subseteq S_{i+2}$. For $x \in e_{i+1}$, (a) gives $d(s, x) = d(s, a) + d_G(a, x)$. By (F1) in $G$, $\pi_G(a, x)$ cannot cross $\pi_G(a, \ell_i)$ or $\pi_G(a, v)$: if it met one of them at a point other than $a$, it would agree with it up to that point. And it cannot leave across $e_{i+1}$ and come back, because the segment of $e_{i+1}$ between the two crossings is shorter. So $\pi_G(a, x)$ lies in the region bounded by the two paths and $e_{i+1}$. The two paths share the part from $a$ to the new apex $a'$, so $\pi_G(a, x)$ runs along that part and then lies in $F_{i+1}$. Hence $d(s, x) = d(s, a') + d_{F_{i+1}}(a', x)$, which is part 3.

**Step 6 (the goal).** After the events of portal $m$, $t \in T_m$. The argument of Step 5 (a) to (c) with $T_m$ in place of $T_i$ and $t$ in place of $v$ applies, because it used only that $v$ lies in the convex triangle beyond $e_i$, so the segment from any point of $e_m$ to $t$ lies in $T_m$. So after the last event, $\textit{done} \cdot R = \pi(s, t)$, which line L12 returns. If $t$ lies on $e_m$, the same holds with the segment of length 0.

**Step 7 (the output).** Every inner point of the output was kept by a strict test in line L9 or moved by a strict test in line L10, so the path turns there, and it is a reflex corner of the polygon. A point can repeat only when $s$ or $t$ coincides with a chain point, and repeats are removed.

**Step 8 (termination).** There are $m + 2$ events by Step 2 when $m \ge 1$, and each pushes one point in line L11. Each iteration of the loop in line L9 pops a point, and each iteration of the loop in line L10 removes the front point of a chain for good. Every removed point was pushed by line L11 or is one of the two copies of $s$ from line L7, so the inner loops run at most $m + 4$ times in total. With $m \le n - 3$, lines L6 to L12 take $O(n)$ steps. $\blacksquare$
