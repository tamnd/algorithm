---
title: ASTRAL finds the best quartet tree in its search space, and is consistent under the multispecies coalescent
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

Let $X$ be the taxon set, $G_1, \dots, G_k$ binary unrooted gene trees on $X$, and $\mathcal{C}$ the set of allowed clusters: all singletons, $X$, and both sides of every bipartition in $\mathcal{X}$. So $\mathcal{C}$ is closed under complement, apart from $X$ and the empty set. For a tree $T$ and a node $u$ of degree $3$, the three components of $T - u$ give a tripartition $\pi_T(u)$ of $X$.

For a tripartition $\pi = (A_1, A_2, A_3)$ and a quartet $\{a, b, c, d\}$, say that $\pi$ *separates* the quartet as $ab|cd$ if $a$ and $b$ lie in two different parts and $c$ and $d$ both lie in the third. Let $w(\pi)$ be the number of pairs $(i, q)$ such that $\pi$ separates $q$ as $ab|cd$ and $G_i$ induces $ab|cd$.

**Lemma 1 (two anchors).** Let $T$ be an unrooted binary tree and $q = \{a, b, c, d\}$ with induced topology $ab|cd$. Then exactly two internal nodes $u$ of $T$ have $\pi_T(u)$ separating $q$, and both separate it as the topology $T$ induces, once as $ab|cd$ and once as $cd|ab$.

*Proof.* Let $P$ be the path in $T$ that joins the path from $a$ to $b$ with the path from $c$ to $d$. Since $T$ induces $ab|cd$, these two paths are disjoint and $P$ has at least one edge. It has two ends $u_1$, where the paths to $a$ and to $b$ part, and $u_2$, where the paths to $c$ and to $d$ part. At $u_1$, $a$ and $b$ lie in two different components and $c$, $d$ in the third, so $\pi_T(u_1)$ separates $q$ as $ab|cd$; likewise $\pi_T(u_2)$ separates it as $cd|ab$. Conversely, if $\pi_T(u)$ separates $q$ as $xy|zt$, then the paths from $u$ to $x$ and to $y$ leave $u$ by different edges and the paths to $z$ and $t$ leave by the third edge. So $T$ induces $xy|zt$, and $u$ is the point where the paths to $x$ and $y$ part, which is $u_1$ or $u_2$. $\square$

**Corollary.** For an unrooted binary tree $T$, $\sum_{u \text{ internal}} w(\pi_T(u)) = 2 \, \mathrm{score}(T)$.

*Proof.* Each pair $(i, q)$ with $G_i$ and $T$ inducing the same topology on $q$ is counted at the two anchors of Lemma 1. A pair where they differ is not counted: at any $u$ that separates $q$, the topology $T$ induces is the one that $\pi_T(u)$ separates, and $G_i$ does not induce it. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (the weight formula).** The sum in the weight formula equals $w(\pi)$.

*Proof.* Apply Lemma 1 to a gene tree $G_i$. A pair $(i, q)$ is counted in $w(\pi)$ when $G_i$ induces $ab|cd$ and $\pi$ separates $q$ as $ab|cd$. Among the two anchors of $q$ in $G_i$, let $v$ be the one where $c, d$ lie in the same gene part $P_r$ and $a, b$ in the other two; the other anchor has $c$ and $d$ in different gene parts. Let $c, d$ lie in species part $A_k$. The term for $(v, k, r)$ counts the unordered pairs $\{c, d\}$ in $A_k \cap P_r$, times the unordered pairs $\{a, b\}$ with one taxon in each of the other two species parts and one in each of the other two gene parts, which is $I_{ip} I_{jq} + I_{iq} I_{jp}$. So each counted pair $(i, q)$ appears in exactly one term with multiplicity one, and every quadruple counted by a term is such a pair. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the recurrence).** For a cluster $A \in \mathcal{C}$ with $|A| \ge 2$, let $\mathcal{R}(A)$ be the set of rooted binary trees on $A$ all of whose clusters lie in $\mathcal{C}$. For $R \in \mathcal{R}(A)$, let $\phi(R) = \sum_v w(A_1(v) \mid A_2(v) \mid X \setminus A(v))$ over the internal nodes $v$ of $R$, where $A(v)$ is the cluster of $v$ and $A_1(v)$, $A_2(v)$ are those of its children. Then lines L2 and L3 compute $V(A) = \max_{R \in \mathcal{R}(A)} \phi(R)$, or leave $V(A)$ undefined if $\mathcal{R}(A)$ is empty, and line L4 records a split of an optimal $R$.

*Proof.* By induction on $|A|$. For a singleton, the only tree has no internal node, and $V = 0$. For larger $A$, a tree $R \in \mathcal{R}(A)$ is a root with cluster $A$ and two subtrees $R_1 \in \mathcal{R}(A_1)$ and $R_2 \in \mathcal{R}(A_2)$, with $A = A_1 \sqcup A_2$ and $A_1, A_2 \in \mathcal{C}$. The term of each node $v$ of $R_1$ depends only on $A(v)$ and its children, which are the same in $R_1$ and in $R$, so $\phi(R) = \phi(R_1) + \phi(R_2) + w(A_1 \mid A_2 \mid X \setminus A)$. The parts can be chosen independently, so the maximum over $R$ is the maximum over splits of $V(A_1) + V(A_2) + w(A_1 \mid A_2 \mid X \setminus A)$, which is line L3. Line L1 processes clusters by size, so $V(A_1)$ and $V(A_2)$ are known. $\square$

**Theorem 1 (exactness).** Line L5 builds a tree $T^*$ whose bipartitions all lie in $\mathcal{X}$, and $\mathrm{score}(T^*) = V(X)/2 = \max \{\mathrm{score}(T) : T \text{ unrooted binary with all bipartitions in } \mathcal{X}\}$.

*Proof.* Let $T$ be unrooted binary with all bipartitions in $\mathcal{X}$. Root it on any edge to get $R$; every cluster of $R$ is a side of a bipartition of $T$ or a singleton or $X$, so $R \in \mathcal{R}(X)$. Conversely, unrooting a tree in $\mathcal{R}(X)$ gives a tree whose bipartitions are the splits $(A, X \setminus A)$ of its non-root clusters, which lie in $\mathcal{X}$. The internal nodes of $R$ are the internal nodes of $T$ plus the root. For a non-root node $v$, $(A_1(v), A_2(v), X \setminus A(v))$ is $\pi_T(v)$. For the root, the third part is empty. A tripartition with an empty part separates no quartet, because separation puts $a$ and $b$ in two different parts and $c, d$ in the third, so all three parts must be nonempty. So $\phi(R) = \sum_u w(\pi_T(u)) = 2\, \mathrm{score}(T)$ by the Corollary. By Lemma 3, $V(X)$ is the maximum of $\phi$ over $\mathcal{R}(X)$, and the traceback of line L5 gives a tree that attains it. $\blacksquare$

**Theorem 2 (statistical consistency).** Let the gene trees be independent draws from the multispecies coalescent on a binary species tree $S$, and let $\mathcal{X}_k$ be the bipartitions of $G_1, \dots, G_k$. Then the probability that ASTRAL with $\mathcal{X}_k$ returns the unrooted topology of $S$ tends to $1$ as $k \to \infty$.

*Proof.* For a quartet $q$ and a topology $t$ on it, let $p_q(t)$ be the probability that a gene tree induces $t$. By Allman, Degnan and Rhodes (2011), $p_q(S|_q) > p_q(t)$ for both other topologies $t$. For a tree $T$, $\mathrm{score}(T)/k \to \sum_q p_q(T|_q)$ almost surely by the law of large numbers. This sum is maximized by $S$, since each term is, and any tree $T$ with a different topology differs from $S$ on some quartet, where its term is strictly smaller. Since there are finitely many trees, with probability tending to $1$ the score of $S$ is strictly larger than that of every other tree. Each bipartition of $S$ is displayed by a gene tree that equals $S$, which has positive probability, so with probability tending to $1$ all bipartitions of $S$ lie in $\mathcal{X}_k$ and $S$ is in the search space. On the intersection of these two events, Theorem 1 gives that ASTRAL returns $S$. $\blacksquare$
