---
title: Correctness of the ancestral recombination graph and of Hudson's algorithm
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

We prove three things about the procedure SIMULATE of the main page and the graph it builds: the material on each edge is exactly the set of positions at which that edge is a branch of a marginal tree, each marginal tree is a binary tree on the $n$ samples that both readings (by paths and by material) give, and the marginal tree at each fixed position has the law of the [Kingman coalescent](/kingman-coalescent/). We also show that the procedure stops with probability 1. The sequence is the interval $[0, 1)$, and $\mathrm{mat}(a)$ is the material of a lineage $a$, a finite union of half-open intervals.

**Definitions.** For a position $x$ and a sample $s$, the *path* of $s$ at $x$ starts at $s$ and goes up one edge at a time: from a sample or a coalescence node along its only parent edge, and from a recombination node with breakpoint $b$ along the left edge if $x < b$ and along the right edge if $x \ge b$. The path stops at a node with no parent on the chosen edge. The *MRCA at* $x$ is the lowest node that is on all $n$ paths, if there is one. Say that an edge $a$ is *on the tree at* $x$ if some path at $x$ uses $a$ and $a$ is below the MRCA at $x$.

**Invariant.** At each moment of SIMULATE, for each position $x$:

1. if the MRCA at $x$ is not yet made, the paths at $x$ end at the live lineages $a$ with $x \in \mathrm{mat}(a)$, and there are at least two of them;
2. if it is made, no live lineage has $x$ in its material;
3. for every edge $a$ of the graph, $x \in \mathrm{mat}(a)$ if and only if $a$ is on the tree at $x$ (taking the MRCA at $x$ as not yet made if it does not exist yet).

*Proof.* At the start, line L1 gives $n \ge 2$ lineages with material $[0, 1)$ and no other edges. Each path at $x$ is one sample, and parts 1 to 3 hold.

*A recombination step* (lines L6 and L7) closes a live lineage $a$ and makes two new ones, $a_\ell$ with $\mathrm{mat}(a) \cap [0, x_0)$ and $a_r$ with $\mathrm{mat}(a) \cap [x_0, 1)$, for the breakpoint $x_0$. A path at $x$ that ended at $a$ now goes on to $a_\ell$ if $x < x_0$ and to $a_r$ if $x \ge x_0$. This is the one new lineage whose material holds $x$, if $x \in \mathrm{mat}(a)$. So the number of live lineages that carry $x$ does not change, and parts 1 to 3 hold again.

*A coalescence step* (lines L8 to L11) closes $a$ and $b$ and makes $w$. Fix $x$. If $x$ is in neither material, nothing changes at $x$. If $x$ is in exactly one, say $\mathrm{mat}(a)$, the paths that ended at $a$ go on to $w$, $x \in \mathrm{mat}(w)$, and the count of carriers stays the same. If $x$ is in both, the paths of both meet at $w$, and the count of carriers drops by one. If some other live lineage still carries $x$, then $x \notin \mathrm{done}$ (line L9), so $x \in \mathrm{mat}(w)$, and at least two carriers remain. If no other lineage carries $x$, then by part 1 all paths at $x$ now end at $w$, so $w$ is the MRCA at $x$. Line L9 puts $x$ in $\mathrm{done}$ and line L10 removes it from $\mathrm{mat}(w)$, which gives part 2 for $x$, and part 3 holds because the edge above $w$ is not below the MRCA. Line L11 drops $w$ only when its material is empty, and by part 1 no unresolved path ends there. $\square$

**The marginal tree.** Fix $x$ after the procedure stops. By part 2 and termination, the MRCA at $x$ exists. Two paths at $x$ that meet stay together, because each node has one chosen parent edge for $x$. So the union of the paths up to the MRCA is a tree whose leaves are the $n$ samples. A recombination node has one child edge and lies on a path only as a node of degree 2, so it is suppressed. A coalescence node $v$ has two child edges, and it is a branching node of the tree exactly when both child edges are used by paths at $x$. Each coalescence step joins two paths at most once, so the tree is binary, with $n - 1$ internal nodes and $2n - 1$ clusters. This is the reading of LOCAL-TREE by paths. By part 3, a child edge of $v$ is used by a path at $x$ below the MRCA if and only if $x$ lies in its material. So the branching nodes are the coalescence nodes whose two child edges both carry $x$, which is the reading by material. The two readings give the same clusters. $\square$

**The marginal law.** Fix $x$, and let $k_x(t)$ be the number of live lineages that carry $x$ at time $t$. A recombination step does not change $k_x$, by the proof of the invariant. Every pair of live lineages coalesces at rate 1, because line L4 waits an exponential time of total rate $C + R$ and line L5 picks a coalescence with probability $C/(C+R)$, then line L8 picks a uniform pair. So each pair of carriers of $x$ coalesces at rate 1, and the coalescences among carriers happen at total rate $k_x(k_x - 1)/2$, independently of the events that do not touch them. Each such coalescence lowers $k_x$ by one and merges a uniform pair of the carriers. This is the Kingman coalescent of the $n$ samples, so the marginal tree at $x$ is a Kingman tree. In particular its height has mean $2(1 - 1/n)$ and its total length has mean $2 \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} 1/i$.

Dropping the lineages without material does not change this law. In the model where they are kept, a coalescence between a lineage and an empty one gives a lineage with the same material, and an event of an empty lineage does not touch any material. So, seen on the lineages that carry material, the two processes have the same rates. $\square$

**Termination.** Couple SIMULATE with the process of Griffiths that keeps every lineage and lets each recombine at rate $\rho/2$ and each pair coalesce at rate 1. Since each span is at most 1, the recombination rate of a lineage in SIMULATE is at most $\rho/2$, and the coupling keeps the live lineages of SIMULATE among those of the larger process. The number of lineages of the larger process is a birth and death chain with birth rate $k\rho/2$ and death rate $k(k-1)/2$. The death rate grows faster than the birth rate, so the chain reaches 1 in finite time with probability 1. At that time no position can have two carriers in SIMULATE, so by part 1 every position is resolved, and the procedure has stopped. $\square$
