We use the model, the sets $\mathcal{V}(i, j)$, $\mathcal{W}_1(i, j)$ and $\mathcal{W}(i, j)$ and their costs from the correctness proof of the [Zuker algorithm](/zuker-algorithm/). For a set $\mathcal{S}$ of partial structures with costs, write $\Sigma\, \mathcal{S} = \sum_{Q \in \mathcal{S}} z^{\mathrm{cost}(Q)}$, with $\Sigma\, \emptyset = 0$.

**Lemma 1 (inside).** After lines L1 to L8, $Q^b[i, j] = \Sigma\, \mathcal{V}(i, j)$, $Q^{M1}[i, j] = \Sigma\, \mathcal{W}_1(i, j)$, $Q^M[i, j] = \Sigma\, \mathcal{W}(i, j)$, and $Q[j]$ is the sum of $z^{E(P)}$ over the allowed structures $P$ of $s_1 \cdots s_j$. In particular $Q[n] = Z$.

*Proof.* The Zuker decomposition lemma states, for each set, a partition into disjoint cases, and in each case a bijection between the members and the tuples of parts, with the cost of a member equal to a fixed loop term plus the costs of its parts. So the sum of $z^{\mathrm{cost}}$ over a case is the fixed factor $z^{\text{loop term}}$ times the product of the sums over the parts, and the sum over a set is the sum over its cases. Lines L3 to L7 compute exactly these sums of products: line L3 the hairpin case, line L4 the cases with one inner pair, line L5 the multiloop cases with the last branch at $u$, line L6 the two cases of $\mathcal{W}_1$, and line L7 the cases of $\mathcal{W}$, where the empty part before $u$ has weight $z^{c (u - i)}$. The induction on the span is the same as for the Zuker tables, with the same order of evaluation. For the exterior loop, the prefix of length $j$ has $j$ unpaired, or $j$ paired with a unique $k$; the exterior loop has energy 0, so line L8 is the sum over these disjoint cases. $\square$

We now view the inside pass as a circuit. Each inside value $X$ is a node, and line L$t$ writes $X$ as a sum of terms, each a constant times a product of other inside values. Unfold the circuit from $Q[n]$: a *derivation* is a tree that starts at $Q[n]$ and picks, at each node, one term of its sum and the nodes of that term as children. The weight of a derivation is the product of the constants of the chosen terms (the leaves have weight 1, or the constant $z^{H}$ of a hairpin).

**Lemma 2 (derivations).** There is a bijection between the allowed structures $P$ of $s$ and the derivations from $Q[n]$, and the weight of the derivation of $P$ is $z^{E(P)}$. The node $Q^b[i, j]$ occurs in the derivation of $P$ exactly when $(i, j) \in P$, and then exactly once.

*Proof.* The decomposition of Lemma 1 is a unique case analysis at each node, so each structure gives one derivation, and each derivation gives back one structure by the converse parts of the Zuker lemma. The weights multiply in the same way as the costs add. A node $Q^b[i, j]$ is created only when the case at its parent pairs $i$ with $j$: in line L8 (an exterior pair), in line L4 (the inner pair of an internal loop) and in line L6 (the first pair of a branch). Conversely, each pair of $P$ is created by exactly one of these steps. Two nodes $Q^b[i, j]$ in one derivation would be the pair $(i, j)$ twice, which a structure does not have. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (outside).** After lines L9 to L14, each outside value $\hat X$ equals the partial derivative $\partial Z / \partial X$, where $Z$ is seen as a function of the inside value $X$ with all values computed before $X$ held fixed and all values computed after $X$ recomputed from it.

*Proof.* This is the chain rule in reverse mode. Line L$t$ defines a value $Y$ as a polynomial in earlier values. If every value after $X$ in the order of the inside pass has its final outside value $\hat Y$, then $\partial Z / \partial X = \sum_{Y} \hat Y \cdot \partial Y / \partial X$, where $Y$ runs over the values whose formulas use $X$. Each line of L10 to L14 adds, for one formula $Y = \sum_t c_t \prod_r X_{t, r}$, the term $\hat Y \cdot c_t \prod_{r \ne r'} X_{t, r}$ to $\hat X_{t, r'}$, which is $\hat Y \cdot \partial Y / \partial X_{t, r'}$. It remains to see that $\hat Y$ is final when it is used. The values are processed in the reverse of the inside order: the exterior values $Q[n], \ldots, Q[1]$ first (line L10), then the intervals by decreasing span, and within one interval $Q^M$, $Q^{M1}$, $Q^b$ (lines L12 to L14). A value $Y$ receives contributions only from formulas of values computed after it in the inside order, and all of those are processed before $Y$. $\square$

**Theorem.** Line L15 gives $p[i, j] = \frac{1}{Z} \sum_{P \ni (i, j)} z^{E(P)}$ for all $i < j$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2, $Z$ is the sum of the weights of the derivations, and $Z$ is a polynomial in the node $X = Q^b[i, j]$ in which each derivation contributes a monomial of degree 1 in $X$ if $(i, j)$ is in its structure and of degree 0 if not. Write $Z = A + X \cdot B$, where $A$ sums the derivations without the node and $X \cdot B$ the derivations with it. Then $X \cdot B$ is the sum of $z^{E(P)}$ over the structures with $(i, j)$, and $\partial Z / \partial X = B$. By Lemma 3, $\hat Q^b[i, j] = B$, so $Q^b[i, j] \cdot \hat Q^b[i, j] / Z$ is the probability of the pair. $\blacksquare$

**Sampling.** The stochastic traceback picks a term of a node with probability equal to its share of the node's value. The probability of a derivation is the product of these shares. In the product, the value of each child cancels the value it contributes as a factor to its parent's term, so the product is the weight of the derivation divided by $Q[n]$. By Lemma 2, each structure $P$ is drawn with probability $z^{E(P)} / Z$.
