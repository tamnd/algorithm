---
title: T-Coffee returns a valid alignment, each merge maximizes the average library weight, and two sequences get the best library score
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

A *valid alignment* of sequences $(S_r)_{r \in R}$ is a set of rows of equal length over $\Sigma \cup \{-\}$ such that row $r$ with its gaps removed equals $S_r$, and no column consists of gaps only. Let $X$ have columns $X_1, \ldots, X_m$ and $Y$ have columns $Y_1, \ldots, Y_n$. A *block alignment* of $X$ and $Y$ is a sequence of columns, each of the form $(X_i, Y_j)$, $(X_i, \text{gaps})$ or $(\text{gaps}, Y_j)$, in which $X_1, \ldots, X_m$ appear once each in order, and so do $Y_1, \ldots, Y_n$. Its *merge score* is the sum of $\mathrm{col}(i, j)$ over its columns of the first kind; columns of the other two kinds score 0, because the gap penalties are zero.

We prove: (1) GROUP-ALIGN returns a block alignment of $X$ and $Y$ with the largest merge score; (2) the output of T-COFFEE is a valid alignment of the input; (3) for $k = 2$, the output has the largest library score $\sum W'(x, y)$, over residue pairs $x, y$ in a common column, among all alignments of the two sequences.

**Claim (1).** Let $F^*[i][j]$ be the largest merge score of a block alignment of $X_1, \ldots, X_i$ and $Y_1, \ldots, Y_j$. Any such block alignment with $i + j > 0$ ends with a column of one of the three kinds, and removing it leaves a block alignment of the prefixes $(i-1, j-1)$, $(i-1, j)$ or $(i, j-1)$, respectively. Conversely, adding such a column to a block alignment of the shorter prefixes gives one of the longer prefixes. The merge score is a sum over columns, so

$$F^*[i][j] = \max\big(F^*[i-1][j-1] + \mathrm{col}(i, j),\ F^*[i-1][j],\ F^*[i][j-1]\big),$$

where a term is left out when its index is negative, and $F^*[0][0] = 0$. On the boundary, the only block alignment of $i$ columns of $X$ with no column of $Y$ uses columns of the second kind only, with score 0, so $F^*[i][0] = 0$, and likewise $F^*[0][j] = 0$. These are line L8 and its boundary values, so by induction on $i + j$, $F = F^*$. The traceback starts at $(m, n)$, and at each cell it takes a step whose term equals $F[i][j]$; such a step exists by the recurrence. It emits the matching column and moves to the predecessor cell. The emitted columns, read backward, form a block alignment of $X$ and $Y$ whose merge score equals $F[m][n] = F^*[m][n]$.

**Claim (2).** If $X$ is a valid alignment of $(S_r)_{r \in R_X}$, $Y$ a valid alignment of $(S_r)_{r \in R_Y}$, and $R_X \cap R_Y = \emptyset$, then every block alignment $N$ of $X$ and $Y$ is a valid alignment of $(S_r)_{r \in R_X \cup R_Y}$. Each row of $X$ appears in $N$ with its symbols in order and gaps added in the columns of the third kind, so it still spells its sequence; the same holds for $Y$. All rows of $N$ have one symbol per column of $N$. A column of the first or second kind contains a column of $X$, and a column of the third kind contains a column of $Y$; both have a letter because $X$ and $Y$ are valid. PROGRESSIVE gives each leaf the one-row alignment of its sequence, which is valid, and at each internal node applies GROUP-ALIGN to the alignments of its two children, whose leaf sets are disjoint. By Claim (1) and induction over the tree, the alignment at the root is valid for all $k$ sequences. Line L6 only reorders rows.

**Claim (3).** For $k = 2$ the tree is the single join of the two leaves, and line L5 makes one call of GROUP-ALIGN on the one-row groups $X = (S_1)$ and $Y = (S_2)$. Then $|X|\,|Y| = 1$, and a column $(X_i, Y_j)$ holds the residues $x = (1, i)$ and $y = (2, j)$, so $\mathrm{col}(i, j) = W'(x, y)$. Block alignments of two one-row groups are exactly the pairwise alignments of $S_1$ and $S_2$, and the merge score of such an alignment is the sum of $W'$ over its residue pairs in a common column, which is its library score. By Claim (1), the output has the largest library score. $\blacksquare$
