---
title: ProbCons computes correct posteriors, returns a valid alignment, maximizes expected accuracy at each merge, and never loses accuracy in refinement
proves: [correctness]
status: full
---

A *valid alignment* of sequences $(S_r)_{r \in R}$ is a set of rows of equal length over $\Sigma \cup \{-\}$ such that row $r$ with its gaps removed equals $S_r$, and no column consists of gaps only. For a matrix family $P$ (one matrix $P_{xy}$ for each ordered pair of distinct sequences), the *expected accuracy* of an alignment is $\mathrm{EA}_P = \sum_{x < y} \sum P_{xy}(i, j)$, where the inner sum runs over the columns that hold residue $x_i$ in row $x$ and residue $y_j$ in row $y$.

Let $X$ have columns $X_1, \ldots, X_m$ and $Y$ have columns $Y_1, \ldots, Y_n$. A *block alignment* of $X$ and $Y$ is a sequence of columns, each of the form $(X_i, Y_j)$, $(X_i, \text{gaps})$ or $(\text{gaps}, Y_j)$, in which $X_1, \ldots, X_m$ appear once each in order, and so do $Y_1, \ldots, Y_n$. Its *cross score* is the sum of $\mathrm{col}(i, j)$ over its columns of the first kind, where $\mathrm{col}(i, j)$ is the sum of $P_{xy}(a, b)$ over the rows $x$ of $X$ with residue $x_a$ in $X_i$ and the rows $y$ of $Y$ with residue $y_b$ in $Y_j$. Columns of the other two kinds score 0.

We prove: (1) line L1 computes the posterior $\Pr(x_i \sim y_j \mid x, y)$; (2) the group merge returns a block alignment of $X$ and $Y$ with the largest cross score; (3) the output is a valid alignment of the input; (4) no refinement round lowers $\mathrm{EA}_{P'}$; (5) for $k = 2$ the output maximizes $\mathrm{EA}_{P'}$ over all alignments of the two sequences.

**Claim (1).** A path of the pair-HMM is a sequence of states that emits $x$ and $y$; it corresponds to one pairwise alignment, and its probability is the product of its transition and emission probabilities. By induction on $i + j$, the forward value $f_s(i, j)$ is the sum of the probabilities of the path prefixes that emit $x_1 \ldots x_i$ and $y_1 \ldots y_j$ and end in state $s$: the recurrence adds, over the previous state, the previous value times the transition and the emission of the last step, and every prefix ends with exactly one such step. In the same way, by induction on $(m - i) + (n - j)$, the backward value $b_s(i, j)$ is the sum, over the path suffixes from state $s$ at $(i, j)$ to the end, of the product of their transitions and emissions. A path matches $x_i$ with $y_j$ if and only if it is in state M at $(i, j)$, and then it splits uniquely into a prefix ending in M at $(i, j)$ and a suffix from there. The probabilities multiply, so $f_M(i, j)\, b_M(i, j)$ is the total probability of the paths that match $x_i$ with $y_j$. The total probability of all paths is $\Pr(x, y) = \sum_s f_s(m, n)$. Their quotient is the posterior.

**Claim (2).** Let $F^*[i][j]$ be the largest cross score of a block alignment of $X_1, \ldots, X_i$ and $Y_1, \ldots, Y_j$. Any such block alignment with $i + j > 0$ ends with a column of one of the three kinds, and removing it leaves a block alignment of the prefixes $(i-1, j-1)$, $(i-1, j)$ or $(i, j-1)$. Conversely, adding such a column to a block alignment of the shorter prefixes gives one of the longer prefixes. The cross score is a sum over columns, so

$$F^*[i][j] = \max\bigl(F^*[i-1][j-1] + \mathrm{col}(i, j),\ F^*[i-1][j],\ F^*[i][j-1]\bigr),$$

with the terms present only when their indices are not negative, and $F^*[0][0] = 0$. The table $F$ of the merge satisfies the same recurrence with the same base cases, so $F = F^*$ by induction on $i + j$. The traceback starts at $(m, n)$ and at each cell picks a term that attains $F[i][j]$, so the columns it collects form a block alignment whose cross score is $F[m][n] = F^*[m][n]$.

**Claim (3).** If $k \le 1$ the input is returned as it is, which is valid. Otherwise every alignment that the program builds is a block alignment of two valid alignments $X$ and $Y$ of disjoint sets of sequences. Each column of the block alignment holds a column of $X$ or a column of $Y$, and these hold a residue, so no column is all gaps. Row by row, the columns of $X$ appear in order with gaps between them, so each row of $X$ still spells its sequence; the same holds for $Y$. So a block alignment of two valid alignments is a valid alignment of their union. Leaves of the guide tree are single sequences, which are valid, so by induction over the tree line L5 returns a valid alignment of all the input. In a refinement round, the two groups taken from a valid alignment spell their sequences, and line L7 removes the columns of gaps only, so both groups are valid; by the same argument line L8 returns a valid alignment.

**Claim (4).** Consider one round with groups $A$ and $B$ and the current alignment $C$. Split $\mathrm{EA}_{P'}$ into three parts: the pairs of rows inside $A$, the pairs inside $B$, and the pairs with one row in $A$ and one in $B$. Removing columns of gaps only does not change which residues share a column, and a block alignment keeps every column of $A$ and of $B$ whole, so the first two parts are the same for $C$ and for the new alignment. Now read $C$ column by column. Each column has a part in $A$ and a part in $B$. Drop the columns where both parts are gaps only (there are none, since $C$ is valid). The parts in $A$ that are not gaps only are exactly the columns of $A'$, in order, where $A'$ is $A$ after line L7; the same holds for $B'$. So $C$ is a block alignment of $A'$ and $B'$, and its third part of $\mathrm{EA}_{P'}$ is its cross score. By Claim (2), line L8 returns a block alignment with a cross score at least as large. So $\mathrm{EA}_{P'}$ does not decrease. Rounds in which all rows fall in one group change nothing.

**Claim (5).** For $k = 2$ the guide tree joins the two sequences, and line L5 is one merge of two one-row groups. A block alignment of two one-row groups is any pairwise alignment of the two sequences, and its cross score is $\mathrm{EA}_{P'}$. By Claim (2) the merge maximizes it, and by Claim (4) the refinement rounds keep the maximum. $\blacksquare$
